The Bid Fight Escalates, a Federal Court Slams a Courthouse Door on Marijuana Contracts, and the Tape Gives Back a Month of Gains

Curaleaf and Aurora trade a second round of "myth vs. fact" rebuttals as Aurora makes a defensive hire; the Sixth Circuit tosses a $31.8M verdict against Curaleaf on the theory that federal courts can't enforce marijuana contracts at all, even post-rescheduling; the D.C. No. 1 · M&A & Governance

Curaleaf and Aurora trade a second round of rebuttals — still no new terms, no truce

The hostile-bid fight for Aurora Cannabis moved from filings to open argument this week. On Sept. 9, Curaleaf published a second "myth vs. fact" rebuttal sheet answering Aurora's Sept. 2 rejection, with Chairman/CEO Boris Jordan stating publicly that Aurora "never signed an NDA, never discussed price, and never made a counteroffer" during prior deal talks. Hours later, Aurora announced a new hire — Faris El Refaie, a biopharma veteran of JAMP Pharma, GSK and AstraZeneca, joining as EVP, Global Commercial, effective Sept. 28 — a business-as-usual signal timed squarely inside the takeover fight. Both companies' management separately presented at the ATB Cormark Fall Institutional Investor Conference in New York on Sept. 9 without addressing the bid directly. Deal terms are unchanged: roughly US$4.00/share implied value (0.3463 Curaleaf shares plus US$0.75 cash, capped at US$5.00), no financing condition, expiring 5pm MT Dec. 1, 2026 unless extended. No poison pill, litigation, sweetened terms or rival bid has surfaced from either side this week.

Why it matters to PGP This is now a three-week-old public rebuttal war with no mechanism forcing resolution before December — a live template for hostile-bid defense mechanics in cannabis M&A (dueling fact sheets, a defensive hire, proxy solicitors retained on both sides: Carson Proxy Advisors for Curaleaf, Kingsdale Advisors for Aurora) that PGP's advisory practice should be cataloguing as a playbook. The lack of a poison pill or litigation six weeks in suggests Aurora is betting on shareholder inertia rather than a structural block — worth testing in any similar defense mandate.
No. 2 · Federal Courts

The Sixth Circuit closes a courthouse door on marijuana contracts — even under Schedule III

In a ruling with implications well beyond the parties, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit on Sept. 10 reversed a $31.8 million jury verdict Michigan grower Hello Farms had won against Curaleaf subsidiaries GR Vending MI and CURA MI in a breach-of-contract dispute. The panel held that federal courts cannot enforce contracts for marijuana sales because the underlying conduct remains a federal crime — a holding the court found unaffected by marijuana's move to Schedule III under the DOJ's April 2026 interim order, since Schedule III substances are still federally controlled and unlicensed distribution remains illegal. The ruling binds Michigan, Ohio, Kentucky and Tennessee. Curaleaf called the outcome favorable while reaffirming its support for federal reform.

Why it matters to PGP Diligence teams have been quietly assuming Schedule III narrows the "illegal contract" defense that has occasionally let cannabis counterparties walk away from deals. This ruling says otherwise, at least in the Sixth Circuit: federal contract enforcement stays closed until the plant is fully de-scheduled or rescheduled with a functioning legal distribution channel. Any M&A or supply agreement relying on federal-court enforceability as a backstop needs a state-court forum clause and, ideally, arbitration — this is a live drafting point, not a theoretical one.
No. 3 · Federal Regulatory

A court protects the existing rescheduling order — while the GAO confirms banking access still won't follow

The D.C. Circuit on Sept. 9 denied a motion to stay the DOJ's April 2026 order moving state-licensed medical and FDA-approved marijuana products to Schedule III, finding the challengers — a drug-testing trade association and a pharmaceutical company — "have not satisfied the stringent requirements for a stay pending review." Two medical-cannabis companies seeking to intervene on the government's side were also turned away. The ruling protects the existing narrow reclassification; it does not touch the separate, larger fight over full-industry Schedule I→III rescheduling, which remains with DEA Chief ALJ Derek Julius nearly eight weeks after hearings closed with no recommended decision in sight. Layered on top, the GAO's Sept. 8 report (GAO-26-107498) on cannabis banking access found no bank has ever been penalized solely for a cannabis relationship — the real deterrent is compliance cost, with roughly 11% of federally insured banks filing marijuana-related SARs in 2024 and some operators paying $100,000+/year in account fees — and follow-on coverage concluded Visa and Mastercard are unlikely to loosen card-network restrictions even after further rescheduling, since those limits are a matter of private risk policy, not federal drug law.

No. 4 · Tax & Litigation

TerrAscend faces the industry's first known federal 280E clawback suit

The Department of Justice's suit against TerrAscend, filed May 18, 2026 and seeking the return of roughly $8.3–8.4 million in disputed tax refunds tied to an amended 2020 return, is understood to be the first federal action of its kind seeking to claw back a 280E-related refund from a cannabis operator. This week's development: TerrAscend's response deadline, previously due imminently, was reported extended to Sept. 18, 2026. Separately, Cresco Labs, Green Thumb Industries and Verano are named jointly (not Cresco alone, as earlier reporting suggested) in a 40-plaintiff RICO and consumer-protection class action in the Northern District of Illinois alleging deceptive marketing of cannabis products as treatment for anxiety, depression, PTSD and pain; Cresco's insurer, Palomar Excess & Surplus, continues a separate suit arguing its policy — capped at $5M per claim and excluding RICO claims — owes no duty to defend.

No. 5 · Markets

MSOS gives back a month of gains, down roughly 7% on the week

The AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF (MSOS) closed the week near $4.75, down roughly 7.0% from last Friday's $5.11 close — its worst week since this desk began tracking the current rally. The decline was broad rather than concentrated: Planet 13 (-13.3%), Glass House Brands (-9.8%), TerrAscend (-8.6%), Cresco Labs (-8.2%) and Trulieve (-6.9%) led the laggards, while Curaleaf, Aurora, Verano and Vireo Growth all eased 3–5%. Ascend Wellness was the sole confirmed gainer, up roughly 4.3%. Trulieve's Wednesday selloff (-5.31% to $11.77, no found catalyst) appears to have set the tone for a broader risk-off drift into Thursday and Friday, compounded by the unresolved Curaleaf-Aurora overhang and a week of unfavorable federal-court and tax headlines rather than any single sector-wide catalyst.

The Rescheduling Docket

A court shields the existing order; a federal 280E clawback suit raises the stakes

Broad Schedule I→III rescheduling still has no fixed timeline, but the week's federal-court and tax news moved the ground under it in both directions.

  • Sep 8, 2026 GAO publishes GAO-26-107498 on cannabis banking access — no bank penalized solely for a cannabis relationship, but compliance cost and account fees remain the practical barrier.
  • Sep 9, 2026 Follow-on coverage finds Visa and Mastercard unlikely to loosen cannabis payment-network restrictions even after further rescheduling, since card-network limits are private risk policy, not federal drug law.
  • Sep 9, 2026 The D.C. Circuit denies a stay motion against the DOJ's April 2026 order moving medical/FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III — the existing narrower reclassification stays in effect.
  • Sep 9-10, 2026 Curaleaf issues a second rebuttal sheet in the Aurora bid fight; Aurora names a new EVP, Global Commercial as a defensive signal.
  • Sep 10, 2026 The Sixth Circuit reverses a $31.8M jury verdict against Curaleaf subsidiaries, holding federal courts cannot enforce marijuana sale contracts even under the interim Schedule III order.
  • Sep 10, 2026 TerrAscend's deadline to respond to the DOJ's federal 280E clawback lawsuit ($8.3-8.4M in disputed refunds) is reported extended to Sep 18.
  • Sep 10, 2026 A federal court upholds Hawaii's stricter hemp regulations against a preemption challenge.
  • Sep 10-11, 2026 Federal data show Q2 2026 state marijuana tax collections reached $986.8M (+11% YoY, ~$1.84B YTD through June); Census Bureau data separately put cumulative state marijuana tax revenue at $15.8B over five years, $3.5B in the last year alone.
