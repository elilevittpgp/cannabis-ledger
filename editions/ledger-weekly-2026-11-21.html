The AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF (NYSE Arca: MSOS) closed Friday, Nov. 20 at $3.14, down 15.6% from last Friday's $3.72 close and down roughly 20.3% month-to-date — its second consecutive double-digit weekly decline — as two distinct, cannabis-unrelated market shocks (Wednesday-into-Thursday profit-taking after Anthropic's blockbuster Nasdaq debut, then a Friday semiconductor/AI-capex-led rout that pushed the Nasdaq negative for 2026) dragged nearly every constituent lower in unison. The week's most dramatic single name was Glass House Brands, which filed on-deadline Q3 results Monday night that triggered a roughly 31% after-hours pop, extended to a nearly 29% intraday rally Tuesday, then violently reversed — down 17.47% Wednesday on a margin-compression miss and a pair of analyst downgrades, then a further 11.68% Thursday after securities-litigation firm Highful Law PLLC announced an investigation into the company's disclosures, joined Friday by a second firm, Kirby McInerney LLP — before the round trip left the stock down a comparatively modest 7.1% net on the week. Off the stock-price drama, Illinois's hemp-THC fight took a sharp turn against the industry: U.S. District Judge Martha Pacold denied, without prejudice, the hemp coalition's bid to block the state's ban, leaving it in force since Nov. 12 as plaintiffs weigh an appeal ahead of a Dec. 15 hearing — even as two separate groups of medical-marijuana operators sued the DEA this week over a Nov. 29 "Timely Applicant" supply-chain compliance deadline they say threatens real shortages. Ascend Wellness provided the week's lone unambiguous growth headline, announcing Friday that subsidiary Ohio Patient Access will acquire a cultivation facility and five dispensaries in Ohio for $35.5 million in cash — even as its stock fell to a 52-week low and its board-approved reverse stock split remains unset 85 days after shareholder approval. And in Washington, the federal hemp-THC fight hardened rather than resolved: the bipartisan Sheehy-Klobuchar "Hemp Guardrails Act" was introduced Tuesday, only for two Republican senators to vow the next day to block any government-funding bill that keeps hemp-derived THC legal — a standoff that now shares its Dec. 11 deadline with the government funding bill itself.

No. 1 · Market Shock · Two Routs, One Week

MSOS falls 15.6% on the week — its second straight double-digit loss — as an Anthropic-IPO hangover and a semiconductor-led AI-capex scare both hit Wall Street broadly, with nearly no cannabis-specific catalyst behind either move

MSOS closed Friday, Nov. 20 at $3.14 on volume of roughly 11.2 million shares, down 15.6% from last Friday's $3.72 close and down about 20.3% versus its Oct. 30 month-to-date baseline of $3.94. The decline built through the week — down 0.81% Monday, 1.08% Tuesday, 1.92% Wednesday — before accelerating sharply into two outsized sessions: a 5.59% drop Thursday, Nov. 19, as broad profit-taking followed Anthropic's blockbuster Nasdaq debut (shares opened as much as 34% above its offer price and closed up 17%, valuing the company near $1.76 trillion, while the Dow fell 1,084 points and the VIX spiked to 29), and a 7.10% drop Friday, Nov. 20, as a semiconductor-led AI-infrastructure-ROI scare sent Nvidia, Micron, AMD and Broadcom all down double digits, pushed the S&P 500 and Dow into correction territory, and erased the Nasdaq Composite's entire 2026 gain in its worst week in more than six years. Nearly every checked MSO constituent fell in both sessions with no distinct company-specific news attached to most of the move — this desk and multiple wire services tie both routs to broad risk-off sentiment spilling over from outside the sector, not to any cannabis catalyst. TerrAscend (-9.81% Friday), Verano (-8.70%) and Planet 13 (-8.58%) were among Friday's sharpest single-day decliners; for the full week, Organigram (-30.1%), Cresco Labs (-26.0%) and Verano (-22.1%) led the constituent list lower, while Glass House Brands — despite a week of extreme single-name volatility — finished as the "least-bad" liquid decliner at -7.1%, essentially in line with the index.

Why it matters to PGP For the second straight week, the dominant driver of MSO-exposed price action was broad market risk sentiment, not sector fundamentals — Thursday's and Friday's routs both originated in AI/tech-capex concerns with no cannabis-specific thread. PGP should continue to discount this month's price moves when read-through to valuation work: a two-week, roughly 29% cumulative MSOS decline (from Nov. 6's $4.24 close to Nov. 20's $3.14) coincides with a period in which multiple MSOs posted in-line-to-better Q3 results, an active M&A calendar kept advancing on schedule, and Ascend added an accretive acquisition — suggesting current MSO-complex pricing may understate company-specific progress for clients evaluating entry points or financing terms this quarter.
No. 2 · Governance · Volatility

Glass House Brands whipsaws from a 31% after-hours pop to a 17.47% crash to a second securities-litigation notice — and still finishes the week as the sector's least-bad decliner

Glass House Brands filed its Q3 2026 results on deadline Monday night, Nov. 16: revenue of $56.8 million (up 117% year-over-year on record biomass production of roughly 319,000 lbs), adjusted EBITDA turning positive at $0.8 million, and an EPS miss of a single penny (-$0.14 versus a -$0.13 estimate) — alongside the board's explanation that new auditor MGO (reappointed Nov. 6, after BDO's disputed resignation over deconsolidation accounting) had already reviewed the same accounting cleanly during its Q2 2026 engagement. Shares surged as much as 31% after hours on roughly 3.6 million shares of volume, then extended the rally Tuesday to as high as $1.66, up nearly 29% intraday on close to 9.6 million shares traded — before reversing hard Wednesday, falling 17.47% to $1.37 on revealed gross-margin compression (26% versus 34% in Q2, as average selling price fell to $151/lb from $211/lb even as production cost per pound fell to $109 from $122) and a same-week Zacks downgrade to Strong Sell plus a Lake Street price-target cut to $3 from $7. The stock fell a further 11.68% Thursday after securities-litigation firm Highful Law PLLC announced an investigation into whether the company's June 2026 retail-business deconsolidation disclosures were misleading; a second firm, Kirby McInerney LLP, issued a similar investor-rights notice Friday, Nov. 20, tied to the same disclosures and to BDO's Nov. 11 auditor resignation. Shares closed the week at $1.18, down 2.48% Friday and down 7.1% net for the week — a comparatively modest net move given the scale of the intraweek swings.

Why it matters to PGP Two securities-litigation notices in two days, layered on top of an already-disputed auditor swap, make Glass House's disclosure history — not its operating results, which were arguably the week's best print among liquid MSOs — the live diligence item. PGP should treat any Glass House-referenced comp or financing discussion this quarter as contingent on resolution of the deconsolidation-accounting question, and should not read the stock's relatively calm net weekly move as evidence the issue has cooled; it reflects a violent round trip, not a return to normal trading.
No. 3 · Regulatory · Hemp & DEA

An Illinois judge lets the state's hemp-THC ban stand, while two separate lawsuits hit the DEA over a Nov. 29 medical-supply-chain deadline

U.S. District Judge Martha Pacold heard oral arguments Thursday, Nov. 19 on the Illinois Healthy Alternatives Association's bid — on behalf of roughly 250 member businesses — to block the state's hemp-THC ban, then denied the motion without prejudice, letting the ban (reclassifying hemp products above 0.4 mg THC per container as cannabis, sellable only through licensed dispensaries) stand as it has since its Nov. 12 effective date. The plaintiffs can refile, and the case's next hearing is set for Dec. 15; a Seventh Circuit appeal remains under consideration. Separately, three medical-marijuana operators — Standard Wellness (Utah), Verde Americano (Arizona) and NW Confections (Oklahoma) — petitioned the D.C. Circuit on Wednesday, Nov. 19 to pause the DEA's Oct. 30 "Medical Marijuana Timely Applicant Information" guidance, which bars "timely applicant" operators from transacting with "non-timely applicant" operators ahead of a Nov. 29 compliance deadline; they are seeking a ruling by Nov. 25. A second, similar group of petitioners filed an overlapping challenge Friday, Nov. 20. Both suits argue the guidance was adopted without notice-and-comment rulemaking and ignores state-market realities; state regulators, including in Massachusetts, say the DEA has given no implementation guidance, raising the risk of real medical-dispensary supply shortages if the Nov. 29 deadline takes effect unresolved. On the legislative side, Missouri's new intoxicating-cannabinoid law kept producing real-world closures, with cumulative state cannabis tax revenue separately reported to have topped $1 billion this month.

No. 4 · M&A · Growth

Ascend Wellness announces a $35.5 million Ohio acquisition even as its stock hits a 52-week low and its reverse split stays unset

Ascend Wellness disclosed Friday, Nov. 20 that wholly owned subsidiary Ohio Patient Access signed an asset purchase agreement to acquire a cultivation facility and five dispensaries in Ohio for $35.5 million in cash, structured as $3.55 million upfront, $4.07 million upon management-services-agreement effectiveness, $15.15 million at closing, and $6.23 million plus $6.5 million in deferred payments — subject to regulatory approval. Under the structure, Ohio Patient Access itself takes the cultivation facility and the Akron retail store, while a third-party buyer, financed and supported by Ascend, takes the other four dispensaries. The deal lands as Ascend's stock continues to slide: shares touched a 52-week low of roughly $0.33 on Thursday, Nov. 19, and closed the week around $0.3245, down 4.56% Friday alone, with no company-specific negative catalyst identified beyond the broad sector rout. The board-approved 1-for-10-to-1-for-50 reverse stock split, authorized by shareholders Aug. 28, remains unset on both ratio and timing — now 85 days out — with the board's authority running through Aug. 28, 2027, or an earlier national-exchange uplisting, whichever comes first. No new Teamsters Local 916 labor developments surfaced this week.

No. 5 · Federal Policy · Hemp Standoff

The Hemp Guardrails Act lands in Congress — and a GOP blockade threat lands a day later, tying the hemp fight to the Dec. 11 government-funding deadline

Sens. Tim Sheehy (R-Mont.) and Amy Klobuchar (D-Minn.) introduced the bipartisan "Hemp Guardrails Act" (S. 5736) Tuesday, Nov. 17, proposing a 1%-total-THC-by-dry-weight threshold, legal delta-9 beverages under a regulated framework, a ban on delta-8/delta-10/THC-O and smokable hemp from interstate commerce, and a 21-plus purchase age — aimed at the Dec. 11 federal "total THC" deadline that is now embedded in the same continuing resolution that funds the government through that date. The next day, Sens. Ted Budd (R-N.C.) and Pete Ricketts (R-Neb.) vowed to block any funding bill that keeps hemp-derived THC products legal, with later reporting clarifying their objection extends specifically to the Guardrails Act's regulatory framework, not merely to a delay. Separately, Smart Approaches to Marijuana's Kevin Sabet called the bill "dead on arrival," citing resistance inside both sponsors' own caucuses. New economic estimates circulating this week peg the ban's potential impact at roughly 38,000 businesses and $28 billion in sales nationally; the Hemp Beverage Alliance ran a Washington lobbying push Nov. 17-18, and industry voices increasingly frame the real negotiating window as the Thanksgiving-to-Christmas stretch rather than before. The SAFE Banking Act saw no movement this week — no hearing has been scheduled since 16 Democratic governors' Nov. 9 letter to Congress, and Senate Majority Leader Thune continues to call attaching cannabis banking to other legislation a "non-starter."
