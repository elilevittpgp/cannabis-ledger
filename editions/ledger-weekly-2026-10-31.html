No. 1 · Regulatory · Rescheduling

The DEA hands out its first Schedule III registrations — a concrete milestone the stayed ALJ docket itself still can't produce

On Oct. 30, the DEA and DOJ announced the first wave of actual federal Schedule III registrations, approving more than 900 state-licensed medical dispensaries across seven states — Alabama, Florida, Mississippi, South Dakota, Texas, Virginia and West Virginia — under the authority of DOJ's April 23, 2026 order that had already moved medical and FDA-approved marijuana products to Schedule III. The move follows an Oct. 29 DEA release listing nearly 7,000 businesses that have applied for the same registration, and came paired with a public DEA warning telling medical cannabis businesses to source product only from federally registered companies. Separately, and further back in the formal rescheduling litigation, the broader Schedule I-to-III docket before Chief ALJ Derek Julius stayed quiet this week on its own merits: the DEA's own Oct. 29 supplemental brief argued the admitted GAO report (GAO-26-108623) deserves "no weight," while three opposing filings — from NDASA/DUID Victim Voices/Dr. Kenneth Finn, SAM alongside Idaho, Indiana and Nebraska, and the Tennessee Bureau of Investigation — argued the opposite; reply briefs are due Nov. 5.

Why it matters to PGP The first tranche of Schedule III registrations is a narrower, state-and-medical-specific action than the industry-wide reclassification still stalled before ALJ Julius, but it is the first time the April 23 order has translated into registered businesses on the ground — in exactly the kind of state-licensed medical markets (AL, FL, MS, SD, TX, VA, WV) where 280E relief and banking access matter most to deal economics. For PGP clients with assets or diligence exposure in those seven states, this is now a checkable fact rather than a hypothetical: has the target applied, and if registered, has it already seen a 280E or banking-access benefit flow through its books.
No. 2 · Markets · Single-Session

A broad, cannabis-unrelated Wall Street selloff erases four days of gains in one Friday session

MSOS rallied in four of five sessions this week — Monday's close near $3.97, Tuesday's $4.04, a modest Wednesday pullback to roughly $3.99-$4.00, and a Thursday close at $4.08 — before a broad, cannabis-unrelated "AI reckoning" selloff across names like Amazon, Alphabet and Intel dragged the whole sector down with it Friday, Oct. 30: MSOS fell 5.39% to $3.86 on no cannabis-specific headline. Planet 13 (-14.49% that single session, -24.2% on the week to $0.072) and Vireo Growth (-12.03% that session, -14.9% on the week to $6.95) — the two companies mid-merger with each other — absorbed the sharpest losses, as the market continued to discount both names' debt-funded roll-up risk (Fluent, Planet 13, Cannabist assets) three weeks after Vireo's Form S-4 for the Planet 13 exchange still had not gone effective. Net for the week, MSOS finished down just 1.3% from last Friday's $3.91 close — a deceptively mild headline number for a week that round-tripped through a seven-week-plus high.

No. 3 · M&A & Governance

Curaleaf loses a round at the Alberta Securities Commission — and both stocks rally anyway

The Alberta Securities Commission dismissed Curaleaf's application to halt Aurora Cannabis's up-to-$100 million at-the-market share program on Oct. 26 — a procedural win for Aurora in the hostile-bid standoff, though the ruling addresses only the ATM dilution challenge, not the underlying takeover fight. Curaleaf Chairman and CEO Boris Jordan responded publicly the next day that the ruling "does not" change the fundamental takeover debate, reiterating Curaleaf's claims that Aurora's ATM pricing undercut its own enhanced $5.00/share offer and that Aurora's board granted BAT-backed Organigram Global diligence access denied to Curaleaf. No signed Organigram term sheet, letter of intent, or bid price has surfaced, and Curaleaf's tender deadline stands at Dec. 4. Despite the setback, CURLF rallied 6.1% on the week to $8.36 and ACB gained 3.6% to $4.02 — both outpacing an otherwise negative tape.

Why it matters to PGP A target's board winning a procedural fight over financing mechanics, while its hostile acquirer's stock still rallies, tells PGP more about market expectations for the ultimate outcome than the ruling itself does — investors appear to be pricing continued escalation, not a Curaleaf retreat. With Aurora's own Q2 FY2027 earnings call now set for Nov. 12 and Curaleaf reporting its own Q3 2026 results Nov. 3, both companies get a fresh public forum to restate their cases within the next two weeks, ahead of the Dec. 4 tender deadline — worth diarizing both calls specifically for strategic-alternatives language.
No. 4 · Earnings

Verano's best quarter in years draws a shrug from the market

Verano Holdings reported Q3 2026 revenue of $212.4 million on Oct. 29, up 5% year-over-year and ahead of the roughly $211.1 million consensus estimate. Net loss narrowed sharply to $4.1 million from $43.8 million a year earlier; adjusted EBITDA came in at $50.4 million (24% margin); and operating cash flow hit $49 million, the company's highest quarterly level in four years. Retail revenue rose 9% to $179 million, with 11 of 13 markets posting gains. The stock's reaction was muted — VRNO finished the week essentially flat, up just 0.2% to $5.01 — even as the company also opened its 18th Pennsylvania dispensary (Zen Leaf Ephrata) and faces no new developments this week in the pending Ambrozewski v. Verano Holdings data-breach suit (N.D. Ill., Case No. 1:2026-cv-11884).

No. 5 · Governance & Earnings

Cresco's redomicile vote goes undisclosed, and Jushi's record wholesale quarter can't stop the stock from falling

Cresco Labs held its special shareholder meeting Oct. 30 on a board-recommended Delaware "TopCo" redomicile (a BCBCA plan of arrangement) and a multiple-voting-share sunset extension; as of this edition, no voting-results press release or SEC 6-K has been filed confirming whether the resolutions passed — an ordinary 1-to-5-business-day lag per the company's past practice, but one this desk could not close out this week. CRLBF fell 5.0% on the week to $0.57 amid the uncertainty. Separately, Jushi Holdings' Q3 2026 print (reported Oct. 28, after close) showed revenue of $70.5 million, up 7% year-over-year, with wholesale revenue jumping 40% to a record $9.7 million and record retail revenue in both Ohio and Virginia; net loss narrowed to $12.5 million from $23.7 million. The stock nonetheless fell roughly 8% across the two sessions following the print, on an apparent miss versus per-share estimates.

The Rescheduling Docket

The first actual Schedule III registrations land, even as the formal ALJ docket stays stayed

Two parallel threads moved this week: a concrete administrative rollout under the April 2026 order, and continued paper-only sparring in the stayed Julius docket ahead of Nov. 5 reply briefs.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E.
  • Jul 15, 2026 Broader Schedule I→III hearings conclude before ALJ Derek Julius.
  • Sep 23, 2026 GAO publishes report GAO-26-108623, finding DEA's scheduling decisions aligned with HHS recommendations in all 84 cases reviewed, but flagging a lack of written DEA/FDA procedural policy.
  • Oct 22, 2026 ALJ Julius admits the GAO report into the record but denies reopening the hearing for new testimony/discovery; orders supplemental briefing due Oct 29, replies Nov 5.
  • Oct 29, 2026 Supplemental briefs filed: the DEA itself argues the GAO report deserves "no weight"; three opposing filings (NDASA/DUID Victim Voices/Dr. Kenneth Finn; SAM with Idaho/Indiana/Nebraska; Tennessee Bureau of Investigation) argue the opposite. Separately, the DEA publishes a list of nearly 7,000 businesses that have applied for Schedule III registration.
  • Oct 30, 2026 DEA/DOJ grants the first wave of actual Schedule III registrations to 900+ state-licensed medical dispensaries across seven states (AL, FL, MS, SD, TX, VA, WV); DEA issues a public warning to source only from registered businesses.
  • Oct 31, 2026 No new docket filing or ruling found; the case remains formally stayed into the Nov 5 reply-brief deadline.
