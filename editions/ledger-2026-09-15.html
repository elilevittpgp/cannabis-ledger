Washington Tries Banking Reform Again — While California Asks What Rescheduling Actually Fixed

A bipartisan Senate bill revives SAFE Banking the same day California regulators convene on payment-processing fallout, a week after federal auditors found the card networks won't budge regardless.

Two long-running cannabis-banking threads collided this week. On Monday, Sens. Catherine Cortez Masto (D-NV) and Jeff Merkley (D-OR) reintroduced the SAFE Banking Act in the Senate — the latest attempt to bar federal regulators from penalizing banks that serve state-legal cannabis businesses — a companion to the House version Rep. Dave Joyce and colleagues reintroduced back in June. The timing is awkward: April's DOJ order already moved medical/FDA-approved cannabis to Schedule III, and last week's GAO report (GAO-26-107498) found no bank has ever been penalized solely for a cannabis relationship — the real deterrent is compliance cost, not legal exposure. More pointedly, GAO's follow-on reporting found Visa and Mastercard are unlikely to loosen their own payment-network restrictions even after further rescheduling, since those limits sit in private card-network risk policy, not federal drug law. SAFE Banking targets deposit accounts, not card rails — a gap the bill does not close.

The reintroduction lands the same day California's Cannabis Advisory Committee convenes by teleconference specifically to discuss federal rescheduling status and the payment-processing constraints still facing licensees — a state-level acknowledgment that federal reclassification alone hasn't solved the access problem; no readout was available as of this edition. Meanwhile DEA Chief ALJ Derek Julius still has not issued a recommended decision on full Schedule I-to-III reclassification, now roughly eight weeks past the July 15 close of hearings. And the Curaleaf-Aurora Cannabis hostile-bid fight sits in an unusual holding pattern: Curaleaf's Sept 14 application to the Alberta Securities Commission seeking to halt Aurora's at-the-market share issuances has drawn no hearing date or ruling as of this morning. Ascend, Verano and Premier Group/PCF all stayed quiet.

Why it matters to PGP SAFE Banking's reintroduction is a low-probability near-term catalyst — it has cleared committee before without finding 60 votes on the floor — but GAO's card-network finding is the more actionable data point for diligence: assume payment-processing friction survives both further rescheduling and any eventual SAFE Banking passage, and price it into working-capital and integration-risk assumptions on any deal. Separately, the still-unresolved ASC dispute is worth tracking as a template for defensive-ATM disputes in future contested cannabis bids, even without a ruling yet.
