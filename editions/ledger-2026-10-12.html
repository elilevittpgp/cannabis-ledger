Page One · The Big Story

MSOS Slides to $4.20 on the Eve of the DEA's Pivotal Oct. 13 Deadline, as Glass House's Unexplained Rout Rolls Into a Third Session and Verano Drops 4.2% With the Pack

A broad, one-directional down day across the MSO complex — not a single-name story — with almost every constituent lower in lockstep as the government's response to the stayed rescheduling case comes due tomorrow. The priority desk (Ascend, Verano, Premier Group/PCF) stayed quiet on substance; this desk also corrects its own prior framing of a Verano debt maturity that turns out to have already been resolved.

The sector tape opened the week lower across the board. MSOS fell 3.23% Monday to $4.20 (from Friday's $4.34 close), per stockanalysis.com with Yahoo Finance and Finviz confirming the figure — extending the ETF's decline to 4.33% on the week and 11.58% month-to-date, on below-average volume of roughly 2.8M shares. No single headline explains the move; this desk reads it as positioning ahead of the DEA's response, due tomorrow, Oct. 13, to ALJ Derek Julius's order on whether to reopen the stayed Schedule I-to-III rescheduling record to admit a new GAO report. Glass House Brands (NYSE: GLAS) fell a further 4.23% to $4.08 — its third straight down session since Oct. 8's unexplained 15.6% plunge — with the only company filing in the window an administrative Form 6-K formalizing CFO Mark Vendetti's already-announced retirement, not a new catalyst. Verano Holdings (OTC: VRNOF) fell 4.21% to $5.01, moving with the broader tape rather than on any identified company-specific news.

On the priority desk, Ascend, Verano and Premier Group/PCF all stayed quiet on substance over the weekend into Monday. The Curaleaf-Aurora-Organigram saga also sits exactly where it did Friday: Curaleaf (market cap $2.6B) repeated its "outraged" framing of reportedly being denied the due-diligence access extended to Organigram Global (~$200M market cap, backed by British American Tobacco) in reported rival merger talks with Aurora, but neither Aurora nor Organigram has confirmed the talks on the record, and no special-committee recommendation on Curaleaf's enhanced $5.00/share bid has surfaced since it was formally filed Oct. 6. Separately, this desk is correcting the record on a figure it has carried for several editions: Verano's CFO-flagged "~$350M October debt maturity" traces to the extended maturity date on a legacy October 2022 credit facility that was in fact paid off and terminated back on March 12, 2026, when Verano closed a new $195M senior secured term loan (9.5%, maturing 2029) and drew the remaining $50M on its revolver — there is no live balance maturing this month on that facility.

Why it matters to PGP A sector-wide, catalyst-free down day the trading session before a binding regulatory deadline is itself a signal — it suggests the market is pricing event risk around the DEA's Oct. 13 filing rather than reacting to fundamentals, which matters for timing any PGP-advised process that touches sector sentiment. Separately, this desk's own correction on Verano's debt maturity is a reminder to re-verify every carried-forward "open item" against the underlying filing before it's used in client-facing diligence — a stale CFO comment from an investor call can outlive the facility it described by months.

The Rescheduling Docket

One day out: the DEA's Oct. 13 response comes due

No filing has leaked or been previewed ahead of tomorrow's deadline. The sector tape's broad Monday decline looks like positioning around it rather than a reaction to any new document.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E.
  • Jul 15, 2026 Broader Schedule I→III hearings conclude before ALJ Derek Julius.
  • Sep 23, 2026 GAO publishes report GAO-26-108623, finding DEA's scheduling decisions have aligned with HHS recommendations but flagging a lack of written DEA/FDA procedural policies — it does not oppose rescheduling on the merits.
  • Sep 28-29, 2026 Rescheduling opponents move to reopen the record to admit the GAO report; ALJ Julius stays the case and orders DEA to file a response of up to 20 pages by Oct 13.
  • Oct 7, 2026 DEA rolls out specialized Form 225 Schedule III registration paperwork for medical-cannabis manufacturers, distributors and labs — an implementation step distinct from the stayed hearing.
  • Oct 7-9, 2026 State cannabis regulators — named officials in Georgia, Vermont, and California — publicly describe DEA "radio silence" on warning labels, registration, tax treatment, and packaging guidance ahead of the Oct 13 deadline.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. Oct 13, 2026 (tomorrow) — DEA's response to the motion to reopen the record (GAO report) is due.
  2. ALJ Julius rules on whether to admit the GAO report and permit added briefing.
  3. If granted, a further briefing round precedes the recommended decision; if denied, the case resumes toward a decision on the existing record.
  4. A 20-day exceptions window, then the DEA Administrator's final call — sell-side analysts have modeled 1H 2027 as the more likely resolution window.
  5. Nov 3, 2026 (22 days out) — Massachusetts voters decide Question 8, which would end licensed adult-use sales as of Jan 1, 2028 if it passes; a Sept 25 UNH poll put opposition at 71%, and the "No" side has also taken a fundraising lead.
  6. Separately: the SAFE Banking Act of 2026 (S.4942) remains parked in the Senate Banking Committee with no markup or floor vote scheduled.
  7. The federal hemp-THC ban takes effect Dec 11, 2026, capping total THC at 0.4mg per container; synthetic cannabinoids (Delta-8, THC-P) are already banned as of Nov 12.
PGP takeaway A binding, government-must-respond deadline landing with zero advance signal is exactly the kind of event risk that produces sector-wide, catalyst-free price action the day before — worth noting as a pattern (not just today's MSOS move) when timing any PGP-advised transaction around a known regulatory date. Whatever the DEA files tomorrow should be read first for procedure (does the GAO report get admitted) rather than outcome, since either path still routes through briefing, a recommended decision, and a 20-day exceptions window before anything is final.
