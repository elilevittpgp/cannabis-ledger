The Hemp-THC Cliff: A $28 Billion Sector Gets a Statutory Clock

The Senate buys time to Dec. 11 rather than ban outright — and every hemp-derived revenue line now trades on a countdown.

The dominant story across the sector this week is not a deal — it is a deadline. The U.S. Senate voted to delay implementation of a federal hemp-THC ban until December 11, 2026, giving lawmakers a narrow window to write regulation instead of prohibition. A bipartisan pair of lawmakers followed with a bill that would keep hemp-THC beverages federally legal and regulate them like alcohol — carving the drinks category out as a potential survivor.

The pressure is already reaching the rails that move product: payments processor Square told merchants to stop selling hemp and CBD ahead of the ban, a live signal that banking and payments tighten before the legal date arrives. The industry is not united — some cannabis groups quietly favor the ban to kill hemp competition, drawing an op-ed rebuke against "arming prohibitionists." Meanwhile Curaleaf is exiting the hemp-THC market altogether.

Why it matters to PGP A ~$28B hemp sector now sits on a legislative countdown. Expect (1) compressed, uncertain valuations on hemp-THC and beverage assets through year-end; (2) forced-seller and distressed opportunities as payments/banking access erodes; and (3) a hard diligence question on every mandate — does this revenue line survive December 11? Beverage assets with an "alcohol-style" regulatory path are the relative winners; smokable and edible hemp-THC exposure is the relative loser.

The benchmark ETF, and what's moving it

The AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF (NYSE Arca: MSOS) is the primary institutional proxy for U.S. plant-touching MSOs. The Rescheduling Docket

Schedule I → III: hearings closed, decision pending

Medical/FDA-approved products already moved to Schedule III; the broader reclassification now sits with the DEA's administrative law judge.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E.
  • Jun 24–29, 2026 DEA ALJ hearings open; opponents call the move "solely political." SAFER Banking reintroduced in both chambers (Jun 25).
  • Jul 15, 2026 Hearings conclude — a CA county sheriff testifies in support of regulated cannabis; no outcome issued.
  • Jun–Aug, 2026 States maneuver: Wyoming opts out (Jul 8), Louisiana drops its challenge (Jun 26), Alabama signals a partial opt-out (Aug 3) — fragmenting 280E & interstate treatment.
