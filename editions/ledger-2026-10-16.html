Page One · The Big Story

This Desk Corrects Its Own Record on Premier Group/PCF — Quietly Absorbed Into March and Ash Five Weeks Ago — as the Sector Tape Craters Anew

A routine license sweep today turned up something this desk had missed for a month: Premier Group/PCF, covered in this space as a privately held California operator with "no M&A," appears to have merged into San Diego's March and Ash chain on Sept. 11 — a deal found only in secondary trade coverage, with no primary wire release yet located. Separately, MSOS fell another 4.4% to $3.94 on a broad, catalyst-free decline, with Glass House Brands cratering 12.4% despite this week's positive export-certification news.

Start with the correction. This desk's standing line on Premier Group/PCF — the privately held Modesto-Ceres operator behind PCF Ceres, Firehouse Turlock, X1 Tracy and sister dispensaries — has for months been "not public, no earnings, no M&A." Today's routine California Dept. of Cannabis Control and local-press sweep turned up nothing new in-window, but a broader search surfaced secondary trade coverage (San Diego Business Journal, MJBizDaily) reporting that San Diego-based retailer March and Ash announced a merger on Sept. 11, 2026 adding "Pacafi, Inc." — operating under the trade name The Premier Group — and its 19 licensed California dispensaries to March and Ash's network. The 19-store count matches Premier Group's own stated footprint exactly, and the DCC's license registry confirms Patient Care First (PCF) in Ceres is held under an entity doing business as Pacafi/Premier Group — corroboration this desk views as reasonably strong, even without a primary wire release. Both the SDBJ article and an MJBizDaily reference returned paywall/bot-block errors (HTTP 402 and 403) on direct fetch, so no deal terms, consideration, or closing status have been independently confirmed; this is a finding to track, not yet a closed transaction this desk can price. Going forward, this desk will treat "privately held, no M&A" as an outdated framing for this name pending primary confirmation.

On the tape, MSOS extended its slide to $3.94 intraday Friday (around 1:45-1:50pm ET), down 4.37% on still-below-average volume (roughly 2.25-2.36 million shares against a ~4.6 million average) — a decline with no single clean catalyst, but one where every tracked constituent fell. Glass House Brands did the most damage among single names, down 12.4% to $3.25 even after Tuesday's Good Agricultural and Collection Practice (GACP) export certification for its Casitas and Padaro farms; a reported 89.6% jump in short interest (to 378,723 shares as of the Sept. 30 settlement date) looks like a more plausible explanation than any fresh negative news. Curaleaf led large-cap losses, down 5.14% to $7.94, with no new catalyst found beyond the still-unresolved Aurora Cannabis bid overhang.

Why it matters to PGP If a name this desk has tracked for months as purely private and deal-free turns out to have already been absorbed into a larger multi-brand platform — without a single wire release this desk could locate — that is a live signal about how quietly sub-scale California consolidation is happening relative to the public-company M&A this desk tracks in the deal table below. PGP's own pipeline screening for lower-middle-market California operators should not assume "no public profile" means "not in play"; it may simply mean the deal hasn't hit a wire service yet. Separately, Glass House's muted-to-negative reaction to genuinely good news (GACP certification) alongside a near-90% short-interest jump is a useful data point on how skeptical the market currently is of operator-specific good news in this tape — relevant context for any PGP client weighing the signal value of a positive regulatory catalyst mid-slide.

The Rescheduling Docket

Still stayed — one more procedural layer, still no ruling

ALJ Julius added a small procedural step Oct 14 but has not ruled on the underlying motion; the case remains exactly where it was — stayed, with DEA's response to the testimony/discovery request still due Oct 21.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E.
  • Jul 15, 2026 Broader Schedule I→III hearings conclude before ALJ Derek Julius.
  • Sep 23, 2026 GAO publishes report GAO-26-108623, finding DEA's scheduling decisions aligned with HHS recommendations in all 84 cases reviewed, but flagging a lack of written DEA/FDA procedural policy.
  • Sep 28-29, 2026 Opponents move to reopen the record to admit the GAO report; ALJ Julius stays the case and orders a DEA response by Oct 13.
  • Oct 9, 2026 DEA files its response four days early: no objection to admitting the GAO report itself, but firm opposition to opponents' broader bid for new testimony and document discovery.
  • Oct 13-14, 2026 ALJ Julius states he has not yet ruled on the GAO-report motion, reserves judgment, and sets a new Oct 21 deadline for DEA to address the testimony/discovery request.
  • Oct 14, 2026 ALJ Julius separately grants rescheduling opponents (DUID Victim Voices, Dr. Kenneth Finn, National Drug and Alcohol Association) a motion allowing them to reply to a DEA filing, reiterating the Oct 21 deadline without changing it.
  • Oct 16, 2026 (today) No ruling or new filing located; docket status unchanged, case remains formally stayed.
