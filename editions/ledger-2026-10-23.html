Page One · The Big Story

Sector Tape Erases Its Rally as MSOS Falls a Third Straight Session — Canopy Growth Clears a Reverse Split, Glass House Resumes Its Slide, and Virginia's Sen. Lucas Walks Free Pending a November Arraignment

MSOS, which closed Thursday at $4.08, traded down roughly 4.2%–4.4% to about $3.90–$3.91 by early Friday afternoon — wiping out the bulk of Tuesday-Wednesday's rally in two sessions; Glass House Brands led decliners at -8.33%, resuming the slide that began with its Oct. 19 preliminary-results miss; Canopy Growth shareholders approved reverse-split authority at a reconvened meeting; Curaleaf accused Aurora's board of granting Organigram diligence access it denied Curaleaf; and Virginia state Sen. L. Louise Lucas was released without pretrial detention a day after her federal indictment, with arraignment set for Nov. 16.

The two-day cannabis-equity rally that this desk flagged as more likely short-covering than a re-rating finished unwinding today. MSOS closed Thursday at $4.08, per Yahoo Finance, after Wednesday's verified $4.13 close — itself the rally's high point. By early Friday afternoon the ETF traded down to roughly $3.90–$3.91, a 4.2%–4.4% single-day decline depending on source (stockanalysis.com vs. Yahoo Finance), on volume of about 2.25 million shares. The move was broad: of the ten constituents this desk could price this run, nine were lower, led by Glass House Brands (-8.33% to $2.09), Trulieve (-5.95% to $10.27), Cresco Labs (-5.53% to $0.60), Vireo Growth (-4.58% to $8.12), TerrAscend (-3.09%), Curaleaf (-2.59%), Green Thumb (-2.15%), Aurora Cannabis (-1.76%) and Planet 13 (-1.32%). Only Verano Holdings bucked the tape, with a secondary-sourced intraday quote showing it up 2.98% to $5.19 — a figure this desk's primary market-data pull could not independently cross-verify this run.

Glass House's decline is the more structurally important one: it extends, rather than begins, a selloff that started Oct. 19 when the company reported preliminary unaudited Q3 revenue of $56.8 million (up from Q2's $47.0 million) alongside the abrupt resignation of director John "Jay" Nichols Jr., sending the stock down roughly 39% that day; Lake Street subsequently cut its price target to $7 from $17. No securities-investigation announcement has surfaced, and no new disclosure explains today's further 8.33% drop — this desk flags that absence rather than speculating on a cause.

Elsewhere, Canopy Growth shareholders approved, at a reconvened annual and special meeting (the original Sept. 25 meeting failed for lack of quorum), authority for the board to execute a share consolidation at a ratio between 1-for-5 and 1-for-15, exercisable within 12 months, per the company's results release cross-referenced against its SEC 8-K filing — a step aimed at curing a Nasdaq delisting risk tied to shares trading below $1. Separately, the Curaleaf-Aurora takeover fight escalated in tone: Curaleaf on Oct. 19 publicly accused Aurora's board of excluding it from confidential due diligence while granting that access to Organigram, the BAT-backed rival suitor analyst Pablo Zuanic estimates could counter near C$8.55 (~US$6) per share — an estimate, this desk notes, that neither company has confirmed. And the Justice Department's marijuana-trafficking indictment of Virginia state Sen. L. Louise Lucas, announced Oct. 22, moved forward Thursday when she appeared before a federal judge in Norfolk; prosecutors did not seek pretrial detention and she was released, posting on X that she "will not be intimidated" and "will not back down." Six co-defendants, including two daughters and a grandson, were indicted alongside her on racketeering, distribution-conspiracy, money-laundering and honest-services-fraud charges tied to an alleged $4.5 million, five-year scheme; arraignment is set for Nov. 16 in Newport News.

Why it matters to PGP A second straight down day — steeper than Thursday's and still without a clean, sector-wide catalyst — reinforces this desk's standing read that Tuesday-Wednesday's advance was positioning, not a re-rating; clients should keep discounting both the rally and this unwind as information about fundamentals. Canopy's vote removes one procedural uncertainty but defers the question that matters (the actual ratio and timing), so it changes little for comparables work. More consequential for active mandates: Curaleaf's public accusation that Aurora granted Organigram, but not Curaleaf, diligence access raises the odds this standoff produces board-conduct litigation alongside the running tender offer — a scenario PGP should model explicitly when advising on either side of a competitive cannabis take-private or strategic process, since asymmetric-access claims are a common precursor to injunctive motions in contested M&A.
