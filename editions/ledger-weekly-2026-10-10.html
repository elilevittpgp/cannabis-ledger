Week of October 4 – 10, 2026

Page One · The Week in Review

A Rival Bidder Lifts Aurora 14.5% Even as Curaleaf Cries Foul, the Sector Steadies After Its Worst Week on Record, and a Second Advanced Flower Capital Auction Result Goes Missing

MSOS closed the week at $4.34, down a comparatively mild 2.91% after the prior week's 13.37% rout, but the calm masked a widening split: Aurora Cannabis rallied on reported rival-bidder interest from Organigram while Glass House Brands and Ascend Wellness each shed more than a fifth of their value. Elsewhere, Trulieve deleveraged, Vireo finally filed paper on its Planet 13 merger, and the rescheduling docket stayed frozen pending Tuesday's DEA deadline.

No. 1 · M&A & Governance

A reported rival suitor turns Curaleaf's hostile Aurora bid into a real auction — and a public fight over access

Curaleaf formally filed its enhanced bid for Aurora Cannabis on Oct. 6 — a Notice of Variation and SEC Form F-80 Amendment No. 2 converting the prior week's announced intent into an actual offer: an implied US$5.00 per share (0.4013 Curaleaf shares plus US$1.00 cash, an 86% premium to Aurora's unaffected price), with the cash-election cap raised to US$6.00 and the minimum deposit period extended to 11:59pm Mountain Time on Dec. 4. Two days later, trade outlet Cultivated reported — and multiple outlets corroborated — that Organigram Global, a roughly $200M-market-cap Canadian operator, had been in merger talks with Aurora "for the past few weeks." Neither company confirmed the talks on the record, but Curaleaf reacted publicly furious, saying it was "outraged" that the "world's largest cannabis company" was reportedly denied the diligence access extended to a rival roughly one-thirteenth its size. As of this edition, Aurora's special committee has issued no recommendation on Curaleaf's revised terms and continues to advise shareholders to take no action; neither side's Alberta Securities Commission application — Curaleaf's bid to freeze Aurora's at-the-market program, or Aurora's disclosure-deficiency complaint — has a hearing date, more than three weeks after the second was filed. The market read the rival-bidder report as a real catalyst: Aurora (ACB) gained 14.54% on the week, the sector's best performer by a wide margin, while Curaleaf itself fell 5.63%.

Why it matters to PGP A credible second bidder surfacing mid-process — paired with a public due-diligence-access complaint from the incumbent hostile bidder — is a live test of how a target's special committee manages competing-bid optics under a Canadian take-over bid regime, and it is directly relevant precedent for any PGP-advised process where access and process fairness become contested. The market's reaction is itself a data point: a rumored, unconfirmed rival bid moved Aurora's stock more than any single piece of confirmed news did all week, a reminder that process risk is pricing risk long before a definitive agreement exists.
No. 2 · Markets

The sector steadies — but the recovery is lopsided, not broad

After posting its worst week on record (-13.37%) the prior Friday, MSOS found a floor this week: a Monday slide to $4.39, a Tuesday bounce, a Thursday leg down to $4.275 on a catalyst-free 15.6% plunge in Glass House Brands, and a Friday close at $4.34 — down a comparatively mild 2.91% on the week and 3.98% month-to-date. The composure was deceptive: breadth split sharply rather than converging. Aurora (+14.54%) and Planet 13 (+3.55%) were the week's gainers, while Glass House Brands (-22.4%) and Ascend Wellness (-23.06%) each lost more than a fifth of their value — both far steeper than Curaleaf (-5.63%), TerrAscend (-4.85%) or Cresco Labs (-3.91%), the next-worst performers. Glass House disclosed CFO Mark Vendetti's planned retirement (effective through March 31, 2027, an orderly transition) on Friday, but that news lands a day after, not before, Thursday's plunge, leaving the move still substantially unexplained. Ascend's decline tracks a thinly traded OTC stock inside a down week for the sector rather than any identified company-specific negative; this desk found no new adverse release.

No. 3 · Distressed Debt

A second Advanced Flower Capital foreclosure result goes undisclosed

Advanced Flower Capital's Article 9 foreclosure sale of Justice Grown's New Jersey dispensary collateral — tied to the same $78.8 million defaulted loan principal behind the Sept. 24 Pennsylvania auction, whose result has never been made public — was held Oct. 6 via SSG Advisors. As of this edition, four days later, no clearing price or winning bidder has surfaced from AFCG, SSG Advisors, an SEC filing, or trade press. That makes two consecutive Justice Grown collateral auctions — covering cultivation and retail assets in two states — with no public pricing outcome, even as AFCG's loan book continues to generate other workout activity (a Sept. 25 revolver upsizing to $130 million, the July $12.5 million Devi Holdings asset sale).

No. 4 · Capital Structure

Trulieve retires debt early — days after a Florida environmental order hit the same site

Trulieve fully repaid the roughly $65 million mortgage note on its Jefferson County, Florida cultivation site ahead of maturity on Oct. 7, cutting total debt to approximately $225 million and freeing capital CEO Kim Rivers said would fund Georgia and Texas expansion. The repayment came two days after Florida's Department of Environmental Protection disclosed a consent order against the same Monticello facility for unpermitted wastewater discharge — Trulieve has 30 days for an interim irrigation plan and elected to fund a roughly $120,000 remediation project rather than pay an $80,000 fine outright.

No. 5 · M&A

Vireo finally files paper on its Planet 13 merger, ten-plus weeks after announcing it

Vireo Growth filed its initial Form S-4 with the SEC on Oct. 9 for its all-stock acquisition of Planet 13 (0.015383618 Vireo shares per Planet 13 share), announced July 27-28 — the first concrete procedural step after more than ten weeks with no shareholder-vote date or effective registration statement. The filing is not yet effective and no vote date has been set. Separately, Vireo closed a $60 million real-estate refinancing (8.5%, maturing 2034) backing its Johnstown, NY and Palatka, FL facilities on Oct. 5, and continues staged closings on its up-to-$35 million purchase of The Cannabist Company's CO/IL/MA/NJ/WV assets — now its third active roll-up thread alongside Planet 13 and its earlier Fluent transaction.

The Rescheduling Docket

Still frozen pending Tuesday, with a parallel appellate track confirming a long runway

No DEA filing or ALJ ruling has surfaced on the stayed Schedule I-to-III case all week. The clearest signal of a longer timeline came not from the stayed docket itself but from a separate appellate proceeding, plus a correction this desk is making to a prior TerrAscend court date.

  • Oct 2, 2026 Judge Esther Salas grants the DOJ a 45-day extension in TerrAscend's $8.36M 280E clawback suit — the government's reply, originally due Oct 5, is now due Nov 19, and the motion-to-dismiss consideration date moves from Oct 19 to Dec 21. (Correcting this desk's Oct 7-9 daily editions, which carried stale Oct 13/Oct 19 placeholder dates for this case.)
  • Oct 5, 2026 DEA opens a new Form 225 Schedule III registration portal for medical-cannabis manufacturers, distributors and labs — an implementation step distinct from the stayed hearing; Ohio's SB 56 purchase limits take effect statewide.
  • Oct 6-8, 2026 State cannabis regulators in Georgia, Vermont and California go public describing DEA "radio silence" on rescheduling implementation guidance ahead of the Oct 13 deadline; Sens. Warren and Markey publicly oppose Massachusetts' Question 8, citing new polling showing 75% opposed.
  • Oct 9, 2026 DOJ and rescheduling opponents jointly propose a briefing schedule in a separate, parallel D.C. Circuit appeal of April's Schedule III medical-use order — one extending into Feb. 19, 2027, corroborating sell-side models of a 1H 2027 resolution window on the broader case; Massachusetts' "No on 8" coalition confirms it has outraised the repeal campaign $2.18M to $1.98M; the Marijuana Policy Project publicly urges Congress to replace the Dec 11 hemp-THC ban with a regulatory framework instead.
  • Oct 10, 2026 No new DEA filing, ALJ ruling, or Massachusetts polling/fundraising update found — a quiet Saturday, as expected with markets and most filings offices closed.

Muha Meds Ypsilanti loses its license and is fined $251,250 for diverting 887 vape carts to social-media influencers and using fake accounts to evade purchase limits. The Marijuana Herald

Washington advances a medical-cannabis "verification certificate"

The state's Liquor and Cannabis Board voted unanimously to begin rulemaking on a Medical Cannabis Verification Certificate, overriding its own staff's negative recommendation, aimed at helping licensees document medical-system participation for banking and insurance access. The Marijuana Herald

Treasury and IRS put 280E guidance on next year's priority plan

The Sept 29 Priority Guidance Plan commits federal resources to Section 280E cannabis-tax guidance through Sept 30, 2027, but sets no completion date or relief amount — a work commitment, not a deadline. The Marijuana Herald
