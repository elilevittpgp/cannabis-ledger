Trulieve Pays Down $65 Million Early — Days After Florida Ordered Fixes at the Very Site

The pre-market release landed clean: debt down to roughly $225M, interest expense falling, growth capital freed for Georgia and Texas. Two days earlier, the same cultivation facility drew a state consent order over wastewater running off the property.

Trulieve said Wednesday it has fully repaid the roughly $65 million mortgage note secured by its cultivation site in Jefferson County, Florida, ahead of maturity, cutting outstanding debt to approximately $225 million. CEO Kim Rivers framed it as balance-sheet discipline funded by "strong cash generation," and said the lower leverage leaves Trulieve, now listed on the NYSE as TRLV, "well positioned to fund growth initiatives in the near term, including expansion in Georgia and Texas, pending regulatory approvals."

The timing is notable for a different reason. On Monday, Florida's Department of Environmental Protection disclosed a consent order against Trulieve over the same Jefferson County facility — the Monticello cultivation site at 1948 Capps Highway — for spraying reverse-osmosis reject water and HVAC/dehumidifier condensate onto an outdoor lawn where it migrated beyond the property line without a discharge permit. Trulieve has 30 days to submit an interim irrigation plan, 120 days to apply for a proper wastewater permit, and has elected to fund a roughly $120,000 DEP-approved environmental improvement project rather than pay an $80,000 penalty outright; further violations could carry civil penalties up to $15,000 per day.

Why it matters to PGP A debt paydown and an environmental consent order hitting the same asset in the same week is a reminder that a clean balance-sheet headline doesn't retire the operating liabilities sitting underneath it. Any PGP diligence on a cultivation-heavy MSO target should treat water-discharge and utility-compliance history at individual grow sites as a distinct line item from corporate leverage — the two can move in opposite directions on the same property, and a lender or acquirer reading only the financing release would miss it.

The Rescheduling Docket

Still stayed, still waiting on Oct 13

No DEA filing or ALJ ruling has surfaced since the Sept 28-29 stay. Wednesday's only rescheduling-adjacent news is political rather than procedural: Rep. Ayanna Pressley became the first member of Massachusetts's congressional delegation to publicly back the "No on 8" campaign ahead of the state's Nov 3 repeal vote.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E.
  • Jul 15, 2026 Broader Schedule I→III hearings conclude before ALJ Derek Julius.
  • Sep 28-29, 2026 ALJ Julius stays the case pending a GAO report on DEA/FDA scheduling procedures; DEA's response due Oct 13.
  • Oct 5, 2026 DEA opens a new Form 225 registration portal for Schedule III medical-cannabis manufacturers, distributors and labs — a separate implementation track from the stayed case.
  • Oct 6, 2026 Massachusetts Gov. and Rep. Pressley publicly urge a "No" vote on Question 8 (Nov 3 repeal initiative); a survey shows 61% of likely voters opposed.
  • Oct 7, 2026 No new DEA filing or ALJ ruling found; MSOS eases to $4.43, trimming part of Tuesday's bounce.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. Oct 13, 2026 — DEA's response to the motion to reopen the record (GAO report) is due.
  2. ALJ Julius rules on whether to admit the GAO report and permit added briefing.
  3. If granted, a further briefing round precedes the recommended decision; if denied, the case resumes toward a decision on the existing record.
  4. A 20-day exceptions window, then the DEA Administrator's final call — sell-side analysts have modeled 1H 2027 as the more likely resolution window.
  5. Nov 3, 2026 — Massachusetts voters decide Question 8, which would end licensed adult-use sales as of Jan 1, 2028 if it passes; 61% of surveyed voters currently oppose it.
  6. Separately: the SAFE Banking Act of 2026 (S.4942) remains referred to the Senate Banking Committee with no markup or floor vote scheduled.
PGP takeaway Massachusetts's Question 8 is now a live state-level tail risk alongside the federal docket — a repeal there would be the first voter reversal of adult-use legalization in a mature market, and the 61%-opposed polling should not be read as a guarantee. Models keyed to a 280E resolution should keep running on a 1H-2H 2027 base case; any MSO with material Massachusetts exposure should be stress-tested against a Jan 1, 2028 sales wind-down scenario, however low-probability it currently looks.
