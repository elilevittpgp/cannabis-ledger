Page One · The Big Story

Verano Posts Its Narrowest Loss in Years on a $212 Million Quarter, as Jushi's Wholesale Business Jumps 40% — and the Tape Shrugs at Both

Two priority-adjacent operators beat on fundamentals this week, but the market rewarded takeover drama instead: Curaleaf and Glass House outran Verano's own earnings-day stock reaction, one day before Cresco's Delaware vote and the DEA's rescheduling brief deadline.

Verano Holdings reported third-quarter 2026 results before Thursday's open: revenue of $212.4 million, up 5% year-over-year, gross profit of $100 million at a 47% margin, and — the number that stands out — a net loss of just $4.1 million, down from a $43.8 million loss in the year-ago quarter. Adjusted EBITDA came in at $50.4 million (24% margin), a touch below last year's $53.1 million, but operating cash flow of $49 million was the company's highest in four years, against $26 million a year ago. Nine-month revenue reached $638.5 million, up roughly 4%, with the nine-month net loss narrowing to $35.3 million from $74.5 million. Verano closed the quarter with $102.1 million in cash, net total debt of roughly $390 million, and raised its full-year 2026 capex guidance to $54-60 million. CEO George Archos pointed to "robust retail performance" and called 2027 "an exciting year for Verano and the industry." (GlobeNewswire; SEC 8-K exhibit; StockTitan.)

Jushi Holdings' Wednesday-evening print told a similar story one tier down: revenue of $70.5 million, up 7% year-over-year and the second straight quarter above $70 million, with wholesale revenue jumping 40% to a record $9.7 million and retail up 3.5% on record results in Ohio and Virginia. Net loss was $12.5 million against $13.6 million of adjusted EBITDA (19.3% margin); cash stood at $30.9 million. (GlobeNewswire, Oct. 28.) Yet neither beat moved its stock much — Verano traded up a muted 1.18% to about $5.16 intraday, while MSOS itself firmed only 0.74% to $4.08. The tape's bigger movers were event-driven, not earnings-driven: Glass House Brands (+2.89% to $2.315) and Curaleaf (+2.18% to $8.67), both still trading off the Aurora Cannabis hostile-bid saga rather than any operating update. Curaleaf's Tuesday rebuttal of the Alberta Securities Commission's ATM ruling — an 86% premium claim against Aurora's unaffected 30-day VWAP, plus an accusation of unequal diligence access — stands with no new terms filed and the Dec. 4 tender deadline unchanged.

Two calendar items converge tomorrow and today. Cresco Labs' shareholders vote Friday, Oct. 30, 12:00pm CDT, on the board-recommended Delaware "TopCo" redomicile, after Wednesday's proxy cutoff passed with no result yet disclosed. And supplemental briefs addressing the admitted GAO report are due today, Oct. 29, before ALJ Derek Julius in the still-stayed DEA rescheduling case — no filing had been confirmed as of this edition's compilation; reply briefs follow Nov. 5.

Why it matters to PGP Verano's and Jushi's prints are a reminder that idiosyncratic operating improvement — margin discipline, cash generation, debt paydown — is available in this tape even while headline volatility is dominated by takeover drama (Curaleaf/Aurora) and unexplained single-name routs (Glass House). PGP's sector model should separate "event-driven" moves from "fundamental-driven" ones before drawing conclusions about sentiment; a quarter like Verano's, read in isolation, supports a constructive underwriting case even as the ETF-level tape stays noisy.

The Rescheduling Docket

The supplemental-brief deadline arrives, unconfirmed

Supplemental briefs addressing the admitted GAO report are due today, Oct. 29, before ALJ Derek Julius. No filing has been confirmed as of this edition's compilation; reply briefs follow Nov. 5. The broader case remains formally stayed.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E.
