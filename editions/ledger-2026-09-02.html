The House Clears the Hemp-Ban Delay as Aurora Cannabis Says No to Curaleaf

A 370-48 vote sends the Dec. 11 hemp-THC reprieve to the President's desk hours before Aurora's board unanimously tells shareholders not to tender to Curaleaf's hostile bid.

Two of the desk's longest-running overhangs moved within a single news cycle. On Tuesday, Sept. 1, the House passed the Senate's continuing resolution funding the government through Dec. 11, 2026 — carrying the delay of the federal hemp-THC redefinition ban from Nov. 12 to that same Dec. 11 date — by a wide 370-48 margin under a suspension-of-the-rules vote requiring two-thirds support. Freedom Caucus chair Rep. Andy Harris (R-Md.) followed through on his threatened opposition, but redirected it: rather than vote down the CR itself, he and Reps. Pete Sessions (R-Texas) and Chip Roy (R-Texas) voted against an unrelated procedural rule in protest of the one-month hemp delay, a move that did not derail the funding bill. The hemp provision, reporting indicates, was inserted at the White House's request. The bill now awaits the President's signature, which had not been reported as of this writing.

Then, Wednesday morning, Aurora Cannabis filed its Directors' Circular at 7:05am ET — the formal board response to Curaleaf's hostile US$4.00-per-share bid, due under Canadian securities law's 15-day rule roughly on this date. The board voted unanimously to reject the offer, telling shareholders to take no action and not tender, and instructing anyone who already tendered to withdraw through Kingsdale Advisors. Aurora's case: the bid is "inadequate" and undervalues the company against peer multiples; Aurora holds $149M in cash and restricted cash with no debt, versus Curaleaf's more than $1B in debt and lease liabilities; and TD Securities opined the offer fails to reflect Aurora's medical-cannabis leadership, EU-GMP manufacturing, and international platform. Curaleaf fired back the same day, calling Aurora's account of the two companies' engagement "insulting to shareholders" and repeating that Aurora has "repeatedly failed to demonstrate a credible plan." The bid remains open through Dec. 1 unless extended. Elsewhere, Cresco Labs completed a $50M acquisition of PharmaCann's nine Pennsylvania dispensaries, becoming the state's largest medical retailer, while Ascend, Premier Group/PCF and Verano stayed quiet.

Why it matters to PGP Both overhangs just hardened into harder dates. The hemp-ban delay is now one signature away from law, giving diligence teams a defensible date to underwrite hemp-THC and beverage exposure against — the residual risk shifts from "will it pass" to the narrower, low-probability question of a veto or lapse. Aurora's rejection, meanwhile, pushes Curaleaf into a genuinely contested run to Dec. 1: expect either a bumped offer, a proxy/consent fight, a white-knight approach, or the bid quietly lapsing. Either path is a live process story worth tracking daily through year-end.

The Rescheduling Docket

The hemp-ban delay clears Congress; the ALJ still hasn't ruled

One dated deadline resolves while the larger Schedule I→III catalyst remains open-ended.

  • Aug 17, 2026 DEA post-hearing briefs deadline passes; Chief ALJ Derek Julius gives no signal on a recommended-decision timeline.
  • Aug 25, 2026 DEA posts the full corrected 2,533-page hearing transcript, a procedural step with no bearing on the ALJ's timing.
  • Sep 1, 2026 House passes the Senate's continuing resolution 370-48, carrying the Dec 11 hemp-THC ban delay; Rep. Andy Harris's opposition lands on an unrelated procedural rule rather than the funding bill itself.
  • Sep 2, 2026 Aurora Cannabis files its Directors' Circular rejecting Curaleaf's hostile bid — a separate M&A-process deadline, not a rescheduling event, but converging on the same news cycle.

PGP takeaway Treat the Dec 11 delay as effectively locked in for modeling purposes — a presidential veto of a bipartisan funding bill already passed by both chambers is a low-probability tail risk. The bigger open catalyst remains the ALJ's rescheduling recommendation, which has no statutory clock; don't let the resolved hemp-ban and bid-rejection news crowd it out of the diligence calendar.

Industry Headlines

Cresco becomes Pennsylvania's largest medical cannabis retailer

The $50M, cash-free/debt-free purchase of PharmaCann's nine PA dispensaries leverages Cresco's existing cultivation and wholesale footprint in the #2 U.S. medical market (>$1.1B in annual sales). The Marijuana Herald

JARS Cannabis builds Arizona's largest vertically integrated retail chain

The acquisition of Sonoran Roots' eight-store Ponderosa Dispensary network brings JARS to 27 Arizona locations; deal terms were not disclosed. PR Newswire · Cannabis Business Times

New York begins covering Metrc tag costs for licensees

Effective Sep 1, the state's Office of Cannabis Management funds most track-and-trace tag costs for 12 months under the 2026-27 budget — up to 24,000 plant tags and 6,000 package tags for cultivators. MJBizDaily

California cracks down on cannabis packaging that appeals to kids

Gov. Newsom signed AB 2249 on Aug 31, barring cartoon imagery and candy-like branding on cannabis packaging and ads; it passed 69-1 in the Assembly and 38-0 in the Senate, effective Jan 1, 2028. Office of the Governor · Marijuana Moment

Montana's new labeling and recall rules take effect

Child-resistant opaque exit packaging and QR-coded labels tied to substance-use resources took effect Aug 22; a 24-hour recall-activation, 48-hour public-notice rule had already taken effect in July, both stemming from 2025's HB 792. The Marijuana Herald

A distressed-credit lane opens in PA and NJ retail cannabis

Advanced Flower Capital's Article 9 foreclosure on cannabis loan collateral sets a Sep 15 court hearing ahead of a Sep 24 Pennsylvania auction and an Oct 6 New Jersey dispensary-collateral auction. cann.dev
