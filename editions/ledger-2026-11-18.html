Page One · The Big Story

Glass House's Rally Reverses Into a 17% Plunge on a Margin Miss and Analyst Downgrades, Aurora Sets Its Record Date for the Organigram Vote, and a Day-Old Hemp Bill Already Faces a GOP Blockade Threat

MSOS falls to $3.58, down 1.92% on the day and a fresh one-week low, as Glass House Brands gives back virtually all of its two-day rally following a margin-compression miss and a pair of analyst actions; Aurora Cannabis sets today as the record date for its Dec. 23 vote on the Organigram deal while Curaleaf's hostile tender stays open through Dec. 4; and two Republican senators vow to block any funding bill that keeps hemp-derived THC products legal, a day after the bipartisan Sheehy-Klobuchar "Hemp Guardrails Act" was introduced.

Glass House Brands gave back virtually all of its two-day rally Wednesday, falling 17.47% to $1.37 — its sharpest single-session drop since the auditor-swap scare earlier this month — on volume again well above average. The reversal followed Monday's Q3 2026 filing: revenue of $56.8 million, up 117% year over year on record biomass output of roughly 319,000 pounds, but gross margin compressed to 26% from 34% in the second quarter on a trim-heavy sales mix and seasonally lower pricing, and adjusted EPS of ($0.14) missed the ($0.13) consensus by a penny. Two sell-side notes followed: Zacks cut the stock to Strong Sell Monday, and Lake Street, while keeping a Buy rating, cut its price target to $3 from $7 Tuesday. The auditor dispute that drove last week's swing is no longer the live issue — BDO's flagged, and company-disputed, characterization of an unfinished deconsolidation-accounting review as a reportable event prompted the switch to MGO, reappointed Nov. 6, which met the Nov. 16 filing deadline and had separately completed a review of the same deconsolidation accounting without a material modification in connection with the company's Q2 interim reporting. Shares traded as high as $1.405 in after-hours action, up 2.55% off the regular-session close.

The broader tape followed Glass House lower. MSOS, the AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF, closed at $3.58, down 1.92% on the day on volume of about 3.69 million shares — a fresh one-week low, roughly 12.3% below last Wednesday's $4.08 close and about 9.1% below Oct. 30's $3.94 month-to-date baseline. Aside from Verano (+0.25% to $3.95), Planet 13 (+1.23% to $0.082) and Organigram (+0.26% to $0.8522), essentially every constituent this desk tracks finished lower: TerrAscend fell 3.98% to $0.3696, Vireo Growth fell 2.91% to $5.78, Curaleaf fell 2.52% to $7.75, Cresco Labs fell 2.54% to $0.46, Aurora Cannabis fell 2.65% to $4.40, Jushi fell 1.54% to $0.3840, Trulieve fell 0.50% to $10.02, Green Thumb fell 0.17% to $5.95 and Ascend Wellness fell 0.44% to $0.3399. AYR Wellness remained effectively untraded and worthless in its ongoing CCAA wind-down.

Today also marks the record date Aurora Cannabis set for its Dec. 23 special shareholder meeting on the board-recommended Organigram Global acquisition (roughly C$561 million, with cash, share-exchange and blended options), which Aurora's board continues to recommend over Curaleaf's unsolicited US$1.00-cash-plus-0.4013-CURLF-share tender offer, open through Dec. 4. No new filing or bid revision surfaced from either side today. Separately, the Department of Justice's reply in TerrAscend's $8.36 million 280E refund clawback suit is due tomorrow, Nov. 19, with a motion-to-dismiss hearing set for Dec. 21 — a day after workers at the company's Apothecarium dispensary in Phillipsburg, N.J. voted to join UFCW Local 360. Trulieve's preliminary addition to the Russell 2000 and 3000 Indices, announced Monday, still awaits final confirmation, due after the Dec. 11 market close.

On the federal hemp side, the fight over the Dec. 11 "total THC" deadline hardened within a day of its first real legislative vehicle taking shape. A day after Sens. Tim Sheehy (R-Mont.) and Amy Klobuchar (D-Minn.) introduced the bipartisan "Hemp Guardrails Act" — a 1% total-THC-by-dry-weight redefinition with age-21 sales rules, child-resistant packaging and a beverage tax — two Republican senators said Wednesday they would try to block any government funding bill that keeps hemp-derived THC products legal, according to Marijuana Moment, pushing in the opposite direction from the bill's own sponsors. Separately, Smart Approaches to Marijuana's Kevin Sabet called the Sheehy-Klobuchar bill "dead on arrival," citing resistance within both sponsors' own caucuses. In Illinois, the enforcement pause the state's Attorney General granted roughly 250 hemp businesses party to a federal suit against the state's hemp-THC ban expires tomorrow, Nov. 19, setting up a same-day test of whether that fight escalates. Missouri's hemp retailers continued working through the fuller effect of the state's Intoxicating Cannabinoid Control Act, with no new Attorney General lawsuit filed this week. Ascend Wellness, Verano Holdings and Premier Group/PCF were all quiet on net-new developments inside today's window; details are in the Priority Coverage cards below.

Why it matters to PGP Glass House's reversal is a useful reminder that a blowout top-line beat does not guarantee a sustained move once margin and EPS details are digested — clients should underwrite growth names on unit economics, not just revenue growth, especially after a rally has already priced in a clean quarter. On hemp, today's GOP blockade threat complicates this desk's prior base case that Dec. 11 resolves via a tidy legislative patch; PGP should widen its modeled outcomes — including a lapse into the stricter ban absent a deal — for any client with hemp-beverage exposure.
