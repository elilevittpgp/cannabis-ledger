Page One · The Week in Review

Glass House Erases a Third of Its Value in a Week-Long Grind Lower With No Bounce, Curaleaf Escalates Its Aurora Fight to a Fiduciary-Breach Accusation, and the DEA Splits the Baby on New Discovery

MSOS closed the week at $3.91, up a negligible 0.5% from last Friday's $3.89 — a deceptively flat headline number masking the sector's widest single-name dispersion in weeks: Glass House Brands fell every session, Monday through Friday, for a cumulative 32.9% decline to $2.14 on a soft preliminary Q3 print and an unexplained board resignation, while Trulieve (+4.1%) and Green Thumb (+3.8%) led a handful of names higher. Curaleaf's hostile-bid fight for Aurora Cannabis sharpened into an explicit fiduciary-breach allegation, Chief ALJ Derek Julius split the difference on the DEA's own rescheduling docket by admitting a GAO report while denying new discovery, a sitting Virginia state senator was indicted on federal marijuana-trafficking charges, and Canopy Growth shareholders finally cleared reverse-split authority at a reconvened meeting.

No. 1 · Markets · Single-Name

Glass House's "bounce" wasn't real — a week of verified closes shows a straight slide, not a rally-and-relapse

Glass House Brands (NYSE: GLAS) disclosed preliminary, unaudited Q3 results on Oct. 19 — record biomass production (~317,000 lbs, up from 246,000 lbs in Q2) and revenue of $56.8M, up from $47.0M — but average selling price fell to roughly $150/lb from $211/lb, and the release was accompanied by the unexplained resignation of director John "Jay" Nichols Jr. The stock fell 22.9% that day on roughly 30x average volume. This desk's own intraday tracking through Tuesday and Wednesday read a modest bounce (+2.99% Wednesday, to a reported $2.41) before the stock resumed falling Thursday and Friday. A check against verified closing prints this weekend, cross-sourced across stockanalysis.com and Yahoo Finance's historical tables, shows that bounce did not survive the close: Glass House fell in every single session this week — Mon $2.46, Tue $2.34, Wed $2.33, Thu $2.28, Fri $2.14 — for a cumulative -32.9% on the week, extending from Friday Oct. 16's $3.19 close. This desk is correcting its own mid-week intraday read rather than carry it forward uncorrected. Canaccord Genuity (to $8 from $15) and Lake Street (to $7 from $17) both cut price targets mid-week; no securities-investigation announcement has surfaced, and no new disclosure explains the continued slide beyond the original margin-compression story.

Correction, this edition Readers of this week's daily editions saw Glass House described as "catching a bid" Wednesday and "resuming its slide" Friday — language this desk now believes mischaracterizes an uninterrupted decline as a round trip. Intraday snapshots and verified closing prints diverged this week specifically on this name; going forward, any single-session intraday move this desk reports mid-week should be read as provisional until the weekly close-to-close reconciliation.
No. 2 · M&A & Governance

Curaleaf accuses Aurora's board of a fiduciary breach as the Organigram shadow fight continues with no signed terms

Aurora's board filed a Notice of Change to its Directors' Circular on Oct. 14 (its fifth amendment since September), reaffirming its rejection of Curaleaf's enhanced hostile bid ($5.00/share implied, $6.00 cash-election cap) without naming BAT-backed Organigram Global as an alternative. Curaleaf responded on Oct. 19 with a release — "Curaleaf Challenges Aurora's Unilateral Rejection of Its Enhanced Proposal" — in which Chairman and CEO Boris Jordan accused Aurora's board of granting due-diligence access to unnamed third parties (understood to include Organigram) while continuing to deny Curaleaf the same access, calling the conduct a breach that "defies all fiduciary logic and reason." Aurora's board separately reaffirmed its rejection on Oct. 21 and said it "welcomes other bids" — public confirmation it is running a multi-party process rather than a binary yes/no on Curaleaf. As of this edition, neither side's Alberta Securities Commission disclosure application has a hearing date, and no signed terms, exchange ratio, or price has been filed by Organigram. Curaleaf's tender stays open to Dec. 4.

Why it matters to PGP A target board moving from "exploring alternatives" to board-confirmed talks with a named rival, while the hostile bidder escalates to a filed fiduciary-breach allegation, is now a real multi-party contest on the public record rather than a rumor PGP has to risk-weight on trade-press sourcing alone — but the absence of any filed Organigram terms six weeks after talks first surfaced keeps the range of outcomes wide (a formal competing bid, a negotiated settlement, or Curaleaf simply raising again). For any PGP client with exposure on either side of a similar hostile/white-knight dynamic, Curaleaf's specific and litigable diligence-access claim is the more consequential development than the reaffirmed rejection itself — asymmetric-access allegations are a common precursor to injunctive motions in contested M&A, and PGP should model elevated process risk accordingly.
No. 3 · The Rescheduling Docket

The DEA's judge splits the difference: the GAO report is in, new discovery is out, and the stay runs at least three more weeks

The DEA filed its response a day early on Oct. 20, calling rescheduling opponents' renewed bid for new testimony and document discovery "an obvious fishing expedition." On Oct. 22, Chief ALJ Derek Julius ruled: he admitted the GAO's Sept. 23 report (GAO-26-108623) into the stayed Schedule I-to-III record, but denied reopening the hearing for new testimony or discovery, and ordered narrow supplemental briefing on the weight the GAO report should carry — due from the parties Oct. 29, with replies Nov. 5. The case remains formally stayed through that window; no new filing or ruling surfaced Oct. 23.

Why it matters to PGP A split ruling that admits one disputed document while denying broader discovery is a narrowing, not a resolution, and it extends rather than shortens the runway to any ALJ recommended decision — this desk continues to treat 1H 2027 as the working assumption for final resolution and sees nothing this week that moves that estimate. Clients should keep discounting a near-term rescheduling catalyst; the practical read is that the Oct. 29/Nov. 5 briefing window is itself now the next hard date, not a step toward imminent closure.
No. 4 · Legal / Enforcement

A sitting state senator is indicted on federal marijuana-trafficking charges — and walks free a day later

The Justice Department announced the indictment of Virginia state Sen. L. Louise Lucas on Oct. 22 on federal racketeering, marijuana-distribution-conspiracy, money-laundering and honest-services-fraud charges tied to an alleged $4.5 million, five-year scheme run through a cannabis business she co-owned; six co-defendants, including two of her daughters and a grandson, were indicted alongside her. Lucas appeared before a federal judge in Norfolk on Oct. 23; prosecutors did not seek pretrial detention, and she was released, posting on social media that she "will not be intimidated." Arraignment is set for Nov. 16 in Newport News.

No. 5 · Governance / Listings

Canopy Growth clears reverse-split authority at a reconvened meeting, resolving one Nasdaq overhang while leaving the hard numbers unset

At a reconvened annual and special meeting on Oct. 23 (the original Sept. 25 meeting failed for lack of quorum), Canopy Growth shareholders approved board authority to execute a share consolidation at a ratio between 1-for-5 and 1-for-15, exercisable within 12 months — a step aimed at curing a Nasdaq minimum-bid-price deficiency tied to shares trading below $1. The specific ratio and implementation date remain unset.
