Virginia's Hemp Market Waits on a Ruling as the Clock Runs to Midnight

A federal judge heard five hours of argument and still hasn't ruled, leaving seven hemp businesses staring down Saturday's THC-cap deadline — while Glass House's Q2 print shows what margin compression looks like mid-transition and MSOS keeps grinding higher on Aurora-bid fallout.

Virginia's hemp industry entered Friday with no answer from the bench. U.S. District Judge Robert S. Ballou (W.D. Va., Roanoke) heard nearly five hours of argument Wednesday from seven hemp businesses — including Northern Virginia Hemp & Agriculture ($735,000 of inventory at risk) and Cypress Hemp II ($1.43 million at risk) — seeking to block House Bill 30's new 2-milligram-per-package THC cap, which eliminates the old 25:1 CBD:THC ratio exception and takes effect at midnight Saturday, August 15. Ballou did not rule from the bench; plaintiffs' counsel Chap Petersen has said a decision is expected "sometime on Friday," and as of this edition's research pass, none had been published. Businesses face potential felony exposure the moment the cap takes hold, and the agricultural argument — crops already planted for a season built around the old rule — got a full airing but no resolution.

Elsewhere, Glass House Brands (NYSE: GLAS) reported second-quarter results after Thursday's close: revenue of $47.0 million, essentially flat against $47.6 million a year ago but up sharply from $28.6 million in Q1, while gross margin compressed to 34% from 55% in the prior-year period as wholesale biomass — now 89% of revenue — weighed on the mix. Adjusted EBITDA came in at a positive $5.7 million, down from $18.1 million a year ago. The company touted record biomass production of 246,000 pounds at a $122/lb cost, reaffirmed full-year production guidance near 1 million pounds, and flagged the deconsolidation of its dual-use California business alongside its recent NYSE uplisting; shares fell roughly 3% Thursday. Separately, a federal judge in New Jersey handed Curaleaf a win Aug. 10, ruling the state's cannabis labor-peace-agreement mandate is preempted by the National Labor Relations Act — a decision New Jersey is appealing to the Third Circuit but one that could ripple into every other state with a similar requirement. Ascend, Verano and Premier Group/PCF were all quiet again in the 24–48 hour window; see Priority Coverage below.

Why it matters to PGP The Virginia case is a live preview of what the December 11 federal hemp-THC cliff will look like fractured across fifty states: litigation outcomes, not statutory text, are what actually determine whether a given hemp-derived revenue line survives past a deadline. A plaintiff loss in Virginia hands other states a enforcement template; a win invites a wave of similar challenges elsewhere before the federal clock runs out. On the labor side, the NJ preemption ruling — if it survives Third Circuit review — removes a real deal-diligence friction point (labor-peace agreements have shaped several MSO union relationships) and is worth flagging to any client with NJ-adjacent labor exposure.

The Rescheduling Docket

A Virginia courtroom, not the DEA, is this week's real hemp deadline

The DEA's Schedule I→III docket itself hasn't moved, but a federal judge's still-pending ruling on Virginia's new THC cap is the nearer-term, sharper-edged catalyst for hemp-touching assets.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; three consolidated challenges remain pending in the D.C. Circuit (Nos. 26-1106, 26-1130, 26-1136), no new activity this window.
  • Jul 15, 2026 DEA hearings conclude; record stays open for post-hearing briefs, due Aug 17 (50-page cap, Chief ALJ Derek Julius). No new filings this window.
  • Aug 3, 2026 Senate passes the CR 90-6, pushing the toughest hemp-THC restrictions to Dec 11.
  • Aug 10, 2026 A New Jersey federal judge rules the state's cannabis labor-peace mandate is preempted by the NLRA — a Curaleaf win; the state is appealing to the Third Circuit.
  • Aug 12, 2026 Five-hour hearing in W.D. Virginia (Judge Robert S. Ballou) on seven hemp businesses' bid to block HB 30's new 2mg-per-package THC cap ends without a ruling from the bench; plaintiffs' counsel expects a decision "by Friday."
  • Aug 14, 2026 No ruling published as of this edition — the Aug 15 midnight enforcement deadline remains in force absent an order. Separately, DEA filed an Aug 14 brief in the Fourth Circuit (Bluestar Operations) defending its position that HHC is a Schedule I substance — a distinct rule from the main rescheduling docket, effective since May 2026.
  • Jun–Aug, 2026 States keep maneuvering: Wyoming opts out (Jul 8), Louisiana withdraws its rescheduling objection (Jun), Alabama's written-comment period closed Aug 5 with no State Board of Health decision yet.

New Jersey's cannabis labor-peace mandate ruled preempted

A federal judge held that New Jersey's requirement that cannabis operators sign labor-peace agreements is preempted by the NLRA — a win for Curaleaf; the state is appealing to the Third Circuit. Cannabis Business Times

Glass House posts flat revenue but sharply thinner margins

Q2 revenue of $47.0M, gross margin down to 34% from 55% a year ago as wholesale biomass grew to 89% of revenue; adjusted EBITDA fell to $5.7M from $18.1M, with shares off ~3% on the print. GlobeNewswire

Texas's THC ban survives an emergency challenge

A federal judge denied hemp retailers' bid for an emergency TRO against the state's delta-8/delta-10 ban, citing a failure to show irreparable harm; a broader preliminary-injunction request remains pending. Texas Tribune

Ohio cannabis prices run well ahead of Michigan's

Ohio's median per-ounce price ($147.35) dwarfs Michigan's ($99), a gap attributed to Ohio's limited licensing versus Michigan's 800-plus active retail licenses. Ohio Capital Journal

Illinois opens a medical-license pathway for adult-use shops

Regulators posted a new form letting adult-use dispensaries apply for medical-cannabis licenses starting Sept. 10 for a $5,000 fee, part of this session's omnibus cannabis law. Marijuana Moment

Houston puts low-level enforcement priority to a vote

City Council approved a November ballot measure that would make misdemeanor marijuana possession the city's lowest law-enforcement priority. Marijuana Moment
