Page One · The Big Story

MSOS Sinks Another 7.1% to $3.14 as a Second Straight AI-Trade Rout Hits Wall Street, an Illinois Judge Lets the State's Hemp-THC Ban Stand, and Medical Operators Sue the DEA Over a Nov. 29 Supply-Chain Deadline

A brutal, semiconductor-led selloff pushes the Nasdaq negative for 2026 and drags cannabis down in sympathy for the second day running, while a federal judge denies — without prejudice — the hemp industry's bid to block Illinois's ban, and three medical-marijuana operators race the DEA's clock in the D.C. Circuit.

Cannabis stocks were swept into a second consecutive, cannabis-unrelated Wall Street rout on Friday, this one driven by a brutal semiconductor and AI-infrastructure selloff rather than Thursday's Anthropic-IPO profit-taking. The S&P 500 fell roughly 3.7% to close at 6,910.39 and the Nasdaq Composite dropped about 5.3% to 22,617.73 — erasing the index's 2026 gain and marking its worst week in more than six years — as both the S&P 500 and the Dow closed in correction territory. Chipmakers led the carnage on investor doubts about whether massive AI-infrastructure capital spending will generate adequate returns: Nvidia, Micron, AMD and Broadcom each fell double digits. None of it has anything to do with cannabis fundamentals, but the sector's benchmark ETF sold off anyway, in a near-mirror of Thursday's move and the Oct. 30 and Nov. 12 "AI reckoning" selloffs this desk has tracked through the fall.

MSOS, the AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF, closed at $3.14 on volume of roughly 11.5 million shares — down 7.10% on the day, its second straight battering. That leaves the ETF about 15.6% below last Friday's (Nov. 13) $3.72 close and roughly 20.3% below the Oct. 30 ($3.94) month-to-date baseline — a two-session cumulative decline of about 12.3% from Wednesday's $3.58 close that is, by this desk's own tracking, among the worst two-day stretches the sector has seen this fall. For a second day running, essentially every name on this desk's tracked list fell together: TerrAscend dropped 9.81%, Verano 8.70%, Curaleaf 8.03%, Planet 13 8.58%, Organigram 7.40%, Trulieve 7.17%, and Green Thumb 5.96%, with Glass House (-2.48%), Vireo (-2.24%) and Aurora (-2.35%) comparatively more stable. AYR Wellness again returned no meaningful live quote, consistent with its ongoing CCAA wind-down.

On the legal docket, U.S. District Judge Martha Pacold denied — without prejudice — the Illinois Healthy Alternatives Association's motion to block the state's hemp-THC restriction, which reclassifies hemp products containing more than 0.4 mg of THC per container as cannabis sellable only through licensed dispensaries. The ban, already in effect since the group's negotiated enforcement pause expired Nov. 12, now stands unless overturned on appeal; the association, representing roughly 250 businesses and 5,000-plus jobs, is weighing a Seventh Circuit appeal while pursuing its underlying Commerce Clause challenge. Separately, three medical-marijuana operators — Standard Wellness (Utah), Verde Americano (Arizona) and NW Confections (Oklahoma) — filed a petition Friday in the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit challenging the DEA's Oct. 30 "Medical Marijuana Timely Applicant Information" guidance, which bars "timely applicant" operators from transacting with "non-timely applicant" operators ahead of a Nov. 29 compliance deadline. The plaintiffs are seeking expedited relief before Nov. 25, arguing the rule was adopted without notice-and-comment and ignores state-market realities; state regulators, including in Massachusetts, say the DEA has given them no implementation guidance, raising the prospect of medical-dispensary shortages.

On the priority desk, Ascend, Verano and Premier Group/PCF all stayed quiet on net-new developments — Verano's only item was a routine director Form 4 disclosing a small, pre-arranged stock sale, with its 89th Florida store (MÜV Tarpon Springs) opening as scheduled today. Glass House Brands drew fresh margin detail rather than fresh news: its Q3 cost per pound fell to $109 from $122, but its average selling price fell faster, to $151 per pound from $211 — the mechanics behind this week's reversal. TerrAscend's $8.36 million 280E refund-clawback suit passed its DOJ reply deadline Thursday with no filing content yet confirmed in public coverage.

Why it matters to PGP Three distinct threads for client conversations: (1) decouple macro-beta risk from fundamentals — this is the third broad, cannabis-unrelated rout since Oct. 30, and entry points created by index-level selling are not the same signal as a deteriorating sector; (2) Illinois's ruling is a real near-term loss for hemp-exposed revenue lines there, with the ban now the operative baseline pending any appeal — flag it to any client with Illinois hemp distribution; and (3) the DEA's Nov. 29 "timely applicant" transacting restriction is a live operational risk for medical-only MSOs sourcing from less-vertically-integrated cultivators — worth an immediate supply-chain audit for any portfolio company touching a non-adult-use state.

The Rescheduling Docket

Illinois's Hemp Ban Survives Its TRO Challenge, and the DEA Faces a New Supply-Chain Lawsuit Ahead of Its Nov. 29 Deadline

Two live legal fronts move today — one against a state hemp ban, one against a federal rescheduling-adjacent rule — while the DEA's core Schedule I-to-III record stays stalled awaiting the ALJ's recommended decision.

  • Nov 5, 2026 DEA rescheduling opponents and proponents file final post-hearing reply briefs with Chief ALJ Derek Julius, completing the written-argument round; no date has been set for his recommended decision.
  • Nov 17, 2026 Sens. Tim Sheehy (R-Mont.) and Amy Klobuchar (D-Minn.) introduce the bipartisan "Hemp Guardrails Act" (S.5736), redefining hemp at a 1% total-THC dry-weight threshold with age-21 rules, child-resistant packaging and a beverage tax, aimed at a year-end spending vehicle ahead of Dec. 11.
  • Nov 18, 2026 Sens. Ted Budd (R-N.C.) and Pete Ricketts (R-Neb.) say they will try to block any government funding bill that "extends, delays, or builds a regulatory framework around" hemp-derived THC. Aurora Cannabis's record date for its Dec. 23 Organigram vote also lands this day.
  • Nov 19, 2026 U.S. District Judge Martha Pacold denies, without prejudice, the Illinois Healthy Alternatives Association's TRO/preliminary-injunction motion against the state's hemp-THC ban — the ban, already in effect since Nov. 12, stands. TerrAscend's $8.36M 280E case also reaches its DOJ reply deadline, with no filing content confirmed.
  • Nov 20, 2026 Three medical-marijuana operators — Standard Wellness (Utah), Verde Americano (Ariz.) and NW Confections (Okla.) — petition the D.C. Circuit to pause the DEA's Oct. 30 "Timely Applicant" supply-chain guidance, seeking a ruling before Nov. 25 ahead of the rule's Nov. 29 compliance deadline.
