Page One · The Big Story

Cannabis Tape Extends Its Slide to an 11.8% Weekly Loss as Glass House Stabilizes From Its Auditor Shock, Planet 13 Quietly Books a Q3 Beat No One Was Watching For, and Missouri's New Hemp Law Starts Shuttering Stores

A second straight down session drags MSOS to roughly $3.74 in the closing minutes of trading, with Curaleaf, TerrAscend and Vireo Growth the day's deepest decliners even as Verano's newest Florida store opens exactly on schedule and Ascend's call clears up yesterday's open question on its wider net loss.

MSOS traded down roughly 3.1% Friday to about $3.74 in the final minutes before the 4:00pm ET close — a late-session read this desk could not get independently re-confirmed against a fully settled print by deadline, but one that converged across two sources (stockanalysis.com and Yahoo Finance) in the closing minutes of the session. That extends Thursday's 5.39% rout into a second straight losing day and puts the ETF down roughly 11.8% versus last Friday's $4.24 close — its worst week in recent memory — and down about 5.1% for the month to date. The damage is broad: Curaleaf (-5.52%), TerrAscend (-6.17%) and Vireo Growth (-6.07%) led Friday's decliners, while Cresco Labs (+1.18%) and Ascend Wellness (+2.01%) posted modest bounces off Thursday's lows. On the week, Glass House Brands remains the sector's deepest laggard at roughly -38%, followed by Cresco (-16.1%), Curaleaf (-16.0%), Verano (-13.5%) and TerrAscend (-13.0%).

Glass House itself found some footing Friday, rising 3.60% to about $1.30 after Thursday's 33.16% collapse on its surprise BDO-to-MGO auditor swap — but the bounce looks like partial stabilization, not resolution. The underlying dispute over deconsolidation accounting that BDO flagged as a reportable event remains unaddressed pending Monday's Nov. 16 Q3 call with newly reappointed auditor MGO; no further company disclosure, analyst note or regulatory filing on the matter surfaced in this desk's search today beyond syndication of Thursday's original release.

In a genuine surprise, Planet 13 Holdings quietly reported Q3 2026 results on Nov. 12 — contradicting this desk's own running assumption, shared with other trade coverage, that the print remained outstanding ahead of its pending all-stock absorption into Vireo Growth. Revenue came in at $23.1 million, up modestly from $22.9 million in Q2 on a 15.1% sequential jump in Florida (+45% year-over-year), while net loss narrowed sharply to $6.4 million from $44.0 million a year earlier — the company's second consecutive quarter of sequential revenue growth and improving adjusted EBITDA. The Dec. 10 special meeting on the Vireo share exchange stands unchanged.

Ascend Wellness's Thursday-evening earnings call clarified the one open question this desk flagged yesterday: the swing in net loss to $28.0 million from $9.8 million in Q2 was driven primarily by a one-time $14.8 million benefit in Q2 from releasing a reserve for uncertain tax positions — not an operating deterioration in Q3. Management also disclosed that third-party wholesale revenue fell 3.6% sequentially to $32.2 million, attributing the softness mainly to July's Barry, Illinois work stoppage (Teamsters Local 916) disrupting deliveries that month, with no indication of new or ongoing labor friction. The reverse-split ratio approved Aug. 28 remains unset. Separately, Verano's MÜV Daytona Beach opened exactly as planned Friday morning with a 9am ribbon-cutting — its 88th Florida dispensary and 165th nationwide — while Premier Group/PCF stayed clean on both the CA DCC registry and Modesto/Stanislaus local press.

Missouri's new intoxicating-cannabinoid law, which reached fuller effect Thursday, is already generating real fallout: St. Louis Public Radio reports hemp retailers across the state clearing shelves and closing shops rather than attempt compliance with the 0.4mg-THC reclassification threshold. At the federal level, the narrower "total THC" ban still lands Dec. 11, and a bipartisan bill to carve out hemp beverages under TTB-style alcohol regulation (Rep. Beth Van Duyne's Beverage Regulatory Parity Act) has not advanced past committee, with the Hemp Beverage Alliance now planning a Washington lobbying push Nov. 17-18 to try to move it before the deadline.

Why it matters to PGP Two weeks of broad, largely catalyst-light declines (MSOS -11.8% this week alone) argue the group is still trading on macro risk sentiment more than fundamentals — a useful caveat against reading too much into any single name's price action right now, including Glass House's partial bounce. Planet 13's quiet Q3 filing is the sharper lesson: with a scheduled merger vote six weeks out, PGP should not assume a target's reporting calendar matches trade-press expectations — verify primary filings directly rather than relying on secondary "has not yet reported" framing, which this desk itself carried into yesterday's edition.

The Rescheduling Docket

Missouri's new hemp law starts closing stores as the federal hemp-beverage carve-out stalls in committee, while the DEA's ALJ record stays quiet

No movement on the stayed Schedule I-to-III record itself since the Nov. 5 reply-brief deadline; today's action is on the state/federal hemp side.

  • Nov 10, 2026 DEA publishes its draft 2027 aggregate production-quota notice in the Federal Register, holding Schedule I marijuana quotas flat and confirming a separate Schedule III marijuana quota proposal remains in development; public comment runs through Dec. 10, 2026.
  • Nov 12, 2026 The federal rule stripping hemp status from synthetic "unnatural" cannabinoids (THC-O, HHC and similar) takes effect, independently confirmed by Friday wire coverage; the broader "total THC" cutoff for natural cannabinoids remains Dec. 11.
  • Nov 12, 2026 Missouri's Intoxicating Cannabinoid Control Act reaches fuller effect, reclassifying hemp products over 0.4mg THC as marijuana sellable only through licensed dispensaries.
  • Nov 13, 2026 St. Louis Public Radio reports Missouri hemp retailers clearing shelves and closing shops in direct response to the new law, with the state's health department estimating impact on roughly 40,000 food/smoke-shop establishments and 1,800 food manufacturers statewide.
  • Nov 13, 2026 MJBizDaily's standing coverage confirms Rep. Beth Van Duyne's Beverage Regulatory Parity Act — which would regulate hemp beverages like alcohol under TTB oversight — has not advanced past committee; the Hemp Beverage Alliance now plans a Washington lobbying push Nov. 17-18 ahead of the Dec. 11 deadline.
