Page One · The Big Story

MSOS Jumps 4.8% to $3.26 as a Historic Market Whipsaw Sparks a Short-Covering Rally Across Cannabis Small-Caps, Curaleaf Sweetens Its Aurora Bid to $6 and Pushes to Dec. 4, and a New Coalition Brief Calls DOJ's Rescheduling "Fatally Flawed"

Glass House, Planet 13, Jushi and Cresco all rip higher by 9%–15% while Trulieve and Ascend barely move — a sign the bounce is short-covering in the most distressed names, not a fundamental repricing, with the Dec. 11 hemp cliff and the DEA's Nov. 29 deadline still unresolved.

Wall Street staged one of its rarest reversals on record Wednesday, Nov. 25, and cannabis small-caps rode it hardest. The S&P 500 plunged as much as 3.2% intraday — rattled by fresh White House tariff threats on China (reportedly rising toward 40%) and the European Union, mounting worry over debt financing the AI-infrastructure buildout, and a hotter-than-expected October PCE inflation print (3.3% year-over-year, the highest since November 2023) — before clawing back nearly all of it to close down just 0.3% at 7,183; the Nasdaq fell as much as 4.6% intraday before finishing down only 0.6%. It was only the ninth such intraday-plunge-to-flat-close session since 1980. Thin, holiday-week volume and short-covering did the rest, and nowhere was the snapback sharper than in cannabis, where weeks of relentless selling had left the sector crowded with short positions and starved of buyers.

The AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF (MSOS) closed at $3.26, up 4.82% on volume of roughly 7.56 million shares — its best day in weeks — but still down 8.9% versus last Wednesday's (Nov. 18) $3.58 close and down 17.3% month-to-date against the Oct. 30 $3.94 baseline. The bounce was uneven in a telling way: Glass House Brands (+14.81%), Planet 13 (+10.61%), Jushi Holdings (+10.11%), Cresco Labs (+9.49%) and TerrAscend (+8.98%) — the lowest-priced, most heavily shorted names on the tape — led by a wide margin, while Trulieve (+0.23%) and Ascend Wellness (+0.50%) barely moved. AYR Wellness again returned no reliable quote, consistent with its CCAA wind-down.

On the deal desk, Curaleaf sweetened its hostile tender for Aurora Cannabis to up to US$6.00 per share (US$1.00 cash plus 0.4013 Curaleaf shares) and pushed its deadline back three days to Dec. 4 after clearing antitrust review in Canada and Germany — though Aurora's board continues to recommend Organigram's rival, friendly deal, for which Organigram filed its joint shareholder circular ahead of the Dec. 23 vote. On the regulatory front, a coalition of anti-rescheduling groups filed a new brief arguing the DOJ's Schedule III reclassification is itself unlawful, adding a fresh line of attack on the core rescheduling case even as the separate, consolidated D.C. Circuit challenge to the DEA's Nov. 29 supply-chain deadline still awaits a ruling. On the priority desk, Ascend Wellness, Verano Holdings and Premier Group/PCF all stayed quiet on net-new developments beyond items already carried in prior editions.

Why it matters to PGP Treat today's bounce as a market-structure event, not a fundamentals story: a historic macro whipsaw landing on a sector crowded with short positions in its most distressed names produced an outsized, uneven snapback — the kind of session that tends to fade once holiday-week liquidity returns, not the start of a trend. The real risk calendar is unchanged and getting closer: the DEA's Nov. 29 supply-chain deadline, Curaleaf's Dec. 4 tender deadline, Planet 13's Dec. 10 vote, the Dec. 11 hemp-THC cliff, and Aurora's Dec. 23 vote are all still live and unresolved. Clients holding cannabis equity exposure into Thanksgiving week should not mistake one green day for a bottom.

The Rescheduling Docket

A New Anti-Rescheduling Brief Opens a Second Legal Front as the D.C. Circuit Still Hasn't Ruled

The core Schedule I-to-III reclassification now faces its own direct legal challenge, layered on top of the still-unresolved D.C. Circuit fight over the DEA's Nov. 29 supply-chain deadline and the unresolved Dec. 11 hemp-THC funding-bill standoff.

  • Nov 17, 2026 Sens. Tim Sheehy (R-Mont.) and Amy Klobuchar (D-Minn.) introduce the bipartisan "Hemp Guardrails Act" (S.5736); it remains in Senate HELP Committee with no hearing, markup or vote scheduled.
  • Nov 19-20, 2026 Judge Martha Pacold denies, without prejudice, the Illinois Healthy Alternatives Association's bid to pause the state's hemp-THC ban; a preliminary-injunction hearing in the underlying case is set for Dec. 15.
  • Nov 23, 2026 DOJ files opposition in the consolidated D.C. Circuit "Timely Applicant" case (Nos. 26-1324/26-1325), calling the operators' stay motions "without merit"; separately, the White House is reported to have sent Capitol Hill a request to keep some hemp-derived products legal in the year-end funding bill.
  • Nov 24, 2026 Company reply briefs conclude the D.C. Circuit's "Timely Applicant" briefing, with no ruling issued; fourteen GOP senators led by Cotton and Budd send a letter urging President Trump to reject any hemp-THC carve-out in the funding bill, warning it could "threaten the entire bill."
  • Nov 25, 2026 A coalition of anti-rescheduling groups files a new brief in the broader Schedule I-to-III proceeding arguing the DOJ's rescheduling move is legally "fatally flawed" — opening a second, direct line of attack on the reclassification itself, distinct from the supply-chain-rule litigation. The DEA separately publishes a Federal Register proposed rule adjusting 2026 aggregate production quotas for Schedule I/II substances tied to the rescheduling process.

PGP takeaway The rescheduling thesis just picked up a new source of tail risk: it is no longer only a question of timing (when will the ALJ rule, when will the Administrator act) but now also a question of durability, with organized opposition actively attacking the legal basis of the DOJ's Schedule III move itself. Combined with the unresolved Dec. 11 hemp-THC standoff and the still-pending D.C. Circuit supply-chain ruling, clients should model all three regulatory threads — rescheduling, the supply-chain rule, and hemp-THC — as independently live through year-end, not as a single resolving event.
