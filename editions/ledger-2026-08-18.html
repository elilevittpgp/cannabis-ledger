Curaleaf's Bid for Aurora Goes Live: The Circular Is Mailed, the Clock Starts at Dec. 1

A week after floating an unsolicited $272M offer, Curaleaf converts intention into a formally commenced take-over bid — no financing condition, a hard expiry, and Aurora's special committee still without a recommendation.

Curaleaf Holdings formally commenced its take-over bid for Aurora Cannabis today, Aug. 18, filing and mailing the Offer and Take-Over Bid Circular to Aurora shareholders. The move converts the Aug. 11 "intention to launch" announcement into a live, running offer. Terms are unchanged from the original proposal: US$4.00 per share implied value — 0.3463 Curaleaf subordinate voting shares plus US$0.75 cash per Aurora share, referenced to Curaleaf's Aug. 10 close — capped at US$5.00/share with an exchange-ratio adjuster if Curaleaf's 20-day VWAP runs above the cap. That is roughly a 45% premium to Aurora's 30-day VWAP of US$2.75, or about 110% ex-cash. Critically, the bid carries no financing or diligence condition — only customary regulatory approvals and a majority-of-the-minority support threshold — and now has a hard expiry of 5:00 p.m. MT, December 1, 2026, unless extended.

Aurora's board has formed a special committee of independent directors to evaluate the proposal but as of this edition has adopted no poison pill and issued no formal recommendation for or against; the company continues to point to its own standalone plan — the Safari Flower acquisition and EU-GMP capacity expansion — as its answer to the bid. No counter-bid has surfaced. The formal commencement is the clearest signal yet that Curaleaf intends to see this through directly to Aurora's shareholders rather than waiting on the board. Elsewhere, Trulieve rang the NYSE closing bell today — the first U.S. plant-touching cannabis operator to do so — a symbolic capital-markets marker for the uplisting wave; Rhode Island's federal court cleared roughly 20 frozen retail cannabis licenses to resume processing; and Virginia's hemp industry, having filed no appeal of last week's injunction denial, is now pushing lawmakers for a special legislative session as store closures mount. Ascend, Verano and Premier Group/PCF were all quiet; see Priority Coverage below.

Why it matters to PGP A formally commenced, no-financing-condition hostile bid with a hard December 1 expiry is a different animal than an "intention to launch" press release — it starts the clock on tender mechanics, forces Aurora's special committee toward an actual recommendation, and gives the market a concrete date to underwrite. Expect volatility to stay concentrated in ACB (already the tape's runaway leader on the week) while CURLF is discounted for deal/dilution risk. For PGP, the live playbook is the one to study: an MSO using its own uplisted stock plus a capped cash sweetener to go hostile on a cross-border target, bypassing a reluctant board. That structure — and Aurora's special-committee response — is now a template worth benchmarking against for any sell-side or defense mandate in the sector.

The Rescheduling Docket

The briefing deadline passes quietly — no confirmation it produced filings

Monday's post-hearing brief deadline came and went without a single trade outlet confirming a filing was made, even as state-level fallout from the federal process keeps compounding — one unlock in Rhode Island, one setback in Virginia.

  • Jul 15, 2026 DEA rescheduling hearings conclude; Chief ALJ Derek Julius sets a 50-page post-hearing brief cap, due Aug 17.
  • Aug 14, 2026 Judge Ballou denies Virginia hemp retailers' injunction bid against HB 30's 2mg THC cap.
  • Aug 15, 2026 HB 30 takes effect; retailers pull an estimated 85–90% of affected inventory.
  • Aug 17, 2026 DEA post-hearing brief deadline passes — no outlet has confirmed briefs were actually filed or what they argued; no timeline exists yet for Julius's recommended decision.
  • Aug 17, 2026 Virginia plaintiffs confirm no appeal filed; industry instead publicly presses lawmakers to convene a special legislative session as store closures mount (a Danville shop has already closed; several others project five- and six-figure revenue losses).
  • Aug 17, 2026 A federal judge dissolves Rhode Island's cannabis-license injunction after the state repeals its residency requirement and the First Circuit dismisses the underlying suits — clearing roughly 20 frozen retail licenses and ~100 applicants to resume.
  • Aug 18, 2026 Georgia's cannabis commission proposes a rule permitting mail delivery of medical cannabis, citing DOJ's Schedule III reclassification as the legal enabler; comment period runs through Sept 9.

PGP takeaway The federal rescheduling record is fully briefed (probably) with no stated decision timeline, while its downstream effects keep diverging by state: Rhode Island just turned a frozen license queue back on, and Georgia is using Schedule III to justify a new delivery channel, even as Virginia's hemp operators absorb real store closures with no litigation path left open. Diligence on any state-exposed hemp or cannabis asset should keep weighting state-specific outcomes over the still-undated federal calendar.
