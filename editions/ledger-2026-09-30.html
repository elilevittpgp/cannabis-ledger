Page One · The Big Story

MSOS Craters ~13% as a DEA Judge Hits Pause on Rescheduling

A stay granted to reschedule's opponents — not a ruling against it — wipes out weeks of gains in a single session; Trulieve and Glass House lead the damage.

The sector's benchmark ETF is having its worst day of the year. The AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF (NYSE Arca: MSOS) traded around $4.45 in the early afternoon, down roughly 12.6%–13% on the session, after sliding some 8% in Tuesday after-hours trading the moment the news broke. The trigger: DEA Chief Administrative Law Judge Derek Julius issued a stay Sept. 29 in the Schedule I-to-III rescheduling proceeding, granting a request from three rescheduling opponents — DUID Victim Voices, Dr. Kenneth Finn, and the National Drug and Alcohol Screening Association — to consider adding a newly released GAO report on drug-scheduling procedural deficiencies to the record. The DEA has until Oct. 13 to respond; Julius has not yet decided whether to actually reopen the record.

The move does not kill rescheduling — the hearing record was already closed and post-hearing briefs filed back in August — but it pushes the timeline further to the right just as the market had priced in a near-term recommended decision. Cantor Fitzgerald reiterated its Overweight rating on Trulieve but conceded a year-end 2026 outcome is now unlikely, calling first-half 2027 "increasingly plausible." Trulieve (TRLV) itself fell roughly 9% in after-hours trading Tuesday before adding a same-day catalyst of its own — a new exclusive Alien Labs/Connected brand license for Florida and Texas — which didn't stop a further pre-open slide Wednesday. Glass House Brands was the session's worst performer among watchlist names, down roughly 15%, with Curaleaf off about 7%.

Why it matters to PGP A procedural stay — triggered by a request to admit one GAO report, not new evidence undermining rescheduling's merits — has erased roughly two weeks of sector gains in under 18 hours. That fragility is the real signal for diligence: models keyed to a Q4 2026 280E resolution need a later, later-2026-or-1H2027 case run, and any mandate pricing on "rescheduling optionality" should stress-test a further multi-quarter delay. The selloff is indiscriminate — quiet names (TerrAscend, roughly flat) got spared no more than loud ones (Glass House, -15%) — reinforcing that this is a macro/regulatory repricing, not stock-specific news.

The Rescheduling Docket

Schedule I → III: a stay, not a setback on the merits

The case record is frozen while the ALJ considers whether to admit a new GAO report — pushing the recommended decision further into 2027 territory by market consensus.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E.
  • Jul 15, 2026 Broader Schedule I→III hearings conclude; ALJ Derek Julius later orders transcript corrections.
  • Aug 17, 2026 Post-hearing briefs due; full 2,533-page transcript published Aug 25.
  • Sep 23, 2026 GAO report finds DEA and FDA lack adequate written drug-scheduling procedures.
  • Sep 28, 2026 Three rescheduling opponents (DUID Victim Voices, Dr. Kenneth Finn, NDASA) move to add the GAO report to the record and delay the recommendation.
  • Sep 29, 2026 ALJ Julius stays the case; DEA given until Oct 13 to respond (up to 20 pages); other parties may respond by the same date.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. Oct 13, 2026 — DEA's response to the motion to reopen the record is due.
  2. ALJ Julius rules on whether to admit the GAO report and permit added briefing.
  3. If granted, a further briefing round precedes the recommended decision; if denied, the case resumes toward a decision on the existing record.
  4. A 20-day exceptions window, then the DEA Administrator's final call — with sell-side analysts (Cantor Fitzgerald) now modeling 1H 2027 as the more likely resolution window.
PGP takeaway This is a procedural delay tied to record-admission, not a ruling against rescheduling's merits — but the market treated it as a multi-week setback, erasing ~13% of sector value in a session. Any model assuming a Q4 2026 or even Q1 2027 280E resolution needs a later base case; the practical effect on deal timing is that acquirers relying on post-reschedule tax relief to underwrite leverage should re-run diligence with a 1H–2H 2027 assumption.
