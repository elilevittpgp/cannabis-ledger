Page One · The Big Story

MSOS Erases Monday's Bounce, Sinks 4.9% to a Fresh Low of $3.11 as 14 GOP Senators Warn Trump Off a Hemp Carve-Out, DEA Reply Briefs Land With No Ruling, and Jushi Strikes a $6.5 Million Illinois Deal

Nearly every tracked constituent closes red Tuesday as the sector's worst stretch this year deepens; Curaleaf slides on "takeover uncertainty" headlines even as its Aurora bid stays open, and Trulieve wins a spot in the Russell 3000's 2026 reconstitution.

Monday's relief rally didn't survive the day. MSOS, the AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF, closed Tuesday, Nov. 24 at $3.11 on volume of roughly 3.6 million shares — down 4.89% on the day, erasing all of Monday's 4.14% bounce and then some. The ETF is now down 14.8% versus last Tuesday's (Nov. 17) $3.65 close and down 21.1% month-to-date against the Oct. 30 $3.94 baseline — one of the sector's worst stretches on record. Every verified constituent closed lower: Ascend Wellness (-11.33%) and Planet 13 (-8.97%) led the declines, while Cresco Labs (-0.46%) and Glass House Brands (-0.92%) were the smallest decliners. AYR Wellness again returned no reliable quote, consistent with its distressed wind-down.

The macro driver sharpened today. Fourteen Senate Republicans led by Tom Cotton (R-Ark.) and Ted Budd (R-N.C.) sent a letter to President Trump urging him to reject a White House push to add hemp-THC carve-out language to the year-end government funding bill. Cotton, posting on X, called the administration's request to keep "gas-station candy marijuana" on shelves unacceptable and warned that insisting on such language would "threaten the entire bill." The letter follows reporting that the White House is separately asking Congress to keep at least some hemp-derived products legal — setting up a direct clash between the administration and hardline Senate Republicans just 17 days before the Dec. 11 hemp-THC ban deadline. The bipartisan Hemp Guardrails Act (S.5736, Sheehy/Klobuchar) remains parked in the Senate HELP Committee with no hearing, markup or vote scheduled, and House Oversight Chair James Comer has said he doesn't expect a standalone hemp vote before the deadline.

Separately, company reply briefs were filed today by 4pm ET in the consolidated, seven-operator D.C. Circuit case over the DEA's Nov. 29 "Timely Applicant" supply-chain deadline — concluding briefing ahead of a ruling the three-judge panel (Rao, Walker, Garcia) could issue any day. On the deal desk, Jushi Holdings agreed to buy an Illinois cultivation-and-processing facility for $6.5 million cash, its sixth vertically integrated state, while Trulieve was added to the Russell 3000 in FTSE Russell's 2026 reconstitution, effective after the close on Dec. 11 — the same day as the hemp-ban deadline. Curaleaf shares fell 6.51% amid "takeover uncertainty" coverage even as its Aurora Cannabis tender offer remains open, unchanged, through its Dec. 4 deadline. On the priority desk, Ascend Wellness and Verano Holdings stayed quiet on net-new developments beyond Monday's items, and Premier Group/PCF was confirmed clean again: no CA DCC enforcement action or Modesto/Stanislaus local-press item surfaced in the window.

Why it matters to PGP Three threads worth a client call today: (1) today's decline is sector-wide and macro-driven — a hemp-cliff standoff plus unresolved DEA litigation, not an idiosyncratic repricing — so Dec. 11 should be modeled as a genuine binary risk date, not a formality; (2) the open rift between hardline Senate Republicans and the White House means outcomes through Dec. 11 are now less predictable than a week ago, and clients with hemp-beverage or hemp-THC revenue exposure should stress-test both a full ban and a delay/carve-out scenario; and (3) Jushi's and Ascend's continued appetite for sub-$40M bolt-on M&A even amid depressed equity values shows consolidation isn't pausing for the macro storm, while Cansortium's CIRO-ordered trading halt is a live cautionary data point on debt-funded roll-up execution risk worth raising with clients evaluating similar structures.

PGP takeaway The Dec. 11 hemp deadline is no longer a one-sided brinksmanship story — the White House and hardline Senate Republicans now publicly disagree on whether to delay it, which widens the range of plausible outcomes. Clients with hemp-beverage or hemp-derived-THC revenue should model a full-ban scenario as the base case but keep a carve-out scenario live until the funding bill's language is actually settled. Medical-only MSOs with Pennsylvania-style supply chains should continue to treat the Nov. 29 deadline as live until a stay actually issues.
