Page One · The Big Story

MSOS Bounces 4.1% to $3.27 as a Seven-Operator D.C. Circuit Case Over the DEA's Nov. 29 Deadline Reaches Its Decisive Hour, Glass House Draws a Second Securities Probe, and Ascend Strikes a $35.5 Million Ohio Deal

A Monday rebound claws back a sliver of the sector's worst two-week stretch on record as reply briefs come due today in the consolidated medical-marijuana supply-chain case, Illinois's hemp-THC ban stands unchallenged on appeal, and Verano quietly opens its 89th Florida store.

Cannabis equities found a floor Monday after two consecutive, cannabis-unrelated Wall Street routs had erased more than a quarter of the sector's value in a fortnight. MSOS, the AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF, closed Nov. 23 at $3.27 on volume of roughly 3.9–4.0 million shares — up 4.14% on the day — though it remains down 11.4% versus last Monday's (Nov. 16) $3.69 close and down 17.0% month-to-date against the Oct. 30 $3.94 baseline. The bounce was led by the sector's smallest, most beaten-down names: TerrAscend jumped 18.17%, Jushi Holdings 17.65% and Verano 12.30%, while Glass House Brands was the lone decliner on the tracked list, falling 7.63%. Jushi was the week's only net gainer among constituents (+5.26%); Cresco Labs (-21.42%), Vireo Growth (-16.53%) and Glass House (-15.50%) remained the week's deepest laggards. AYR Wellness again returned no reliable quote, consistent with its distressed wind-down.

The sharper story sits in federal court. A consolidated, seven-operator case before the D.C. Circuit challenging the DEA's Oct. 30 "Medical Marijuana Timely Applicant Information" guidance reaches its most critical point yet today: after Standard Wellness (Utah), Verde Americano (Ariz.) and NW Confections (Okla.) filed the original emergency petition Nov. 20, a second group of Pennsylvania-based medical operators — G.P.M. II, MLH Explorations, Tri-Mountain Pure and Story of PA CR, an entity operating under the Ascend Wellness business umbrella — filed a parallel challenge, now consolidated before a three-judge panel (Rao, Walker, Garcia). DOJ filed its opposition brief Nov. 23 calling the motions "without merit"; company reply briefs are due today, Nov. 24, by 4pm ET, with a ruling expected any day ahead of the rule's Nov. 29 compliance deadline. Separately, the Illinois Healthy Alternatives Association's bid to block the state's own hemp-THC ban remains denied without prejudice since Judge Martha Pacold's Nov. 20 order — the group says it is "exploring" a Seventh Circuit appeal but has not yet filed one, leaving the ban (in effect since Nov. 12) as the operative baseline.

On the priority desk, Ascend Wellness is the live name: a subsidiary, Ohio Patient Access, agreed Nov. 20 to acquire an Ohio cultivation facility and five dispensaries for $35.5 million in cash, even as the board-approved reverse stock split shareholders authorized back on Aug. 28 remains unset roughly 90 days later and the stock sits near its 52-week low. Verano stayed quiet on financials but held a ribbon-cutting Nov. 23 for MÜV Tarpon Springs, its 89th Florida store and 166th nationwide. Premier Group/PCF was confirmed clean: no CA DCC enforcement action or Modesto/Stanislaus local-press item surfaced in the window. Glass House Brands drew a second securities-claims investigation (Kirby McInerney, following Highful Law) over its June 2026 deconsolidation disclosures, and TerrAscend workers at its Phillipsburg, N.J. Apothecarium confirmed their vote to join UFCW Local 360.

Why it matters to PGP Three threads worth a client call today: (1) Monday's bounce is a relief rally inside a still-brutal drawdown, not a reversal — MSOS remains down double digits on the week and the month, so entry-point framing should lean on valuation, not momentum; (2) the D.C. Circuit's ruling on the Nov. 29 "timely applicant" deadline is genuinely imminent and directly touches portfolio exposure with Pennsylvania/medical-only supply chains — including an Ascend affiliate — so counterpart diligence should happen before Thanksgiving, not after; and (3) Glass House's widening securities-investigation roster is now a two-firm pattern worth flagging on any disclosure-quality diligence checklist touching deconsolidation accounting.

The Rescheduling Docket

The DEA's Nov. 29 Deadline Reaches Its Decisive Hour as Illinois's Hemp Ban Stands and the Dec. 11 Cliff Hardens

A consolidated seven-operator D.C. Circuit case moves to its final briefing stage today, while the core Schedule I-to-III record stays stalled awaiting the ALJ's recommended decision.

  • Nov 5, 2026 DEA rescheduling opponents and proponents close the final post-hearing briefing round before Chief ALJ Derek Julius; no date set for his recommended decision.
  • Nov 17, 2026 Sens. Tim Sheehy (R-Mont.) and Amy Klobuchar (D-Minn.) introduce the bipartisan "Hemp Guardrails Act" (S.5736), redefining hemp at a 1% total-THC threshold ahead of the Dec. 11 deadline.
  • Nov 18, 2026 Sens. Ted Budd (R-N.C.) and Pete Ricketts (R-Neb.) vow to block any government funding bill that preserves hemp-THC legality, hardening the path to Dec. 11.
  • Nov 20, 2026 Judge Martha Pacold's denial of Illinois's hemp-ban TRO stands unappealed; the state's ban remains in force. Three medical operators petition the D.C. Circuit over the DEA's Nov. 29 "Timely Applicant" deadline.
  • Nov 21-23, 2026 A second group of Pennsylvania operators (G.P.M. II, MLH Explorations, Tri-Mountain Pure, Story of PA CR) files a parallel D.C. Circuit challenge, consolidated before a three-judge panel (Rao, Walker, Garcia); DOJ opposes Nov. 23.
  • Nov 24, 2026 Company reply briefs due today, 4pm ET, in the consolidated D.C. Circuit case — a ruling is expected any day ahead of the Nov. 29 compliance deadline.
