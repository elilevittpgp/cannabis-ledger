Page One · The Big Story

DEA's Rescheduling Case Splits the Baby — the Judge Admits the GAO Report but Denies New Discovery — as the Sector Tape Gives Back Two Days of Gains

Chief ALJ Derek Julius rules on the Oct. 21 deadline's aftermath: the GAO's September report enters the stayed Schedule I-to-III record, but rescheduling opponents' bid for fresh testimony and document discovery is denied, with narrow supplemental briefing now due Oct. 29 and Nov. 5; MSOS gives back roughly a third of Tuesday-Wednesday's advance, Jushi reverses most of a 9.59% pop, and a Virginia state senator is indicted on federal marijuana-trafficking charges.

The DEA's own rescheduling docket produced genuine news today rather than another missed deadline. The agency's response — filed Oct. 20, one day ahead of ALJ Julius's Oct. 21 deadline — argued that opponents' renewed bid for new testimony and document discovery was "an obvious fishing expedition," according to The Marijuana Herald. Julius agreed on that narrow point this morning and denied reopening the hearing record for new testimony or discovery. But he split the difference on the GAO's Sept. 23 report (GAO-26-108623): rather than keep it out, he admitted it into the record and ordered supplemental briefing limited to what weight the report should carry — due from the parties by Oct. 29, with replies due Nov. 5 — while the case remains formally stayed through that window, per Marijuana Moment and NORML's own same-day coverage.

The sector tape gave back ground. MSOS, which closed Wednesday at a verified $4.13 (+3.51%) after touching an intraday high near $4.20, traded down 1.69% to $4.06 by early Thursday afternoon on partial-day volume of roughly 3.12 million shares — unwinding a meaningful slice of Tuesday-Wednesday's broad, largely catalyst-free advance. Ten of eleven constituents checked this run were lower, with only Trulieve (+0.73%) in the green; Jushi Holdings, which had jumped 9.59% Wednesday on sector optimism ahead of its Oct. 28 earnings call, reversed to -6.32%, while Glass House Brands gave back part of Wednesday's bounce, falling 5.36%. Cresco Labs (-5.19%) and Verano (-4.73%) were the next-largest decliners; Verano's reversal comes a day after Kalkine reported the stock up 4.17% Wednesday.

Elsewhere, the Justice Department announced the indictment of Virginia state Sen. L. Louise Lucas on federal racketeering and marijuana-trafficking charges tied to a cannabis business she co-owned — a rare instance of a sitting state legislator facing federal cannabis-trafficking charges even as the executive branch pursues administrative rescheduling. On the M&A front, Aurora Cannabis's board reaffirmed its rejection of Curaleaf's hostile bid and said it "welcomes other bids," per MJBizDaily, while Vireo Growth disclosed a separate $21 million purchase of a leased Florida cultivation facility even as its Nevada-cleared, all-stock Planet 13 acquisition sits with an S-4 not yet effective 13 days after filing.

Why it matters to PGP A split ruling — GAO report in, new discovery out — is not a victory for either side, and it extends rather than shortens the runway: the Oct. 29/Nov. 5 supplemental-briefing window pushes any ALJ recommended decision further out, reinforcing this desk's standing view that clients should keep discounting a near-term rescheduling resolution. Separately, Thursday's broad giveback — on no identified company-specific catalyst for most names — is itself a useful data point: Tuesday-Wednesday's rally looked more like generalized short-covering than a re-rating, and today's reversal (especially Jushi's round trip) is consistent with that read rather than a contradiction of it. Clients should not treat either the rally or the pullback as information about sector fundamentals.

Industry Headlines

Virginia state Sen. L. Louise Lucas indicted on federal racketeering and marijuana-trafficking charges

The Justice Department announced the indictment, tied to a cannabis business the sitting state senator co-owned — a rare federal trafficking case against an elected official even as the executive branch separately pursues administrative rescheduling. Dept. of Justice

NORML's Sixth Circuit amicus brief surfaces in full: urges a case-by-case test over a blanket bar on cannabis-contract enforcement

The brief, filed Oct. 15 via attorney William Swor and posted Oct. 19, argues the court's Sept. 16 reversal of Hello Farms' $31.8M verdict against Curaleaf subsidiaries should not become a categorical rule voiding all cannabis-industry contracts. NORML · Marijuana Moment

Jushi Holdings whipsaws from a 9.59% gain to a 6.32% loss inside 24 hours ahead of its Oct. 28 earnings call

The reversal, alongside similar round trips elsewhere on the tape, underscores how much of this week's sector move has been positioning rather than news-driven. Kalkine · stockanalysis.com

Consumer approval of the administration's cannabis-policy handling slides to 29% as rescheduling delays continue

A Marijuana Moment/NuggMD poll published Oct. 21 found approval down from 73% two quarters ago, with respondents citing the rescheduling docket's repeated delays explicitly. Marijuana Moment

Two new Massachusetts polls break further against Question 8's adult-use repeal push

A Suffolk University/Boston Globe poll (fielded Oct. 14-18) found 71% opposed to repeal versus 21% in favor; a UMass Amherst/WCVB poll found 61% opposed versus 31% in favor — both wider margins than prior surveys ahead of the Nov. 3 vote. Boston Globe · MJBizDaily

Delaware's new hemp THC-beverage framework took effect this week

The state's dual-channel regulatory structure for hemp-derived THC beverages, permitting sales at liquor stores and licensed dispensaries, became effective Oct. 21 — a rare state move toward structured regulation ahead of the federal Dec. 11 ban deadline. The Marijuana Herald
