Page One · The Week in Review

The Hemp Delay Becomes Law — With a Catch Nobody Priced In

Congress hands the industry a Dec. 11 reprieve, then a day of reporting reveals it's really two deadlines five weeks apart; Aurora formally rejects Curaleaf's hostile bid and the fight stalls; Cresco closes a $50M Pennsylvania bolt-on; a distressed lender's foreclosure gets a real auction calendar; and MSOS notches a second straight weekly gain while thinly-traded names post rallies nobody can explain.

No. 1 · Federal Regulatory

The signed hemp-ban delay turns out to be narrower than the headline

President Trump signed H.R. 6500, the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027, into law on Sept. 2, funding the government through Dec. 11 and, in the same bill, pushing the federal hemp-THC redefinition's effective date from Nov. 12 to that same date. The catch, clarified in trade coverage over the following two days: the extension only applies to naturally-occurring hemp cannabinoids — THCA flower, hemp-derived delta-9 — now measured against a far stricter 0.4-milligram total-THC-per-container cap than the old 0.3%-dry-weight standard. Converted or synthetic cannabinoids — delta-8, HHC, THC-O — get no reprieve at all and lose federal hemp status on the original Nov. 12, 2026 date. For a category that built much of its shelf space on delta-8, "the ban got delayed" and "the ban got delayed for me" are two different questions. Momentum moved fastest on the fix for the surviving half: the Wine & Spirits Wholesalers of America, joined by Total Wine, the American Beverage Licensees and the Hemp Beverage Alliance, publicly endorsed Reps. Beth Van Duyne (R-TX) and Greg Landsman's (D-OH) Beverage Regulatory Parity Act — a 5mg-delta-9-per-serving cap, 21-plus purchase rule, and TTB-led alcohol-style three-tier distribution. Neither that bill nor Rep. Comer's separate packaging/testing bill has floor time scheduled.

Why it matters to PGP Diligence files built around a single Dec. 11 cliff now need a second, earlier one: any target or portfolio company with delta-8/HHC/THC-O exposure faces its compliance deadline Nov. 12 — five fewer weeks than the topline "delay" implies, and with no legislative rescue vehicle currently moving on its behalf. Separately, alcohol-distribution incumbents publicly backing a hemp-beverage carve-out is a real signal that the category is now viewed as investable, not just tolerable — a beverage asset built on naturally-occurring cannabinoids with a credible path through Van Duyne-Landsman is a materially different underwrite than one built on converted cannabinoids with no bill moving.
No. 2 · M&A & Governance

Aurora says no to Curaleaf — and the hostile bid goes quiet

Aurora Cannabis's board unanimously rejected Curaleaf's hostile bid via a formal Directors' Circular on Sept. 2, urging shareholders not to tender and calling the offer inadequate — citing a debt-free balance sheet with roughly C$149M cash against Curaleaf's C$1B-plus in debt. Curaleaf reaffirmed its offer the same day, disputing Aurora's framing and noting the company has raised roughly $398M via equity since 2020 (~31% cumulative dilution). Neither side has said anything new since. The bid — US$4.00/share implied (0.3463 Curaleaf shares plus US$0.75 cash), capped at US$5.00/share — carries no financing condition and expires 5pm MT Dec. 1, 2026 unless extended. No poison pill, litigation, sweetened terms, or rival bid has surfaced.

No. 3 · M&A

Cresco closes its Pennsylvania bolt-on, becomes the state's largest medical retailer

Cresco Labs' $50 million acquisition of nine PharmaCann Pennsylvania medical dispensaries — previously run under a management-services arrangement since April — fully closed Sept. 2, cash-plus-note, cash-free and debt-free. The deal makes Cresco the largest medical-cannabis retailer and wholesaler in a state generating an estimated $1.1–1.8 billion in annual sales. Separately, Cresco's own insurer, Palomar Excess & Surplus, continues to press its Northern District of Illinois suit seeking a declaration it owes no duty to defend or indemnify Cresco in the Murray et al. RICO/consumer-fraud class actions spanning 12 states — no ruling or update surfaced this window beyond the original August filing.

No. 4 · Distressed Credit

A foreclosure gets a real calendar: Justice Grown's collateral heads to auction

Advanced Flower Capital's Article 9 foreclosure against Justice Grown (Justice Cannabis Company) — a $78.8M loan in maturity default since May 1, carried at a $42.0M fair value as of June 30 — now has dated, sourced milestones: a court hearing Sept. 15, followed by Pennsylvania retail-collateral auctions on Sept. 24 and New Jersey dispensary-collateral auctions on Oct. 6, with SSG Advisors retained to market the assets. No new developments surfaced specifically in the Aug. 29–Sept. 4 window beyond the calendar itself remaining on track — but it is now the desk's clearest live, dated distressed-asset entry point.

The AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF (MSOS) closed the week at $5.05, up 1.20% versus last Friday's $4.99 close and up 2.02% month-to-date versus the Aug. 31 close of $4.95. Eleventh Circuit revives a challenge to Georgia's MMJ licensing program

The federal appeals court reinstated a constitutional challenge to how Georgia's medical-cannabis commission awarded its production licenses, reopening litigation risk around the state's licensing framework. Justia · 11th Circuit

Connecticut loosens its medical-dispensing rule

New state guidance permits medical-cannabis sales without a pharmacist physically on-site, easing a staffing constraint that has weighed on smaller dispensary operators. The Marijuana Herald

San Diego County approves a cannabis social-equity licensing program

The county joins a small group of California jurisdictions building dedicated equity pathways into local retail licensing, alongside continued local scrutiny of testing and packaging compliance statewide. The Marijuana Herald
