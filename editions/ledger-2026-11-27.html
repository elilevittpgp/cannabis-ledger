Page One · The Big Story

MSOS Climbs Another 6.75% to $3.48 in a Thin Black Friday Session as the Rescheduling-Stay Rally Extends to a Second Day, While Ascend, Verano and Premier Group/PCF Stay Quiet into the Holiday Weekend

No single fresh catalyst explains the broad-based buying — holiday volume ran a little over half of Wednesday's pace — even as the Aurora Cannabis contest between Organigram and Curaleaf sits at a standoff and the DEA's Nov. 29 "Timely Applicant" deadline arrives in two days for every category outside Tuesday's narrow court stay.

U.S. cannabis equities printed their first new session since Wednesday's close — Thursday was shut for Thanksgiving — in Friday's historically shortened, post-holiday half-day. MSOS (AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF) closed at $3.48, up 6.75% on roughly 4.31 million shares, per stockanalysis.com, cross-checked against Yahoo Finance; that is a second consecutive advance after Wednesday's 4.82% bounce and extends the rebound off Tuesday's $3.11 closing low to nearly 12% in two sessions. Gains were broad: Verano Holdings (VRNOF) led at +9.86% to $3.90, with TerrAscend (TSNDF, +9–10% to roughly $0.40–0.41), Cresco Labs (CRLBF, +8.90% to $0.46) and Vireo Growth (VREOF, +7–8% to roughly $5.75–5.80) close behind; Green Thumb (GTBIF, +5.29%) and Curaleaf (CURLF, +3.57%) lagged the leaders but still finished green. Planet 13 (PLNHF) was the lone decliner the desk could independently confirm, down 1.73% to $0.87. No trade-press source identified a company-specific or sector-specific catalyst for today's move; coverage attributed it loosely to lingering optimism from Tuesday's D.C. Circuit stay of DEA's medical-marijuana guidance, which is two sessions old news, not a fresh print.

On the priority desk, all three names stayed quiet. Ascend Wellness (AAWH) had no net-new corporate news in the window; a direct fetch of investors.awholdings.com again returned HTTP 403, consistent with recent editions, and the one Nov. 26-dated item found — an automated third-party stock-comparison post — is not a company disclosure. Verano Holdings had no net-new item; the IR domain this desk tracks, investors.veranoholdings.com, failed to resolve (DNS error), and a search of Verano's newsroom turned up nothing dated Nov. 26–27. Premier Group/PCF came back clean again: no CA Department of Cannabis Control enforcement action and no Modesto/Stanislaus local-press item touching PCF Ceres/209, X1 Tracy, pHenos Modesto, Flavors Santa Cruz or Firehouse Turlock; a statewide enforcement sweep reported this month (a Butte County processing-operation bust) was confirmed unrelated, roughly 170 miles north of the Premier Group's Stanislaus County footprint. Separately, Jushi Holdings disclosed that director Denis Arsenault purchased 101,000 shares on Nov. 24 (filed Nov. 25) — a modest insider-buying signal layered on top of its standing Illinois and Virginia real-estate items, not a new corporate action.

Why it matters to PGP Treat today's near-7% MSOS print as a thin-volume, sentiment-driven continuation, not confirmation of a new fundamental catalyst — at ~4.3 million shares the tape traded a little over half Wednesday's volume, and no outlet tied the move to a specific disclosure. For clients modeling the Aurora Cannabis contest, both deadlines that matter are now close and uninformed by anything new: Curaleaf's unchanged $6.00/share tender cap expires Dec. 4, no ISS or Glass Lewis recommendation has issued on the competing Organigram arrangement ahead of the Dec. 23 vote, and the DEA's Nov. 29 "Timely Applicant" deadline — stayed only for a narrow slice of state-licensed medical activity — otherwise takes effect in two days. A two-session, nearly 12% equity bounce built on holiday-thin volume is not a basis for re-pricing stock-based consideration; price any current-week structure off it with that caveat attached.

The Rescheduling Docket

No New Court Action Since Tuesday's Targeted Stay — the Nov. 29 Deadline Now Arrives in Two Days for Everything It Doesn't Cover

The D.C. Circuit's administrative stay narrows the immediate harm from DEA's supply-chain guidance for one category of state-licensed medical activity; a separate direct challenge to the Schedule I-to-III reclassification and the Dec. 11 hemp-THC funding-bill standoff both remain unresolved and independent of it.

  • Nov 18, 2026 Standard Wellness Utah, Verde Americano, NW Confections OK and affiliated petitioners file emergency stay motions at the D.C. Circuit (Nos. 26-1324, consolidated with 26-1325 and 26-1329) against DEA's Oct. 30 "Timely Applicant" guidance.
  • Nov 23, 2026 North Dakota, Utah and West Virginia file an amicus brief backing the petitioners; DEA files a brief the same day calling the challenges "without merit."
  • Nov 25, 2026 The D.C. Circuit grants a targeted administrative stay — not a merits ruling — pausing enforcement specifically against state-licensed medical-marijuana activity involving entities that are neither federal registrants nor Timely Applicants, days ahead of the Nov. 29 deadline. Separately, a coalition led by MMJ International Holdings files a brief calling DOJ's Schedule III reclassification itself "fatally flawed" (DOJ's response is not due until Jan. 13, 2027).
  • Nov 26, 2026 U.S. markets closed for Thanksgiving; no new court filing reported. Organigram and Aurora file their joint circular on the separate M&A track (see tracker above).
  • Nov 27, 2026 No further D.C. Circuit action, DEA response or ALJ development reported; the underlying Nov. 29 deadline otherwise stands for every category outside Tuesday's narrow stay, now two days out.
