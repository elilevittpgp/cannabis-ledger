Page One · The Week in Review

A Procedural Stay Erases 13% of Sector Value, Verano Draws a Second Data-Breach Suit, and Justice Grown's Pennsylvania Collateral Stays a Mystery for a Ninth Day

A DEA administrative law judge's decision to pause the Schedule I-to-III rescheduling case — not a ruling against it — triggered the sector's worst week this desk has tracked, with MSOS down roughly 13% and nearly every liquid constituent posting a double-digit loss; a newly docketed Illinois federal class action adds a formal second data-breach claim against Verano; the Cannabist Company closed a landmark $248 million Chapter 15 restructuring; and Advanced Flower Capital's foreclosure auction of Justice Grown's Pennsylvania collateral, held Sept. 24, still has no public result nine days on.

No. 1 · Markets & Regulatory

A one-paragraph procedural stay turns into the sector's worst week on record

The trigger was narrow: on Sept. 28, three rescheduling opponents — DUID Victim Voices, Dr. Kenneth Finn and the National Drug and Alcohol Screening Association — asked DEA Chief Administrative Law Judge Derek Julius to admit a newly published GAO report critiquing DEA and FDA drug-scheduling procedures into the Schedule I-to-III record. Judge Julius granted a stay the next day, Sept. 29, giving the DEA until Oct. 13 to respond; he has not yet ruled on whether to actually reopen the record, and per MJBizDaily's reporting, the ruling itself carries no binding deadline and Attorney General Pamela Bondi's successor need not even follow it. The market's reaction was anything but narrow. MSOS slid modestly Monday night and Tuesday (Sept. 29), then cratered on Wednesday, Sept. 30, closing near $4.52 — its single steepest session this desk has tracked. A partial Thursday bounce (+1.77%, to $4.60) fully reversed Friday, and the ETF closed the week at $4.47, down 13.37% from the prior Friday's $5.16. The breadth was the real story: Glass House Brands (-19.26%) and TerrAscend (-19.23%) were the week's steepest decliners, followed by Vireo Growth (-15.71%), Green Thumb (-14.71%), Jushi (-12.45%), Planet 13 (-12.38%), Trulieve (-12.20%), Verano (-11.88%, C$) and Cresco Labs (-11.84%); Curaleaf (-10.12%) fared only marginally better. Ascend Wellness (-6.78%) and Aurora Cannabis (-4.39%) were the week's relative outperformers — still down, but by less than half the sector's median decline. Cantor Fitzgerald, reiterating its Overweight Trulieve rating after the stay, now models a 1H 2027 resolution rather than year-end 2026.

Why it matters to PGP A motion to admit one GAO report — not new evidence bearing on rescheduling's merits — erased roughly two weeks of sector gains in a single session and the market still hadn't recovered a week later. That fragility is itself the diligence finding: any model keyed to a near-term 280E or rescheduling catalyst needs to run a materially later base case, and the near-uniform breadth of the selloff (virtually every liquid name down double digits, regardless of balance-sheet quality or news flow) confirms this is a macro/regulatory repricing the market is treating as a real multi-quarter delay, not noise to be faded.
No. 2 · M&A & Restructuring

The Cannabist Company closes a landmark Chapter 15 restructuring

The Cannabist Company became the first U.S. cannabis MSO to secure Chapter 15 protection, with the U.S. court recognizing its underlying Ontario CCAA case and more than 70% of senior secured noteholders in support. The closing, completed Sept. 28, carried over $248 million in state-by-state asset sales: Virginia operations to Parma Holdco LLC ($130 million), Ohio to Holistic Industries ($47 million), and Delaware to Parma ($16.5 million). A Maryland sale to Free State Botanicals and a remaining-states package (Colorado, Illinois, Massachusetts, New Jersey and West Virginia) — the latter the subject of Vireo Growth's own pending asset purchase — both remain in motion.

No. 3 · Litigation

Verano draws a second, formally docketed data-breach class action

A new proposed class action — Ambrozewski v. Verano Holdings Corp. et al., Case No. 1:26-cv-11884, filed Sept. 28 in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Illinois — names Verano and Healthway Services of West Illinois LLC, the operator of its St. Charles-area dispensary, alleging the exposure of roughly 2.4 million lines of patient database records. The filing is distinct from an earlier 2026 receipt-privacy suit against Verano, in which the company moved to compel arbitration or dismiss. As of this edition, no public statement, SEC filing or spokesperson comment has surfaced from Verano on the new suit specifically.

No. 4 · M&A & Governance

Curaleaf answers Aurora's disclosure complaint point by point — still no ASC hearing date

On Sept. 26, Curaleaf publicly rebutted the three disclosure deficiencies Aurora Cannabis alleged in its Sept. 23 Alberta Securities Commission filing: it offered to extend its bid period by seven hours, argued pro forma combined financials aren't legally required for a hostile circular (while offering to make them available via due diligence access), and said the French-language Québec notice Aurora claimed was missing had in fact already been published bilingually. Neither side's ASC application — Curaleaf's Sept. 14 bid to halt Aurora's dilutive at-the-market program, or Aurora's Sept. 23 disclosure complaint — has a hearing date as of this edition. The underlying bid (US$4.00/share base, US$5.00 cap, 0.3463 Curaleaf shares plus US$0.75 cash) remains open through Dec. 1 unless extended.

No. 5 · Distressed / Restructuring

Justice Grown's Pennsylvania auction result stays sealed for a ninth day

Advanced Flower Capital's Article 9 foreclosure auction of Justice Grown's Pennsylvania cultivation and retail collateral — securing a $78.8 million loan principal in maturity default since May 1, carried on AFCG's books at a $42.0 million fair value as of June 30 — was held Sept. 24. Nine days later, no clearing price or buyer has been made public by AFCG, its advisor SSG Advisors, or any trade-press or SEC filing this desk could locate; AFCG's only 8-K this week covered an unrelated $130 million revolver upsizing, not the auction. The lone tangential signal: AFCG director Leonard Tannenbaum bought 95,397 shares at $3.57–$3.63 between Sept. 28 and 30 — insider buying, not an auction result. A companion auction of Justice Grown's New Jersey dispensary collateral, guided for around Oct. 6, has no confirmed status either.

Why it matters to PGP Cannabist's Ch. 15 closing is the clearest proof yet that the U.S. bankruptcy toolkit works for plant-touching operators — a cross-border CCAA case recognized stateside, with named strategic buyers paying real dollars for carved-out state assets, and a template any distressed single- or multi-state sell-side mandate should study: segment by state, sell to the natural strategic in each geography, use Ch. 15 to bind U.S. creditors. The AFCG/Justice Grown Pennsylvania auction result — now the longest-undisclosed data point on this desk's board — deserves a direct call to AFCG or SSG Advisors rather than waiting on trade-press pickup; once disclosed, it remains the cleanest available read this year on actual clearing prices for distressed cannabis retail and cultivation collateral. Separately, the Curaleaf-Aurora fight's descent into a point-by-point disclosure rebuttal is a reminder to budget real time for dual-track regulatory litigation in any cross-border hostile mandate — Dec. 1 increasingly looks like a waypoint, not a resolution date.

A procedural stay reshapes the market's timeline more than it reshapes the case

The federal rescheduling record stays frozen pending a GAO-report admission ruling, Congress's banking and hemp-beverage tracks remain stalled, and Massachusetts polling continues to show lopsided opposition to a market-repeal vote.

  • Sep 26, 2026 Curaleaf publicly rebuts Aurora Cannabis's ASC disclosure-deficiency claims point by point; no hearing date set for either side's application.

Glass House Brands' CFO Mark Vendetti to retire

A transition running through roughly March 31, 2027 while the company searches for a successor — disclosed the same day as Vireo's unexplained slide. GlobeNewswire

The creditor vehicle now holds AYR's former Florida, New Jersey, Nevada, Virginia and Ohio operations under its state-by-state CCAA wind-down, with further transfers flagged as still to come. AYR IR · Newswire

GOP senators press DOJ to probe a hemp "loophole"

Sens. Cotton, Collins, Ricketts and Budd urge an investigation into Chinese transnational criminal organizations allegedly exploiting hemp-derived THC products, opposing any further delay of the Dec 11 ban. MJBizDaily · Sen. Cotton's office

Newsom signs three cannabis bills touching every CA retailer

AB 1965 expands lab-testing transparency and DCC access; AB 2250 exempts CBN isolate from the concentrate definition starting 2028; AB 2532 adds Poison Help labeling and curbs multi-serving THC drinks marketed as single-serve. The Marijuana Herald · Marijuana Moment

Georgia becomes the 31st state to allow medical-cannabis delivery

A new rule approved Oct. 1 permits home delivery of medical cannabis products, expanding patient access without a dispensary visit. Marijuana Moment

Connecticut flattens its cannabis tax and drops its THC potency cap

A flat 10.75% adult-use sales tax replaced the state's potency-based excise structure effective Oct. 1, alongside removal of the prior THC-potency ceiling. The Marijuana Herald

IM Cannabis sells its European subsidiary to a related party

The company closed the sale of IMC Holdings Ltd. (Adjupharm, Xinteza, Shiran) to Slil.com Holding Ltd., a vehicle controlled by CEO Oren Shuster, for C$3M plus assumption of up to C$9.4M in liabilities. PR Newswire

A Fourth Circuit panel awaits ruling on the DEA's hemp-HHC rule

Judges hearing Bluestar Operations v. DEA appeared skeptical at Sept. 18 oral argument of the agency's rationale for classifying hemp-derived HHC as Schedule I; no opinion has issued as of this edition, with a parallel Ninth Circuit case (IHC Investments v. DEA) also pending. Law360
