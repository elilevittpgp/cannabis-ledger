Page One · The Big Story

Aurora Cancels Its Investor Call and Locks In a December Vote on the Organigram Deal as It Narrows Its Fiscal Loss — Cresco Labs Misses on Revenue and Draws a Downgrade, TerrAscend Beats, and MSOS Rallies 3.4% to Close the Week

A fourth straight day of MSO earnings turns up one more miss and one more beat, while Aurora's own numbers take a back seat to deal mechanics in the Curaleaf-Organigram fight.

Aurora Cannabis filed its fiscal 2027 second-quarter results Nov. 5, reporting net revenue of $86.0 million (+9% year-over-year), including record international medical cannabis revenue of $61.4 million (+44%); its net loss from continuing operations narrowed to $21.1 million from $27.2 million, though adjusted EBITDA slipped to $14.0 million from $15.4 million on pricing compression tied to an April 1 change to Canada's federal medical-reimbursement program. The company holds $133.0 million of cash with no debt. Buried in the same release: Aurora confirmed it has cancelled its previously scheduled Nov. 12 investor call and will instead hold a special shareholder meeting in December 2026 to seek approval of its definitive acquisition agreement with Organigram Global (C$7.80/share cash, 5.8626 Organigram shares, or a C$3.75-cash-plus-3.0431-share mixed election; targeted close January 2027). Curaleaf, whose hostile tender for Aurora remains open through its Dec. 4 deadline, issued its own Nov. 3 statement maintaining that its enhanced offer "remains outstanding" and represents "better value," while calling Organigram "a significantly smaller, underperforming and less liquid company" — but notably did not commit to raising its bid in that statement. (SEC 6-K; Curaleaf statement via Yahoo Finance Canada.)

On the earnings tape, Cresco Labs reported Q3 2026 revenue of roughly $172.8-$173 million (+5% year-over-year), missing consensus by about $0.9 million, with a net loss that narrowed 34% to $14.5 million, adjusted EBITDA of $35 million (20.2% margin) and $21 million of free cash flow; Zacks Research downgraded the stock to Hold the same day. TerrAscend beat, reporting net revenue of $68.2 million (+4.8%, against a $67.4 million estimate), a GAAP net loss of $5.5 million that narrowed 45% year-over-year, 52.3% gross margin and its 17th consecutive quarter of positive operating cash flow, alongside the closed acquisition of a fifth New Jersey dispensary (Aunt Mary's). MSOS rallied 3.39% to $4.270 on volume of roughly 3.83 million shares by mid-afternoon, led by Green Thumb Industries (+8.60%) and Trulieve (+5.60%, still carrying Tuesday's EPS beat and DEA-registration news) — a broad move with no single fresh catalyst beyond the week's earnings cycle and a same-day Seaport Research price-target raise on Green Thumb to $18.

Why it matters to PGP A board that cancels its own investor call while locking in a firm December vote date is sending a signal as much as filing a number: skipping the Q&A during an active topping fight reads as counsel-driven caution against saying anything that could be used against the board in a later fairness challenge, and it reinforces that Aurora intends to run the clock to its December shareholder vote rather than reopen diligence to Curaleaf. PGP should track whether Curaleaf's Nov. 3 statement — notably silent on actually raising its bid despite CEO Boris Jordan's Nov. 3 earnings-call remarks about a "substantial" increase — is maximalist positioning or a sign the topping fight is already lost. Separately, this week's fourth straight day of mixed MSO prints (one more miss, one more beat) argues against reading "earnings season" as a single uniform signal across comp sets.

Why it matters to PGP The BAT-Organigram private placement is a useful template: a strategic financing a friendly topping bid at a steep per-share discount to pre-deal trading levels protects the acquirer's downside but visibly dilutes the target-adjacent stock, which is exactly what is pressuring Organigram's own shares even as its Aurora bid holds. FLUENT's Nov. 5 credit-agreement amendment — a fresh $10.5M in term loans plus an explicit lender consent carved out for the Vireo arrangement — is a concrete precedent for how PGP should expect existing lenders to price consent into a target's balance sheet in the final weeks before a roll-up closes, rather than treating consent as a formality.

The Rescheduling Docket

Reply briefs are in and the case moves toward a recommendation, while two states' hemp-ban fights head in opposite directions

The stayed Schedule I-to-III case clears its final briefing deadline; Missouri's own THC ban survives a court challenge while Illinois hemp businesses sue to block theirs; and a decisive midterm night keeps reshaping state-level legalization odds.

  • Nov 4, 2026 A federal judge in Missouri (Harpool, W.D. Mo.) dismisses the hemp industry's challenge to the state's own THC ban (Mohemp Trade Assn. v. Kehoe), clearing it to take effect Nov. 12 alongside the federal deadline.
  • Nov 5, 2026 Reply briefs are filed as scheduled before Chief ALJ Derek Julius: the DEA's reply argues the admitted GAO report deserves "no weight," while a joint reply from rescheduling opponents (NDASA, DUID Victim Voices, Dr. Kenneth Finn) argues it shows "fatal flaws" in the agency's record — closing the briefing round with no recommendation timeline yet announced.
  • Nov 5, 2026 The Illinois Healthy Alternatives Association and hemp businesses file an emergency motion for a temporary restraining order and preliminary injunction against the state's own Nov. 12 Hemp Act THC ban, arguing a Commerce Clause violation — the mirror image of Missouri's now-failed challenge.
  • Nov 5, 2026 Aurora Cannabis reports fiscal Q2 2027 results and confirms its December shareholder vote on the Organigram deal; Cresco Labs and TerrAscend round out the week's MSO earnings slate.
  • Nov 6, 2026 (today) Wisconsin's incoming Democratic legislative majority and Gov.-elect David Crowley pledge to legalize marijuana within one to two years, following a sweep that gave Democrats full control of state government for the first time in 16 years; Illinois officials separately reiterate their call for the Dec. 11 federal "total THC" deadline to proceed without further delay.
