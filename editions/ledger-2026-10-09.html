Page One · The Big Story

A Rival Suitor Surfaces for Aurora Cannabis — Curaleaf Calls the Process "Outraged" — as the Sector Claws Back From Thursday's Rout

Organigram Global is reportedly weeks into merger talks with Aurora even as Curaleaf's enhanced hostile bid sits with an unconvinced special committee. Separately, MSOS bounces 1.3% intraday but stays down double digits for the month.

The Curaleaf–Aurora fight gained a second bidder this week. Trade outlet Cultivated reported, and multiple outlets including Cannabis Equipment News confirmed, that Organigram Global — a roughly $200M-market-cap Canadian operator — has been in merger talks with Aurora "for the past few weeks." Neither company confirmed the discussions on the record, but Curaleaf reacted publicly furious: "We are outraged, and Aurora shareholders should be too," the company said, framing it as a fiduciary double standard that the "world's largest cannabis company" (its own $2.6B market cap) is reportedly denied customary diligence access while a company roughly one-thirteenth its size is "welcomed into the process." Curaleaf's enhanced hostile bid — an implied $5.00/share (0.4013 Curaleaf shares plus $1.00 cash), with the cash-election cap raised to $6.00 — was formally filed Oct. 6 via a Notice of Variation; Aurora's special committee has still issued no recommendation on the revised terms, and shareholders have until at least Dec. 4 to decide.

Away from the bid fight, the broader tape is stabilizing after Thursday's rout. MSOS traded at $4.325 Friday afternoon, up 1.29% on the day, per stockanalysis.com with Yahoo Finance confirming the direction and rough magnitude — but the ETF remains down roughly 3.5% on the week and 10.7% month-to-date per Finviz. Glass House Brands (NYSE: GLAS), Thursday's steepest decliner at -15.6% with no identified catalyst, is still red (-2.3% intraday) but has stopped falling as sharply, and still no company release, filing, or news item has surfaced to explain the move. On the priority desk, Ascend opened its 7th Ohio dispensary's doors to a formal grand-opening event today in Lewis Center, while Verano and Premier Group/PCF stayed quiet.

Why it matters to PGP A credible second bidder emerging mid-process, paired with a public due-diligence-access complaint, is a live test of how a target's special committee manages competing-bid optics under a Canadian take-over bid regime — directly relevant precedent for any PGP-advised process where access and process fairness become contested. Separately, a catalyst-free 15%+ single-day move in a liquid, exchange-listed name (Glass House) two trading sessions running is a volatility signal worth pricing into entry timing on any name in the comp set, independent of the Aurora fight's outcome.

The Rescheduling Docket

Four days to the DEA's Oct. 13 response — states go public on "radio silence"

No DEA filing or ALJ ruling has surfaced since the Sept 28-29 stay. The week's real movement has been implementation friction and, in Massachusetts, campaign-finance disclosure.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E.
  • Jul 15, 2026 Broader Schedule I→III hearings conclude before ALJ Derek Julius.
  • Sep 28-29, 2026 ALJ Julius stays the case pending a GAO report on DEA/FDA scheduling procedures; DEA's response due Oct 13.
  • Oct 7, 2026 DEA rolls out specialized Form 225 Schedule III registration paperwork for medical-cannabis manufacturers, distributors and labs — an implementation step distinct from the stayed hearing.
  • Oct 7, 2026 State cannabis regulators — named officials in Georgia, Vermont, and California — publicly describe DEA "radio silence" on warning labels, registration, tax treatment, and packaging guidance ahead of the Oct 13 deadline.
  • Oct 7-9, 2026 Massachusetts' "No on 8" coalition discloses it has outraised the Question 8 repeal campaign $2.18M to $1.98M in 2026, including new $265K (INSA) and $250K (Ascend Wellness) contributions; separate reporting finds the repeal campaign's war chest is funded almost entirely by SAM Action, a Virginia dark-money nonprofit, rather than Massachusetts donors.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. Oct 13, 2026 — DEA's response to the motion to reopen the record (GAO report) is due.
  2. ALJ Julius rules on whether to admit the GAO report and permit added briefing.
  3. If granted, a further briefing round precedes the recommended decision; if denied, the case resumes toward a decision on the existing record.
  4. A 20-day exceptions window, then the DEA Administrator's final call — sell-side analysts have modeled 1H 2027 as the more likely resolution window.
  5. Nov 3, 2026 — Massachusetts voters decide Question 8, which would end licensed adult-use sales as of Jan 1, 2028 if it passes; recent polling put opposition at 75%, and the "No" side now also leads on fundraising.
  6. Separately: the SAFE Banking Act of 2026 (S.4942) remains referred to the Senate Banking Committee with no markup or floor vote scheduled.
PGP takeaway Named state officials going on the record about DEA "radio silence" signals the rollout's operational risk is becoming a public political liability for the agency, which may add pressure toward the Oct 13 deadline being treated seriously rather than slipping again. The Massachusetts fundraising and dark-money disclosures are reassuring for MA-exposed MSOs but do not change the underlying Jan 1, 2028 wind-down scenario that any MA-exposed mandate should still stress-test.
