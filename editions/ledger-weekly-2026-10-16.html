Page One · The Week in Review

A Second Double-Digit Sector Rout in Three Weeks Sends MSOS Down 10.4%, Aurora's Board Formally Rejects Curaleaf Again While Keeping Organigram Alive, and This Desk Catches a 19-Store Operator It Had Called "Private, No M&A" Already Absorbed Into a Rival

MSOS closed the week at $3.89, down 10.37% — its second double-digit weekly decline in the past three weeks, following the Sept. 19–25 week's 13.37% rout — and now off roughly 15.4% month-to-date, with every tracked constituent lower and Glass House Brands the week's single worst performer at -25.1%. Aurora Cannabis's board formally rejected Curaleaf's enhanced hostile bid for a second time and said it is "actively exploring alternatives" including reported BAT-backed suitor Organigram Global, though the market gave back Aurora's prior week's rival-bid pop. Elsewhere, this desk's own routine license sweep turned up a five-week-old merger it had missed entirely, and Massachusetts' cannabis industry mounted its most visible legislative counteroffensive yet against the Nov. 3 repeal vote.

No. 1 · Markets

The sector's second gut-punch in three weeks — and this time nothing bounced

After "steadying" the prior week at a comparatively mild -2.91%, MSOS fell back hard: down 10.37% on the week to $3.89 (from last Friday's $4.34 close), on volume of roughly 6.25 million shares — confirmed identically across stockanalysis.com, Yahoo Finance, and Finviz. Month-to-date the ETF is now down roughly 15.4% (from Oct. 1's $4.60 close). Unlike the prior week's split tape, breadth was uniformly negative: every one of the twelve constituents this desk tracked fell, with Glass House Brands (-25.1%, to $3.19) the week's steepest decliner, followed by Curaleaf (-16.6%, to $7.83) and Aurora Cannabis (-11.1%, to $3.99) — Aurora giving back more than its entire prior-week rival-bid rally. Glass House's slide came despite Tuesday's genuinely positive GACP export-certification news; this desk traced the oft-cited "89.6% short-interest jump" explanation to a Sept. 30 settlement-date figure recirculated by an Oct. 15 alert, not fresh data, and found no company-specific release dated this week that explains the move. The best-performing names were still red: Jushi Holdings (-1.2%) and Vireo Growth (-4.0%) were the week's "winners" only in a relative sense.

Why it matters to PGP Two double-digit weekly declines inside a three-week span — with no single identifiable catalyst behind either the Sept. 19–25 rout or this week's — is a pattern PGP should treat as a standing feature of this tape, not noise around a specific event. A sector-wide move that outsizes every individual name's news flow (Glass House falling 25% through a week that included a genuinely positive certification) argues for pricing any PGP-advised process at a discount to recent comps and for stress-testing deal valuations against further air pockets, rather than assuming this week's prices reflect fundamentals.
No. 2 · M&A & Governance

Aurora's board rejects Curaleaf a second time, keeps the door open to Organigram — but the paper trail still hasn't caught up

On Oct. 14, Aurora's special committee formally reaffirmed its rejection of Curaleaf's enhanced hostile bid (the Oct. 6-filed Notice of Variation implying roughly $5.00/share, with a $6.00 cash-election cap), stating it is "actively exploring a range of alternatives" that "may offer shareholders greater value" — language this desk reads as a direct, if unconfirmed, nod to BAT-backed Organigram Global, which trade press (MJBizDaily) has reported held talks with Aurora "in recent weeks." Neither company will confirm a deal on the record. As of this edition, Aurora's amended Directors' Circular responding to Curaleaf's revised terms has still not been filed — Aurora's own Oct. 14 release says it "will be made available" on SEDAR+/EDGAR, in future tense, with Business of Cannabis independently corroborating a "week of Oct. 20" timeline. Neither side's Alberta Securities Commission application (Aurora's disclosure-deficiency complaint against Curaleaf; Curaleaf's bid to freeze Aurora's at-the-market program) has a hearing date, now more than four weeks after the second was filed. Curaleaf's tender offer stays open to Dec. 4.

Why it matters to PGP A target board rejecting a hostile bid while publicly signaling a credible alternative — without yet filing the document that would substantiate that signal — is a live test of how long a special committee can run on rhetoric alone before shareholders demand paper. For any PGP-advised process facing a similar hostile-bid/white-knight dynamic, the lesson so far is that the market has treated the Organigram narrative as real (Aurora's +14.5% the week it broke) even though nothing has moved procedurally since; that gap between price action and documentation is exactly the kind of mispricing a well-timed, well-informed advisory client could exploit on either side of a competing process.
No. 3 · Premier Group / PCF

This desk catches its own blind spot: a 19-store California operator it tracked as "private, no M&A" for months had already changed hands

A routine California DCC and local-press sweep on Oct. 16 turned up secondary trade coverage (San Diego Business Journal, MJBizDaily) reporting that San Diego retailer March and Ash announced a Sept. 11, 2026 merger absorbing Pacafi, Inc. — operating as The Premier Group — and its 19 licensed California dispensaries, including PCF Ceres, Firehouse Turlock, and X1 Tracy. This desk had carried Premier Group/PCF in every prior edition this year as "privately held, no M&A." No primary wire release has been located (both the SDBJ and MJBizDaily articles returned HTTP 402/403 on direct fetch), but a direct check of Pacafi's cannabis distributor license (#C11-0000353-LIC) on the state's license-lookup tool shows the registered owner of record is still listed as Pacafi's original principal, with DCC data last refreshed Sept. 16 — meaning the ownership-transfer paperwork, if filed, has not yet worked through the state's registry five weeks after the deal was announced. No deal terms, consideration, or closing status have been independently confirmed.

No. 4 · M&A — Procedural Progress

Two deals that had been stuck actually moved: Vireo's Nevada clearance, Cronos's on-time Dutch close

The Nevada Cannabis Compliance Board approved the transfer of ownership/control of Planet 13's Nevada businesses to Vireo Growth on Oct. 15 — a concrete regulatory milestone in a merger that had sat with no visible progress since its Oct. 9 Form S-4 filing. The deal still awaits SEC effectiveness of that S-4, CSE listing approval for the new Vireo shares, and a shareholder-meeting date. Separately, Cronos Group closed its €57.5 million acquisition of CanAdelaar B.V. on Oct. 15 — exactly on its twice-extended long-stop date — making Cronos the top market-share operator in the Netherlands' "Wietexperiment" adult-use pilot.

No. 5 · Politics / Ballot Risk

Massachusetts' cannabis industry mounts its biggest legislative show of force yet against Question 8

More than 60 Massachusetts state legislators — including House Speaker Ronald Mariano and Senate President Karen Spilka — publicly endorsed the "No on 8" campaign on Oct. 16, joining Boston Mayor Michelle Wu and Gov. Maura Healey in opposing the Nov. 3 adult-use repeal vote. The endorsement wave is the most visible political consolidation against the measure to date, landing on top of the most recent (Sept. 17–21) UNH Bay State Poll showing roughly 71% of voters opposed. This desk could not independently verify any new polling released this specific week.

The Rescheduling Docket

Still stayed, still waiting on two open motions

ALJ Derek Julius has not ruled on either the GAO-report admission motion or the opponents' broader discovery request this week; both remain open, with the next hard deadline on Oct 21.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E.
  • Jul 15, 2026 Broader Schedule I→III hearings conclude before ALJ Derek Julius.
  • Sep 23, 2026 GAO publishes report GAO-26-108623, finding DEA's scheduling decisions aligned with HHS recommendations in all 84 cases reviewed, but flagging a lack of written DEA/FDA procedural policy.
  • Sep 28-29, 2026 Opponents move to reopen the record to admit the GAO report; ALJ Julius stays the case and orders a DEA response by Oct 13.
  • Oct 9, 2026 DEA files its response four days early: no objection to admitting the GAO report itself, but firm opposition to opponents' broader bid for new testimony and document discovery.
  • Oct 13-14, 2026 ALJ Julius states he has not yet ruled on the GAO-report motion, reserves judgment, and sets a new Oct 21 deadline for DEA to address the testimony/discovery request.
  • Oct 14, 2026 ALJ Julius separately grants rescheduling opponents a motion to reply to a DEA filing, reiterating the Oct 21 deadline without changing it — a procedural layer, not a ruling.
  • Oct 15-16, 2026 No new filing or ruling located either day; the docket carries two open motions (GAO-report admission, and the discovery/testimony request) into next week unresolved.
