Virginia's Hemp Cap Takes Effect After a Judge Says No to the Injunction

Judge Robert Ballou denies the TRO, HB 30's 2mg THC cap goes live at midnight Saturday, and hemp retailers lose the state's first courtroom test of a hard THC limit — just as the DEA's own rescheduling record closes for briefing.

Virginia hemp businesses got their answer, and it wasn't the one they wanted. U.S. District Judge Robert S. Ballou (W.D. Va., Roanoke) denied the preliminary injunction sought by seven hemp retailers — including Nova Hemp and District Hemp Botanicals — against House Bill 30's new 2-milligram-per-package THC cap on Friday, Aug. 14. Ballou found the plaintiffs failed to show a likely win on their Takings, Due Process, or Equal Protection claims, holding that Virginia acted within its police power to regulate a "heavily regulated and rapidly evolving industry," that compliant low-THC products remain sellable, and that criminal prosecution wasn't shown to be imminent. He declined to second-guess the legislature's chosen effective date. HB 30 took effect as scheduled at midnight Saturday, Aug. 15, ending the old 25:1 CBD:THC ratio exception; retailers reportedly pulled an estimated 85–90% of shelf inventory ahead of the deadline, and no weekend enforcement actions or arrests have been reported. Plaintiffs' counsel called the ruling disappointing and left an appeal open, though none has been confirmed filed as of this edition.

The bench loss lands the same day the DEA's own Schedule I→III record was set to close: post-hearing briefs were due today, Aug. 17, under Chief ALJ Derek Julius's 50-page cap, with no confirmation yet that filings were made or of their content — and no timeline given for a recommended decision. Alabama's 280E opt-out decision remains unresolved (comment period closed Aug. 5, no Board of Health ruling), and the D.C. Circuit's three consolidated rescheduling challenges have sat without a ruling since briefing closed July 17. Elsewhere, the Curaleaf–Aurora Cannabis hostile-bid standoff is unchanged after a week — no formal bid circular, no counter-bid, no special-committee recommendation — while Ascend, Verano and Premier Group/PCF were all quiet over the weekend; see Priority Coverage below.

Why it matters to PGP Virginia is now the first state where a THC-cap-style hemp law has been affirmatively tested — and upheld — in federal court against a takings/due-process challenge. That gives every other state (and eventually federal enforcement past Dec. 11) a ready-made judicial template: courts are inclined to defer to legislatures on effective dates and treat compliant reformulation as sufficient due process, even when it means writing off existing inventory. Any hemp-THC or hemp-beverage diligence should now price in litigation risk state-by-state using Virginia's reasoning as the operative test, not just the federal calendar.
