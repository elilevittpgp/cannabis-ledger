Page One · The Big Story

Glass House Brands Jumps Roughly 31% After Hours as Its Q3 Print Clears the Nov. 16 Filing Deadline and Explains Away Its Auditor Dispute, Trulieve Lands a Russell-Index Nod, and MSOS Steadies Near Flat After Its Worst Week on Record

The sector catches its breath Monday — MSOS closes down just 0.81% at $3.69 after last week's roughly 12% rout — while Glass House's after-hours surge on a revenue beat and a plausible explanation for its BDO-to-MGO auditor swap becomes the session's defining story, Trulieve earns a small-cap institutional badge, and Missouri's hemp crackdown keeps working its way through the courts.

Glass House Brands met its Monday, Nov. 16 deadline to file interim Q3 2026 financials — the very deadline that triggered its surprise BDO-to-MGO auditor swap four days ago — and the numbers, released before an after-market call, read better than the stock's recent 38%-on-the-week battering implied. Revenue rose to $56.8 million, up from $47.0 million in Q2 and more than double the $26.2 million booked a year ago, on record biomass production of roughly 319,052 pounds. Net loss narrowed to $10.1 million from $12.2 million a year earlier, even as it widened sequentially from Q2's $6.7 million; adjusted EBITDA turned positive at $0.8 million (down from Q2's $5.7 million) as gross margin compressed to 26% from 34% on a trim-heavy sales mix and softer seasonal pricing. EPS of -$0.14 missed consensus by a penny. Board member John Nichols Jr. resigned concurrent with the release, with the company offering only a routine thank-you-for-your-service statement. The stock, which closed the regular session up a modest 1.57% at $1.29, kept climbing into the evening — up roughly 31% after hours to about $1.69 on volume near 3.55 million shares versus a typical 699,000, as the market digested both the beat and the company's account of the accounting dispute.

On that dispute: outgoing auditor BDO had told the company it had not completed its evaluation of the June 2026 deconsolidation of Glass House Retail LLC — the transaction that split the company's dual-use and medical cannabis businesses — and considered its unfinished review an "unresolved issue" constituting a reportable event under Canadian disclosure rule NI 51-102. The company disagrees with that characterization; its board has concluded no reportable event in fact occurred during BDO's tenure, and points to newly reappointed auditor MGO's own prior review of the identical deconsolidation accounting during its Q2 2026 engagement, which flagged no required material modification. MGO, reappointed Nov. 6 specifically to complete the Q3 review inside today's filing window, did so on schedule. This desk treats that as a credible, if not yet fully adjudicated, explanation — MGO's completed Q3 review itself, once published, remains the harder confirmation to watch for.

Elsewhere on the tape, Trulieve Cannabis (NYSE: TRLV) disclosed Friday that FTSE Russell has preliminarily added it to the Russell 2000® and 3000® Indices as part of the annual reconstitution, with final membership effective after the Dec. 11 close; CEO Kim Rivers tied the inclusion directly to the company's NYSE uplisting and U.S. domestication, building on its earlier addition to S&P and Morningstar benchmark indices. The index win did not translate to today's price action — TRLV fell 5.23% to $10.33, in line with broader softness among the larger MSOs. MSOS itself closed at $3.69, down 0.81% on the day — a far calmer session than the roughly 3% daily swings of the prior week — but still down about 9.3% versus last Monday's $4.07 close and roughly 6.3% month-to-date against Oct. 30's $3.94. Organigram Global (-12.62% to $0.724) and Aurora Cannabis (-6.68% to $4.61) were the session's sharpest decliners, with no company-specific catalyst identified for either beyond the standing, unresolved Curaleaf-versus-Organigram fight for Aurora.

Missouri's hemp crackdown continued working through both the market and the courts: St. Louis-area and Ozarks public-radio coverage (KRCU, Nov. 13; KRPS, Nov. 16) confirmed retailers statewide are still clearing shelves and closing shops under the Intoxicating Cannabinoid Control Act that took fuller effect Nov. 12, with Attorney General Catherine Hanaway's office describing an enforcement focus on "store owners and sellers," not consumers, backed by a Nov. 4 federal court ruling (W.D. Mo.) that rejected a constitutional and federal-preemption challenge to the law and four standing AG lawsuits against named retailers. Hemp beverages carrying only naturally occurring cannabinoids remain temporarily exempt until Dec. 11. Ascend Wellness, Verano Holdings and Premier Group/PCF were all quiet on net-new developments inside today's window; details are in the Priority Coverage cards below.

Why it matters to PGP Glass House is a live test of how fast a market will forgive a disclosed governance scare once management offers a specific, falsifiable counter-narrative — here, MGO's own clean prior review of the same accounting question. PGP should treat today's after-hours pop as a reasonable but not final signal: the credibility test is MGO's completed Q3 review itself, not the rally that preceded it. Separately, Trulieve's Russell inclusion is a reminder that uplisting-driven index eligibility is becoming a real, measurable catalyst in this sector — worth tracking for any other MSO pursuing a U.S. exchange listing.

The Rescheduling Docket

Missouri's hemp crackdown keeps moving through the courts as retailers keep closing, while the DEA's ALJ record stays silent and the federal hemp-beverage carve-out clock keeps running

No new movement on the stayed Schedule I-to-III record since the Nov. 5 reply-brief deadline; this window's action is entirely on the state/federal hemp side.

  • Nov 4, 2026 A federal judge in the Western District of Missouri rejects a constitutional and federal-preemption challenge to the state's Intoxicating Cannabinoid Control Act (HB 2641), clearing the way for continued AG enforcement against four named retailers (Puffers Choice, CBD Kratom Cannabis Dispensary, Herb Depot and Token THC Dispensary).
  • Nov 12, 2026 Missouri's law reaches fuller effect, reclassifying hemp products over a 0.4mg THC threshold as marijuana sellable only through licensed dispensaries; hemp beverages with only naturally occurring cannabinoids get a temporary exemption through Dec. 11.
  • Nov 13-16, 2026 Public-radio coverage (KRCU, KRPS) confirms hemp retailers statewide are still clearing shelves and closing shops; AG Catherine Hanaway's office describes an enforcement focus on "store owners and sellers," not consumers.
  • Nov 13, 2026 FTSE Russell's preliminary Russell 2000/3000 reconstitution list adds Trulieve (final membership effective after the Dec. 11 close) — a market-structure, not regulatory, milestone, but one tied directly to its NYSE uplisting.
  • Nov 16, 2026 No DEA ALJ recommended decision yet on the Schedule I-to-III record; no new date has been set. The Illinois hemp-THC litigation's Nov. 19 TRO hearing and the Beverage Regulatory Parity Act both remain unchanged and pending.
