Page One · The Big Story

MSOS Gives Back Half Its Rally as Aurora Turns the Regulatory Tables on Curaleaf

A dueling-complaint escalation at the Alberta Securities Commission overshadows a priority desk that stayed entirely dark, while today's foreclosure auction of Justice Grown's Pennsylvania collateral — the sector's clearest distressed-pricing test this year — remains undecided as this edition goes to press.

The AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF (MSOS) traded around $5.17 early Thursday afternoon, down roughly 4.8% on the session — giving back close to half of Wednesday's blockbuster close and trimming the trailing week's gain to roughly 4.2% (still up ~4.4% month-to-date). The pullback followed a sharp turn in the Curaleaf–Aurora Cannabis fight: Aurora filed its own application with the Alberta Securities Commission on Sept. 23, alleging Curaleaf's hostile-bid circular has multiple disclosure deficiencies — missing pro forma combined financials, a bid not held open the statutory minimum deposit period, and no required French-language notice — while its board continues unanimously recommending shareholders reject the offer. The regulator now has dueling applications in front of it (Curaleaf's five-week-old ATM-halt request and Aurora's new deficiency complaint) with no hearing date set for either.

The priority desk produced nothing new. Ascend Wellness's reverse-split ratio remains unset 27 days after stockholder approval, and the stock fell roughly 7% intraday with no identified catalyst — a second straight session of unexplained weakness. Verano's only item stays Tuesday's routine Q3 earnings-date notice; direct IR-page fetches again timed out. Premier Group/PCF's six California license entities were re-checked and remain active and clean, with no Modesto Bee, Turlock Journal or Santa Cruz Sentinel item in the window. Elsewhere, a Government Accountability Office report published Sept. 23 found that neither the DEA nor the FDA has adequate written procedures for evaluating and scheduling controlled substances — a process critique that lands as ALJ Derek Julius's rescheduling recommendation remains unissued more than ten weeks past the July 15 hearing close.

Why it matters to PGP Today's tape move is a reversal, not a new catalyst — treat it as profit-taking after an outsized run rather than a change in sector fundamentals. The item worth a direct follow-up is the Justice Grown Pennsylvania auction: once Advanced Flower Capital or its advisor discloses a result, it will be the cleanest distressed-cannabis-collateral clearing-price comp available this year, and this desk would rather have that number from a phone call than from next week's trade coverage. Separately, Aurora's own ASC filing is a useful cautionary template — a hostile, cross-border cannabis bid can get bogged down in disclosure-technicality litigation well before the substantive terms are ever tested.

The Rescheduling Docket

A federal watchdog flags the process itself as broken

A GAO report finds neither the DEA nor the FDA has adequate written scheduling procedures, landing as ALJ Derek Julius's recommendation sits unissued more than ten weeks past the July 15 hearing close.

  • Sep 17-18, 2026 TerrAscend moves to dismiss the DOJ's federal 280E clawback suit; Alabama's public-health board withdraws its objection to state-level rescheduling.
  • Sep 22, 2026 The Worcester Regional Chamber of Commerce opposes Massachusetts' Question 8 repeal vote; a Third Circuit panel rejects a hemp-law challenge to a prior marijuana sentence.
  • Sep 23, 2026 MJBizDaily reports Louisiana has dropped out of its federal rescheduling challenge; Aurora Cannabis files its own ASC complaint against Curaleaf's bid circular; the U.S. Government Accountability Office reports the DEA and FDA lack adequate written policies for evaluating and scheduling controlled substances.
  • Sep 24, 2026 The Missouri Supreme Court hears oral arguments on whether the state must award cannabis company Hippos LLC 13 facility licenses after an appeals court found the 2019 scoring process inconsistent; Advanced Flower Capital's Justice Grown Pennsylvania foreclosure auction is scheduled today, result pending.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. ALJ Julius's recommended decision on full Schedule I→III rescheduling — still no fixed date, now more than ten weeks past the hearing close.
  2. Oct 5, 2026 — DOJ's response due on TerrAscend's motion to dismiss the 280E clawback suit.
  3. Oct 6, 2026 — scheduled Advanced Flower Capital foreclosure auction of Justice Grown's New Jersey collateral.
  4. Nov 3, 2026 — up to six states could vote on marijuana- or psychedelics-related ballot measures, including Massachusetts' Question 8 to repeal its adult-use market.
  5. Nov 12, 2026 — converted/synthetic cannabinoids (delta-8, HHC, THC-O) lose federal hemp status; no extension applies.
  6. Dec 1, 2026 — Curaleaf's bid for Aurora Cannabis expires unless extended.
  7. Dec 11, 2026 — the enacted effective date for naturally-occurring hemp cannabinoids, under the 0.4mg-total-THC-per-container standard.
PGP takeaway The GAO's process critique doesn't change the substance of the rescheduling case, but it is a fresh reason to expect the administrative timeline to keep slipping — a DEA and FDA the GAO says lack written scheduling procedures are not obvious candidates to move quickly once ALJ Julius does rule. Model a longer runway to full adult-use 280E clarity than the headline "rescheduling is coming" framing implies, and keep the Massachusetts repeal vote and Missouri licensing litigation on the same watch list as the federal docket — state-level legal risk hasn't gone quiet just because the federal process has stalled.
