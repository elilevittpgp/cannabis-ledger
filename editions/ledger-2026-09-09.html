The GAO Puts a Price Tag on Cannabis's Banking Problem

A federal watchdog report quantifies what operators have said for years — the barrier isn't legal risk, it's compliance cost — while the Curaleaf-Aurora fight holds at Tuesday's terms and a watchlist CEO buys a million dollars of his own stock.

The closest thing to a scoop on a quiet Wednesday wasn't a company release — it was a federal watchdog report. The Government Accountability Office published GAO-26-107498, "Banking Services: Cannabis Businesses Face Access Challenges," requested by Sens. Warren, Warnock, Smith and Fetterman. Its central finding cuts against the industry's usual framing: no bank has ever been penalized solely for serving a cannabis-related client. What actually deters banks is compliance overhead — enhanced due diligence and suspicious-activity-report burden, not legal exposure. Roughly 1,000 of the country's ~9,000 federally insured banks and credit unions, about 11%, filed marijuana-related SARs in 2024, and some operators told the GAO they pay upward of $100,000 a year in account fees just to keep a banking relationship open.

Away from Washington, the desk's live threads held in place. Jushi Holdings disclosed that CEO, Chairman and founder Jim Cacioppo bought roughly 2.02 million shares (~$1.01M) in the open market between Aug 24 and Sept 1 via One East Partners — a sizeable insider buy on an otherwise quiet watchlist name. The Curaleaf-Aurora hostile-bid fight held at Tuesday's fact-sheet terms, with no new statement from either side. Verano's leadership attended today's ATB Cormark investor conference in New York alongside Curaleaf, TerrAscend and Vireo, though no conference materials or wire release had surfaced by this edition's close. Ascend and Premier Group/PCF stayed quiet on filings.

Why it matters to PGP The GAO report isn't a policy change, but it's ammunition for the SAFE/SAFER Banking push and a data point PGP can use directly — quantified banking-access costs (SAR filing rates, six-figure account fees) sharpen the "cost of capital" argument in any financing memo, and they explain why cannabis debt still prices punitively even amid post-rescheduling optimism. Insider buying at Jushi, while modest in absolute size, is worth tracking across the watchlist as a sentiment signal independent of the broader tape.

The Rescheduling Docket

Eight weeks since hearings closed, the ALJ stays silent — while adjacent federal actions keep stacking up

No recommended decision yet from Chief ALJ Derek Julius; today's federal news comes from banking policy, not the docket itself.

  • Sep 2, 2026 President Trump signs H.R. 6500 into law — the Dec 11 hemp-ban delay is enacted for naturally-occurring cannabinoids under a stricter 0.4mg-total-THC-per-container standard; converted/synthetic cannabinoids (delta-8, HHC, THC-O) stay excluded and lose hemp status Nov 12.
  • Sep 4, 2026 FDA opens a public docket (FDA-2026-N-9550) on botanical drug development, a pathway that can include cannabis-derived medicines; comments due Nov 3.
  • Sep 8, 2026 A CRS report flags a circuit split (1st/2nd vs. 9th Circuits) over residency-based cannabis licensing preferences; Curaleaf publishes a fact sheet reopening the Aurora bid fight.
  • Sep 8-9, 2026 The GAO publishes GAO-26-107498 on cannabis banking access — no bank penalized solely for cannabis relationships, but SAR filings and account-fee burden remain the practical barrier banks cite.
  • Sep 9, 2026 No recommended decision issued; Chief ALJ Julius's timeline remains unset, roughly eight weeks after hearings closed July 15.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. ALJ Julius's recommended decision on broad Schedule I→III rescheduling — still no fixed date; DEA's own post-hearing brief already urges Schedule III.
  2. Sep 15, 2026 — CA Cannabis Advisory Committee meets on rescheduling and payment-processing constraints; the Advanced Flower Capital/Justice Grown court hearing is separately guided for this date but unconfirmed.
  3. Sep 30, 2026 — deadline for the administration to answer lawmakers' rescheduling-implementation letter.
  4. Nov 12, 2026 — converted/synthetic cannabinoids (delta-8, HHC, THC-O) lose federal hemp status; no extension applies to this category.
  5. Dec 1, 2026 — Curaleaf's bid for Aurora Cannabis expires unless extended.
  6. Dec 11, 2026 — the enacted effective date for naturally-occurring hemp cannabinoids, under the 0.4mg-total-THC-per-container standard.
PGP takeaway The GAO's banking-access findings arm the SAFE/SAFER Banking push with numbers lawmakers and lenders can cite directly — worth flagging in any financing memo that references the "banking overhang" on cannabis credit. The rescheduling recommendation remains the single largest unscheduled catalyst on the desk's board; 280E relief flows straight to operator EBITDA and re-rates every model.
