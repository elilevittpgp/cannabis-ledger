Page One · The Big Story

The DEA Argues Its Own Case for Schedule III — But Won't Say When a Decision Comes

Post-hearing briefs land on deadline, with the agency's own filing urging its judge toward rescheduling — yet no timeline attaches to what happens next.

Chief Administrative Law Judge Derek Julius received his post-hearing briefs on schedule August 17, and the headline is who filed what. The DEA's own brief urges the judge to recommend moving marijuana from Schedule I to Schedule III, arguing the hearing record no longer supports Schedule I classification — the clearest documentary confirmation yet that the agency's position hasn't wavered since April's order moved medical and FDA-approved products to Schedule III. Opposing briefs came from Smart Approaches to Marijuana, the states of Idaho, Indiana and Nebraska, the Tennessee Bureau of Investigation, the National Drug and Alcohol Screening Association, DUID Victim Voices, and two physicians. What the filings do not produce is a clock: no outlet has reported a timeline for Julius's recommended decision, with trade coverage pointing to "weeks or months," a subsequent 20-day exceptions window, and a final call from the DEA Administrator that may not land before late 2026 or early 2027.

Elsewhere, Curaleaf's take-over bid for Aurora Cannabis kept moving without much fresh drama: Aurora shares rose a muted 1.9% on Aug. 18's formal mailing of the Offer and Take-Over Bid Circular — a fraction of the ~20%+ pop the stock saw when Curaleaf first floated the idea on Aug. 11, suggesting the market had already priced in formal commencement. Aurora issued a statement urging shareholders to "take no action at this time," with a formal Directors' Circular recommendation due within 15 days under Canadian take-over bid rules — roughly Sept. 2. Trulieve rang the NYSE closing bell to mark its uplisting; New Jersey regulators filed a notice of appeal to the Third Circuit over the labor-peace-mandate ruling that favored Curaleaf; and IM Cannabis agreed to sell its European-focused assets, expected to close by Sept. 30. Ascend, Verano and Premier Group/PCF were all quiet in the review window; see Priority Coverage below.

Why it matters to PGP A DEA brief explicitly arguing for Schedule III is the strongest documentary signal to date that the agency's own advocacy hasn't shifted — but with zero attached timeline, 280E relief remains a "when, not if" bet that keeps sliding further out in diligence models. Pair that with the market's shrug at Curaleaf's formal Aurora bid commencement: staged-announcement M&A (float intent, then formally commence) appears to get priced almost entirely on the first headline, a structuring lesson worth carrying into any live-bid mandate. And NJ's labor-peace fight now heading to the Third Circuit is a case to track closely — a ruling there would bind Pennsylvania and Delaware federal courts too.

The Rescheduling Docket

Briefs are filed, DEA argues for Schedule III — the calendar stays blank

The record is now fully briefed on both sides, but no outlet has produced a date for what comes next — even as state-level fallout from the federal process keeps compounding.

  • Aug 10, 2026 A New Jersey federal judge rules the state's cannabis labor-peace mandate is NLRA-preempted — a Curaleaf win.
  • Aug 13, 2026 New Jersey regulators file a notice of appeal to the Third Circuit, which would also bind Pennsylvania and Delaware if it rules.
  • Aug 15, 2026 Virginia's HB 30 2mg THC cap takes effect after a federal judge denies an injunction bid.
  • Aug 17, 2026 DEA post-hearing brief deadline is met: the agency's own brief urges Julius to recommend Schedule III; opposition briefs filed by SAM, Idaho/Indiana/Nebraska, the Tennessee Bureau of Investigation and others.
  • Aug 17, 2026 A federal judge dissolves Rhode Island's cannabis-license injunction, clearing ~20 frozen retail licenses to resume; Virginia hemp plaintiffs confirm no appeal, instead pressing lawmakers for a special session.
  • Aug 18, 2026 Georgia's medical-cannabis commission sets a Sept 16 public hearing and possible Sept 30 adoption vote on its Schedule III-enabled mail-delivery rule, with written comments due Sept 9.

PGP takeaway A DEA brief that explicitly argues for Schedule III is the clearest paper evidence yet that the agency's advocacy hasn't moved since April — but the absence of any decision timeline means 280E-relief modeling should keep treating the benefit as "when, not if" rather than underwriting a date. State-level outcomes remain the faster-moving variable: Rhode Island just reopened a license queue and Georgia is building a delivery channel off Schedule III, while New Jersey's labor-peace fight now heads to an appellate court whose ruling would carry regional weight.
