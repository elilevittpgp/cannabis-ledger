The Rally Holds Through a Long Weekend: MSOS Closes at a Multi-Week High as Vireo and Ascend Post Unexplained Double-Digit Gains

Friday's session shook off a weak morning to post the best MSOS close this desk has tracked since early August, even as the corporate calendar stayed empty into the Labor Day holiday.

Today is Labor Day — U.S. equity markets are closed, and this edition's review window (Friday afternoon, Sept. 4, through this morning) landed on a genuinely quiet weekend across the priority names and the MSO watchlist alike. The one live thread is the tape itself: the AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF (MSOS) closed Friday at $5.11, up 3.23% on the day — a reversal from Friday morning's soft open (Thursday's edition had it down slightly, intraday) — and the highest close this desk has recorded since it began tracking the sector in early August, edging out Aug. 28's $4.99 print. The move was broad, but two names stood out for the size of their gain against no confirmed news: Vireo Growth (VREOF) rose 15.67% on the day and 18.40% on the week, and Ascend Wellness (AAWH) rose 9.11% on the day and 7.70% on the week — both continuing a pattern this desk has flagged repeatedly since Aug. 20, in which thinly-traded names post outsized single-session moves with no press release, filing, or news item to explain them.

On the corporate side, nothing moved. Ascend, Premier Group/PCF and Verano were all quiet on filings; live fetches to both Ascend's and Verano's investor-relations pages did not return a response during this run, so today's read on those two leans on wire-service indexing (StockTitan, GlobeNewswire) rather than a direct page check — flagged accordingly in each card below. The Curaleaf-Aurora Cannabis hostile-bid stalemate, dated to Aurora's Sept. 2 rejection and Curaleaf's same-day reaffirmation, carried through the weekend with no extension, escalation, or withdrawal from either side.

Why it matters to PGP Two structural things to flag for the desk. First, the recurring pattern of double-digit, no-catalyst moves in Vireo and Ascend is now a multi-week trend, not a one-off — any valuation work leaning on recent trading history for either name should treat the last month of price action as noisy, not fundamentals-driven, until a specific driver surfaces. Second, three separate governance clocks (Ascend's reverse-split ratio, TerrAscend's consolidation ratio, and the Curaleaf/Aurora bid) all remain unresolved with no fixed date — worth a standing line in any live diligence file that touches these names, since each resolution is a real catalyst whenever it lands.

The Rescheduling Docket

Nearly eight weeks past the close of hearings, still no recommended decision

The docket itself was quiet this weekend; the larger Schedule I→III catalyst remains open-ended.

  • Sep 1, 2026 House passes the Senate's continuing resolution 370-48, carrying the Dec 11 hemp-THC ban delay.
  • Sep 2, 2026 President Trump signs H.R. 6500 into law — the Dec 11 hemp-ban delay is enacted, alongside a stricter 0.4mg-total-THC-per-container standard for naturally-occurring cannabinoids.
  • Sep 2, 2026 Aurora Cannabis formally rejects Curaleaf's hostile bid via its Directors' Circular; Curaleaf reaffirms the offer same-day.
  • Sep 3, 2026 Coverage clarifies that converted/synthetic cannabinoids (delta-8, HHC, THC-O) are excluded from the Dec 11 delay and still lose hemp status on the original Nov 12 date.
  • Sep 3-4, 2026 WSWA leads a retail/beverage-industry coalition publicly endorsing the Van Duyne-Landsman Beverage Regulatory Parity Act.
  • Sep 4-7, 2026 No DEA recommended decision issued over the long weekend; ALJ Julius's timeline remains unset, nearly eight weeks after hearings closed Jul 15.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. Nov 12, 2026 — converted/synthetic cannabinoids (delta-8, HHC, THC-O) lose federal hemp status; no extension applies to this category.
  2. Whether the Van Duyne-Landsman beverage carve-out or a broader hemp bill gets floor time before Dec 11 — the difference between a hemp-beverage category that survives and one that doesn't.
  3. ALJ Julius's recommended decision on broad Schedule I→III rescheduling — still no fixed date; DEA's own post-hearing brief already urges Schedule III.
  4. Sep 15, 2026 — court hearing on Advanced Flower Capital's foreclosure against Justice Grown's PA/NJ collateral.
  5. Sep 30, 2026 — deadline for the administration to answer lawmakers' rescheduling-implementation letter.
  6. Dec 1, 2026 — Curaleaf's bid for Aurora Cannabis expires unless extended.
  7. Dec 11, 2026 — the enacted effective date for naturally-occurring hemp cannabinoids, under the 0.4mg-total-THC-per-container standard.
PGP takeaway The docket's silence over a long weekend isn't a signal either way — but the calendar is compressing: five federal deadlines (Nov 12, Sep 15, Sep 30, Dec 1, Dec 11) now sit inside a 13-week window with no ALJ decision yet in hand. Treat the rescheduling recommendation as the single largest unscheduled catalyst on the desk's board.
