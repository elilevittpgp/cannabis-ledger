Page One · The Week in Review

A Hostile Bid, a Broken Strike, and a Deadline That Finally Landed

Curaleaf's unsolicited run at Aurora Cannabis dominates the M&A tape, Ascend and Vireo both post real earnings beats, and — hours before this edition closed — a federal judge let Virginia's hemp-THC cap take effect at midnight, the clearest preview yet of what December's national cliff will look like state by state.

No. 1 · M&A

Curaleaf goes hostile: a $272 million unsolicited bid for Aurora Cannabis

Curaleaf Holdings said Aug. 11 it intends to launch an unsolicited take-over bid for Aurora Cannabis (NASDAQ/TSX: ACB) — roughly $4.00 per share (0.3463 Curaleaf shares plus $0.75 cash, capped at $5.00/share), about $272 million total and a ~45% premium to Aurora's 30-day VWAP. Curaleaf says two outreach letters (June 23, July 7) went unanswered; Aurora disputes that account, noting its lead independent director corresponded with Curaleaf CEO Boris Jordan as recently as July 24, and has formed a special committee of independent directors to evaluate the proposal rather than reject it outright. Aurora shares jumped roughly 22% on the news and have added another ~5–9% since. TD Cowen called the capped offer light against Aurora's medical-cannabis franchise. Aurora reported its first earnings since the bid on Aug. 12 — Q1 FY2027 revenue of CAD$67.6 million (down 9% YoY) with a narrower-than-expected per-share loss — giving investors a fresh data point mid-review. As of this edition, no counter-bid, formal rejection, or negotiated deal has surfaced.

Why it matters to PGP This is the first hostile approach of scale the U.S. cannabis sector has seen, and it uses a structure worth studying: a Nasdaq/OTC-listed MSO bidding cash-plus-stock directly for a dual-listed Canadian LP, rather than the asset-carve-out or bankruptcy-driven deals (Vireo/Cannabist, SNDL/Parallel, AYR/Arboretum) that dominated 2026 dealflow before it. Whether Aurora's committee negotiates a bump, seeks a competing bidder, or adopts defensive measures will set an early precedent for how non-U.S., federally-legal platforms respond to U.S. MSO approaches — directly relevant to any client-side defense or buy-side cross-border mandate.
No. 2 · Regulatory · Breaking

Virginia's hemp-THC cap survives a court challenge — the law takes effect at midnight

Hours before this edition closed, U.S. District Judge Robert S. Ballou (W.D. Va., Roanoke) denied the temporary restraining order and preliminary injunction sought by seven Virginia hemp businesses trying to block House Bill 30's new 2-milligram-per-package THC cap, which eliminates the old 25:1 CBD:THC ratio exception. Ballou acknowledged the businesses would likely suffer "severe financial losses" but held that doesn't meet the legal standard for irreparable harm, reasoning the businesses should have anticipated regulatory shifts. The cap takes effect at midnight tonight, Aug. 15; no appeal has been reported as of this edition. The ruling follows a five-hour hearing Aug. 12 that ended without a bench decision, and lands in the same week a federal judge in Texas denied hemp retailers' own emergency TRO bid against that state's delta-8/delta-10 ban (Aug. 9–10) — two state-level tests, two losses for hemp plaintiffs, in the same seven days.

Why it matters to PGP Virginia is a live preview of what the December 11 federal hemp-THC cliff will look like fractured across fifty states: litigation outcomes, not statutory text, are what actually determine whether a hemp-derived revenue line survives past a deadline. Two state courts denying emergency relief in the same week — on nearly identical "irreparable harm" reasoning — hands other states an enforcement template ahead of the federal deadline and should pull forward, not push back, any diligence timeline on hemp-THC or hemp-beverage exposure.
No. 3 · Earnings

Ascend beats on Q2 as Illinois returns to full capacity post-strike

Ascend Wellness (OTCQX: AAWH) reported Q2 net revenue of $126.1 million Aug. 12, up 7.9% sequentially and ahead of the roughly $119.7 million consensus estimate. The GAAP net loss narrowed to $9.8 million from $29.5 million in Q1, and adjusted EBITDA rose to $29.1 million (23.1% margin, up from 22.5%). CFO Roman Nemchenko said the Barry, Illinois cultivation and processing facility — site of June's roughly 30-day Teamsters Local 916 strike, the largest in U.S. cannabis history — is "back and operating at full capacity," though residual wholesale-delivery disruption will still weigh on the next quarter or two. No new grievance or labor escalation surfaced anywhere in the window. The reverse-split vote (1-for-10 to 1-for-50) that clears the way for a U.S. exchange uplisting remains set for Aug. 28.

No. 4 · Roll-Up

Vireo Growth's five-deal week: a 335% revenue jump, a closed acquisition, a new credit line and a buyback

Vireo Growth posted Q2 GAAP revenue of $209.3 million (+335% YoY, pro forma $254.9 million) and adjusted EBITDA of $41.5 million on Aug. 11, while confirming its Ohio (FarmaceuticalRx, ~$208M in stock) and Cannabist-estate asset deals as definitive. The same week it closed its ~$49 million PharmaCann Colorado acquisition and a Pennsylvania (PhytoNatural) entry (Aug. 7), drew on a new $65 million asset-based credit facility (expandable to $105 million), and authorized a roughly 2.4-million-share buyback (Aug. 13). The pending all-stock merger with Planet 13 (a ~24% premium, announced July 27) still awaits a shareholder vote, and the FLUENT (formerly Cansortium) acquisition — shareholder-approved July 28 — remains on track for a Q4 2026 close per Vireo's own release.

No. 5 · Structural

Trulieve completes its move to Delaware; a New Jersey court hands Curaleaf a labor win

Trulieve Cannabis completed its redomiciliation from British Columbia to Delaware on Aug. 11, following the BC Supreme Court's Aug. 10 final order and an Aug. 5 shareholder vote; shares continue trading on the NYSE under the new ticker TRLV. Separately, a federal judge in New Jersey ruled Aug. 10 that the state's cannabis labor-peace-agreement mandate is preempted by the National Labor Relations Act — a win for Curaleaf, which challenged the rule; New Jersey is appealing to the Third Circuit. Elsewhere, Glass House Brands' Aug. 13 Q2 print showed the other side of a transition story: revenue flat at $47.0 million, but gross margin compressed to 34% from 55% a year ago as wholesale biomass grew to 89% of revenue, and adjusted EBITDA fell to $5.7 million from $18.1 million.

The Rescheduling Docket

Courts, not the DEA, resolved the week's biggest regulatory questions

The DEA's own Schedule I→III docket didn't move this week, but two state-court rulings — one in Virginia, one in New Jersey — did more to define near-term cannabis and hemp legal exposure than anything out of Washington.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; three consolidated challenges remain pending in the D.C. Circuit (Nos. 26-1106, 26-1130, 26-1136), no new activity this week.
  • Jul 15, 2026 DEA hearings conclude; record stays open for post-hearing briefs, due Aug 17 (50-page cap, Chief ALJ Derek Julius). No new filings this week.
  • Aug 3, 2026 Senate votes 61-32 to table a Budd (R-NC) amendment, then passes the underlying CR 90-6, pushing the toughest federal hemp-THC restrictions to Dec 11.
  • Aug 9–10, 2026 A federal judge in Texas denies hemp retailers' emergency TRO against the state's delta-8/delta-10 ban, citing a failure to show irreparable harm; a broader preliminary-injunction request remains pending.
  • Aug 10, 2026 Reps. Van Duyne (R-TX) and Landsman (D-OH) formally introduce the Beverage Regulatory Parity Act — a 5mg/serving THC cap, three-tier alcohol-style distribution, and an 8-cents-per-mg federal excise tax; the Wine & Spirits Wholesalers Association and broader alcohol-industry groups have endorsed it.
  • Aug 10, 2026 A federal judge in New Jersey rules the state's cannabis labor-peace-agreement mandate is preempted by the NLRA — a Curaleaf win; the state is appealing to the Third Circuit.
  • Aug 12, 2026 A five-hour hearing in W.D. Virginia (Judge Robert S. Ballou) on seven hemp businesses' bid to block HB 30's new 2mg-per-package THC cap ends without a bench ruling.
  • Aug 14, 2026 Judge Ballou denies the TRO and preliminary injunction — HB 30's THC cap takes effect at midnight Aug 15. The court found businesses would likely suffer "severe financial losses" but that this does not meet the legal standard for irreparable harm.
  • Jun–Aug, 2026 States keep maneuvering: Wyoming opts out (Jul 8), Louisiana withdraws its rescheduling objection (Jun), Alabama's written-comment period closed Aug 5 with no State Board of Health decision yet.

PGP takeaway Two state courts denied emergency relief to hemp plaintiffs on nearly identical "irreparable harm" reasoning in the same week — Virginia and Texas both. That's a meaningfully firmer signal than a single ruling would be: courts appear disinclined to block THC-cap enforcement on financial-hardship grounds alone, which should pull forward, not push back, any diligence timeline on hemp-THC or hemp-beverage exposure ahead of the federal Dec 11 cliff. Separately, New Jersey's labor-peace preemption ruling — if it survives Third Circuit review — removes a real deal-diligence friction point relevant to any client with NJ-adjacent labor exposure.
