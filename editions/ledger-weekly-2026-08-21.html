Curaleaf's Bid for Aurora Turns Real — and Aurora Fights Back With a Deal of Its Own

A hostile take-over bid moves from press release to formally mailed circular, the target keeps dealmaking through the fight, the DEA's own lawyers argue for Schedule III with still no clock attached, and MSOS rallies to a fresh month-to-date high, up 17% in August.

No. 1 · M&A

Curaleaf formally commences its Aurora bid; five days later, Aurora goes shopping itself

Curaleaf Holdings converted its Aug. 11 "intention to launch" into a live, formally commenced take-over bid on Aug. 18, filing and mailing the Offer and Take-Over Bid Circular to Aurora Cannabis shareholders. Terms were unchanged — US$4.00/share implied (0.3463 Curaleaf shares plus US$0.75 cash), capped at US$5.00/share, roughly a 45% premium to Aurora's 30-day VWAP — but now carry no financing or diligence condition and a hard expiry of 5 p.m. MT on Dec. 1, 2026. Aurora's reaction was telling: shares rose a muted 1.9% on the mailing, a fraction of the 20%-plus pop the stock saw when Curaleaf first floated the idea, suggesting the market had already priced in formal commencement. Aurora's special committee has adopted no poison pill and issued no recommendation; a formal Directors' Circular is due under Canadian take-over rules roughly Sept. 2. Then, five days into fielding the bid, Aurora went shopping of its own on Aug. 19 — acquiring UK-based Internode Pharma and HAP Pharma, a Birmingham pharmacy, for roughly £2.1 million cash to tighten its UK medical-cannabis distribution chain. Aurora shares jumped 6.54% the same session Curaleaf rose 6.96%, amid a broadly risk-on tape. No counter-bid, poison pill, or negotiated deal has surfaced as of this edition.

Why it matters to PGP Two structuring lessons. First, staged M&A disclosure — float an "intention," then formally commence weeks later — gets priced almost entirely on the first headline; Aurora's 1.9% reaction to a legally significant escalation (no financing condition, hard expiry) versus its 20%-plus pop on the original announcement is a data point worth carrying into how PGP times and sequences any live-bid mandate. Second, a hostile-bid target that keeps signing its own deals rather than freezing to defend complicates the acquirer's "hold value hostage" thesis — Aurora's board is signaling it intends to keep running the company through the fight, a posture worth benchmarking for both buy-side and defense-side hostile-bid work.
No. 2 · Regulatory

The DEA makes its own case for Schedule III — and still won't say when a decision comes

Chief Administrative Law Judge Derek Julius received post-hearing briefs on schedule Aug. 17, and the notable filer was the DEA itself: the agency's own brief urges Julius to recommend moving marijuana from Schedule I to Schedule III, the clearest documentary confirmation yet that its position hasn't wavered since April's order. Opposition briefs came from Smart Approaches to Marijuana, the states of Idaho, Indiana and Nebraska, the Tennessee Bureau of Investigation and others. No outlet has reported a timeline for Julius's recommended decision — trade coverage points to "weeks or months," then a 20-day exceptions window, then a final call from the DEA Administrator that may not land before late 2026 or early 2027. On Aug. 19, a bipartisan group of representatives (Titus, Joyce, Omar, Steube) sent a letter pressing the Trump administration for Schedule III implementation guidance — including whether Medicare will cover medical cannabis — with answers requested by Sept. 30. On Aug. 20, Julius ordered the 11-day hearing transcript corrected by Aug. 26, a procedural step rather than a signal on timing.

Why it matters to PGP A DEA brief explicitly arguing for Schedule III is the strongest paper evidence yet that the agency's advocacy hasn't shifted — but with zero attached timeline, 280E relief remains a "when, not if" bet that keeps sliding further out in diligence models. The bipartisan congressional letter is itself a signal worth tracking: political patience with the DEA's silence is thinning, and Sept. 30 is now a real date to diarize for a possible administration response, even though it moves nothing on the actual docket.
No. 3 · Governance & Litigation

A board buys its own stock as an insurer tries to duck a class-action bill

TerrAscend renewed and replenished its normal-course-issuer-bid on Aug. 20 — up to $10 million, running Aug. 24, 2026 through Aug. 23, 2027 — with Executive Chairman Jason Wild telling shareholders the stock trades "at a substantial discount to intrinsic value." The renewal lands the same day as TerrAscend's special meeting on a share consolidation tied to a potential U.S. uplisting, making the buyback as much a governance-vote signal as a capital-allocation one. Separately, Cresco Labs' own insurer, Palomar Excess & Surplus, sued the company in the Northern District of Illinois seeking a declaration it owes no duty to defend or indemnify Cresco against the Murray et al. RICO/consumer-fraud class actions spanning 12 states — shifting legal-defense cost risk back onto Cresco's balance sheet if Palomar prevails.

No. 4 · Markets

MSOS rallies to a fresh month-to-date high, up 17% in August

The AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF (MSOS) closed the week at $4.96 — up 6.70% versus last Friday's $4.63 and up 17.26% month-to-date versus the July 31 close of $4.23. The week's path was uneven: down Monday and Tuesday, then a sharp +5.31% Wednesday on Aurora's UK bolt-on and a broadly risk-on tape, a small Thursday pause, and a fresh +4.64% leg up Friday. Trulieve (newly uplisted to the NYSE as TRLV) and Ascend Wellness both posted the week's largest gains, each in the mid-to-high teens by percentage, though Ascend's move came with no company release found to explain it. TerrAscend and Aurora Cannabis were the next-strongest movers on M&A-linked news; Vireo Growth was the week's laggard, essentially flat while it works through its pending Planet 13 and Cannabist deals.

No. 5 · State Regulatory

States keep moving faster than Washington

Virginia's HB 30 2-milligram THC cap took effect at midnight Aug. 15 with no appeal filed; industry groups are instead pressing lawmakers for a special legislative session as store closures mount. Rhode Island's federal court lifted its cannabis-license freeze Aug. 17, clearing roughly 20 stalled retail licenses. New Jersey formally appealed its labor-peace-mandate defeat to the Third Circuit (filed Aug. 13). Georgia's cannabis commission proposed a Schedule-III-enabled mail-delivery rule, setting a Sept. 9 comment deadline, a Sept. 16 hearing and a possible Sept. 30 vote. New Hampshire's legislature overrode a gubernatorial veto Aug. 19 to legalize greenhouse cultivation for medical operators. And Trulieve rang the NYSE closing bell Aug. 18, the first U.S. plant-touching operator to do so, marking its completed redomicile and uplisting.

The Rescheduling Docket

The federal record is fully briefed; the states keep setting the pace

The DEA's own brief argues for Schedule III, Congress presses the White House for a timeline, and a housekeeping transcript order is the only concrete date on the federal calendar — while five separate states moved on cannabis or hemp policy in the same week.

  • Aug 15, 2026 Virginia's HB 30 2mg-per-package THC cap takes effect at midnight, following Judge Ballou's denied injunction; retailers pulled an estimated 85–90% of affected inventory ahead of the deadline.
  • Aug 13, 2026 New Jersey regulators file a notice of appeal to the Third Circuit over the labor-peace-mandate preemption ruling that favored Curaleaf — a ruling there would also bind Pennsylvania and Delaware.
  • Aug 17, 2026 DEA post-hearing brief deadline is met: the agency's own brief urges Chief ALJ Derek Julius to recommend Schedule III; opposition briefs filed by SAM, Idaho/Indiana/Nebraska, the Tennessee Bureau of Investigation and others.
  • Aug 17, 2026 Virginia hemp plaintiffs confirm no appeal filed; industry instead presses lawmakers for a special legislative session as store closures mount.
  • Aug 17, 2026 A federal judge dissolves Rhode Island's cannabis-license injunction after the state repeals its residency requirement, clearing ~20 frozen retail licenses and ~100 applicants to resume.
  • Aug 18, 2026 Georgia's Access to Medical Cannabis Commission proposes a Schedule-III-enabled mail-delivery rule, citing DOJ's April reclassification as the legal enabler.
  • Aug 19, 2026 Reps. Titus, Joyce, Omar and Steube send a bipartisan letter pressing the Trump administration for Schedule III implementation guidance, including whether Medicare will cover medical cannabis, with answers requested by Sept 30.
