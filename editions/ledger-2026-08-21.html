TerrAscend Renews a $10 Million Bet on Its Own Stock as the Sector Cools Off

MSOS gives back a sliver of Wednesday's rally, closing Thursday at $4.74 (−0.42%), as TerrAscend refreshes its buyback ahead of Monday's consolidation vote and Cresco Labs draws a coverage-fight lawsuit from its own insurer.

On an otherwise quiet Friday, the clearest signal in the sector came from TerrAscend, which on August 20 renewed and replenished its normal-course-issuer-bid share-repurchase program — up to $10 million, running Aug. 24, 2026 through Aug. 23, 2027, executed through Cormark at up to 58,784 shares a day. Executive Chairman Jason Wild told shareholders directly that the stock is trading "at a substantial discount to intrinsic value"; the prior program (Aug. 2025–Aug. 2026) repurchased 653,500 shares for roughly $417,000 at an average $0.64. The renewal lands the same day TerrAscend's special meeting on a share consolidation convenes — Monday, Aug. 24 — making the buyback as much a governance-vote signal as a capital-allocation one.

Elsewhere, Cresco Labs' own insurer, Palomar Excess & Surplus, sued the company in the Northern District of Illinois seeking a declaration that it owes no duty to defend or indemnify Cresco against the Murray et al. RICO/consumer-fraud class actions spanning 12 states — a coverage fight that shifts legal-defense cost risk back onto the balance sheet if Palomar prevails. Verano's Easy Landings THC beverage line, announced Wednesday, went on sale today at Zen Leaf dispensaries in Arizona, Illinois and Nevada. Ascend Wellness shares moved sharply intraday Thursday — roughly +11–12% — with no company release found to explain the move, seven days out from its Aug. 28 special meeting on the reverse stock split. Premier Group/PCF remained clean and quiet on a direct check of the state licensing log and local press. On the docket, the DEA's chief ALJ ordered the 11-day rescheduling-hearing transcript corrected by Aug. 26 — a procedural step, not a signal on when the actual recommended decision lands.

Why it matters to PGP A board buying its own stock while calling it a "substantial discount to intrinsic value" is a real sector-valuation data point worth marking against comps, and doing so heading into a governance vote (share consolidation) reads as a conviction signal worth tracking as a template for how other small-cap MSOs might defend against dilution or a depressed multiple. Separately, an insurer disclaiming coverage on a live class action is a diligence flag for any MSO target or comparable: confirm insurance-tower language and carve-outs explicitly rather than assuming litigation exposure is capped by policy limits — legacy conduct claims can outlive the underlying business event by years.

The Rescheduling Docket

A procedural footnote while the real decision still waits

The DEA's chief ALJ orders the hearing transcript corrected by Aug. 26 — housekeeping, not a signal on timing for the recommended decision everyone is actually waiting on.

  • Jul 15, 2026 DEA rescheduling hearings conclude; Chief ALJ Derek Julius sets a post-hearing brief deadline of Aug 17.
  • Aug 17, 2026 Brief deadline met: DEA's own brief urges Julius to recommend Schedule III; opposition briefs filed by SAM, Idaho/Indiana/Nebraska, the Tennessee Bureau of Investigation and others.
  • Aug 19, 2026 Reps. Titus, Joyce, Omar and Steube send a bipartisan letter to President Trump pressing for Schedule III implementation guidance, including on Medicare coverage, with answers requested by Sept 30.
  • Aug 20, 2026 Chief ALJ Julius orders corrections to the official 11-day hearing transcript (mistranscriptions and misidentifications throughout); a corrected version is due on the DEA's website no later than Aug 26 — a procedural step, not a ruling.
