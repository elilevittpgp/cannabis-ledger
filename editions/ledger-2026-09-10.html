Page One · The Big Story

A Federal Court Clears One Hurdle for Rescheduling — While Curaleaf and Aurora Keep Trading Blows

The D.C. Circuit refuses to freeze the DOJ's Schedule III order, but the bigger prize — full Schedule I→III reclassification — still sits with a silent ALJ. Meanwhile the hostile bid for Aurora Cannabis escalates with a second rebuttal sheet and a defensive hire.

The closest thing to hard news on a Thursday otherwise light on filings came from a courtroom. The U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit denied a motion to stay the DOJ's April 2026 order moving state-licensed medical marijuana and FDA-approved marijuana drug products from Schedule I to Schedule III. A drug-testing industry association and a pharmaceutical company had sought the freeze; the court found they "have not satisfied the stringent requirements for a stay pending review." Two medical-cannabis companies that tried to intervene on the government's side were also turned away. The ruling keeps the narrower Schedule III reclassification in force — it does not touch the separate, larger fight over full-industry rescheduling, which remains with DEA Chief ALJ Derek Julius nearly two months after hearings closed with no recommended decision in sight.

Away from the docket, the desk's live story kept moving. Curaleaf published a second "myth vs. fact" rebuttal to Aurora Cannabis's board, with Chairman/CEO Boris Jordan stating publicly that Aurora "never signed an NDA, never discussed price, and never made a counteroffer." Hours later, Aurora announced a new hire — Faris El Refaie, a biopharma veteran of JAMP Pharma, GSK and AstraZeneca, joins as EVP, Global Commercial, effective Sept. 28 — a defensive signal of business-as-usual timed squarely inside the takeover fight. Separately, follow-on coverage of Tuesday's GAO banking report added a sharper point: Visa and Mastercard are unlikely to loosen cannabis payment-network restrictions even after further rescheduling, since card-network limits are a matter of private risk policy, not federal drug scheduling. Ascend, Premier Group/PCF and Verano all stayed quiet on filings.

Why it matters to PGP Two threads worth separating in any client conversation this week: rescheduling risk is narrowing (a court just declined to unwind the existing medical Schedule III order) even as rescheduling reward stays undated (the ALJ's recommendation, and the 280E relief riding on it, has no fixed timeline). And the Curaleaf/Aurora fight is now a live template for hostile-bid mechanics in this sector — dueling fact sheets, a defensive hire, a Dec. 1 expiry — that PGP's advisory practice should be tracking as a playbook, not a spectator sport.

The Rescheduling Docket

A court clears one hurdle; the ALJ still hasn't spoken

The D.C. Circuit protects the existing medical Schedule III order from a stay, but the broader industry-wide reclassification stays parked with Chief ALJ Derek Julius.

  • Sep 8-9, 2026 GAO publishes GAO-26-107498 on cannabis banking access — no bank penalized solely for cannabis relationships, but SAR filings and account-fee burden remain the practical barrier banks cite.
  • Sep 9, 2026 Follow-on coverage of the GAO report finds Visa and Mastercard are unlikely to loosen cannabis payment-network restrictions even after further rescheduling, since card-network limits stem from private risk policy rather than federal drug scheduling.
  • Sep 9, 2026 The D.C. Circuit denies a stay motion against the DOJ's April 2026 order moving medical/FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III — the existing narrower reclassification stays in effect while the underlying challenge continues on the merits.
  • Sep 10, 2026 No recommended decision issued; Chief ALJ Julius's timeline remains unset, nearly eight weeks after hearings closed July 15.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. ALJ Julius's recommended decision on broad Schedule I→III rescheduling — still no fixed date.
  2. Sep 15, 2026 — CA Cannabis Advisory Committee meets on rescheduling and payment-processing constraints; the Advanced Flower Capital/Justice Grown court hearing is separately guided for this date but unconfirmed.
  3. Sep 30, 2026 — deadline for the administration to answer lawmakers' rescheduling-implementation letter.
  4. Nov 12, 2026 — converted/synthetic cannabinoids (delta-8, HHC, THC-O) lose federal hemp status; no extension applies to this category.
  5. Dec 1, 2026 — Curaleaf's bid for Aurora Cannabis expires unless extended.
  6. Dec 11, 2026 — the enacted effective date for naturally-occurring hemp cannabinoids, under the 0.4mg-total-THC-per-container standard.
PGP takeaway The stay denial removes a tail risk (a court unwinding the existing medical Schedule III order) but doesn't pull forward the bigger prize — the ALJ's recommendation, and the 280E relief riding on it, remain undated. Pair that with the GAO's card-network finding and don't model near-term payments relief even once the ALJ rules; 280E is the more probable near-term catalyst.
