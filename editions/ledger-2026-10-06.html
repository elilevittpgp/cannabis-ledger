Curaleaf Formally Files Its Enhanced Aurora Bid — Raising the Cap to $6.00 as Aurora Says the Clock Hasn't Started Yet

Tuesday's SEC and Canadian filings convert Monday's announced intent into an actual offer, but Aurora's board calls the special-committee review clock newly started, not nearly finished, while the Alberta Securities Commission still has no hearing date for either side's disclosure fight.

Curaleaf on Tuesday filed a Notice of Variation, Change and Extension with Canadian regulators and an Amendment No. 2 to its Schedule 14D-1F/Form F-80 with the SEC, formally converting Monday's announced intent into an actual enhanced bid for Aurora Cannabis: an implied US$5.00 per share (0.4013 Curaleaf subordinate voting shares plus US$1.00 cash, based on Curaleaf's Oct 2 close), an 86% premium to Aurora's unaffected price — with the offer's cash-election cap now raised to US$6.00 per share. The minimum deposit period is extended to 11:59pm Mountain Time on Dec 4.

Aurora's camp wasted no time reframing the timeline: CEO Miguel Martin said Monday's announcement was "not a formal revised bid," and that the special committee would only formally evaluate an enhanced offer once it was actually filed — which, as of Tuesday, it now has been. The board's unanimous August rejection of the original US$4.00 offer stands as its only public position on the record; it has retained Fort Capital to evaluate standalone alternatives and other potential suitors. Neither side's Alberta Securities Commission application — Curaleaf's bid to halt further issuances under Aurora's at-the-market equity program, and Aurora's counter-complaint alleging disclosure deficiencies in Curaleaf's bid circular — has a hearing date, roughly three weeks after the second was filed.

Why it matters to PGP Filing the formal variation doesn't start a fresh clock so much as confirm whose clock it is: Aurora's camp is treating "announced" and "filed" as two different triggers for its own review obligations — a distinction that can buy a target weeks without changing the underlying economics. Any PGP mandate on a contested Canadian bid should model the gap between press-release intent and the actual filed variation as a real source of delay, and treat an unscheduled regulator hearing as standing option value for the target, not an afterthought.

The Rescheduling Docket

Still stayed — but the medical track keeps moving

No DEA filing or ALJ ruling has surfaced on the stayed broader case, but the agency opened a new registration track for Schedule III medical-cannabis manufacturers this week — a reminder that April's narrower reclassification keeps advancing even while the bigger fight waits on Oct 13.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E.
  • Jul 15, 2026 Broader Schedule I→III hearings conclude before ALJ Derek Julius.
  • Sep 23, 2026 GAO report finds DEA and FDA lack adequate written drug-scheduling procedures.
  • Sep 28-29, 2026 Rescheduling opponents move to add the GAO report to the record; ALJ Julius stays the case, giving the DEA until Oct 13 to respond.
  • Oct 5, 2026 DEA opens a new Form 225 registration portal for Schedule III medical-cannabis manufacturers, distributors and labs — implementation of the April order continues on a separate track from the stayed case.
  • Oct 6, 2026 No new DEA filing or ALJ ruling found; MSOS attempts its first bounce (~+1.6% intraday) after three losing weeks.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. Oct 13, 2026 — DEA's response to the motion to reopen the record (GAO report) is due.
  2. ALJ Julius rules on whether to admit the GAO report and permit added briefing.
  3. If granted, a further briefing round precedes the recommended decision; if denied, the case resumes toward a decision on the existing record.
  4. A 20-day exceptions window, then the DEA Administrator's final call — sell-side analysts (Cantor Fitzgerald) have modeled 1H 2027 as the more likely resolution window.
  5. Separately: the SAFE Banking Act of 2026 (S.4942) remains referred to the Senate Banking Committee with no markup or floor vote scheduled.
PGP takeaway Tuesday's bounce shouldn't be read as resolution — no docket movement has occurred, and the DEA's new Schedule III manufacturer-registration track is an operational implementation step on the medical side, not a signal on the broader case. Models keyed to a 280E resolution should keep running on a 1H-2H 2027 base case; models keyed to banking reform should not assume S.4942 moves before a markup is actually scheduled.
