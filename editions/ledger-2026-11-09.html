Page One · The Big Story

Vireo Growth Closes Its FLUENT Buy to Reach 210 Dispensaries Across 10 States and Aurora Locks In a Dec. 23 Shareholder Vote on the Organigram Deal — MSOS Slides Roughly 4% in a Broad Monday Selloff

A quiet weekend across the priority desk gives way to a Monday of deal mechanics, even as the sector gives back a chunk of last week's rally with no single catalyst behind the move.

Vireo Growth completed its acquisition of FLUENT Corp. on Nov. 9, delisting FLUENT from the CSE and OTCQB and pushing the combined company's footprint to 210 dispensaries across 10 states. The deal was financed through a debt-for-equity structure: roughly $35.7 million of FLUENT debt owed to lender Chicago Atlantic was equitized (the original $30 million balance plus an incremental $5.7 million conversion), lifting Chicago Atlantic's stake in Vireo to about 11.7% from 6.2%. Separately, Aurora Cannabis filed an SEC Form 6-K confirming its special virtual shareholder meeting to vote on Organigram Global's C$7.80-per-share acquisition is now set for December 23, 2026, with a November 18, 2026 record date — the first firm date on a calendar previously described only as "on or before Dec. 23." Curaleaf's rival hostile tender remains open, unsweetened, through its own Dec. 4 deadline, with no amended terms filed since Nov. 3 despite CEO Boris Jordan's earlier pledge to "improve our bid substantially."

Those two deal-mechanics stories aside, Monday was dominated by a broad, largely unexplained sector pullback: MSOS closed around $4.07, down roughly 4% on the day and about 3.1% on the week, giving back a meaningful share of Friday's rally. Trulieve led liquid-name declines (-4.76%), Green Thumb and Curaleaf each fell more than 3%, and Cresco Labs stayed soft while still digesting Friday's Zacks downgrade to Hold; Glass House Brands fell another 8.65%, extending a slide that predates today and has now cut the stock roughly 81% since June. Vireo was the session's lone gainer among checked names (+3.83%), tracking its FLUENT close. Off the tape, Missouri's attorney general sued four retailers for allegedly marketing marijuana as hemp and promised more suits once the state's THC-potency law takes fuller effect Nov. 12; Texas's 15th Court of Appeals canceled December oral arguments in the state's own hemp-rules challenge after voters ousted all three sitting Republican justices, pushing the case into 2027 before the incoming Democratic bench; and Illinois's emergency motion to block its own Nov. 12 hemp-THC ban remained undecided as of this edition, three days before the deadline.

Why it matters to PGP Vireo's FLUENT close is a clean financing template worth benchmarking: a consolidator retiring acquired debt by rolling it into a rising lender equity stake, rather than raising fresh cash, is exactly the structure PGP should expect to see again as the roll-up reaches its next targets (Planet 13, the Cannabist asset package). On the Aurora front, the calendar is now concrete — a Nov. 18 record date, Curaleaf's Dec. 4 tender deadline, and Aurora's Dec. 23 vote — which gives PGP a specific, three-week window to watch whether Curaleaf's "substantial" bid talk turns into a filed offer or simply expires unexercised. And today's ~4% sector-wide drop, with no identifiable single catalyst across names that just reported strong quarters, is a reminder that MSO-exposed positions are currently carrying broad sector beta as much as any idiosyncratic company risk.

The Rescheduling Docket

The ALJ's record is closed with no recommendation timeline, the DEA previews a Schedule III production-quota framework, and two state hemp-ban fights head toward Thursday's deadline from opposite positions

Missouri's attorney general opens a new enforcement front as her state's ban nears; Illinois's own ban fight remains undecided three days out; and Texas's hemp-rules court fight is pushed into 2027 by an election result.

  • Nov 5-6, 2026 Reply briefs close the evidentiary record before Chief ALJ Derek Julius in the Schedule I-to-III case; the DEA argues the admitted GAO report deserves "no weight," opponents argue it shows "fatal flaws." No recommendation timeline has been announced.
  • Nov 4-6, 2026 Missouri Attorney General Catherine Hanaway's office sues four retailers (including shops in Aurora and Independence, MO) for allegedly marketing marijuana as hemp, and says to expect "more lawsuits" as the state's THC-potency law takes fuller effect Nov. 12 — the same week a federal judge cleared that ban to stand.
  • Nov 6, 2026 Texas's 15th Court of Appeals cancels Dec. 8 oral arguments in the state's hemp-THC rules challenge, days after voters ousted all three sitting Republican justices; the case will now be heard by the incoming Democratic bench in 2027, with no new date yet set.
  • Nov 9, 2026 As of this edition, Illinois's emergency motion for a TRO/preliminary injunction against its own Nov. 12 Hemp Act ban remains undecided; local coverage describes Chicago-area hemp retailers still bracing for the deadline absent a ruling.
  • Nov 9, 2026 The DEA's draft 2027 annual production-quota notice confirms the agency is developing a separate quota framework for Schedule III marijuana and marijuana extracts, distinct from the Schedule I quotas it proposes holding flat at 2026 levels; no publication date has been set for the Schedule III proposal itself.
