Page One · The Big Story

Aurora Cannabis Buys a Business While Fielding a Hostile Bid for Its Own

MSOS posts its sharpest one-day gain in weeks — up 5.31% to a 12.53% month-to-date advance — as Aurora's UK bolt-on complicates the optics of the Curaleaf takeover fight.

Five days into fielding a hostile take-over bid from Curaleaf, Aurora Cannabis went shopping of its own on August 19 — acquiring UK-based Internode Pharma, a licensed importer/wholesaler, and HAP Pharma, a Birmingham pharmacy, for roughly £2.1 million cash to tighten its grip on the UK medical-cannabis distribution chain. It is a modest bolt-on next to Curaleaf's ~US$260M unsolicited offer for Aurora itself, but the timing reads as a message: Aurora's special committee is still reviewing the Curaleaf bid and telling shareholders to "take no action," yet the company is running its own playbook rather than sitting still while the bid clock runs. Aurora shares rose 6.54% on the session — an unusual dynamic in which a hostile-bid target's own dealmaking lifts both the target's stock and, at +6.96%, the acquirer's.

The whole complex caught a bid alongside it: the AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF (MSOS) closed Wednesday at $4.76, its best single day in weeks, with TerrAscend (+8.93%) leading gainers and nearly every constituent in the green — see the Sector Tape below. Elsewhere, Verano launched its first national THC beverage brand, AYR Wellness opened the first medical dispensary in Virginia's Shenandoah Valley, New Hampshire's legislature overrode a gubernatorial veto to legalize greenhouse cultivation for medical operators, and a bipartisan group of lawmakers pressed the Trump administration for guidance on the Schedule III rollout — including whether Medicare will cover medical cannabis. Ascend Wellness and Premier Group/PCF were both quiet in the review window; see Priority Coverage below.

Why it matters to PGP A hostile-bid target that keeps dealmaking complicates the acquirer's "hold value hostage" thesis and is worth tracking as a precedent for how PGP times and structures any unsolicited approach — Aurora's board is signaling it intends to keep running the company, not just defend it. Separately, a same-day rally that lifts both a bidder and its target (Curaleaf +6.96%, Aurora +6.54%) is a reminder that sector-wide risk-on tape can mask or exaggerate deal-specific signal — read single-name moves against the MSOS benchmark, not in isolation.

The Sector Tape · MSOS & the MSO Complex

MSOS posts its best day in weeks as the whole complex rallies

Nearly every constituent closed higher Wednesday, with Aurora's own dealmaking and a broadly risk-on tape driving the ETF to a fresh month-to-date high.

The AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF (NYSE Arca: MSOS) closed Wednesday, Aug 19 at $4.76, up 5.31% versus Tuesday Aug 18's $4.52 close — its sharpest one-day gain in weeks — and up 4.39% on the week versus Aug 12's $4.56 close. The fund is now up 12.53% month-to-date versus the July 31 close of $4.23, on volume of ~6.83 million shares. (A Thursday intraday quote in the $4.68–4.71 range, down roughly 1.5–1.7% on the session, is cited for color only — the session is not complete and is not used as the headline figure.)

TerrAscend (TSNDF) led the tape, +8.93% on the day and +5.17% on the week, with a special meeting on a share consolidation set for Aug 24 keeping the name in focus. Curaleaf (+6.96%) and Aurora Cannabis (+6.54%) both rallied around Aurora's UK bolt-on news — the target and the bidder rising together. Green Thumb, Verano and Cresco Labs all posted solid gains too. The laggards were narrow: Glass House Brands fell further behind on the week (-5.73%) still working off its Q2 margin miss, and Vireo Growth was the only constituent to close lower on the day (-0.09%) as it works through its pending Planet 13 and Cannabist deals.

The Rescheduling Docket

Congress presses the White House for answers the DEA hasn't given

With the ALJ's record fully briefed and no timeline in sight, a bipartisan group of lawmakers is now asking the administration directly what comes next — including whether Medicare will cover medical cannabis.

  • Jul 15, 2026 DEA rescheduling hearings conclude; Chief ALJ Derek Julius sets a post-hearing brief deadline of Aug 17.
  • Aug 17, 2026 Brief deadline met: DEA's own brief urges Julius to recommend Schedule III; opposition briefs filed by SAM, Idaho/Indiana/Nebraska, the Tennessee Bureau of Investigation and others.
  • Aug 19, 2026 Reps. Dina Titus (D-NV), David Joyce (R-OH), Ilhan Omar (D-MN) and Greg Steube (R-FL) send a bipartisan letter to President Trump and Cabinet officials pressing for implementation guidance on the Schedule III rollout — including whether Medicare will cover medical cannabis — with answers requested by Sept 30.
  • Aug 19, 2026 New Hampshire's legislature overrides Gov. Ayotte's veto (Senate 16-8, House 257-44), legalizing greenhouse cultivation for medical-marijuana operators — a state-level move continuing to run ahead of the stalled federal process.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. ALJ Julius's recommended decision — still no timeline reported even with briefing complete; a 20-day exceptions window and a final DEA Administrator call follow.
  2. ~Sept 2, 2026 — Aurora's Directors' Circular recommendation on the Curaleaf bid is due.
  3. Sept 9 / Sept 16 / Sept 30, 2026 — Georgia's mail-delivery rule comment deadline, public hearing, and possible adoption vote.
  4. Sept 30, 2026 — deadline lawmakers gave the Trump administration to answer their rescheduling-implementation letter.
  5. Dec 1, 2026 — Curaleaf's formally commenced bid for Aurora Cannabis expires unless extended.
  6. Watch Alabama's Board of Health 280E/opt-out ruling, still pending since its comment period closed Aug 5.
PGP takeaway A bipartisan letter demanding implementation guidance is itself a signal that political patience with the DEA's silence is thinning — but it doesn't move the actual docket, so 280E-relief modeling should keep treating the benefit as "when, not if." State legislatures continue to move faster than Washington: New Hampshire's veto override is a reminder that near-term diligence catalysts still sit at the state level, not the federal one.

Industry Headlines

New Hampshire overrides veto on medical-cannabis greenhouses

The legislature reversed Gov. Ayotte's veto of SB 468 (Senate 16-8, House 257-44), letting therapeutic cannabis centers cultivate in a secure greenhouse to ease indoor-lighting cost pressure on patients. Marijuana Moment

Bipartisan lawmakers press Trump for rescheduling guidance

Reps. Titus, Joyce, Omar and Steube want answers by Sept 30 on the Schedule III rollout, including whether Medicare will cover medical cannabis — a follow-up to April's DOJ order. Marijuana Moment

AYR opens the Shenandoah Valley's first medical dispensary

The Winchester, VA store is the first in the state's Health Service Area I; AYR holds the vertical license for HSA I and IV and plans an in-house KYND harvest there by January. WMRA

NIDA director revisits cannabis-addiction messaging

Nora Volkow said the notion that cannabis isn't addictive "may basically be incorrect," as NHTSA separately highlighted a public-education push on impaired driving. Marijuana Moment

Michigan regulators file complaints against cannabis businesses

State regulators moved against multiple licensees over alleged violations, part of ongoing compliance enforcement in one of the country's largest adult-use markets. Marijuana Moment
