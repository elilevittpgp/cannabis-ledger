Curaleaf Takes the Aurora Fight to a Regulator, Alleging a Defensive Dilution Play

A same-day filing-and-rebuttal at the Alberta Securities Commission sharpens the hostile-bid playbook, while the DEA's rescheduling clock keeps running past every marker anyone expected.

The Curaleaf–Aurora Cannabis fight found a new venue this morning: a securities regulator. Curaleaf filed an application with the Alberta Securities Commission seeking an expedited hearing to halt further issuances under Aurora's at-the-market equity program while its hostile bid remains outstanding. Curaleaf alleges Aurora has issued roughly 2.81 million shares at an average of US$3.04 since June, diluting existing holders by about 4.9% and adding more than $11 million to the effective cost of the deal — framing it as a defensive tactic designed to erode the bid's value. Aurora fired back hours later, calling the filing a distraction and noting its ATM program was announced in February 2026 — more than six months before Curaleaf's bid — has sat inactive for several weeks, and funds a growth strategy rather than a takeover defense.

The dispute layers onto an already-contested record: Curaleaf's Sept. 8 "myth vs. fact" sheet highlighted roughly C$5 billion in Aurora impairments since fiscal 2021 and a 97% share-price decline, while Aurora's board formally rejected the US$4.00-to-US$5.00/share bid on Sept. 2 (backed by a Fort Capital fairness opinion) and named a defensive EVP hire on Sept. 9. The bid — 0.3463 Curaleaf shares plus US$0.75 cash per Aurora share — still expires Dec. 1 unless extended. Away from the bid fight, the desk's other live threads held steady: DEA Chief ALJ Derek Julius has still not issued a recommended decision on full Schedule I-to-III rescheduling, now roughly eight weeks past the close of hearings, and a fresh attorney analysis published today argues the existing narrower medical Schedule III order — which survived a stay motion last week — could still be undone on the merits. Ascend, Verano and Premier Group/PCF all stayed quiet over the weekend.

Why it matters to PGP The ASC filing pushes hostile-bid mechanics in cannabis into new territory — using a securities regulator to police a target's capital-raising activity mid-bid is a maneuver PGP's advisory practice should treat as a template, not a one-off. It also sharpens the defense-side playbook worth pricing into any sell-side or target-defense mandate: ATM timing, poison-pill design, and fairness-opinion sequencing all just became live issues, not theoretical ones. Separately, the still-undated ALJ decision remains the single largest unpriced catalyst in the sector — every week it doesn't land is a week of deferred 280E relief that every MSO model is implicitly assuming will eventually arrive.

Why it matters to PGP Curaleaf/Aurora's move to a securities regulator is a new escalation tier in cannabis hostile-bid mechanics — the desk should treat contested-ATM and defensive-issuance disputes as a live diligence item on both the buy and sell side going forward. The Rescheduling Docket

No decision, and a fresh warning the existing order isn't settled either

Chief ALJ Derek Julius still hasn't ruled on full Schedule I→III reclassification, and a new analysis argues even last week's court win for the narrower medical order could still unravel on the merits.

  • Aug 17, 2026 Post-hearing briefs filed with the DEA following the July 15 close of ALJ hearings on full Schedule I→III rescheduling.
  • Sep 8-9, 2026 GAO publishes GAO-26-107498 on cannabis banking access; follow-on coverage finds Visa and Mastercard unlikely to loosen payment-network restrictions even after further rescheduling, since card-network limits are private risk policy, not federal drug law.
  • Sep 9, 2026 The D.C. Circuit denies a stay motion against the DOJ's April 2026 order moving medical/FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III — the order stays in effect while the underlying merits challenge continues.
  • Sep 14, 2026 A cannabis-attorney analysis argues the Schedule III order "may not survive" the pending merits review despite surviving the stay motion; separately, a bipartisan pair of lawmakers presses the administration for answers on the stalled rescheduling timeline.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. ALJ Julius's recommended decision on full Schedule I→III rescheduling — still no fixed date, ~8 weeks past the hearing close.
  2. Sep 15, 2026 — CA Cannabis Advisory Committee meets (teleconference) on federal rescheduling and payment-processing fallout.
  3. Sep 18, 2026 — TerrAscend's response due in the DOJ's federal 280E clawback suit.

  6. Dec 1, 2026 — Curaleaf's bid for Aurora Cannabis expires unless extended.

PGP takeaway Treat the ALJ's silence as the sector's single largest unpriced variable, not a formality — every week without a recommendation defers the 280E relief most MSO models already assume. And don't treat last week's stay denial as the end of the story: today's analysis is a reminder the medical Schedule III order remains under active merits challenge even as it stands. Layer the Sept 15 CA committee meeting and Sept 30 federal deadline in as near-term signal events, not catalysts in themselves.
