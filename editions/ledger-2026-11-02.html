Page One · The Big Story

Cannabis Stocks Erase the "AI Reckoning" Rout as MSOS Jumps 6.6% — Trulieve Becomes the First MSO to Confirm Its Own DEA Registration, Cresco's Delaware Vote Clears at 94%, and Fluent Tidies Its Books Ahead of the Vireo Merger

A broad Wall Street rebound and a company-specific rescheduling confirmation combine to reverse more than Friday's selloff across the board — while two capital-structure items, one approved and one proposed, move forward without a single new change-of-control deal in sight.

The AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF (MSOS) jumped 6.60% to $4.20 Monday on volume of roughly 10.73 million shares (stockanalysis.com, 4:00pm ET close), recovering more than Friday's 5.39% "AI reckoning" selloff as the broader U.S. equity market rebounded to open November. Every reliably-quoted name on this desk's watchlist finished higher: Curaleaf led with a 10.76% gain to $9.16, Glass House Brands rose 8.26% to $2.36, TerrAscend climbed 8.45% to $0.52, Trulieve added 7.98% to $11.10, Verano gained 5.19% to C$7.29 on Cboe Canada, Cresco Labs rose 4.78% to $0.6001, Aurora Cannabis added 3.94% to $4.22, Organigram gained 3.43% to $0.9651, and Green Thumb rose 2.39% to $6.42. (Vireo Growth, Planet 13, AYR Wellness and Jushi Holdings returned no reliably current quote this run and are omitted rather than estimated.)

Trulieve supplied the session's clearest company-specific catalyst. The company confirmed it has received federal DEA registrations covering several of its state-licensed medical-marijuana dispensaries — part of the first wave of registrations the Justice Department and DEA disclosed Friday, Oct. 30, covering more than 900 dispensaries across seven states (Alabama, Florida, Mississippi, South Dakota, Texas, Virginia and West Virginia) out of roughly 7,000 applications pending nationally. Trulieve operates medical-marijuana businesses in Florida, Georgia, Pennsylvania and West Virginia; only Florida and West Virginia fall inside Friday's seven-state wave, so Monday's confirmation covers a subset of its footprint, not the whole company. It is nonetheless the first name on this desk's watchlist to attach its own ticker to a registration, turning Friday's abstract national count into a concrete, company-specific data point — and the stock traded accordingly. (Cannabis Business Times; The Marijuana Herald; MarketBeat.)

Elsewhere, two capital-structure items advanced without a change-of-control deal behind either one. Cresco Labs disclosed today that shareholders approved, by at least 94% on every resolution, a restructuring that creates a new parent company ("TopCo") and banks — but does not yet execute — a potential redomicile of that entity from British Columbia to Delaware, alongside an extended sunset on its multiple-voting shares; the vote itself was held Friday, Oct. 30, with results only now disclosed. Separately, FLUENT Corp. — the Florida operator Vireo Growth is absorbing in an all-stock deal — agreed to convert roughly $5.7 million of senior secured debt into 310,194,363 shares at a deemed price of $0.0182 per share (11.8% of its post-settlement share count), pending Canadian Securities Exchange approval and timed to close immediately before the Vireo arrangement completes. (StockTitan; SEC EDGAR; Cannabis Business Times.)

Why it matters to PGP Monday is a reminder that rescheduling's economic benefit is arriving piecemeal and operator-by-operator, not as a single sector-wide switch — Trulieve can claim today's DEA news for only two of its four medical states, and PGP's diligence on any MSO's "rescheduling upside" should map state-by-state registration status rather than assume uniform benefit across a footprint. Separately, Cresco's TopCo vote and FLUENT's pre-close debt conversion are both capital-structure optionality plays, not closed transactions — the Delaware redomicile remains entirely at Cresco's board's discretion, and FLUENT's $0.0182 conversion price is a fresh, heavily-discounted data point for pricing distressed-seller equity immediately ahead of a roll-up absorption.

The Rescheduling Docket

A federal registration gets its first ticker-specific face

Friday's first-wave DEA registrations move from an abstract national count to a company-specific confirmation today, even as the broader Schedule I-to-III case before ALJ Julius stays formally stayed ahead of Nov. 5 reply briefs.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E.
  • Jul 15, 2026 Broader Schedule I→III hearings conclude before ALJ Derek Julius.
  • Sep 23, 2026 GAO publishes report GAO-26-108623, finding DEA's scheduling decisions aligned with HHS recommendations in all 84 cases reviewed.
  • Oct 20-22, 2026 ALJ Julius admits the GAO report but denies reopening for new testimony/discovery, setting the Oct. 29/Nov. 5 briefing schedule.
  • Oct 29, 2026 Supplemental briefs filed as scheduled: the DEA argues the GAO report deserves "no weight"; three groups file opposing briefs.
  • Oct 30, 2026 DOJ/DEA grant the first wave of federal registrations under the April Schedule III order — more than 900 state-licensed medical dispensaries across seven states (AL, FL, MS, SD, TX, VA, WV), out of roughly 7,000 applications pending nationally.
  • Nov 2, 2026 (today) Trulieve confirms it holds new DEA registrations covering dispensaries in two of its four medical states (FL, WV) — the first watchlist name to attach its own ticker to Friday's wave; shares rise 7.98%.
