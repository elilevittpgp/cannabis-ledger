Page One · The Big Story

Curaleaf Clears Every Antitrust Hurdle on Its Aurora Bid, Illinois Blinks on Its Hemp-THC Ban Two Days Before the Deadline, and Vireo Posts 177% Revenue Growth in Its First Post-FLUENT Quarter

Three live storylines collide on a Tuesday where the sector tape itself goes nearly nowhere, still digesting Monday's broad selloff rather than reacting to any one of today's headlines.

Curaleaf confirmed Tuesday it has received all required competition-law clearances — Canada's Competition Bureau issued a no-action letter Nov. 2 and Germany's Federal Cartel Office cleared the deal Nov. 3 — for its enhanced hostile tender for Aurora Cannabis (US$1.00 cash plus 0.4013 CURLF shares per Aurora share), which remains open, unchanged, through its Dec. 4, 11:59pm MT deadline. The clearance removes the last regulatory contingency from Curaleaf's offer even as Aurora's board continues to recommend a rival, friendly deal: Organigram Global's board-approved, Nov. 3-signed plan of arrangement (C$7.80 cash, or 5.8626 Organigram shares, or a blended C$3.75 cash plus 3.0431 shares per Aurora share, valuing Aurora at roughly C$561 million with more than $50 million in claimed synergies), which carries a Dec. 23 shareholder vote, a Nov. 18 record date, and a targeted close in January 2027.

Separately, Illinois and the plaintiffs in its own hemp-THC fight — the Illinois Healthy Alternatives Association, representing roughly 250 member businesses, plus five named individual businesses — filed a joint agreement in Chicago federal court pausing enforcement of the state's Nov. 12 ban against those litigants while their TRO/preliminary-injunction motion remains pending. Oral arguments on that motion are now set for Nov. 19; the ban still nominally takes effect Thursday for every hemp retailer not party to the suit. And Vireo Growth, reporting its first quarter since closing the FLUENT Corp. acquisition, posted Q3 2026 revenue of $253.7 million, up 177% year-over-year and roughly $23.6 million ahead of consensus, on an annualized run-rate now exceeding $1 billion; adjusted EBITDA rose 68.1% to $42.7 million and the company ended the quarter with $106.8 million in cash and launched a new share buyback — against a GAAP EPS miss of -$0.49. Elsewhere, 15 Democratic governors led by Maryland's Wes Moore, chair of the National Governors Association, sent Congress a letter urging passage of the SAFE Banking Act (S. 4942 / H.R. 9471), and two Texas House Democrats filed bills proposing statewide adult-use legalization for the 2027 session. Through all of it, MSOS traded essentially flat on the day near $4.06-4.07 — still down roughly 9-10% from a week ago and up about 3% month-to-date — suggesting the market is treating today's news as incremental rather than a fresh catalyst.

Why it matters to PGP Curaleaf clearing antitrust in both Canada and Germany converts the Aurora fight from a regulatory-timing question into a pure price-and-governance contest with a hard Dec. 4 deadline — PGP should now weight the probability Curaleaf files amended tender terms more heavily than the probability it simply lapses. Vireo's 177% revenue print is the clearest live comp for any client pursuing a similar debt-funded roll-up: scale compounds fast once a consolidator starts closing, but the GAAP EPS miss underneath it is a reminder that top-line growth and bottom-line integration costs are not converging yet. And Illinois's litigation-driven carve-out for 250 businesses — days before its ban's effective date — is a template worth flagging to any portfolio company with multi-state hemp-beverage exposure ahead of the federal Dec. 11 deadline: a well-organized trade association can buy real time through the courts even when legislative relief has gone nowhere.

The Rescheduling Docket

Illinois buys litigants two more weeks on its hemp ban, Missouri sharpens its enforcement rhetoric ahead of Thursday, and a 15-governor coalition presses Congress on SAFE Banking

No new ALJ action on the DEA's Schedule I-to-III record, but a cluster of state and federal-adjacent developments moves the broader hemp and banking fights forward.

  • Nov 3, 2026 DEA Administrator Terrance Cole signs the agency's draft 2027 production-quota notice, confirmed in follow-on reporting this week: Schedule I marijuana quotas held flat (6,675 kg cannabis / 1,000 kg extract, matching 2026), with a separate Federal Register notice confirmed as in development to set Schedule III marijuana/extract quotas — still no publication date.
  • Nov 8, 2026 Missouri Attorney General Catherine Hanaway escalates her public rhetoric ahead of the state's THC-potency law taking fuller effect Nov. 12, calling the new law "stronger and easier for us to enforce" and citing undercover buys showing THC levels more than 10 times the legal hemp limit; a federal court separately rejected an industry constitutional challenge to the law as premature.
  • Nov 9, 2026 A coalition of 15 Democratic governors, led by Maryland's Wes Moore as National Governors Association chair, sends Congress a letter urging passage of the SAFE Banking Act (S. 4942 / H.R. 9471), citing cash-robbery and safety risks to state-legal cannabis businesses; both bills remain pending in committee with no hearing or vote scheduled.
  • Nov 9-10, 2026 Texas Reps. Joe Moody and John Bucy III file HB 369 and HB 362, the "Texas Regulation of Cannabis Act," proposing statewide adult-use legalization (a 15% cultivator-to-dispensary tax and 20% retail tax) for the 2027 legislative session — a bill, not a vote; no hearing scheduled.
  • Nov 9-10, 2026 Illinois and the plaintiffs in its hemp-THC fight — the Illinois Healthy Alternatives Association (~250 member businesses) plus five named businesses — file a joint agreement in N.D. Ill. federal court pausing enforcement of the state's Nov. 12 ban against those litigants while their TRO/preliminary-injunction motion remains pending. Oral arguments on the injunction are now set for Nov. 19; the ban still nominally takes effect Thursday for every business not party to the suit.
