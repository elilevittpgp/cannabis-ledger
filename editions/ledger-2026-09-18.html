Page One · The Big Story

TerrAscend Answers the DOJ's 280E Suit — With a Motion to Dismiss, Not a Check

A day ahead of today's response deadline, TerrAscend argues the government sued the wrong entity, in the wrong court, without the congressional review the law requires — the first real test of the industry's first federal 280E clawback case.

TerrAscend used the day it had left. With its response in the DOJ's federal 280E refund-clawback suit due today, Sept 18, TerrAscend instead filed a 38-page motion to dismiss on Sept 17 in U.S. District Court for the District of New Jersey. The motion doesn't contest whether the underlying $8.36M refund was properly owed — it argues the case can't proceed at all: the government sued "TerrAscend USA, Inc. & Subsidiaries," a consolidated tax-return label rather than a real legal entity; New Jersey lacks personal jurisdiction over a Delaware-organized company that operates from Florida, Ohio and Connecticut; venue is improper because the return was filed from California and the amended return was mailed to the IRS in Utah; service was defective and delayed more than 40 days after TerrAscend's registered-agent information was available; and, most substantively, the government allegedly never routed the refund through the Joint Committee on Taxation review the law requires before a refund of this size can be issued. A briefing schedule is now set: the government's response is due Oct 5, TerrAscend's reply Oct 13, with a motion date of Oct 19.

Elsewhere, the sector's other running story stayed in place: Curaleaf's hostile bid for Aurora Cannabis produced no new statement from either side following Wednesday's live shareholder Q&A, and the Alberta Securities Commission still hasn't set a hearing date on Curaleaf's Sept 14 application to halt Aurora's ATM program. Vireo Growth issued 462,963 subordinate voting shares under a confidential settlement with Altmore Debt I, LP, extinguishing secured obligations and liens Altmore held against the company. Ascend, Verano and Premier Group/PCF all stayed quiet on new filings — though a re-check of Verano's balance sheet turns up a correction to how this desk has been framing it (see the Verano card, below). MSOS pulled back roughly 3% intraday, led lower by a 5.7% drop in Trulieve.

Why it matters to PGP TerrAscend's dismissal motion is a template worth filing across the portfolio: wrong-entity, wrong-venue and missing-JCT-review arguments are procedural defenses, not substantive ones — a win keeps the underlying 280E-refund question undecided industry-wide, while a loss pushes the case toward the merits that actually determine how retroactive Schedule III-era refund claims get treated. Separately, this desk's own multi-week characterization of Verano's October 2026 debt maturity as an open, unaddressed risk turns out to be stale — it was resolved back in March. Diligence teams should re-verify capital-structure assumptions directly against the latest IR filings rather than carrying forward an earlier day's framing, including this one's.

The Rescheduling Docket

Still no ALJ decision — but the sector's sharpest live legal fight is now a tax case

TerrAscend's motion to dismiss becomes the most concrete near-term legal test in the sector, even as the DEA's rescheduling recommendation stays undated and a newly identified Supreme Court petition opens a second front on licensing.

  • Aug 17, 2026 Post-hearing briefs filed with the DEA following the July 15 close of ALJ hearings on full Schedule I→III rescheduling.
  • Aug 18, 2026 A bipartisan House group (Titus, Joyce, Omar, Steube) sends a letter to the administration seeking answers on rescheduling mechanics by Sept 30.
  • Sep 8-9, 2026 GAO publishes GAO-26-107498 on cannabis banking access; the D.C. Circuit denies a stay motion against the DOJ's April 2026 Schedule III order.
  • Sep 14, 2026 Sens. Cortez Masto and Merkley reintroduce the SAFE Banking Act with a seven-member bipartisan cosponsor list.
  • Sep 17, 2026 Curaleaf CEO Boris Jordan holds a live shareholder Q&A in the Aurora bid fight; TerrAscend files a motion to dismiss the DOJ's federal 280E clawback suit rather than answering it on the merits.
  • Sep 18, 2026 No ALJ recommended decision yet — over nine weeks past the hearing close. Separately, a Supreme Court petition on cannabis-licensing residency preferences now has a name: Peridot Tree WA, Inc. v. Washington State Liquor and Cannabis Control Board, filed by Michigan resident Kenneth Gay, arguing a circuit split between the Ninth Circuit (which found the dormant Commerce Clause inapplicable to cannabis licensing) and the First and Second Circuits (which found residency preferences likely unconstitutional).

What's Next on the Docket

  1. ALJ Julius's recommended decision on full Schedule I→III rescheduling — still no fixed date.
  2. Oct 5 / 13 / 19, 2026 — briefing schedule on TerrAscend's motion to dismiss the DOJ's 280E clawback suit.
  3. Sep 24 / Oct 6, 2026 — scheduled Advanced Flower Capital foreclosure auctions of Justice Grown's PA and NJ collateral.
  4. Sep 30, 2026 — deadline for the administration to answer lawmakers' rescheduling-mechanics letter.
  5. Nov 12, 2026 — converted/synthetic cannabinoids (delta-8, HHC, THC-O) lose federal hemp status; no extension applies.
  6. Dec 1, 2026 — Curaleaf's bid for Aurora Cannabis expires unless extended.
  7. Dec 11, 2026 — the enacted effective date for naturally-occurring hemp cannabinoids, under the 0.4mg-total-THC-per-container standard.
PGP takeaway A second Supreme Court petition on cannabis-licensing residency preferences means the sector now has two live, undated legal fronts running in parallel — underwrite deal timelines assuming both stay open through year-end. And a residency-preference ruling in Peridot Tree would reach every state's social-equity licensing framework, not just cannabis, so track it independently of the Schedule III fight.
