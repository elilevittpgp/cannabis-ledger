Page One · The Big Story

Curaleaf Sweetens Its Hostile Bid for Aurora Cannabis 25% to $5.00 a Share — While the Sector Tape Keeps Grinding Lower

The improved offer lifts the implied premium to roughly 86% and pushes the deposit deadline to Dec. 4, but Aurora's board has not yet responded to the new terms as of this edition; MSOS closes Monday at $4.39, extending the sector's slide to roughly 15% on the week with the DEA's Oct. 13 response deadline on the rescheduling stay still the only hard catalyst on the calendar.

Curaleaf said Monday it intends to file a Notice of Variation and Change raising its unsolicited offer for Aurora Cannabis to an implied US$5.00 per share — 0.4013 Curaleaf subordinate voting shares plus US$1.00 in cash, up from the original US$4.00 offer (0.3463 shares plus US$0.75 cash) launched in August. The cash component rises 33%, to roughly 20% of the total consideration mix. Curaleaf also extended the bid's minimum deposit period, previously set to expire at 5pm Mountain Time on Dec. 1, to 11:59pm MT on Dec. 4, and said it will file a new Form F-80 registration statement with the SEC alongside the Canadian variation notice. Aurora's board — which unanimously urged shareholders in August to reject the original bid as an undervaluation of the company's cash position and EU-GMP platform — had issued no public statement on the enhanced terms as of this edition.

The improved bid lands with the Alberta Securities Commission still sitting on dueling applications: Curaleaf's bid to halt further issuances under Aurora's at-the-market equity program, and Aurora's counter-complaint alleging disclosure deficiencies in Curaleaf's bid circular. Neither application has a hearing date. Separately, TerrAscend's federal 280E refund-clawback case hit its own deadline today — the government's response to TerrAscend's motion to dismiss the DOJ's $8.36M suit was due Oct. 5, with no filing found in the docket as of this edition; TerrAscend's reply is due Oct. 13, with an Oct. 19 motion date.

Why it matters to PGP A 25% bid increase that still can't produce a target-board response — or an ASC hearing date — in six weeks is a timeline signal, not just a price signal: contested Canadian take-over bids with a live disclosure dispute can run well past the economics getting "fixed." Any PGP mandate advising a target board facing a sweetened hostile bid should model the regulatory-fight clock as the binding constraint, not the headline premium.

The Rescheduling Docket

Still stayed, still waiting on Oct 13

No DEA filing or ALJ ruling has surfaced since the Sept 29 stay; the tape keeps drifting lower in the absence of a fresh catalyst, and the Senate's banking-reform vehicle remains untouched in committee.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E.
  • Jul 15, 2026 Broader Schedule I→III hearings conclude; ALJ Derek Julius later orders transcript corrections.
  • Sep 23, 2026 GAO report finds DEA and FDA lack adequate written drug-scheduling procedures.
  • Sep 28-29, 2026 Three rescheduling opponents move to add the GAO report to the record; ALJ Julius stays the case and gives the DEA until Oct 13 to respond.
  • Oct 2-5, 2026 No DEA filing or ALJ ruling found; MSOS extends its slide to $4.39, down roughly 14.9% on the week with no new sector catalyst identified.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. Oct 13, 2026 — DEA's response to the motion to reopen the record is due.
  2. ALJ Julius rules on whether to admit the GAO report and permit added briefing.
  3. If granted, a further briefing round precedes the recommended decision; if denied, the case resumes toward a decision on the existing record.
  4. A 20-day exceptions window, then the DEA Administrator's final call — sell-side analysts (Cantor Fitzgerald) have modeled 1H 2027 as the more likely resolution window.
  5. Separately: the SAFE Banking Act of 2026 (S.4942) remains referred to the Senate Banking Committee with no markup or floor vote scheduled.
PGP takeaway A full week of continued drift — rather than stabilization — since the stay argues the market is still pricing the Oct 13 deadline, not a near-term resolution, as the operative catalyst. Models keyed to a 280E resolution should keep running on a 1H-2H 2027 base case; models keyed to banking reform should not assume S.4942 moves before a markup is actually scheduled.
