Page One · The Big Story

Sector Rallies a Second Straight Session to a Seven-Week High as Glass House Catches a Bid — and the DEA's Own Deadline Day Arrives Quietly

MSOS climbs another 5.26% to $4.20, building on Tuesday's catalyst-free rebound, while Glass House Brands bounces nearly 3% off its three-session, ~27% rout; the DEA's self-set Oct. 21 deadline to respond on rescheduling-hearing discovery arrives with no filing yet confirmed in press coverage, and Curaleaf's fight for Aurora Cannabis keeps simmering with BAT-backed Organigram still circling.

MSOS traded near $4.20 Wednesday afternoon, up 5.26% intraday on volume of roughly 4.15 million shares — its highest level in about seven weeks and a second consecutive broad-based advance building on Tuesday's catalyst-free 4.33% move. The rally was again unusually broad: Green Thumb (+6.28%), Verano (+5.95%), Curaleaf (+5.22%), TerrAscend (+5.04%), Cresco Labs (+4.96%) and Trulieve (+4.63%) all firmer, with Jushi Holdings (+3.32%) and even beaten-down Glass House Brands (+2.99%) participating. Only Planet 13 (-0.85%) sat out the move among names with clean data this run; AYR Wellness continues to print a stale, sub-penny quote consistent with its ongoing CCAA insolvency wind-down, so this desk again declines to report a figure for it.

Glass House Brands (NYSE: GLAS) found its footing Wednesday, adding 2.99% to $2.41 after a brutal three-session slide of roughly 27% triggered by Monday's soft preliminary Q3 print (average selling price falling to $150/lb from $211/lb despite record biomass production) and the unexplained resignation of director John "Jay" Nichols Jr. Canaccord Genuity's Tuesday cut to $8 from $15 was followed the same day by Lake Street trimming its own target to $7 from $17 — both houses effectively ratifying the sell-off rather than leading it. No securities-class-action investigation into the drop has been announced as of this writing, though plaintiffs' firms often lag a move of this size by days to weeks.

Today is also the DEA's own deadline: ALJ Derek Julius's Oct. 14 order gave the agency until today to file a response, capped at 10 pages, addressing rescheduling opponents' renewed bid to reopen the hearing record for new testimony and document discovery tied to the GAO's September report. As of this writing, no outlet has reported that the agency actually filed; the case remains formally stayed, and Julius has not yet ruled on whether to admit the GAO report itself. Separately, the Curaleaf-Aurora standoff keeps reverberating: Curaleaf's Oct. 19 SEC filing accused Aurora's board of a fiduciary breach for granting due-diligence access to unnamed alternative parties — understood to include BAT-backed Organigram Holdings — while continuing to deny Curaleaf the same access despite what Curaleaf calls the only binding, fully financed offer on the table ($5.00/share implied, $6.00 cash-election cap, an 86% premium to Aurora's unaffected 30-day VWAP). Aurora CEO Miguel Martin reiterated that shareholders "gain nothing by tendering now" given the Dec. 4 deadline; no new filing from either side specifically dated today was found.

Why it matters to PGP Two straight days of a broad, largely catalyst-free rally is itself a data point: it suggests generalized multiple expansion or short-covering across the complex rather than name-specific re-rating, which matters for how PGP should read any single name's move this week — including Glass House's bounce, which looks more like participation in the sector-wide advance than a reversal of its own fundamental story. On rescheduling, a deadline passing without a confirmed same-day filing is itself informative: it is the kind of procedural slippage that has characterized this docket for months, and clients modeling a near-term resolution should continue to discount the timeline accordingly. And the Curaleaf-Aurora fight's shift from "public rejection" to an alleged breach of fiduciary duty raises, not lowers, the stakes for any PGP client with secondary exposure to either name — a contested, increasingly litigious combination fight tends to extend timelines and widen the bid-ask on the ultimate outcome.

The Rescheduling Docket

Deadline day arrives quietly

ALJ Derek Julius's Oct 14 order gave the DEA until today, Oct 21, to file its response on rescheduling opponents' renewed bid for testimony and document discovery; no outlet has yet reported that the agency actually filed, and the case remains formally stayed.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E.
  • Jul 15, 2026 Broader Schedule I→III hearings conclude before ALJ Derek Julius.
  • Sep 23, 2026 GAO publishes report GAO-26-108623, finding DEA's scheduling decisions aligned with HHS recommendations in all 84 cases reviewed, but flagging a lack of written DEA/FDA procedural policy.
  • Sep 28-29, 2026 Opponents move to reopen the record to admit the GAO report; ALJ Julius stays the case and orders a DEA response by Oct 13.
  • Oct 14, 2026 ALJ Julius grants opponents a reply motion, reiterates the agency's Oct 21 deadline to address the testimony/discovery request, and states he has not yet ruled on admitting the GAO report; DEA has separately argued the report has "no bearing" on the scientific basis for Schedule III.
  • Oct 21, 2026 (today) The DEA's ≤10-page response is due before ALJ Julius. As of this writing, no outlet has reported an actual filing, and Julius's ruling on GAO-report admission remains reserved. Case stays formally stayed. No new state 280E/opt-out news found.
