Page One · The Big Story

Glass House's Post-Earnings Rally Extends to Roughly 29% as the Broader Cannabis Tape Sinks to a Fresh One-Week Low, TerrAscend Workers Vote to Unionize, and a Bipartisan Senate Duo Moves on the Dec. 11 Hemp Deadline

MSOS falls to $3.65, down 1.08% on the day and now roughly 10.3% below last Tuesday's level, as nearly every constituent sells off except Glass House Brands, which rides Monday's Q3 beat into a second straight day of double-digit gains; TerrAscend's Phillipsburg, N.J. dispensary workers vote to join UFCW Local 360, and Sens. Tim Sheehy and Amy Klobuchar introduce a bill to redefine hemp ahead of next month's federal deadline.

Glass House Brands kept climbing Tuesday, a day after its Q3 print and an explained-away auditor dispute sent the stock up roughly 31% after hours. Shares traded as high as $1.66, up about 28.7% versus Monday's $1.29 close, on volume near 9.6 million shares against a roughly 2.78 million average — the loudest single-name move on the tape for a second consecutive session. No separate press release or analyst note disclosing auditor MGO's completed Q3 review has surfaced yet; that remains, as this desk noted Monday, the harder confirmation point once it lands. The rest of the sector moved in the opposite direction. MSOS, the AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF, closed at $3.65, down 1.08% on the day on volume of about 4.85 million shares, ticking back up to roughly $3.68 after hours. That leaves the ETF down approximately 10.3% versus last Tuesday's level (Nov. 9's $4.07) — a deeper weekly hole than Monday's 9.3% comparison — and down about 7.4% month-to-date against Oct. 30's $3.94, also worse than Monday's 6.3% MTD reading. Aside from Glass House, essentially every constituent this desk tracks finished lower: Vireo Growth fell 5.35% to $5.95, Planet 13 fell 6.57% to $0.081, Trulieve fell 2.52% to $10.07 even as its preliminary Russell 2000/3000 index addition stands unchanged, Curaleaf fell 1.73% to $7.95, Verano fell 2.23% to $3.94, Organigram fell 2.36% to $0.7069, Aurora fell 1.95% to $4.52, Cresco Labs fell 2.68% to $0.472 (near its 52-week low), Green Thumb fell 0.83% to $5.96, TerrAscend fell 0.80% to $0.3849 and Jushi fell 2.48% to $0.4291. AYR Wellness remained effectively untraded and worthless in its ongoing CCAA wind-down.

TerrAscend drew a distinct, name-specific headline of its own: workers at its Apothecarium dispensary in Phillipsburg, N.J. voted Tuesday to join United Food and Commercial Workers Local 360, syndicated wire coverage confirmed — the latest instance of cannabis-retail labor organizing following Ascend Wellness's Teamsters contract in Illinois earlier this year. TerrAscend's standing $8.36 million 280E refund clawback suit against the Department of Justice remains otherwise unchanged, with the government's reply due around Nov. 19 and a motion-to-dismiss hearing set for Dec. 21. Elsewhere on the operating side, Curaleaf opened its 81st Florida dispensary, in Sebring, with first sales set for Wednesday — bringing its nationwide store count to 191 — a routine footprint addition rather than a market-moving event, and one that did not stop CURLF from trading lower on the day alongside the rest of the sector.

On the federal hemp side, the clock toward the Dec. 11 "total THC" deadline produced genuine new movement. Total Wine & More launched a public campaign Monday urging Congress to replace the pending outright ban on hemp-derived THC beverages with alcohol-style, three-tier regulation, citing survey data showing roughly 85% public opposition to a flat ban. Separately, Sens. Tim Sheehy (R-Mont.) and Amy Klobuchar (D-Minn.) introduced a bipartisan bill that would redefine hemp using a 1% total-THC-by-dry-weight threshold, impose age-21 purchase rules, and levy a 5-cent-per-milligram-of-THC beverage tax — an effort aimed at attaching to a must-pass year-end spending vehicle before the Dec. 11 cutoff, according to Marijuana Moment's report on the filing. Cannabis Business Times reported Tuesday that industry lobbyists still see a government funding bill as the most realistic vehicle to delay or kill the ban outright, rather than any standalone measure. Meanwhile in Missouri, local coverage (Missouri Independent, News Tribune) confirmed hemp retailers kept closing or converting through the week under the Intoxicating Cannabinoid Control Act's fuller effect — among them Slaphappy Hemporium, which rebranded, and South Point Hemp, which closed outright — with no new Attorney General lawsuits filed since last week's round of four. Ascend Wellness, Verano Holdings and Premier Group/PCF were all quiet on net-new developments inside today's window; details are in the Priority Coverage cards below.

Why it matters to PGP Glass House's second straight day of outsized gains against an otherwise broadly red tape is a useful reminder that idiosyncratic, company-specific catalysts can diverge sharply from sector beta — clients evaluating entry points on sector weakness should separate "the tape is down" from "this name has a specific reason to be up." On the hemp side, the Sheehy-Klobuchar bill and the spending-bill vehicle both point to Dec. 11 resolving via a legislative patch rather than a clean deadline — PGP should model a delay or redefinition as the base case, not a hard ban, while watching for the bill's actual text to assess tax and age-gating cost impact on any hemp-beverage portfolio exposure.

The Rescheduling Docket

A bipartisan Senate bill and a retailer-led campaign both target the Dec. 11 hemp deadline, as Missouri's closures continue and the DEA's ALJ record stays silent

No new movement on the stayed Schedule I-to-III record; this window's action is entirely on the federal and state hemp side, plus a fresh procedural detail on the already-effective medical-Schedule III track.

  • Nov 4, 2026 A federal judge in the Western District of Missouri rejects a constitutional and federal-preemption challenge to the state's Intoxicating Cannabinoid Control Act (HB 2641), clearing the way for continued AG enforcement against four named retailers.
  • Nov 12, 2026 Missouri's law reaches fuller effect, reclassifying hemp products over a 0.4mg THC threshold as marijuana sellable only through licensed dispensaries; hemp beverages with only naturally occurring cannabinoids get a temporary exemption through Dec. 11.
  • Nov 13-16, 2026 Missouri retailers keep closing or converting — Missouri Independent and News Tribune report Slaphappy Hemporium rebranding and South Point Hemp closing outright — with no new AG lawsuits filed since last week's round of four.
  • Nov 16, 2026 Total Wine & More launches a public campaign urging Congress to regulate hemp-THC beverages like alcohol (three-tier system) rather than ban them outright, citing survey data showing roughly 85% public opposition to a flat ban.
  • Nov 17, 2026 Sens. Tim Sheehy (R-Mont.) and Amy Klobuchar (D-Minn.) introduce a bipartisan bill redefining hemp at a 1% total-THC dry-weight threshold with age-21 rules and a 5-cent-per-mg-THC beverage tax, aimed at a must-pass year-end spending vehicle ahead of Dec. 11; Cannabis Business Times separately reports industry lobbyists still see a government funding bill as the likeliest vehicle to delay or kill the ban.
  • Nov 17, 2026 TerrAscend's Apothecarium dispensary workers in Phillipsburg, N.J. vote to join UFCW Local 360 — the latest cannabis-retail labor organizing win following Ascend's Teamsters contract earlier this year. No DEA ALJ recommended decision yet on the Schedule I-to-III record.
