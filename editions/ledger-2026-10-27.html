Page One · The Big Story

The Sector Tape Rallies a Second Straight Session as Glass House Rips 10.75% Despite Two Fresh Downgrades — Curaleaf's Boris Jordan Fires Back at the ASC, and Ascend Quietly Pushes Its Q3 Call a Week Later, to Nov. 12

MSOS traded near $4.07 Tuesday afternoon, up roughly 2.5% from Monday's $3.97 close and the fund's second consecutive gain; Glass House Brands led all names with a 10.75% rebound despite a Saturday "Strong Sell" downgrade; Curaleaf chairman Boris Jordan publicly rebutted Monday's Alberta Securities Commission ruling against his company; Canopy Growth disclosed its reconvened-AGM vote tally; and Ascend Wellness moved its newly confirmed Q3 earnings date back a full week, onto the same day as the first federal hemp-THC ban deadline.

The sector tape extended Monday's rebound into a second session Tuesday. MSOS traded near $4.07 by mid-afternoon (stockanalysis.com, roughly 2:45-3:12pm ET), up between 2.52% and 2.64% depending on the source checked from Monday's close of $3.97, putting the fund modestly positive on the week (+2.13%, Yahoo Finance) even as it remains down a steep 21.03% month-to-date. The move was led by a sharp reversal in Glass House Brands, up 10.75% to $2.37 — despite Wall Street Zen's Saturday downgrade to "Strong Sell" and Lake Street's Oct. 20 price-target cut to $7 — with no new company disclosure found this run to explain the bounce.

Aurora Cannabis added a further 5.90% to $4.13, extending Monday's gain, as Curaleaf chairman and CEO Boris Jordan escalated the hostile-bid standoff with a same-day public rebuttal to Monday's Alberta Securities Commission ruling, which dismissed Curaleaf's application to halt Aurora's at-the-market share program. Jordan called the ruling "irrelevant to the fundamental debate" and asked why Aurora sold ATM shares below its own implied $5.00 offer price once a bid was imminent. The rhetoric did not help Curaleaf's own stock, which gave back 1.3% of Monday's gain to roughly $8.01 on corroborated reads (one outlier quote of $8.55 on only 10,287 shares of volume is treated as unreliable and excluded). No Organigram terms have been filed by either side, and Curaleaf's enhanced bid — 0.4013 Curaleaf shares plus up to $1 cash, a $6 cash cap, implying roughly $5.00/share — remains open to its Dec. 4 tender deadline.

Elsewhere, Ascend Wellness Holdings quietly moved its own calendar: a Tuesday StockTitan filing confirms the company's Q3 2026 earnings call is now set for Thursday, Nov. 12, 2026, at 5:00pm ET — a full week later than the Nov. 5 date this desk reported as newly confirmed just yesterday, with no reason disclosed for the change. The new date happens to land on the same day as the first of the two federal hemp-THC ban deadlines (the cap on converted cannabinoids such as Delta-8 and THC-P). Ascend's reverse-split ratio remains unset 60 days after its Aug. 28 approval. Separately, Canopy Growth formally disclosed the results of Monday's reconvened annual meeting: 154,837,897 shares, 36.60% of those outstanding, were voted, and shareholders approved all resolutions, including board authority to execute a share consolidation at a ratio between 1-for-5 and 1-for-15 within 12 months — the specific ratio and implementation date remain unset.

Why it matters to PGP Today's rally is broad but still says little about any single catalyst — Glass House's double-digit bounce arrived against, not because of, fresh analyst skepticism, and should not be read as a fundamental turn absent a new disclosure. The Aurora standoff remains a war of words, not terms: PGP should keep modeling both the Curaleaf tender and a possible Organigram counter as live. And Ascend's second earnings-date change in two days is a reminder to re-verify calendar-dependent client scheduling right up to the day of any call this desk is asked to staff.
