No. 1 · Market Shock · Whipsaw Into Litigation Relief

MSOS swings from a fresh $3.11 low to a 10.8% weekly gain as a historic, cannabis-unrelated market whipsaw and a targeted D.C. Circuit stay combine to spark a two-session, nearly 12% rebound

MSOS closed Friday, Nov. 27 at $3.48 on roughly 4.31 million shares traded in a shortened post-Thanksgiving session — up 10.8% from last Friday's (Nov. 20) $3.14 close, but still down about 11.7% versus the Oct. 30 month-to-date baseline of $3.94. The week's path was anything but smooth: Monday's 4.14% bounce to $3.27 (led by TerrAscend, Jushi and Verano, all up double digits off depressed bases) was fully erased Tuesday, when the ETF fell 4.89% to a fresh closing low of $3.11 as nearly every constituent closed red and 14 Senate Republicans led by Tom Cotton and Ted Budd sent President Trump a letter warning that White House-backed hemp-THC carve-out language could "threaten the entire" year-end funding bill. The real pivot came Wednesday, Nov. 25, when a historic Wall Street round trip — the S&P 500 plunged as much as 3.2% intraday on fresh tariff and inflation fears (October PCE inflation hit 3.3% year-over-year, the hottest since November 2023) before closing down just 0.3%, only the ninth such intraday-plunge-to-flat-close session since 1980 — triggered an aggressive, uneven short-covering rally in cannabis small-caps: Glass House Brands (+14.81%), Planet 13 (+10.61%), Jushi (+10.11%) and Cresco Labs (+9.49%) led by a wide margin, while Trulieve (+0.23%) and Ascend Wellness (+0.50%) barely moved — a sign the bounce was concentrated in the most distressed, heavily shorted names rather than a fundamental re-rating. Markets were closed Thursday for Thanksgiving, and Friday's shortened session extended the rally a second day (+6.75% to $3.48 on roughly 4.31 million shares, a little over half Wednesday's volume), led this time by Verano (+9.86%), TerrAscend (+9–10%) and Cresco Labs (+8.90%), with no single fresh catalyst identified by trade press beyond lingering optimism from Tuesday night's D.C. Circuit ruling.

Why it matters to PGP Treat this week's 10.8% MSOS gain as a market-structure and litigation-relief event, not a fundamentals story: a historic macro whipsaw landing on a sector crowded with short positions, layered with a narrow (not blanket) court stay, produced an outsized two-session bounce on holiday-thin volume. None of the real risk calendar moved in the industry's favor this week — the DEA's Nov. 29 deadline, Curaleaf's Dec. 4 tender deadline, Planet 13's Dec. 10 vote, the Dec. 11 hemp-THC cliff, and Aurora's Dec. 23 vote are all still live and, in most cases, no closer to resolution than seven days ago. PGP should not price any current-week financing or deal structure off this bounce, and should flag to clients that a nearly 12% two-day equity move on thin volume is not a reliable mark for stock-based consideration.
No. 2 · Regulatory · A Narrow Win, Not a Resolution

The D.C. Circuit grants a targeted stay of DEA's Nov. 29 medical-supply-chain deadline days before it would have hit — while a new coalition brief calls the underlying rescheduling itself "fatally flawed"

Three medical-marijuana operators — Standard Wellness (Utah), Verde Americano (Arizona) and NW Confections (Oklahoma), joined by additional petitioners — had asked the D.C. Circuit (Nos. 26-1324, consolidated with 26-1325 and 26-1329) to pause the DEA's Oct. 30 "Timely Applicant" guidance, which bars Schedule III medical-marijuana supply chains from transacting with state-licensed entities that are neither federal registrants nor "timely applicants," effective Nov. 29. North Dakota, Utah and West Virginia filed an amicus brief Nov. 23 backing the petitioners on state-regulatory-disruption grounds; DOJ filed opposition Nov. 24 calling the stay motions "without merit"; company reply briefs closed the briefing record that same day. On Wednesday, Nov. 25, the three-judge panel (Rao, Walker, Garcia) granted a targeted administrative stay — explicitly not a ruling on the merits — covering only "medical marijuana activities that are in conformity with a state-issued license and are with entities that are neither federal registrants nor Timely Applicants," the precise category the petitioners argued would otherwise be cut off days before the deadline. Separately and on a wholly distinct legal track, a coalition of anti-rescheduling groups filed a new brief the same day arguing the DOJ's Schedule III reclassification is itself unlawful and "fatally flawed" — opening a second front against rescheduling with DOJ's response not due until Jan. 13, 2027. No further court, DEA or ALJ action was reported through Friday, Nov. 27, or as of this edition Saturday, Nov. 28, with the underlying Nov. 29 deadline otherwise standing for every transacting category outside the stay's narrow carve-out.

Why it matters to PGP The stay reduces risk at the margin, not across the board: operators in state-licensed medical markets transacting with unregistered counterparties get breathing room into the new year, but the broader supply-chain guidance, the separate "fatally flawed" reclassification challenge (live through at least mid-January 2027), and the unresolved Dec. 11 hemp-THC cliff all remain independently open. Clients with medical-supply-chain exposure outside the stayed category should still model the Nov. 29 deadline as binding this weekend, and no client-facing diligence should treat rescheduling's legal durability as settled while an organized coalition is now directly attacking the DOJ's authority to act.
No. 3 · M&A · The Aurora Contest Sharpens

Curaleaf sweetens its hostile Aurora bid to $6.00/share and clears antitrust review in two jurisdictions, while Organigram formally fixes its own election terms ahead of the Dec. 23 vote

Curaleaf raised its tender for Aurora Cannabis to up to US$6.00 per share (US$1.00 cash plus 0.4013 Curaleaf shares) and extended its deadline three days to Dec. 4, 11:59pm MT, after securing antitrust clearance in both Canada and Germany — the clearest signal yet that Curaleaf intends to press the hostile bid to a shareholder decision rather than let it lapse unrevised. Aurora's board continues to unanimously recommend the rival, friendly Organigram arrangement, and on Thursday, Nov. 26, Organigram and Aurora formally filed their joint management information circular (via SEDAR+/EDGAR 6-K), converting prior "deal signed" status into bindable election mechanics: Aurora holders may elect C$7.80 in cash, 5.8626 Organigram shares, or a prorated blend of C$3.75 cash plus 3.0431 shares, ahead of a Dec. 21 proxy cutoff and the Dec. 23 special meeting. As of this edition, neither ISS nor Glass Lewis has issued a proxy-advisory recommendation on the Organigram arrangement — meaning Curaleaf's Dec. 4 tender deadline will likely arrive before the market has independent third-party guidance on how Aurora holders are likely to vote three weeks later. Elsewhere on the deal desk, Jushi Holdings paired its standing $6.5 million Illinois cultivation-facility purchase (announced Nov. 24) with disclosures of a $15–20 million expansion of its Manassas, VA mortgage and a five-year extension of its roughly $6.3 million Arlington, VA mortgage, while director Denis Arsenault disclosed a 101,000-share open-market purchase Nov. 24. Grown Rogue International separately signed definitive agreements Nov. 23 to acquire PharmaCann of New York's Hamptonburgh cultivation facility and four Verilife-branded dispensaries for $1.8 million cash — down from an originally announced $3.1 million in August 2026, the reduction tied to PharmaCann's 50% share of a pending New York cannabis-tax assessment currently under appeal; price reverts toward $3.0 million if that appeal succeeds before closing, which is subject to New York Office of Cannabis Management change-of-control approval with an outside date of March 30, 2027.

Why it matters to PGP The Aurora contest has moved from "two competing term sheets" to "one fully bindable election against one fixed cash cap," which gives any client advising either side of a competing-bid structure a real relative-value basis rather than a headline-only one — but the sequencing gap (Curaleaf's deadline landing three weeks before any proxy-advisor guidance on the rival deal) is itself a diligence point worth flagging. Separately, Grown Rogue's price cut on the PharmaCann New York deal is a useful live comp: buyers are successfully re-trading signed cannabis M&A lower when a liability (here, a tax assessment) surfaces between signing and close, a dynamic PGP should build into any letter of intent or purchase agreement it structures for clients on either side of a deal with contingent tax or regulatory exposure.
