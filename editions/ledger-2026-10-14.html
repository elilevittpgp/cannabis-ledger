Page One · The Big Story

The "Pivotal" Oct. 13 Deadline Wasn't: DEA Filed Four Days Early, the Judge Reserved Ruling, and the Case Grinds On to a New Oct. 21 Date

Monday's edition flagged Oct. 13 as the DEA's response deadline on the stayed rescheduling case — but the agency actually filed Oct. 9, opponents countered with their own expansive brief, and ALJ Derek Julius has reserved judgment rather than ruled. Separately, Glass House's unexplained slide rolls into a fourth session near 52-week lows, and Organigram is now confirmed — not just rumored — as a genuine rival suitor for Aurora Cannabis.

This desk is correcting its own framing from Monday's edition. The DEA's response to the motion to reopen the stayed Schedule I-to-III rescheduling record was never due to land on Oct. 13 as a dramatic single event — the agency actually filed its brief Oct. 9, four days ahead of ALJ Derek Julius's deadline. In it, DEA said it does not object to admitting the new GAO report (GAO-26-108623) into the record, but firmly opposed the rescheduling opponents' broader request for additional testimony and document discovery, arguing the report has "no bearing" on whether marijuana should move to Schedule III. The three opponent parties then filed their own counter-brief pressing to reopen testimony more broadly. Judge Julius has since stated on the record that he has not yet ruled on the GAO-report motion and is reserving judgment pending further responses — he has set a new Oct. 21 deadline for DEA to answer the opponents' discovery request. The stay continues; there is no recommended decision, no 20-day exceptions window, and no DEA Administrator final call in sight. A parallel D.C. Circuit appeal over April's Schedule III order now has a proposed briefing schedule stretching to February 2027.

Elsewhere, the sector tape found a tentative floor: MSOS traded at $4.05 intraday Wednesday, down a modest 1.22% on below-average volume — a far quieter session than Monday's broad 3.23% rout. The exception is Glass House Brands (NYSE: GLAS), down a further ~6% to roughly $3.61, a fourth consecutive losing session since Oct. 8's unexplained 15.6% plunge and now trading near its 52-week low of $3.57; analysts are belatedly pointing to withdrawn FY guidance, a Q2 revenue miss, an auditor transition, and the CFO's retirement as a cluster of overhangs rather than any single catalyst. On the Curaleaf-Aurora front, MJBizDaily has now directly confirmed Organigram Global has held acquisition talks with Aurora "in recent weeks" — though both companies still decline to comment on the record — and Aurora's formal Directors' Circular responding to Curaleaf's revised bid is now expected the week of Oct. 20.

Why it matters to PGP A "hard" regulatory deadline that turns out to be a filing date, not a ruling date, is a useful lesson for diligence: calendar risk around agency/court dockets should be modeled as a range of plausible process steps, not a single binary event, especially once a judge has shown a pattern (as here) of reserving judgment to invite more briefing. On the M&A side, trade-press confirmation of Organigram's talks — even without an on-the-record acknowledgment from either party — meaningfully raises the odds Aurora's Oct. 20 Directors' Circular addresses a genuine two-bidder contest rather than a single hostile process, which changes how PGP should model both the time-to-close and the clearing price on any Aurora-linked engagement.

The Rescheduling Docket

A filing, a counter-filing, and a reserved ruling

The "pivotal Oct 13 deadline" this desk flagged Monday produced procedure, not resolution — the record stays open, the judge is still deciding whether to decide, and the next hard date is Oct 21.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E.
  • Jul 15, 2026 Broader Schedule I→III hearings conclude before ALJ Derek Julius.
  • Sep 23, 2026 GAO publishes report GAO-26-108623, finding DEA's scheduling decisions aligned with HHS recommendations in all 84 cases reviewed, but flagging a lack of written DEA/FDA procedural policy — not a finding specific to cannabis.
  • Sep 28-29, 2026 Opponents move to reopen the record to admit the GAO report; ALJ Julius stays the case and orders a DEA response by Oct 13.
  • Oct 9, 2026 DEA files its response four days early: no objection to admitting the GAO report itself, but firm opposition to opponents' broader bid for new testimony and document discovery, which DEA calls unsupported by a report with "no bearing" on the Schedule III question.
  • Oct 9-13, 2026 Opponents (DUID Victim Voices, Dr. Kenneth Finn, National Drug and Alcohol Association) file a counter-brief pressing for the broader reopening.
  • Oct 13-14, 2026 ALJ Julius states he has not yet ruled on the GAO-report motion, reserves judgment pending more responses, and sets a new Oct 21 deadline for DEA to address the testimony/discovery request.

Industry Headlines

Bedrocan, the Netherlands' only government-contracted medical cannabis grower, is declared bankrupt

A Dutch court's Oct 7 ruling followed the government's termination of its supply contract; the Danish subsidiary separately entered restructuring Oct 1, threatening supply for roughly 7,000 Dutch medical patients, though the Canadian unit is unaffected. MJBizDaily · NL Times · Forbes

Boston Mayor Michelle Wu joins the fight to protect Massachusetts' legal cannabis market

Wu's endorsement of "No on 8" follows Gov. Healey's; a new MassINC/Commonwealth Beacon poll of ~8,000 likely voters puts opposition to the Nov 3 repeal measure at 61% versus 27% in favor. Cannabis Business Times · WBUR

Hello Farms asks the Sixth Circuit to rehear its reversed $31.8M Curaleaf verdict

A September ruling overturned the Michigan cultivator's jury award on grounds federal courts can't enforce cannabis supply contracts tied to federally illegal activity — a precedent with broad implications for MSO contract enforceability generally. The Marijuana Herald

US legal cannabis sales hit $2.52B in September, $21.6B year-to-date

The fresh monthly tracker figure puts 2026 sales up roughly 8% year-over-year, a steady macro backdrop against an otherwise volatile equity tape. Trade tracker data via Marijuana Moment

A federal judge grants a Jersey City dispensary a temporary stay in a labor-peace licensing fight

U.S. District Judge Zahid Quraishi's 14-day order blocks expiration of the store's municipal license pending further review of New Jersey's labor-peace-agreement mandate; about 20 jobs were at stake. The Marijuana Herald

MediPharm Labs and Tilray Medical expand international medical-cannabis footprints

MediPharm shipped its first CBD metered-dose inhaler to a Panama distributor, while Tilray Medical won Portuguese (INFARMED) approval for in-house EU-GMP lab testing at its Cantanhede facility — both incremental steps in the sector's export build-out. GlobeNewswire
