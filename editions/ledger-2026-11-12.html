Page One · The Big Story

Cannabis Tape Craters as a Broad Market Rout Drags MSOS Down 5.4% to $3.86, Glass House's Surprise Auditor Swap Sinks Its Stock 33%, and Ascend's Net Loss Widens to $28 Million With Its Reverse-Split Ratio Still Unset

Nearly every checked MSO sheds five to nine percent on a day this desk — and Business of Cannabis's own trading desk — ties to spillover from a broader, cannabis-unrelated Wall Street selloff rather than any single industry catalyst, even as Illinois's and the first federal hemp-THC deadlines take effect and Glass House's mid-review change of auditors proves the day's sharpest single-name shock.

MSOS fell 5.39% Thursday to $3.86 at the 4:00pm ET close, a move independently confirmed by both stockanalysis.com and Yahoo Finance against Wednesday's $4.08 close — the ETF's worst session since an identical $3.86 print on Oct. 30's unrelated "AI reckoning" selloff, and now down roughly 6.5% versus last Thursday. The breadth argues for a market-wide, not cannabis-specific, cause: of the names this desk checked, only Vireo Growth (+0.30%) and Planet 13 (+1.67%, off a sub-dime base) finished higher, while Cresco Labs (-7.64%), TerrAscend (-6.63%), Jushi (-5.67%), Green Thumb (-5.45%), Verano (-8.67%) and Curaleaf (-5.41%) led the complex lower with no distinct company news attached to most of the move. Business of Cannabis's trading desk separately flagged a sharp drop in ancillary name GrowGeneration (-16.56%, on a Q3 revenue miss) and a Needham price-target cut on Turning Point Brands (-6.37%) as further evidence of a broad, not cannabis-specific, risk-off day.

The day's one genuine single-name shock came from Glass House Brands, whose stock cratered 33.16% to $1.25 after it disclosed a surprise auditor change: BDO USA resigned at the company's request after Glass House concluded BDO could not finish reviewing its Q3 numbers — specifically the accounting for a deconsolidation of its dual-use and medical-cannabis businesses ahead of a planned NYSE listing — in time for the Nov. 16 reporting deadline. BDO disagreed, characterizing the unresolved review as a reportable "unresolved issue" under Canadian securities rules; Glass House disputes that characterization and has reappointed Macias Gini & O'Connell (MGO) — its auditor until a September handoff to BDO — saying MGO already reviewed the same deconsolidation accounting in Q2 without flagging concerns.

Ascend Wellness reported Q3 2026 results this morning ahead of tonight's 5pm ET call: net revenue of $130.0 million, up 4.2% year-over-year and 3.1% sequentially, with adjusted EBITDA of $31.1 million (23.9% margin, an 80-basis-point sequential improvement) on a richer retail sales mix. Net loss nonetheless widened to $28.0 million from $9.8 million in Q2 — the release does not itemize the swing beyond standard operating costs and interest. The company held $63.6 million in cash against $260.4 million in net debt, generated $5.4 million of operating cash flow after a $19.1 million semiannual interest payment, and now operates 61 stores including partner-owned locations, already past its year-end target of 60. The board still has not set a ratio on the 1-for-10-to-1-for-50 reverse split shareholders approved Aug. 28 — 76 days and counting, with the release again tying it only to "a potential listing on a major U.S. exchange." Shares fell 4.29% to $0.3503, roughly in line with the sector's broader slide. Verano and Premier Group/PCF were otherwise quiet on operations (see Verano's new dispensary, below); no Teamsters Local 916 or Illinois-specific commentary appeared in Ascend's release.

Separately, two hemp-THC deadlines flagged in prior editions arrived today, Nov. 12: Illinois's ban on hemp-derived intoxicating products took effect for every business except the roughly 250-member Illinois Healthy Alternatives Association coalition, which remains under a litigation-driven enforcement delay running to a Nov. 19 TRO hearing; and the first federal hemp rule deadline stripped hemp status from synthetic "unnatural" cannabinoids such as THC-O and HHC — a narrower cut than the Dec. 11 "total THC" deadline still to come, which will separately cover delta-8, delta-10, THCA flower and ordinary hemp-derived delta-9. Missouri's own new THC-potency restrictions also reached fuller effect today, reclassifying many intoxicating hemp products as marijuana sellable only through licensed dispensaries; local press quoted hemp retailers saying the rule is forcing them to close.

Why it matters to PGP A broad, catalyst-light selloff of this size is itself a useful data point: it argues the group is trading more on macro risk appetite than on cannabis fundamentals right now, which should temper any read-through from today's price action into company-specific valuation work. Glass House's auditor episode is the sharper signal — a disputed "reportable event" tied to deconsolidation accounting, surfacing days before a reporting deadline, is exactly the kind of governance flag PGP should probe directly with management (not take at face value from either side's framing) before relying on Glass House's numbers in any comp set or financing discussion this quarter.

The Rescheduling Docket

Illinois's and the first federal hemp-THC deadlines take effect, Missouri's potency law reaches fuller force, and the DEA's ALJ record still awaits a recommendation

No new action on the stayed Schedule I-to-III record itself, but three separate state/federal hemp deadlines converge on the same day.

  • Nov 9, 2026 16 Democratic governors, led by Maryland's Wes Moore as National Governors Association chair, send Congress a letter urging passage of the SAFE Banking Act; both chambers' bills remain pending in committee with no hearing scheduled. No update today.
  • Nov 10, 2026 The DEA's draft 2027 aggregate production-quota notice, holding Schedule I marijuana quotas flat, is published in the Federal Register; a separate Schedule III quota notice remains in development.
  • Nov 12, 2026 Illinois's ban on hemp-derived intoxicating products takes effect for every business outside the ~250-member Illinois Healthy Alternatives Association coalition, which remains under a litigation delay pending a Nov. 19 TRO hearing.
  • Nov 12, 2026 The first federal hemp rule deadline arrives, stripping hemp status from synthetic "unnatural" cannabinoids (THC-O, HHC and similar); natural cannabinoids including delta-8, delta-10 and THCA flower remain governed by the Dec. 11 "total THC" deadline, unchanged.
  • Nov 12, 2026 Missouri's own THC-potency restrictions reach fuller effect, reclassifying many intoxicating hemp products as marijuana sellable only through licensed dispensaries; local press reports hemp retailers citing the rule as a reason for closing.

PGP takeaway Today's split hemp-rule effective dates — Illinois and the first federal "synthetic cannabinoid" cut both landing Nov. 12, while the broader federal "total THC" test waits until Dec. 11 — mean two legally distinct product categories (synthetic cannabinoids vs. natural hemp-derived THC) now face different compliance clocks; any client with hemp-adjacent exposure needs a category-by-category read, not a single "hemp ban" date, to avoid misjudging which products are already restricted.
