Ascend Clears Its Reverse Split as Three Deadlines Converge on Cannabis This Week

Shareholders approved the split that clears Ascend's path to a U.S. listing over the weekend; the House returns today to set up a Tuesday floor fight over the Dec. 11 hemp-ban delay, and Aurora's verdict on Curaleaf's hostile bid is due within days.

Ascend Wellness Holdings' cliffhanger from Friday has an answer. Stockholders at the company's Aug. 28 special meeting approved authorizing the board to execute a reverse stock split of Class A common stock at a ratio between 1-for-10 and 1-for-50 — "a necessary step on our path to a listing on a major U.S. exchange," in CEO Sam Brill's words. The authorization runs until Ascend actually lists on Nasdaq or NYSE American, or one year from the meeting, whichever comes first; the company was explicit that approval does not guarantee an uplisting will follow. AAWH shares, which closed Friday at $0.3962 after diverging from a broad sector rally, changed hands around $0.42 Monday morning, up roughly 2.4% intraday.

The rest of the desk's calendar for the week is now visible. The House returns from a month-long recess today and GOP leadership is aiming to bring the Senate-passed government-funding measure — which carries the delay of the federal hemp-THC ban from Nov. 12 to Dec. 11 — to the floor Tuesday under a suspension of the rules, requiring a two-thirds majority. House Agriculture Appropriations Subcommittee chair Rep. Andy Harris (R-Md.) has signaled he will fight to strip the delay, though reporting characterizes his ability to actually block it as limited. Separately, Aurora Cannabis's Directors' Circular responding to Curaleaf's hostile take-over bid is due within 15 days of the Aug. 18 bid launch — putting a formal board recommendation around Sept. 2–3, with no new statement from either company since their dueling Aug. 24 releases. The cannabis tape opened the week giving back some of Friday's gain: MSOS traded around $4.90 in early Monday action, down roughly 1.8% from Friday's $4.99 close.

Why it matters to PGP None of this week's three open items is fully resolved by today's news. Ascend's shareholder approval is a prerequisite, not a listing — the ratio, timing, and whether an uplisting follows are all still board discretion. The House vote is a two-thirds procedural hurdle, not a formality, on a delay the sector has been pricing as settled. And Aurora's board recommendation, whenever it lands, will be the first hard data point on whether Curaleaf's bid has any real path to 50%-plus tender. Treat all three as live diligence items this week, not closed ones.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. Sept 1, 2026 — targeted House floor vote (suspension of the rules, two-thirds needed) on the Dec 11 hemp-ban delay.
  2. ALJ Julius's recommended decision on broad rescheduling — still expected in "weeks or months," not on a fixed date.
  3. ~Sept 2-3, 2026 — Aurora's Directors' Circular on the Curaleaf bid is due.
  4. Sept 30, 2026 — deadline for the administration to answer lawmakers' rescheduling-implementation letter; also the federal funding deadline tied to the hemp-ban fight.
  5. Dec 1, 2026 — Curaleaf's bid for Aurora Cannabis expires unless extended.

PGP takeaway A two-thirds suspension vote is a real hurdle, not a formality — model both a clean pass and a scenario where the delay gets stripped or delayed further, which would move the hemp-THC cliff materially closer for beverage and smokable-hemp exposure. The ALJ's recommendation remains the larger, slower-moving catalyst; don't let this week's House vote crowd it out of the diligence calendar.

Industry Headlines

Curaleaf opens its 76th Florida store as the Aurora bid fight enters its decisive week

A new Cutler Bay, Miami-Dade dispensary — the company's eighth in the county — opens Sept 1, bringing Curaleaf to 177 stores nationwide even as its board waits on Aurora's response to its hostile bid. PR Newswire

Aurora's international pivot faces its highest-stakes test yet

Aurora's Q1 FY2027 results (reported Aug 5) showed international medical cannabis, driven by Germany, at 64% of net revenue ($67.6M total, -9% YoY) — the case Aurora's board must now weigh against Curaleaf's takeover math. Company results, various transcripts

Gallup: cannabis use now outpaces cigarette smoking nationally

The pollster's July Consumption Habits survey found 17% of Americans use marijuana or edibles versus 11% who smoke cigarettes — more than double cannabis's 2013 baseline reading. Gallup, via Mondaq's Week in Weed

Missouri's cannabis workers gain collective-bargaining rights

A new state law guaranteeing bargaining rights across cultivation, processing, manufacturing, distribution and retail roles took effect Aug 28 — a labor-relations marker as unionization spreads through the industry. State law, effective Aug 28

California carves out CBN isolate from its cannabis-concentrate definition

AB 2250, passed unanimously (Senate 38-0, Assembly 78-0), excludes CBN isolate above 99% purity from cannabis-concentrate rules starting Jan 1, 2028 — a narrow but unanimous hemp/cannabinoid line-drawing exercise.

Rhode Island opens the field for 24 new retail licenses

Applications are open through Nov 23 across six geographic zones, with social-equity and worker-cooperative set-asides — the state's first major license expansion since its adult-use launch. State regulatory filing
