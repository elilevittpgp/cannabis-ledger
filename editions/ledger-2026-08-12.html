Curaleaf Goes Hostile: A $272 Million Unsolicited Bid for Aurora Cannabis

The largest U.S. MSO by revenue is trying to force its way into a Canadian licensed producer after two unanswered outreach letters — sending Aurora shares up more than 20% and MSOS to its best session in weeks.

Curaleaf Holdings said Aug. 11 it intends to launch an unsolicited take-over bid for Aurora Cannabis (NASDAQ/TSX: ACB), offering roughly $4.00 per share — 0.3463 Curaleaf shares plus $0.75 in cash — for a total outlay of about $272 million and a roughly 45% premium to Aurora's 30-day volume-weighted average price. Curaleaf said it sent Aurora's board letters on June 23 and July 7 proposing a combination that went unanswered, prompting the public approach. Aurora responded the same day disputing Curaleaf's account of the prior outreach and said its board has formed a special committee to evaluate the proposal — stopping short of an outright rejection. Aurora shares jumped roughly 21–22% to around $3.48–3.50; Curaleaf shares rose about 6%. A completed deal would push Curaleaf into 17 countries and combine two of the industry's largest cultivation footprints, spanning U.S. state-legal and Canadian/EU federally-legal operations under one roof for the first time at this scale.

Elsewhere, Ascend Wellness holds its Q2 2026 earnings call today at 5pm ET — no results had been published as of this edition's research pass, consistent with a same-day release ahead of the call. Verano Holdings and Premier Group/PCF were both quiet in the 24–48 hour window; see Priority Coverage below.

Why it matters to PGP This is the first hostile approach of scale the U.S. cannabis sector has seen, and it uses a structure worth studying: a Nasdaq/OTC-listed MSO bidding directly for a dual-listed Canadian LP in stock-plus-cash, rather than the asset-carve-out or bankruptcy-driven deals (Vireo/Cannabist, SNDL/Parallel, AYR/Arboretum) that have dominated 2026 dealflow. If Aurora's special committee engages rather than adopts a defense, it opens a template for other well-capitalized MSOs to pursue federally-legal, non-U.S. platforms as a growth lane distinct from the domestic roll-up plays PGP has been tracking — worth flagging to any client evaluating cross-border cannabis exposure or defense-side mandates.

The Rescheduling Docket

Schedule I → III: status quo holds, briefs still due Aug 17, Alabama still undecided

No new docket filings or state decisions surfaced in the window — the desk confirms the timeline below is unchanged since the last edition.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E.
  • Jun 24–29, 2026 DEA ALJ hearings open; SAFER Banking reintroduced in both chambers.
  • Jul 15, 2026 Hearings conclude; record stays open for post-hearing briefs, due Aug 17. DEA Chief ALJ Derek Julius capped briefs at 50 pages, covering closing arguments and transcript corrections.
  • Aug 3, 2026 Senate votes 61-32 to table a Budd (R-NC) amendment, then passes the underlying CR 90-6 — pushing the toughest hemp-THC restrictions to Dec 11.
  • Aug 10, 2026 Reps. Van Duyne (R-TX) and Landsman (D-OH) formally introduce the Beverage Regulatory Parity Act — a 5mg THC/serving cap, three-tier alcohol-style distribution, and an 8-cents-per-mg federal excise tax; WSWA has endorsed it. No new co-sponsors or reactions surfaced since introduction.
  • Jun–Aug, 2026 States keep maneuvering: Wyoming opts out (Jul 8), Louisiana withdraws its rescheduling objection (Jun), Alabama holds a public hearing (Jul 22) with a written-comment deadline of Aug 5 — still no final State Board of Health decision as of this edition.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. Aug 17, 2026 — post-hearing briefs due (unchanged; 50-page cap set by the Chief ALJ).
  2. ALJ issues a recommended decision — no date has surfaced; trackers still flag late 2026 as more likely than an earlier call.
  3. 20-day exceptions window for parties.
  4. DEA Administrator's final call — a Final Rule could slip into early 2027 if the process runs long.
  5. Watch for Alabama's State Board of Health decision, still pending after the Aug 5 comment deadline.
PGP takeaway A quiet docket day is itself informative: no SAFE/SAFER Banking committee movement has surfaced since the bills were refiled in June, and no state has moved on rescheduling-linked 280E relief since Louisiana in June. Diligence timelines should keep assuming late-2026/early-2027 resolution rather than pulling forward expected relief dates.
