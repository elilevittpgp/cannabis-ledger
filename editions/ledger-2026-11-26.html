Page One · The Big Story

D.C. Circuit Grants a Targeted Stay of DEA's Nov. 29 "Timely Applicant" Deadline Days Before It Would Have Hit, Organigram and Aurora Formally File Their Joint Proxy Circular, and the Tape Goes Dark for Thanksgiving

The stay pauses enforcement only against state-licensed-but-unregistered medical operators — a narrow win, not a merits ruling — while U.S. cannabis equities sit on Wednesday's close with markets shut for the holiday.

With U.S. markets closed for Thanksgiving, the desk's lead story is legal rather than market-moving. On Tuesday, Nov. 24, DOJ filed its opposition urging the D.C. Circuit to reject stay motions brought by three medical-marijuana operators — Standard Wellness Utah, Verde Americano and NW Confections OK — against the DEA's Oct. 30 "Timely Applicant" guidance, which would bar Schedule III medical-marijuana supply chains from transacting with state-licensed entities that are neither federal registrants nor "timely applicants," effective Nov. 29. On Wednesday, Nov. 25, the court granted a targeted administrative stay: not a ruling on the merits, and not a blanket pause of the guidance, but explicit relief covering "medical marijuana activities that are in conformity with a state-issued license and are with entities that are neither federal registrants nor Timely Applicants" — the exact category the petitioners (case No. 26-1324, consolidated with Nos. 26-1325 and 26-1329) argued would otherwise be cut off days before the deadline. North Dakota, Utah and West Virginia filed an amicus brief backing the petitioners, arguing the guidance disrupts state regulatory frameworks; coverage out of Utah on Thursday, Nov. 26 tracked local industry reaction to the reprieve.

Separately, Organigram and Aurora Cannabis formally filed their joint management information circular on Thursday, Nov. 26 (via SEDAR+/EDGAR 6-K) in support of their board-recommended arrangement — converting what had been a "circular filed" status line into full election mechanics: Aurora holders may elect C$7.80 in cash, 5.8626 Organigram shares, or a prorated blend of C$3.75 cash plus 3.0431 shares, ahead of a Dec. 21 proxy cutoff and the Dec. 23 special meeting. Aurora's board continues to unanimously recommend the Organigram deal over Curaleaf's competing, unchanged hostile tender ($6.00/share, Dec. 4 deadline). No ISS or Glass Lewis recommendation has issued yet, and Curaleaf has not reacted publicly to the filing. On the priority desk, Ascend Wellness, Verano Holdings and Premier Group/PCF all stayed quiet on net-new developments into the holiday.

Why it matters to PGP Read the D.C. Circuit's move as risk reduction at the margin, not resolution: the stay protects one specific transaction category — state-licensed medical operators dealing with unregistered counterparties — while the broader "Timely Applicant" guidance, the separate "fatally flawed" rescheduling challenge (DOJ response not due until Jan. 13, 2027) and the Dec. 11 hemp-THC cliff all remain independently live. The Organigram/Aurora circular is the more concrete near-term signal for deal desks: it fixes real, bindable consideration (cash/share/blend election) three weeks before Curaleaf's Dec. 4 deadline, giving clients on either side of a competing-bid structure a hard reference price to model against Curaleaf's unchanged $6.00 cap.

The Rescheduling Docket

A Targeted Win at the D.C. Circuit, Days Before the Nov. 29 Deadline — but Three Separate Legal Threads Remain Open

The administrative stay narrows the immediate harm from DEA's supply-chain guidance without resolving it, while a direct legal challenge to the Schedule I-to-III reclassification itself and the Dec. 11 hemp-THC funding-bill standoff both remain unresolved and independent of this ruling.

  • Nov 18, 2026 Standard Wellness Utah, Verde Americano and NW Confections OK file emergency stay motions at the D.C. Circuit (Nos. 26-1324, consolidated with 26-1325 and 26-1329) against DEA's Oct. 30 "Timely Applicant" guidance.
  • Nov 23, 2026 North Dakota, Utah and West Virginia file an amicus brief backing the petitioners, arguing the guidance disrupts state cannabis regulatory frameworks.
  • Nov 24, 2026 DOJ files its opposition, urging the court to reject the stay motions as "without merit."
  • Nov 25, 2026 The D.C. Circuit grants a targeted administrative stay — not a merits ruling — pausing enforcement specifically against state-licensed medical-marijuana activity involving entities that are neither federal registrants nor Timely Applicants, days ahead of the Nov. 29 deadline. Separately, a coalition of anti-rescheduling groups files a new brief calling the DOJ's Schedule III reclassification itself "fatally flawed," opening a second, distinct line of attack (DOJ's response is not due until Jan. 13, 2027).
  • Nov 26, 2026 Utah trade press covers industry reaction to the stay; no further court action or DOJ/DEA response reported.
