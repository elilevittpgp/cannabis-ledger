Curaleaf Takes Its Aurora Bid Straight to the Shareholders

A direct letter, a live shareholder Q&A and a brochure filing mark an escalation from regulator combat to a retail-investor campaign — while the Alberta Securities Commission still hasn't set a hearing date on the underlying ATM dispute.

Curaleaf's hostile bid for Aurora Cannabis moved into a new phase this week. Having filed with the Alberta Securities Commission on Sept 14 to halt Aurora's at-the-market share program — and drawn a same-day public rebuttal from Aurora calling the move a "distraction" — Curaleaf pivoted on Sept 15 to a direct appeal: a letter to Aurora shareholders reiterating its offer (0.3463 Curaleaf shares plus US$0.75 cash per Aurora share, a stated 45% premium to the unaffected 30-day VWAP) and announcing a live shareholder Q&A with CEO Boris Jordan for Sept 17. On Sept 16, Curaleaf followed with an SEC filing disclosing a shareholder "brochure" continuing the tender push. As of this morning, the ASC has neither scheduled a hearing nor ruled on Curaleaf's ATM application, and Aurora's board continues to recommend shareholders take no action on the bid, which expires Dec 1.

Elsewhere, TerrAscend closed its previously announced acquisition of Aunt Mary's Dispensary in Hunterdon County, New Jersey — its fifth NJ retail location — for up to US$9M in cash and convertible debt, a reminder that bolt-on M&A continues even as the company's Sept 18 deadline to respond to the DOJ's first-of-its-kind 280E refund-clawback suit sits two days out with no filing yet. On the rescheduling docket, DEA Chief ALJ Derek Julius still has not issued a recommended decision on full Schedule I-to-III reclassification, now roughly nine weeks past the July 15 close of hearings, and the administration's Sept 30 deadline to answer a bipartisan House letter on rescheduling mechanics is two weeks out. Ascend, Verano and Premier Group/PCF all stayed quiet.

Why it matters to PGP A hostile bidder moving from a regulatory complaint to a direct-to-shareholder letter and live Q&A is a template worth filing away for future contested cross-border cannabis takeovers — it signals Curaleaf sees the ASC route as necessary but not sufficient to move the needle before Dec 1. For diligence purposes, treat the ASC's silence on a hearing date as the more important fact than either side's public statements: until a regulator actually rules on ATM-during-bid conduct, there's no precedent to underwrite future defensive-dilution disputes on.

The Rescheduling Docket

Nine weeks past the hearing close, still no ALJ decision

The Schedule III merits fight survives its first stay motion; Congress presses the administration for answers by Sept 30 while banking reform gets a broader Senate coalition.

  • Aug 17, 2026 Post-hearing briefs filed with the DEA following the July 15 close of ALJ hearings on full Schedule I→III rescheduling.
  • Aug 18, 2026 A bipartisan House group (Titus, Joyce, Omar, Steube) sends a letter to the administration seeking answers on rescheduling mechanics by Sept 30.
  • Sep 8-9, 2026 GAO publishes GAO-26-107498 on cannabis banking access; follow-on coverage finds Visa and Mastercard unlikely to loosen payment-network restrictions even after further rescheduling.
  • Sep 9, 2026 The D.C. Circuit denies a stay motion by anti-rescheduling petitioners (NDASA, MMJ International, joined by the AGs of NE, IN and LA) seeking to freeze the DOJ's April 2026 Schedule III order pending review — the underlying "hybrid scheduling regime" merits challenge continues.
  • Sep 14, 2026 Sens. Cortez Masto and Merkley reintroduce the SAFE Banking Act with a seven-member bipartisan cosponsor list (Murkowski, Daines, Warren, Sullivan, Cramer, Murray).
  • Sep 16, 2026 No ALJ recommended decision yet — roughly nine weeks past the hearing close; no administration response yet to the Aug 18 House letter.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. ALJ Julius's recommended decision on full Schedule I→III rescheduling — still no fixed date.
  2. Sep 18, 2026 — TerrAscend's response due in the DOJ's federal 280E clawback suit.
  3. Sep 24 / Oct 6, 2026 — scheduled Advanced Flower Capital foreclosure auctions of Justice Grown's PA and NJ collateral.
  4. Sep 30, 2026 — deadline for the administration to answer lawmakers' rescheduling-mechanics letter.
  5. Nov 12, 2026 — converted/synthetic cannabinoids (delta-8, HHC, THC-O) lose federal hemp status; no extension applies.
  6. Dec 1, 2026 — Curaleaf's bid for Aurora Cannabis expires unless extended.
  7. Dec 11, 2026 — the enacted effective date for naturally-occurring hemp cannabinoids, under the 0.4mg-total-THC-per-container standard.
PGP takeaway Every new date on this docket is a checkpoint added by Congress or the courts — not a resolution of the one that actually matters. Underwrite on the assumption that the ALJ's recommendation remains undated through year-end, and keep the Sept 30 letter deadline on the calendar as the next real test of administration intent.
