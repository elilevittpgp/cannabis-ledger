Page One · The Big Story

Curaleaf Hits Back, TerrAscend Clears a Hurdle, and the Tape Cools Off Its Highs

Hours after Aurora's point-by-point rebuttal, Curaleaf calls Aurora's protests "hollow" and cites a 63% sequential EBITDA drop — while TerrAscend shareholders wave through a share consolidation by a 99.25% margin, and MSOS gives back some of last week's rally.

The Curaleaf–Aurora fight stopped being polite on Monday. Hours after Aurora Cannabis used its 7:05 a.m. rebuttal to dispute Curaleaf's characterization of its German business, Curaleaf answered back at 3:45 p.m. ET with a release calling Aurora's protests "hollow" and its statistics "completely misleading." Curaleaf said Aurora shareholders have absorbed more than C$400 million in inventory impairments and business-transformation costs, that Aurora shares fell roughly 35% over the past year while Curaleaf's rose 56%, and that Aurora's June-quarter adjusted EBITDA came in 63% below the March quarter. Curaleaf also disputed Aurora's cultivation-yield comparison, claiming its own yields are "more than double" Aurora's disclosed 114 grams per plant, and flagged Aurora's fiscal 2027 guidance — revenue near 2025 levels, gross margin sliding to the mid-to-high 50% range — as evidence of stalled execution. The hostile bid (0.3463 Curaleaf shares plus $0.75 cash per share, implying roughly $4.00, capped at $5) remains open until Dec. 1 absent an extension.

The same day, TerrAscend shareholders approved a share-consolidation resolution at a special meeting by a 99.25% margin (143,095,552 shares for; 141,435 withheld) — a step toward a U.S. exchange uplisting, with the exact ratio (a range of 1-for-5 to 1-for-20) still to be set by the board. And the sector's benchmark cooled: MSOS closed Monday, Aug. 24 at $4.89, down 1.41% from Friday's $4.96, snapping a three-session run even as the fund remains up roughly 15.6% for the month. Planet 13 was the day's outlier, falling 13.48% with no company disclosure to explain the move even as it awaits a shareholder vote on its pending all-stock sale to Vireo Growth.

Why it matters to PGP A bidder and target now trading specific plant-yield and EBITDA figures in dueling press releases, rather than through advisors, signals both sides are positioning for a long fight — likely an extended tender or a public shareholder campaign, not a quick friendly resolution; neither side's cited numbers should be treated as reconciled until independently verified. Separately, TerrAscend's 99%-plus vote is the second small-cap consolidation to clear this month, reinforcing that reverse splits ahead of a U.S. uplisting are now the default playbook — Ascend's own Aug. 28 vote, three days out, is the next test, and a name still trading unexplained (see below) going into that vote is a diligence flag worth carrying forward.

The Rescheduling Docket

TerrAscend clears its uplisting vote as the House's hemp fight comes into view

The corrected hearing transcript is still due Wednesday, not yet posted; a bigger near-term risk is emerging in the House over whether the Dec. 11 hemp-ban delay survives a floor fight.

  • Aug 17, 2026 Post-hearing brief deadline met: DEA's own brief urges Chief ALJ Derek C. Julius to recommend Schedule III; opposition briefs filed by SAM, several states and others.
  • Aug 19, 2026 Reps. Titus, Joyce, Omar and Steube send a bipartisan letter pressing the administration for rescheduling-implementation guidance, with answers requested by Sept 30.

  4. ALJ Julius's recommended decision on broad rescheduling — still no timeline reported; a 20-day exceptions window and a final DEA Administrator call follow.
  5. Sept 30, 2026 — deadline lawmakers gave the administration to answer their rescheduling-implementation letter; also the federal funding deadline tied to the hemp-ban fight.
  6. Dec 1, 2026 — Curaleaf's bid for Aurora Cannabis expires unless extended.
  7. Dec 11, 2026 — the federal hemp-THC ban is due to take effect if the delay survives the House. Alabama's Board of Health 280E/opt-out ruling remains pending since its comment period closed Aug 5.
PGP takeaway The Dec 11 hemp date has been treated on this desk as settled; it is not. If Harris's faction strips the delay in the House, the cliff could move earlier than Dec 11 or get entangled in a broader funding fight — hemp-exposed diligence should model both the current Dec 11 date and a scenario where the delay itself lapses. On rescheduling proper, 280E relief should still be modeled as "when, not if," but a transcript correction due mid-week is a procedural step, not a signal the ALJ's recommendation is imminent.
