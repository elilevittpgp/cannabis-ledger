Alabama Stands Down on Rescheduling — the Only Loud Signal in an Otherwise Silent Weekend

The state's public-health board unanimously withdrew its objection to state-level Schedule III reclassification; across the rest of the desk's watchlist, Green Thumb's $50M buyback renewal was the lone piece of company-specific news through three quiet days.

Alabama's State Committee of Public Health voted unanimously on Thursday, Sept 18, to withdraw the objection it lodged in May against state-level marijuana rescheduling, formally aligning the state with the Trump administration's federal Schedule III move. The board had delayed action earlier this year to study whether reclassification would disturb Alabama's existing medical-cannabis framework; following a July public hearing, State Health Officer Dr. Scott Harris concluded it would not, since the state's program was written to function as a decriminalization statute independent of the federal schedule. Officials characterized the reversal as procedural, but it removes one of the loudest state-level holdouts this desk has tracked since Wyoming and Louisiana resolved their own positions over the summer.

Everywhere else, the weekend produced almost nothing. Ascend, Verano and Premier Group/PCF all stayed silent on filings. The Curaleaf-Aurora Cannabis hostile-bid standoff holds exactly where it sat Friday — no new statement from either side, and the Alberta Securities Commission still hasn't set a hearing date a full week after Curaleaf's application to halt Aurora's ATM program. Green Thumb Industries renewed its share-repurchase authorization for another $50M today, Sept 21, the only fresh MSO-specific release found in the window. The tape drifted broadly higher Monday — MSOS up roughly 1.6% intraday, led by Aurora Cannabis (+4.65%) and Trulieve (+3.97%) — on no single discrete catalyst, consistent with the sector's slow-burning rescheduling-optimism drift.

Why it matters to PGP Alabama's reversal trims one more variable off the state-by-state opt-out map this desk has been tracking since the April Schedule III order — fewer holdouts means a cleaner, more uniform path for 280E relief and interstate deal structuring once the DEA Administrator finally rules. It does not, however, move the load-bearing question: ALJ Derek Julius's recommended decision is now closing in on ten weeks past the July 15 hearing close, well beyond every earlier informal estimate, and every uplisting and refinancing timeline on this desk's watchlist is still implicitly priced against a decision that hasn't arrived.

A bipartisan bill would shield past cannabis use from federal hiring decisions
