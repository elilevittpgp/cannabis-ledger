Page One · The Big Story

Aurora's Board Formally Rejects Curaleaf's Sweetened Bid and Opens the Door to Organigram, as Cronos Closes Its Netherlands Deal on the Dot

Aurora's special committee reaffirmed its rejection of Curaleaf's enhanced hostile offer and said it is "actively exploring a range of alternatives" — a direct nod to BAT-backed Organigram — while Curaleaf's tender deadline stretches to Dec. 4. Separately, Cronos Group closed its €57.5 million Netherlands acquisition exactly on its twice-extended long-stop date, and the sector tape split hard between winners and laggards on below-average volume.

The Curaleaf-Aurora-Organigram fight took its sharpest turn yet this week. Aurora's board, acting on the unanimous recommendation of its independent special committee, publicly reaffirmed on Oct. 14 that shareholders should reject Curaleaf's enhanced hostile bid — the Oct. 6 Notice of Variation implying roughly $5.00/share (0.4013 Curaleaf shares plus $1.00 cash, priced off Curaleaf's Oct. 2 close). Aurora's committee went further than a simple rejection, stating it is "actively exploring a range of alternatives" that "may offer shareholders greater value," including a possible transaction with a third party or remaining independent — language that lines up with MJBizDaily's reporting that BAT-backed Organigram Global has held talks with Aurora "in recent weeks," though neither company will confirm a deal on the record. Curaleaf, for its part, says it is "outraged" that Aurora is reportedly extending diligence access to "a small Canadian company" while denying it the same, and continues to tout its own debt load (over $1 billion) against Aurora's debt-free balance sheet as a point of leverage. One number this desk can now verify directly: British American Tobacco's stake in Organigram sits at 29.9% of common shares (non-diluted) plus Class A preferred shares convertible up to a 49% fully-diluted cap — a narrower, harder number than the "30-45%, unverified" range this desk had been carrying. Curaleaf's tender offer now stays open until Dec. 4, 2026; the formal amended Directors' Circular responding to the enhanced terms is still due the week of Oct. 20, and neither side's Alberta Securities Commission disclosure complaint has a scheduled hearing.

Elsewhere, Cronos Group actually closed something: its acquisition of CanAdelaar B.V., the Netherlands' largest adult-use cannabis supplier, completed today, Oct. 15 — exactly on the long-stop date that had already been extended twice (June 9 → Sept. 9 → Oct. 15). The €57.5 million deal (plus a contingent earnout of 0.5x normalized EBITDA in both 2026 and 2027) makes Cronos the No. 1 market-share operator in the Netherlands' "Wietexperiment" legal adult-use pilot, supplying nearly all 69 participating coffee shops from CanAdelaar's 540,000-square-foot Voorne aan Zee greenhouse. On the tape, MSOS ticked up to $4.12 intraday (+0.98%) on notably light volume — roughly 2.17 million shares against a ~4.6 million average — but the move masked a genuinely split sector: Glass House (+3.25%, on fresh GACP export certification), Trulieve (+3.61%) and TerrAscend (+6.5%, with no identified catalyst) rallied, while Curaleaf (-1.79%) and Cresco Labs (-2.21%) lagged, plausibly on Aurora-bid-related uncertainty.

Why it matters to PGP A quantified BAT stake (29.9% non-diluted, up to 49% fully diluted) is now a usable input for modeling how much balance-sheet support Organigram could realistically draw on in a competitive bid for Aurora — that's a meaningfully different diligence posture than carrying an "unverified 30-45%" placeholder. Aurora's explicit "exploring alternatives" language, paired with Curaleaf's extended Dec. 4 deadline, suggests the timeline for any PGP-advised engagement tied to this situation should assume a multi-week, potentially multi-bidder process rather than a near-term resolution. Cronos's on-time Netherlands closing is also a clean data point: long-stop-date discipline held here even after two extensions, a useful comparable when modeling closing-risk clauses in cross-border cannabis deals.

The Rescheduling Docket

Still stayed, still waiting on Oct 21

No ruling, no new filing, and no change to the docket since yesterday — the case remains exactly where ALJ Julius left it: reserved judgment, with DEA's response to the opponents' testimony/discovery request due Oct 21.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E.
  • Jul 15, 2026 Broader Schedule I→III hearings conclude before ALJ Derek Julius.
  • Sep 23, 2026 GAO publishes report GAO-26-108623, finding DEA's scheduling decisions aligned with HHS recommendations in all 84 cases reviewed, but flagging a lack of written DEA/FDA procedural policy.
  • Sep 28-29, 2026 Opponents move to reopen the record to admit the GAO report; ALJ Julius stays the case and orders a DEA response by Oct 13.
  • Oct 9, 2026 DEA files its response four days early: no objection to admitting the GAO report itself, but firm opposition to opponents' broader bid for new testimony and document discovery, calling the report having "no bearing" on the Schedule III question.
  • Oct 9-13, 2026 Opponents (DUID Victim Voices, Dr. Kenneth Finn, National Drug and Alcohol Association) file a counter-brief pressing for the broader reopening.
  • Oct 13-14, 2026 ALJ Julius states he has not yet ruled on the GAO-report motion, reserves judgment, and sets a new Oct 21 deadline for DEA to address the testimony/discovery request.
  • Oct 15, 2026 (today) No new filing or ruling located on the docket — status unchanged.

PGP takeaway The hemp-THC ban is not one deadline but two: converted cannabinoids (Delta-8, THC-P) face restrictions a full month before natural cannabinoids (THCa, hemp Delta-9) under the Dec 11 cap. Clients with inventory or supply-chain exposure across both product categories should be modeling two separate compliance dates, not one — a nuance this desk had not previously broken out and is flagging now because it changes near-term working-capital and SKU-transition timing for hemp-beverage and hemp-derived-THC clients specifically.
