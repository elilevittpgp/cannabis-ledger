Page One · The Big Story

Glass House Sheds a Quarter of Its Value in a Day as Curaleaf Accuses Aurora's Board of a Fiduciary Breach

A soft preliminary Q3 print and an unexplained board resignation send Glass House Brands down 24% on ~30x average volume — the sector's sharpest single-name move in weeks — while Curaleaf escalates its Aurora hostile-bid fight with explicit fiduciary-duty language. Separately, this desk corrects its own standing ticker for Verano Holdings.

Glass House Brands (NYSE: GLAS) disclosed preliminary, unaudited third-quarter results today, Oct. 19 — record biomass production of roughly 317,000 lbs, up from 246,000 lbs in Q2, and revenue of $56.8M, up from $47.0M in Q2 — alongside an unexplained resignation from the board by director John "Jay" Nichols Jr. The headline growth numbers masked a margin story: average selling price fell to roughly $150/lb from $211/lb in Q2, even as cost of production improved to about $110/lb from $122/lb. The market's verdict was swift and large — GLAS fell roughly 24% intraday on volume running near 30 times its average, by far the sector's biggest single-name move since early October, eclipsing even last Friday's 12.4% drop on a short-interest-driven move this desk flagged at the time. The company reaffirmed full-year guidance (~1M lbs production, a long-term $95/lb cost target) and gave no reason for Nichols's departure.

Separately, the Curaleaf-Aurora fight sharpened. Curaleaf published a release today titled "Curaleaf Challenges Aurora's Unilateral Rejection of Its Enhanced Proposal," with Chairman and CEO Boris Jordan accusing Aurora's board of granting due-diligence access to unnamed third parties while denying it to Curaleaf — behavior he said "defies all fiduciary logic and reason." Curaleaf argues its enhanced bid (0.4013 Curaleaf shares plus $1.00 cash per Aurora share, implying roughly $5.00/share with a $6.00 cash-election cap) carries an 86% premium to Aurora's 30-day VWAP and a 14.3x multiple on 2026 adjusted EBITDA — above Curaleaf's own 13.7x — and notes Aurora's adjusted EBITDA fell 63% over its last two quarters. Aurora's board, for its part, filed a Notice of Change to its Directors' Circular on Oct. 15 (its fifth amendment since September) reaffirming its rejection recommendation without naming Organigram Global, the BAT-backed suitor trade press continues to report is in talks with Aurora. Neither side's Alberta Securities Commission disclosure complaint has a hearing date.

This desk is also correcting the record on its own standing ticker for Verano Holdings. Prior editions have carried "OTC: VRNOF." That symbol is stale: Verano completed a 1-for-5 reverse stock split effective June 11, 2026, after which its shares have traded as VRNO on Cboe Canada and OTCQX — confirmed independently via SEC EDGAR's own filing-name convention for Verano's Q2 10-Q ("vrno-20260630.htm") and corroborating secondary coverage. This desk will use VRNO going forward.

Why it matters to PGP Glass House's move is a clean reminder that in this tape, a "beat" on volume and biomass can still be a sell if realized price is falling faster than cost — the margin line, not the growth line, is what the market is pricing on cultivation-heavy operators right now, and any PGP model on a cultivator should stress-test ASP compression ahead of cost deflation. The Curaleaf/Aurora escalation is a second data point that this fight is heading toward a forced resolution (an ASC ruling, a named competing bid, or a tender deadline) rather than settling quietly — worth tracking as a template for how hostile cross-border cannabis M&A gets fought. And the Verano ticker correction is a small but real diligence lesson: legacy symbols persist in aggregators and even in this desk's own prior editions well after a reverse split, and PGP's own screens should confirm current tickers against a primary filing, not a cached quote page.
