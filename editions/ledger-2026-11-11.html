Page One · The Big Story

Cannabis Tape Extends Its Post-Clearance Fade to a One-Week Low as Glass House Pushes Its Q3 Report to Monday, Jushi Posts a Record Wholesale Quarter, and the SAFE Banking Coalition Firms Up at 16 Governors

A quiet Wednesday on the three priority names gives way to a broader, catalyst-light pullback across the sector tape, which keeps unwinding the pop it caught on last week's Aurora-deal headlines.

MSOS eased roughly 1% Wednesday to around $4.06, extending its slide to about 6.2% versus a week ago as the ETF continues giving back most of the gain it caught on Nov. 3's Organigram-Aurora deal news. Decliners outnumbered gainers across the checked constituent list, led by Cresco Labs (-5.11%), Glass House Brands (-4.31%) and TerrAscend (-4.55% on the TSX, -3.24% OTC), with Verano (-0.85%) and Ascend Wellness (-0.27%) also lower; Vireo Growth (+0.93%) and Planet 13 (+7.44%, off a sub-dime base) were the notable exceptions. No single headline explains the broad move — this desk found no new company-specific catalyst behind most of today's declines.

The day's one concrete calendar surprise came from Glass House Brands, which pushed its Q3 2026 earnings call from today to Monday, Nov. 16 after market close, while reaffirming preliminary guidance of roughly $56.8 million in Q3 revenue and a $110-per-pound production cost. Jushi Holdings filed Q3 2026 results via 8-K Tuesday evening, posting $70.5 million in revenue (+7.3% YoY) on a record $9.7 million wholesale quarter (+40% YoY) and retail growth in Ohio and Virginia — routine, not a market-moving print. Separately, this desk corrects a headcount from yesterday's edition: the governors' letter pressing Congress to pass the SAFE Banking Act was signed by 16 Democratic governors, not 15, led by Maryland's Wes Moore as NGA chair; the bills remain stalled in committee with no hearing scheduled. All three priority names — Ascend, Premier Group/PCF, and Verano — were quiet, with Ascend's Q3 2026 earnings call still confirmed for tomorrow, Nov. 12, 5pm ET.

Why it matters to PGP A sector-wide fade with no identifiable single cause is itself useful signal: it suggests the market is still digesting the Nov. 3 M&A-news spike rather than pricing in anything new today, which argues for patience on timing any financing or secondary activity tied to current trading levels rather than chasing yesterday's pop. Glass House's one-week earnings delay is a minor calendar item on its own, but it bunches three MSO prints (Glass House Nov. 16, on top of Ascend Nov. 12) into a compressed window — worth flagging to any client watching the group's cost-per-pound trajectory (Glass House's reaffirmed $110/lb versus its $95/lb long-term target) as a read on margin durability heading into 2027.

The Rescheduling Docket

DEA's 2027 quota notice clears the Federal Register, Missouri regulators warn licensees off interstate commerce even under future rescheduling, and North Carolina's incoming House Speaker floats decriminalization

No ALJ action on the DEA's Schedule I-to-III record — the briefing round closed Nov. 5 with no recommendation timeline yet — but procedural and state-level threads keep moving.

  • Nov 5, 2026 The DEA and rescheduling opponents file their final reply briefs before Chief ALJ Derek Julius, closing the last scheduled round of written argument on the stayed Schedule I-to-III record. No recommended-decision timeline has been set.
  • Nov 9, 2026 Missouri's Division of Cannabis Regulation guidance, reported this week, warns licensed marijuana businesses that federal Schedule III rescheduling would not permit interstate commerce under state law, and that licensees risk their registration if they attempt it regardless of any future federal rule change.
  • Nov 9, 2026 A coalition of 16 Democratic governors — not 15, as reported in yesterday's edition — led by Maryland's Wes Moore as National Governors Association chair, sends Congress a letter urging passage of the SAFE Banking Act (S. 4942 / H.R. 9471); both bills remain pending in committee with no hearing or vote scheduled.
  • Nov 9-10, 2026 Illinois's joint agreement with the Illinois Healthy Alternatives Association (~250 member businesses) pausing enforcement of the state's Nov. 12 hemp-THC ban against those litigants stands confirmed; the Nov. 12 effective date still applies to every non-litigant business, per an Illinois Department of Revenue bulletin. Oral arguments on the litigants' TRO/injunction motion remain set for Nov. 19.
  • Nov 10, 2026 The DEA's draft 2027 aggregate production-quota notice — signed Nov. 3, holding Schedule I marijuana quotas flat at 6,675 kg cannabis / 1,000 kg extract — is formally published in the Federal Register; a separate notice to set Schedule III marijuana/extract quotas remains in development with no publication date.
  • Nov 10, 2026 Incoming North Carolina House Speaker Robert Reives (D), after Democrats' midterm gains in the state House, says marijuana decriminalization is "on top of everybody's minds" and floats it as a possible bipartisan opening with the GOP-controlled Senate — no bill text filed yet.

PGP takeaway Missouri's new interstate-commerce guidance is a useful marker for any client assuming federal rescheduling alone will open multi-state product flow — state medical-marijuana statutes like Missouri's can independently block that outcome regardless of the DEA's final Schedule III action, a distinction worth building into any post-rescheduling distribution-strategy memo.
