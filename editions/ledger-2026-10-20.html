Page One · The Big Story

Aurora Pulls Organigram Deeper Into the Fight as Curaleaf Cries Foul — While Glass House's Rout Rolls Into a Second Day Even as the Broader Tape Rallies 4%

Aurora's board publicly confirms it is in active talks with BAT-backed Organigram as a rival option, prompting an "outrageous" rebuke from Curaleaf, as Glass House Brands keeps bleeding a day after its 24% collapse and Canaccord slashes its price target to $8 from $15 — even as MSOS and most of the sector rally broadly.

The Curaleaf-Aurora fight sharpened again today. Per Shanken News Daily, Aurora's board on Oct. 20 again publicly rebuffed Curaleaf's enhanced hostile bid while confirming it is in active discussions with Organigram Holdings — the BAT-backed Canadian operator trade press has linked to Aurora since mid-October — as a potential alternative, though neither side has filed signed terms or a price. Curaleaf called the framing "outrageous," arguing Aurora is using the Organigram talks as leverage rather than disclosing a real competing bid, and reiterated that its own enhanced offer (0.4013 Curaleaf shares plus $1.00 cash, implying roughly $5.00/share with a $6.00 cash-election cap) carries an 86% premium to Aurora's unaffected 30-day VWAP. Curaleaf's tender deposit deadline remains Dec. 4; no Alberta Securities Commission hearing date has been set for either side's disclosure complaint.

Glass House Brands (NYSE: GLAS) is not done falling. A day after it plunged 24.14% on a soft preliminary Q3 revenue preview and an unexplained board resignation, the stock traded down a further 4.27% to $2.355 Tuesday afternoon — still well above average volume. Canaccord Genuity cut its price target on the stock to $8 from $15 today, a steep cut that formalizes the market's verdict on the margin-compression story (average selling price falling to roughly $150/lb from $211/lb) this desk flagged Monday. Elsewhere, Curaleaf opened its 80th Florida dispensary, in Port Orange — routine retail expansion, not market-moving.

The rest of the tape told a different story. MSOS traded near $3.975 Tuesday afternoon (~2:00-2:20pm ET), up 4.33% intraday and rebounding off Friday Oct. 16's verified close of $3.89 — a broad-based move, with Verano (+5.35%), Cresco Labs (+4.90%), Trulieve (+4.57%), TerrAscend (+3.84%), Curaleaf (+3.34%), Green Thumb (+2.37%) and Aurora itself (+0.51%) all firmer, and no single company-specific catalyst identified for the rally as a group. Glass House and Planet 13 (-2.04%) were the only decliners among names checked this run.

Why it matters to PGP Today is a clean split-screen for how idiosyncratic and sector risk diverge in this tape: a broad, catalyst-free 4%+ rally in MSOS sat alongside a single name (Glass House) still being marked down a full day after its own news, and an analyst cut that lands two sessions late relative to the stock's move — a reminder that sell-side price targets in this sector often confirm price action rather than lead it, and PGP's own marks should not wait on them. The Aurora/Organigram escalation is now explicitly acknowledged by Aurora's own board rather than inferred from trade-press sourcing, which moves this from "rumored rival" to "disclosed alternative under live consideration" — still short of a term sheet, but a meaningfully firmer basis for modeling a competing bid in any PGP valuation work on Aurora or on Curaleaf's standalone economics if the deal fails.
