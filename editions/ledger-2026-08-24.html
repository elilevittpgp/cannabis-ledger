Aurora Fires Back at Curaleaf as the Tape Notches a Fresh High

A same-day rebuttal disputes Curaleaf's framing of Aurora's German business hours before TerrAscend shareholders vote on their own share consolidation, while MSOS closes Friday at its best level of the past two weeks.

On a Monday with two capital-markets storylines running at once, Aurora Cannabis used its own investor-relations channel this morning to push back directly against the company trying to buy it. In a release published at 7:05 a.m. ET titled "Aurora Cannabis Corrects Inaccurate Statements Made in Support of Curaleaf Holdings' Hostile Bid," Aurora said reimbursement changes affect "less than 10 percent" of its German volume — not the broader threat Curaleaf has suggested — and pointed to 17% year-over-year international revenue growth in fiscal Q1'27, EU-GMP capacity up more than 40% over five years, three straight years of positive adjusted EBITDA, and the #1 revenue share position in Poland. CEO Miguel Martin called Curaleaf's bid "a transparent attempt to pressure Aurora shareholders." Aurora confirmed "several discussions" with Curaleaf since June, most recently Aug. 12, but again told shareholders to take no action pending the Special Committee's formal recommendation; the take-over offer, formally commenced Aug. 18, remains open at least 105 days.

The rebuttal lands hours before TerrAscend's own shareholders vote, at 1 p.m. ET today, on a share consolidation (ratio 1-for-5 to 1-for-20, requiring two-thirds approval) meant to lift the stock toward exchange minimum-bid-price territory — the outcome was not yet public at this edition's publication. It also lands against a tape still climbing: MSOS closed Friday, Aug. 21 at $4.96, up 4.64% on the day, 7.13% on the week and 17.26% month-to-date — its strongest close of the past two weeks. Every name on this desk's watchlist gained on both the day and the week, led by Trulieve (+8.29% day, +15.03% week) in its first full week as a clean NYSE listing.

Why it matters to PGP A target firing back point-by-point at a hostile bidder's public claims is itself a data point — it signals Aurora's Special Committee is more likely to resist than wave the offer through, and it raises the diligence bar on how any bidder characterizes a target's overseas or wholesale exposure; verify, don't inherit, a suitor's framing of a business it doesn't yet own. Separately, TerrAscend's consolidation vote is a template worth tracking: small-cap MSOs sitting below exchange minimum-bid thresholds are increasingly reaching for structural fixes — reverse splits, consolidations — rather than organic price recovery to unlock a U.S. uplisting. Ascend's own Aug. 28 vote is the next test of the same playbook.

The Rescheduling Docket

294 corrections to the record, still no timeline on the ruling

The transcript fix ordered Aug 20 turns out to run to 294 line-item corrections across the ~2,470-page hearing record — due this week, but still not a signal on when the actual recommended decision lands.

  • Aug 17, 2026 Post-hearing brief deadline met: DEA's own brief urges Chief ALJ Julius to recommend Schedule III; opposition briefs filed by SAM, three states, and others.
  • Aug 19, 2026 Reps. Titus, Joyce, Omar and Steube send a bipartisan letter to the Trump administration pressing for Schedule III implementation guidance, with answers requested by Sept 30.
  • Aug 20, 2026 Chief ALJ Julius orders 294 corrections to the official 11-day hearing transcript (submitted by DEA, SAM, Nebraska/Idaho/Indiana and others); a corrected, red-lined version is due on the DEA's website by Aug 26 — procedural, not a ruling.
  • Aug 24, 2026 South Dakota officials say federal agencies still haven't answered states' questions on rescheduling's practical implications — echoing the still-open Sept 30 deadline lawmakers gave the administration.
