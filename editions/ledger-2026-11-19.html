Page One · The Big Story

Wall Street's Anthropic-IPO Hangover Drags MSOS Down 5.6% to a Fresh Multi-Week Low as Nearly Every Cannabis Name Falls in Unison, Illinois's Hemp-THC Court Hearing Ends Without a Ruling, and Glass House Draws a Securities Investigation

MSOS closes at $3.38, down 5.59% on the day and roughly 12.4% below last Thursday's level, as a broad, cannabis-unrelated selloff tied to profit-taking after Anthropic's blockbuster Nasdaq debut drags nearly every sector constituent lower in unison; a federal judge in Chicago hears, but does not yet rule on, Illinois hemp retailers' bid to block the state's THC ban; Glass House Brands draws a securities-claims investigation a day after its margin-miss reversal; and Verano opens its 89th Florida dispensary despite the rout.

Cannabis stocks were swept into a broad, cannabis-unrelated Wall Street selloff Thursday that traced back to Anthropic's blockbuster Nasdaq debut: shares opened as much as 34% above their offer price before closing up 17% at $97.17, valuing the AI lab at roughly $1.76 trillion — a print strong enough to trigger a wave of profit-taking across the broader "AI trade" and the market at large. The Dow fell about 1,084 points (-2.2%), the S&P 500 dropped 2.0% for its sixth straight losing session (its longest skid since 2024), the Nasdaq fell 2.2%, and the VIX spiked to 29, its highest reading since March. None of it has anything to do with cannabis fundamentals, but the sector's benchmark ETF sold off in sympathy anyway, in a near-mirror of the Oct. 30 and Nov. 12 "AI reckoning" selloffs this desk has tracked through the fall.

MSOS, the AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF, closed at $3.38 on volume of roughly 8.0 million shares — down 5.59% on the day, about 12.4% below last Thursday's (Nov. 12) $3.86 close, and roughly 14.2% below Oct. 30's $3.94 month-to-date baseline. Unlike recent sessions, which left a handful of names green, today's tape showed no exceptions: Planet 13 fell 13.29%, Verano fell 12.31%, Glass House fell 11.68%, Jushi fell 10.90%, Cresco Labs fell 8.73%, TerrAscend fell 7.78%, Vireo Growth fell 7.44%, Organigram fell 6.95%, Trulieve fell 6.69%, Aurora Cannabis fell 3.18%, Ascend Wellness fell 2.62%, Green Thumb fell 1.85%, and Curaleaf fell 1.42%. AYR Wellness again returned no meaningful live quote, consistent with its ongoing CCAA wind-down. The scale of Verano's decline is the day's clearest evidence that this was a market event, not a company one — the stock fell harder than nearly every peer on a morning it announced a new store.

On the priority desk: Verano opened MÜV Tarpon Springs, its 89th Florida dispensary and 166th nationwide (fifth in Pinellas County), with a ribbon-cutting set for Nov. 23 — its third new Florida store in a month. In Illinois, U.S. District Judge Martha Pacold heard oral arguments today on the Illinois Healthy Alternatives Association's motion to block the state's hemp-THC ban for roughly 250 member businesses, whose negotiated enforcement pause was set to expire today; no ruling had been issued as of this edition. In TerrAscend's $8.36 million 280E refund clawback suit, the Department of Justice's court-ordered reply deadline landed today; no filing content had surfaced in trade or legal coverage as of this edition, with the case's motion-to-dismiss hearing still set for Dec. 21. Ascend Wellness and Premier Group/PCF were both quiet on net-new developments; a sweep of the CA Department of Cannabis Control's license registry found PCF of Ceres, X1 Tracy and pHenos Modesto all showing active, unflagged status.

Glass House Brands, still absorbing Wednesday's 17.47% reversal, drew a fresh headline today: Highful Law PLLC announced it is investigating whether the company and its officers made misleading statements tied to the June 2026 retail-business deconsolidation — the stock's second credibility-adjacent headline inside two weeks, after the BDO-to-MGO auditor swap. On the federal hemp side, no fresh escalation surfaced beyond Tuesday-Wednesday's standoff: the bipartisan Sheehy-Klobuchar "Hemp Guardrails Act" and Sens. Budd and Ricketts's vow to block any funding bill that keeps hemp-THC products legal both stand unchanged ahead of the Dec. 11 deadline. The DEA's Schedule I-to-III record likewise shows no movement — Chief ALJ Derek Julius has set no date for his recommended decision.

Why it matters to PGP Today is a clean case study in decoupling macro-beta risk from idiosyncratic catalysts: Verano's double-digit decline on a day it opened a new store, and Glass House's additional headline risk layering onto an already-wounded stock, both argue against reading single-session price action as a verdict on fundamentals during a systemic selloff. Separately, Illinois's still-unresolved TRO and the unconfirmed DOJ reply in TerrAscend's 280E suit are two live diligence items PGP should flag to any client with Illinois hemp exposure or 280E-refund-dependent modeling — both could move fast once a ruling or filing actually surfaces.

The Rescheduling Docket

Illinois's Hemp-TRO Hearing Comes and Goes Without a Ruling as the DEA's ALJ Record and the Federal Hemp Standoff Both Hold Steady

Today's only new docket movement is procedural: a hearing was held, not decided. The DEA's stayed Schedule I-to-III record and the federal hemp-THC legislative standoff are both unchanged from yesterday.

  • Nov 5, 2026 DEA rescheduling opponents and proponents file final post-hearing reply briefs with Chief ALJ Derek Julius, completing the written-argument round; no date has been set for his recommended decision.
  • Nov 17, 2026 Sens. Tim Sheehy (R-Mont.) and Amy Klobuchar (D-Minn.) introduce the bipartisan "Hemp Guardrails Act" (S.5736), redefining hemp at a 1% total-THC dry-weight threshold with age-21 rules, child-resistant packaging and a beverage tax, aimed at a year-end spending vehicle ahead of Dec. 11.
  • Nov 18, 2026 Sens. Ted Budd (R-N.C.) and Pete Ricketts (R-Neb.) say they will try to block any government funding bill that "extends, delays, or builds a regulatory framework around" hemp-derived THC, pushing against the Sheehy-Klobuchar bill one day after its introduction. Aurora Cannabis's record date for its Dec. 23 Organigram vote also lands this day.
  • Nov 19, 2026 U.S. District Judge Martha Pacold hears oral arguments on the Illinois Healthy Alternatives Association's TRO/preliminary-injunction motion against the state's hemp-THC ban, covering roughly 250 member businesses whose negotiated enforcement pause expired today; no ruling issued as of this edition. TerrAscend's $8.36M 280E case also reaches its DOJ reply deadline, with no filing content confirmed.
