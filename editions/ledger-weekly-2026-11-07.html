Organigram Topples Curaleaf's Hostile Bid With a Signed C$561 Million Deal for Aurora Cannabis, as Four MSOs Report a Mixed Earnings Week and Massachusetts Crushes a Legalization-Repeal Vote 76%–24%

MSOS closed Friday, Nov. 6 at $4.24, up 7.6% from last Friday's $3.94 close — lifted above all by Aurora Cannabis's 29.1% weekly surge after Organigram Global signed a definitive, board-recommended C$7.80-per-share acquisition agreement for Aurora on Nov. 3, instantly besting Curaleaf's standing hostile tender and setting up a standoff that now runs through Curaleaf's Dec. 4 deadline. Four MSO watchlist names reported Q3 2026 results this week — Curaleaf (record $353M revenue, beat), Green Thumb ($324.4M revenue beat, EPS miss), Trulieve ($230.2M, revenue miss, EPS beat, return to profit) and TerrAscend (beat, 17th straight quarter of positive operating cash flow) — alongside Cresco Labs's own Q3 miss and same-day Zacks downgrade to Hold. Off the tape, a decisive Election Night saw Massachusetts voters crush an organized Question 8 repeal effort 76%–24%, Texas Democrat Vikki Goodwin unseat 12-year hemp-crackdown architect Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick, and Wisconsin Democrats — newly in full control of state government for the first time in 16 years — pledge to legalize marijuana within one to two years. No. 1 · M&A · Hostile Topped

Organigram signs a board-recommended $561 million deal for Aurora Cannabis, topping Curaleaf's hostile bid — which stays open, unsweetened, through Dec. 4

On Nov. 3, Organigram Global and Aurora Cannabis announced a definitive arrangement agreement under which Aurora shareholders can elect C$7.80 per share in cash, 5.8626 Organigram shares, or a mixed C$3.75-cash-plus-3.0431-share alternative — a fully diluted equity value of roughly C$561 million (~US$401 million), a 93% premium to Aurora's unaffected Aug. 10 close and a 21% premium over Curaleaf's amended hostile offer. Aurora's board unanimously recommends the Organigram deal and advises shareholders not to tender into Curaleaf's bid. The transaction is financed in part by Organigram's largest shareholder, British American Tobacco, which agreed to buy 141 million Organigram shares at C$1.33 apiece (roughly £100 million) in a private placement, plus bank financing from ATB Financial. Aurora shares were halted by CIRO for "pending news" at 8:26am ET and resumed up as much as 22.5% on Nasdaq; they finished the week up 29.1% to $5.24. Curaleaf, whose own enhanced tender ($1.00 cash plus 0.4013 Curaleaf shares, capped at $6.00) remains open through 11:59pm MT on Dec. 4, responded the same day by accusing Aurora's board of "gross dereliction of their fiduciary duties" for granting Organigram diligence access it denied Curaleaf, and maintained its own bid "remains outstanding." Two days later, on Curaleaf's own Nov. 3 earnings call, Chairman and CEO Boris Jordan told analysts Curaleaf is "going to improve our bid substantially" — conditioned on full due-diligence access he called an "absolute condition" — but as of this edition Curaleaf has filed no revised terms with any regulator. TD Cowen downgraded Aurora to Sell on Nov. 4, not on the deal's merits but because it views the contest as effectively settled, recommending holders tender into Organigram's offer. Aurora and Organigram are each targeting special shareholder meetings on or before Dec. 23, 2026 (in any event by Dec. 31), with a targeted close in January 2027; Aurora separately confirmed it has cancelled its own Nov. 12 investor call rather than hold one mid-contest.

Why it matters to PGP A signed, board-recommended deal at a 21% premium to the standing hostile bid is normally dispositive — except Curaleaf's CEO has now publicly promised a "substantial" higher offer without filing one, which is exactly the kind of unresolved gap between rhetoric and paper that should stop any client from pricing this deal as closed. PGP should track two things over the next 30 days: whether Curaleaf actually files amended tender terms with its own diligence-access condition met or waived, and whether Aurora's board grants that access at all — its Nov. 4 cancellation of a shareholder-facing investor call, mid-contest, reads as a signal it does not intend to reopen that door before the still-unscheduled December vote.
No. 2 · Earnings

A split verdict on Q3: Curaleaf and TerrAscend beat, Trulieve and Green Thumb miss on one line each, and Cresco's miss draws a same-day downgrade

Curaleaf reported record Q3 2026 revenue of $353 million (+11% YoY; domestic +8%, international +28%), adjusted EBITDA of $78 million (22% margin) and $105 million of cash, prompting a 10.5% single-session stock pop that partly reversed by midweek. Green Thumb Industries posted revenue of $324.4 million (+11.3%, ahead of a $313.2 million estimate) and its first positive comparable-sales quarter (+3.0%) since Q3 2024, but swung to a $4.8 million GAAP net loss that missed a $0.05-per-share estimate; Roth Capital trimmed its price target to $18 from $20 while keeping a Buy, and Seaport Research raised its own target to $18 on Friday. Trulieve's revenue fell 20% year-over-year to $230.2 million, missing a $273.7 million estimate, but the company returned to GAAP profitability ($1 million net income), posted a 39%-margin, $89 million adjusted EBITDA quarter and confirmed it has received its final federal DEA registrations under the Schedule III order. TerrAscend beat with $68.2 million in revenue (+4.8%, against a $67.4 million estimate), a GAAP net loss that narrowed 45% to $5.5 million, 52.3% gross margin and its 17th consecutive quarter of positive operating cash flow, alongside the closed acquisition of a fifth New Jersey dispensary. Cresco Labs missed on revenue (roughly $172.8–$173 million, about $0.9 million short of consensus) even as its net loss narrowed 34% to $14.5 million and free cash flow hit $21 million; Zacks Research downgraded the stock to Hold the same day. Separately, Cresco disclosed that shareholders approved its board-recommended Delaware "TopCo" restructuring and a multiple-voting-share sunset extension by more than 94% on every resolution — a vote held Oct. 30 but not disclosed until Nov. 2.

Why it matters to PGP This week's four-earnings, one-restructuring-vote slate is a reminder that "beat" and "miss" are splitting along different lines for different MSOs — Green Thumb and TerrAscend are proving operating discipline can coexist with either revenue growth or an EPS miss, while Trulieve's double-digit revenue decline masks a genuine return to profitability. For diligence on any of these four names, the per-line read (revenue vs. EPS vs. cash flow) now matters more than a single headline "beat/miss" framing.
No. 3 · Politics · Election Night

Massachusetts crushes a legalization-repeal vote 76%–24%, Texas unseats its hemp-crackdown architect, and Wisconsin Democrats pledge to legalize

Massachusetts voters rejected Question 8 — a ballot measure to repeal the state's 2016 adult-use legalization — by an unofficial 76%–24% margin (86-90% of precincts reporting as of Wednesday morning), far outpacing pre-election polling that had shown only 61-71% opposed; it is the first state to decisively defeat an organized legalization-repeal effort, and the most expensive ballot question in state history, with the pro-repeal "Coalition for a Healthy Massachusetts" funded almost entirely by the Virginia-based dark-money group SAM Action. The same Nov. 3 election night, Texas Democrat Vikki Goodwin unseated 12-year Republican incumbent Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick 51.7%–45.6% — becoming the state's first woman elected to the office and removing the chief legislative architect of Texas's hemp-derived-THC crackdown. And in Wisconsin, Democrats swept to full control of state government for the first time in 16 years; incoming legislative leaders and Governor-elect David Crowley pledged Nov. 6 to legalize marijuana within one to two years, citing an estimated $80-170 million in annual state revenue. Separately, Houston voters passed Prop A, deprioritizing marijuana enforcement and banning no-knock warrants.

No. 4 · Regulatory · Rescheduling & Hemp-THC

The DEA's Schedule I-to-III case closes its final briefing round, while Missouri's and Illinois's hemp-THC ban fights resolve in opposite directions

Reply briefs were filed as scheduled Nov. 5 before Chief ALJ Derek Julius, closing out the final round of written argument in the stayed Schedule I-to-III rescheduling case: the DEA's reply reiterates that the admitted GAO report (GAO-26-108623) deserves "no weight," while a joint reply from opponents (NDASA, DUID Victim Voices, Dr. Kenneth Finn) argues the same report shows "fatal flaws" in the agency's record. No recommendation timeline has been announced; this desk holds its working 1H 2027 estimate for a recommended decision unchanged. Separately, two states' own hemp-derived-THC ban fights moved in opposite directions ahead of the same Nov. 12 effective date: a federal judge in Missouri (Harpool, W.D. Mo.) dismissed the hemp industry's challenge to that state's ban on Nov. 4, clearing it to take effect; a day later, the Illinois Healthy Alternatives Association and hemp businesses filed an emergency motion for a TRO and preliminary injunction against Illinois's own Hemp Act ban, arguing a Commerce Clause violation — with no ruling yet as of this edition. Illinois's governor, lieutenant governor and a U.S. senator separately reiterated calls for the federal Dec. 11 "total THC" deadline to proceed without further delay.
