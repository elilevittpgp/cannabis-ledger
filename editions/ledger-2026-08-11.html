Page One · The Big Story

Six MSOs, One Earnings Week — And Vireo Won't Stop Buying

Green Thumb, Trulieve, Curaleaf, Cresco, TerrAscend and Verano all post Q2 2026 growth inside five trading days, while Vireo Growth closes another roll-up target and keeps building toward Planet 13.

The dominant story on the desk this week is breadth, not a single deal. In the five trading days between Aug. 4 and Aug. 10, six MSO watchlist names — Green Thumb Industries, Trulieve, Curaleaf, Cresco Labs, TerrAscend and Verano — all reported second-quarter 2026 results, and every one showed sequential or year-over-year growth: Green Thumb's revenue rose to $306.7M (27.5% EBITDA margin), Trulieve posted $271M on a 60% gross margin, Verano grew revenue 8% YoY to $218M with EBITDA up to $51M, and Cresco and TerrAscend both delivered sequential gains. Verano shares jumped roughly 4.3% on its print.

Vireo Growth kept moving through the same window: it closed its acquisition of PharmaCann's 17 Colorado retail assets for roughly $49M in stock on Aug. 7, lined up a new $65M asset-based credit facility the same day, and reports its own Q2 results today — all while its all-stock, 24%-premium bid for Planet 13 (announced Jul. 27) still awaits a shareholder vote. The uplisting wave is also still running: Verano has already completed a 1-for-5 reverse split, TerrAscend set an Aug. 24 vote on a share consolidation, and Ascend Wellness is pushing holders to approve its own reverse split ahead of an Aug. 28 meeting.

Why it matters to PGP A synchronized earnings beat across the MSO complex — alongside a stack of reverse-split/uplisting mechanics moving toward completion (Verano done, TerrAscend and Ascend voting this month) — points to a sector building institutional-grade capital structures faster than the rescheduling docket is resolving. That widens the gap between names executing cleanly (Green Thumb, Trulieve, Verano) and distressed or thinly-traded names still working through restructuring (AYR, Jushi, Cannabist). Expect continued sell-side flow from Vireo's roll-up appetite and from Cannabist's bankruptcy estate — both plausible sourcing lanes for PGP mandates.

The Rescheduling Docket

Schedule I → III: briefing period underway, and a new fault line in hemp

The DEA record stays open for post-hearing briefs while Congress tries to carve hemp beverages out of the December cliff facing the rest of the hemp-THC category.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E.
  • Jun 24–29, 2026 DEA ALJ hearings open; SAFER Banking reintroduced in both chambers.
  • Jul 15, 2026 Hearings conclude; record stays open for post-hearing briefs.
  • Aug 3, 2026 Senate votes 89-4 to advance a stopgap funding bill, pushing the hemp-THC ban's toughest restrictions to Dec 11 and rejecting an amendment that would have kept the earlier Nov 12 deadline.
  • Early Aug 2026 Reps. Van Duyne (R-TX) and Landsman (D-OH) introduce the Beverage Regulatory Parity Act — a three-tier, alcohol-style federal framework (5mg THC/serving cap) that would carve hemp-THC drinks out of the broader ban.
  • Jun–Aug, 2026 States keep maneuvering: Wyoming opts out (Jul 8), Louisiana withdraws its rescheduling objection (Jun), Alabama opens a 30-day State Board of Health review (Aug 3) — fragmenting 280E & interstate treatment.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. Aug 17, 2026 — post-hearing briefs due.
  2. ALJ issues a recommended decision — trackers now flag late 2026 as more likely than an earlier call.
  3. 20-day exceptions window for parties.
  4. DEA Administrator's final call — a Final Rule could slip into early 2027 if the process runs long.
PGP takeaway The Beverage Regulatory Parity Act creates a durable split within hemp: an alcohol-style compliance path for THC drinks versus a hard Dec 11 cliff for smokable/edible SKUs. Combine that with rescheduling-timeline slippage into late 2026 / early 2027, and 280E-relief modeling should push out expected timing rather than assume a near-term resolution — diligence stays state-by-state and category-by-category.
