Page One · The Big Story

The DEA Publishes Its Rescheduling Record as Ascend's Proxy Clock Runs Out

A 2,533-page corrected hearing transcript goes live a day ahead of deadline, with Judge Julius giving no hint of timing — while, hours away, Ascend Wellness's reverse-split proxy window closes with its Aug. 20 stock spike still unexplained.

The DEA closed out a procedural chapter of the rescheduling fight on Tuesday, posting the full corrected transcript of the 11-day Schedule I→III hearing — 2,533 pages, incorporating the 294 corrections Chief Administrative Law Judge Derek C. Julius ordered on Aug. 20 — a day ahead of Wednesday's Aug. 26 deadline. The record now includes the DEA's own post-hearing brief argument, via attorney James Schwartz, that "marijuana can no longer remain in Schedule I." Judge Julius offered no hint of timing beyond saying he would "thoroughly consider and evaluate all the evidence," with coverage of the docket expecting a recommended decision to DEA Administrator Terry Cole in weeks or months, not days — the transcript's release is a milestone in the record, not a signal the recommendation itself is imminent.

Ascend Wellness's own clock is shorter and more concrete. A reminder release confirms that proxy votes on the company's 1-for-10-to-1-for-50 reverse stock split — the mechanism meant to clear Ascend for a U.S. exchange uplisting — are due today, Wednesday, Aug. 26, at 11 a.m. ET, two days ahead of the Aug. 28 special meeting itself. No ISS or Glass Lewis recommendation on the vote has surfaced despite repeated searches, and the sharp, high-volume intraday spike in Ascend's stock on Aug. 20 remains without a company statement, filing, or analyst note to explain it, six days on. Elsewhere the sector kept climbing: MSOS closed Tuesday, Aug. 25 at $4.92, up 0.61% on the day and roughly 16.3% month-to-date, while the Curaleaf–Aurora Cannabis bid fight produced no new statements beyond Monday's dueling releases.

Why it matters to PGP A public, corrected hearing record is a genuine procedural milestone, but it resolves nothing about timing — model the ALJ's recommendation, the 20-day exceptions window, and the Administrator's final call as a multi-month tail risk, not a near-term catalyst, even as headlines about the transcript circulate. Closer to home, an unexplained volume spike sitting on top of an active reverse-split vote is exactly the kind of pattern a diligence team should flag before relying on a target's public float or insider-ownership assumptions — the absence of an explanation is itself the data point.

Industry Headlines

House GOP's hemp fight sharpens ahead of the Aug. 31 return

Leadership may need a two-thirds suspension-of-the-rules vote to preserve the Dec. 11 hemp-ban delay if Freedom Caucus members balk at a standard floor rule — a hurdle Andy Harris's faction has resisted before, on the same day roughly 40% of Senate Republicans opposed the delay on Aug 8. Roll Call

Fourth Circuit: a vacated marijuana conviction isn't a "conviction"

In Piedra v. Blanche, the appeals court rejected the BIA's framework for treating a vacated North Carolina marijuana conviction as grounds for inadmissibility — a narrow but real precedent for noncitizens with vacated cannabis records. Fourth Circuit

Florida's medical market posts a record sales pace

August sales are tracking toward roughly $198.2M across 939,639 active patients, a record monthly pace for the state's medical program even as the plant-touching MSO tape stays mixed. The Marijuana Herald

Illinois expands its qualifying-conditions list to 58

Regulators added sickle cell disease and polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome, effective immediately — a small but steady widening of the patient base in one of the sector's largest state markets. The Marijuana Herald

Germany's medical cannabis boom is redrawing the global supply map

German-grown medical cannabis is pressuring Canadian exporters' historical dominance of the import market, a supply-chain shift with implications for any cross-border cultivation or export mandate. MJBizDaily
