Page One · The Week in Review

Curaleaf Takes the Aurora Fight Straight to the Shareholders, TerrAscend Answers a Tax Suit With a Procedural Counterpunch, and the Tape Claws Back a Rough Week

A four-day escalation — a securities-regulator filing, a shareholder letter, a brochure filing and a live CEO Q&A — still hasn't produced an Alberta Securities Commission hearing date; TerrAscend moves to dismiss the industry's first federal 280E clawback suit on venue and entity-naming grounds rather than contest it on the merits; the DEA's rescheduling recommendation passes nine weeks overdue as a new Supreme Court petition opens a second licensing front; and MSOS recovers roughly 3% on the week even as Curaleaf's own stock is the sector's standout gainer.

No. 1 · M&A & Governance

Curaleaf escalates from regulator to retail investors — still no ASC hearing date on the underlying dispute

The Curaleaf–Aurora Cannabis hostile-bid fight moved through four distinct phases in five days. On Sept 14, Curaleaf filed an application with the Alberta Securities Commission seeking an expedited hearing to halt further issuances under Aurora's at-the-market equity program, alleging Aurora had issued roughly 2.81 million shares at an average of US$3.04 since June — about 4.9% dilution and more than $11 million of added deal cost — as a defensive tactic. Aurora rebutted hours later, calling the filing a distraction and noting the ATM was announced in February, months before the bid, and funds growth rather than takeover defense. Rather than wait on a ruling, Curaleaf pivoted directly to Aurora's shareholder base: a Sept 15 letter reiterating the offer (0.3463 Curaleaf shares plus US$0.75 cash per Aurora share, a stated 45% premium to the unaffected 30-day VWAP), a Sept 16 SEC brochure filing, and a live, webcast shareholder Q&A with Chairman/CEO Boris Jordan on Sept 17. As of Friday afternoon, the ASC has neither scheduled a hearing nor ruled on the ATM dispute, and Aurora's board continues to recommend shareholders take no action. The bid — capped at $5.00/share — still expires Dec 1 unless extended.

Why it matters to PGP This week supplies a complete playbook for contested cross-border cannabis takeovers: a securities-regulator complaint over defensive dilution, followed — without waiting for a ruling — by a direct-to-shareholder letter, an SEC brochure filing, and a live CEO Q&A. That a bidder escalated past the regulator before it even ruled suggests Curaleaf doesn't expect the ASC route alone to move Aurora's board before December. PGP's advisory practice should treat ATM-during-bid conduct, and the four-stage escalation ladder itself, as a template worth pricing into any future target-defense or hostile-bid mandate — the ASC's continued silence on a hearing date is itself the operative fact, more than either side's public statements.
No. 2 · Tax & Litigation

TerrAscend answers the DOJ's 280E suit with a motion to dismiss, not a check

A day ahead of its Sept 18 response deadline, TerrAscend filed a 38-page motion to dismiss on Sept 17 in the U.S. District Court for the District of New Jersey, in what's understood to be the industry's first federal 280E refund-clawback suit. The motion doesn't contest whether the underlying $8.36M refund was properly owed; it argues the case can't proceed at all: the government sued "TerrAscend USA, Inc. & Subsidiaries," a consolidated tax-return label rather than a real legal entity; New Jersey lacks personal jurisdiction over a Delaware-organized company operating from Florida, Ohio and Connecticut; venue is improper because the original return was filed from California and the amended return mailed to the IRS in Utah; service was defective and more than 40 days late; and the government allegedly never routed the refund through the Joint Committee on Taxation review the law requires above a certain size. A briefing schedule is now set: government response due Oct 5, TerrAscend's reply Oct 13, motion date Oct 19. The same week, TerrAscend closed its acquisition of Aunt Mary's Dispensary in Hunterdon County, NJ — its fifth New Jersey location, for up to $9M — a reminder that bolt-on M&A continues at the same operator fighting the clawback suit.

Why it matters to PGP TerrAscend's motion is a template worth filing across the portfolio: wrong-entity, wrong-venue and missing-JCT-review arguments are procedural defenses, not substantive ones. A win keeps the underlying 280E-refund question undecided industry-wide and deters future DOJ clawback suits on paperwork grounds; a loss pushes the case toward the merits that actually determine how retroactive Schedule III-era refund claims get treated. Any client holding a similar refund should have Oct 5/13/19 on the calendar regardless of TerrAscend's outcome.
No. 3 · Federal Regulatory

The ALJ's silence passes nine weeks as a new Supreme Court petition opens a second licensing front

DEA Chief ALJ Derek Julius still had not issued a recommended decision on full Schedule I→III rescheduling as of Friday, now more than nine weeks past the July 15 close of hearings — even after the DEA published the complete 2,533-page hearing transcript earlier this month as a procedural step. Separately, a Supreme Court petition surfaced this week with a name: Peridot Tree WA, Inc. v. Washington State Liquor and Cannabis Control Board, filed by Michigan resident Kenneth Gay, asking the justices to resolve a circuit split — the Ninth Circuit found the dormant Commerce Clause inapplicable to cannabis licensing, while the First and Second Circuits found residency-based licensing preferences likely unconstitutional. On banking, Sens. Catherine Cortez Masto and Jeff Merkley pushed the Senate SAFE Banking Act with a seven-member bipartisan cosponsor list (Murkowski, Daines, Warren, Sullivan, Cramer, Murray) as a companion to the House's June reintroduction — timed awkwardly against last week's GAO finding that Visa and Mastercard are unlikely to loosen card-network restrictions regardless of further rescheduling.

No. 4 · M&A & Restructuring

Vireo Growth extinguishes a lender's claims with stock as its two largest pending deals stay unclosed

Vireo Growth issued 462,963 subordinate voting shares (six-month lock-up) on Sept 17 to Altmore Debt I, LP under a confidential settlement, extinguishing secured obligations and related liens Altmore held against the company — a cash-free mechanism worth noting for any distressed-roll-up financing. Vireo's two largest pending acquisitions remain unclosed: the all-stock merger with Planet 13 (0.015383618 Vireo shares per Planet 13 share) still has no SEC S-4 effectiveness or shareholder-vote date eight-plus weeks after signing, and the staged asset purchase of Cannabist Company's CO/IL/MA/NJ/WV operations out of its CCAA estate continues without a new milestone this window. Elsewhere in restructuring, Advanced Flower Capital's Article 9 foreclosure on Justice Grown's collateral ($78.8M loan principal in maturity default) still guides to a Pennsylvania auction around Sept 24 and New Jersey collateral around Oct 6, unconfirmed this week.
