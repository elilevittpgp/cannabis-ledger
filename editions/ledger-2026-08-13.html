Ascend Beats: $126.1 Million in Q2 Revenue as Illinois Comes Back Online

Losses narrow sharply, EBITDA margin expands, and the Barry, Illinois plant returns to full capacity after June's strike — while Curaleaf's hostile bid for Aurora Cannabis keeps drawing fire, and Vireo Growth's roll-up posts another triple-digit revenue jump.

Ascend Wellness Holdings (OTCQX: AAWH) reported second-quarter 2026 net revenue of $126.1 million Aug. 12, up 7.9% sequentially and ahead of the roughly $119.7 million analysts had modeled. The GAAP net loss narrowed to $9.8 million from $29.5 million in Q1, and adjusted EBITDA rose to $29.1 million — a 23.1% margin, up from 22.5% the prior quarter. Retail sales climbed 11.5% sequentially to $92.7 million on 55 open stores, and management reiterated a year-end target of 60. CFO Roman Nemchenko told analysts the Barry, Illinois cultivation and processing facility — site of June's roughly 30-day Teamsters Local 916 strike, the largest in U.S. cannabis history — is "back and operating at full capacity," though residual wholesale-delivery disruption from July will still weigh on the next quarter or two. CEO Sam Brill guided Q3 sequential revenue growth of 2-4% and said the company is "engaged in dozens of M&A conversations" for distressed, cash-generating retail tuck-ins. Brill also tied Ascend's planned reverse split and exchange uplisting — up for a shareholder vote Aug. 28 — to progress on federal rescheduling, noting DEA registration applications already filed under an "exercises pathway" for its state-licensed medical operations.

Elsewhere, Aurora Cannabis's special committee is now formally reviewing Curaleaf's unsolicited $272 million bid, with Aurora disputing Curaleaf's account of prior outreach and TD Cowen analysts calling the offer undervalued. Vireo Growth posted Q2 revenue up 335% year-over-year and confirmed it closed its PharmaCann Colorado deal and entered Pennsylvania, while Planet 13 filed its last standalone earnings before the pending Vireo merger closes. Verano and Premier Group/PCF were both quiet in the 24–48 hour window; see Priority Coverage below.

Why it matters to PGP A genuine earnings beat — not just a pre-announced catalyst — with narrowing losses, expanding margin, and a strike-recovery narrative gives Ascend's board a stronger negotiating position heading into the Aug. 28 reverse-split/uplisting vote than it had a week ago. For any client evaluating Ascend as a counterparty (financing, M&A, or otherwise), the read-through is that operational execution is improving faster than the balance sheet is de-risking — net debt still sits near $252 million — so diligence should weight the improving EBITDA trajectory against the still-elevated leverage rather than treating either signal alone as dispositive.

The Rescheduling Docket

Hemp-THC fight spreads to the courts as GOP cracks on the White House's line

The DEA's Schedule I→III docket itself is unchanged, but hemp-THC enforcement is now actively litigated in two states, and a sitting Republican senator is publicly breaking with the White House over the Dec 11 ban.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E. Three consolidated challenges to the order remain pending in the D.C. Circuit (Nos. 26-1106, 26-1130, 26-1136), no new activity this window.
  • Jul 15, 2026 DEA hearings conclude; record stays open for post-hearing briefs, due Aug 17 (50-page cap, Chief ALJ Derek Julius). No new filings or ALJ statements this window.
  • Aug 3, 2026 Senate passes the CR 90-6, pushing the toughest hemp-THC restrictions to Dec 11.
  • Aug 10, 2026 Beverage Regulatory Parity Act introduced (Van Duyne/Landsman); no new co-sponsors or competing bills since introduction.
  • Aug 11, 2026 A federal judge in Texas denies an emergency TRO sought by hemp retailers against the state's delta-8/delta-10 ban; a broader preliminary-injunction request remains pending.
  • Aug 12, 2026 A five-hour hearing in E.D. Virginia (Judge Robert S. Ballou) on seven hemp businesses' bid to block HB 30's new 2mg-per-package THC cap ends without a ruling; a decision is expected before an Aug 15 enforcement deadline.
  • Aug 12, 2026 Sen. Ted Budd (R-NC) publicly says Trump is "misinformed" by White House staff on the hemp-THC ban; reporting ties the Dec 11 delay in part to a letter from CMS Director Mehmet Oz citing "grave concern" that an early ban would undercut a Medicare hemp-coverage initiative.
  • Jun–Aug, 2026 States keep maneuvering: Wyoming opts out (Jul 8), Louisiana withdraws its rescheduling objection (Jun), Alabama's written-comment period closed Aug 5 with no State Board of Health decision yet.

  2. Aug 15, 2026 — Virginia HB 30 THC-cap enforcement deadline.

  4. Aug 24, 2026 — TerrAscend special shareholder meeting on a share consolidation tied to a potential U.S. uplisting.
  5. Watch for Alabama's State Board of Health decision, still pending after the Aug 5 comment deadline.
PGP takeaway The DEA's core Schedule III timeline hasn't moved, but the hemp-THC fight is now a live litigation risk in multiple states ahead of Dec 11, and the Budd/Oz reporting is the first real window into why the administration delayed the ban — a Medicare-coverage angle, not purely industry lobbying. Diligence on hemp-beverage or hemp-THC exposure should track the Virginia ruling closely; a plaintiff win there could embolden similar challenges elsewhere before the federal deadline hits.
