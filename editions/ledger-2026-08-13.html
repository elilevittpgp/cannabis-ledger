Ascend Beats on Q2 as Trulieve Completes Its Move to Delaware

Ascend's $126.1 million quarter and post-strike recovery in Illinois anchor the week, while Trulieve closes its British Columbia-to-Delaware redomicile, Curaleaf's hostile bid for Aurora Cannabis grinds through special-committee review, and Vireo Growth backs up its 335% revenue jump with a new buyback.

Ascend Wellness Holdings (OTCQX: AAWH) reported second-quarter 2026 net revenue of $126.1 million Aug. 12, up 7.9% sequentially and ahead of the roughly $119.7 million analysts had modeled. The GAAP net loss narrowed to $9.8 million from $29.5 million in Q1, and adjusted EBITDA rose to $29.1 million — a 23.1% margin, up from 22.5% the prior quarter. Retail sales climbed 11.5% sequentially to $92.7 million on 55 open stores, and management reiterated a year-end target of 60. CFO Roman Nemchenko told analysts the Barry, Illinois cultivation and processing facility — site of June's roughly 30-day Teamsters Local 916 strike, the largest in U.S. cannabis history — is "back and operating at full capacity," though residual wholesale-delivery disruption from July will still weigh on the next quarter or two. A fresh sweep of Ascend's IR feed, wire services and labor channels through Thursday afternoon turned up nothing new beyond that Q2 release — no follow-on grievance activity at Barry, no fresh analyst notes, and no new M&A disclosure beyond the "dozens of conversations" CEO Sam Brill described on the call.

Elsewhere, Trulieve Cannabis completed its previously announced redomiciliation from British Columbia to Delaware on Aug. 11, following the BC Supreme Court's final order (Aug. 10) and shareholder approval (Aug. 5); all BC subordinate and multiple voting shares converted 1-for-1 into Delaware Class A and Class B common stock, with trading continuing on the NYSE under the new ticker TRLV. Aurora Cannabis's special committee continues reviewing Curaleaf's unsolicited $272 million bid, with TD Cowen still calling the offer light against Aurora's medical-cannabis franchise and no counter-bid yet in sight. Vireo Growth, fresh off Q2 revenue up 335% year-over-year, authorized a buyback of up to roughly 2.4 million shares Aug. 13 and confirmed it drew on a new $65 million asset-based credit facility (expandable to $105 million) to help fund its PharmaCann Colorado close. Verano and Premier Group/PCF were both quiet again in the 24–48 hour window; see Priority Coverage below. Glass House Brands reports second-quarter results after today's close, with a call set for 5 p.m. ET.

Why it matters to PGP A genuine earnings beat — not just a pre-announced catalyst — with narrowing losses, expanding margin, and a strike-recovery narrative gives Ascend's board a stronger negotiating position heading into the Aug. 28 reverse-split/uplisting vote than it had a week ago; net debt still sits near $252 million, so diligence should weight the improving EBITDA trajectory against the still-elevated leverage rather than treating either signal alone as dispositive. Separately, Trulieve's completed redomicile is a useful template for any MSO client weighing a similar structural move ahead of potential rescheduling-driven index inclusion — court approval, shareholder vote and a 1-for-1 conversion closed in roughly five weeks once the process was filed.

The Rescheduling Docket

Virginia judge holds off on a ruling as the Dec 11 hemp-THC deadline looms

The DEA's Schedule I→III docket itself is unchanged, but the Virginia hemp-THC case that could set precedent ahead of the federal deadline is still awaiting a ruling, with one expected within days.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E. Three consolidated challenges to the order remain pending in the D.C. Circuit (Nos. 26-1106, 26-1130, 26-1136), no new activity this window.
  • Jul 15, 2026 DEA hearings conclude; record stays open for post-hearing briefs, due Aug 17 (50-page cap, Chief ALJ Derek Julius). No new filings or ALJ statements this window.
  • Aug 3, 2026 Senate passes the CR 90-6, pushing the toughest hemp-THC restrictions to Dec 11, over floor objections from Sens. Ted Budd (R-NC) and Pete Ricketts (R-NE).
  • Aug 10, 2026 Beverage Regulatory Parity Act introduced (Van Duyne/Landsman) — a 5mg/serving THC cap, three-tier framework, 8-cents-per-mg excise tax; no new co-sponsors or competing bills since introduction.
  • Aug 9–11, 2026 A federal judge in Texas (S.D. Tex., Judge Jeffrey Brown) denies an emergency TRO sought by hemp retailers against the state's delta-8/delta-10 ban, citing a failure to show irreparable harm on due-process and Commerce Clause claims; a broader preliminary-injunction request, with fuller briefing, remains pending.
  • Aug 12, 2026 A five-hour hearing in E.D. Virginia (Judge Robert S. Ballou) on seven hemp businesses' bid to block HB 30's new 2mg-per-package THC cap ends without a ruling from the bench; plaintiffs' counsel Chap Petersen says a decision is expected "by Friday," ahead of the Aug 15 enforcement deadline.
  • Jun–Aug, 2026 States keep maneuvering: Wyoming opts out (Jul 8), Louisiana withdraws its rescheduling objection (Jun), Alabama's written-comment period closed Aug 5 with no State Board of Health decision yet — State Health Officer Scott Harris has said the department "fully intends" to implement rescheduling once federal action is final.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. Aug 14, 2026 (expected) — ruling in the Virginia hemp-THC case, per plaintiffs' counsel Chap Petersen.
  2. Aug 15, 2026 — Virginia HB 30 THC-cap enforcement deadline.
  3. Aug 17, 2026 — DEA post-hearing briefs due (unchanged; 50-page cap).
  4. Aug 24, 2026 — TerrAscend special shareholder meeting on a share consolidation tied to a potential U.S. uplisting.
  5. Watch for Alabama's State Board of Health decision, still pending after the Aug 5 comment deadline.
PGP takeaway The DEA's core Schedule III timeline hasn't moved, but the Virginia ruling — expected within a day or two — is the nearer-term catalyst: a plaintiff win there ahead of the Aug 15 deadline could embolden similar hemp-THC challenges in other states before the federal Dec 11 ban takes effect, while a loss would hand ban proponents a template state legislatures elsewhere may look to replicate. Diligence on hemp-beverage or hemp-THC exposure should track this ruling closely.
