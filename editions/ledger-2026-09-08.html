Curaleaf Goes on Offense: A Public Fact-Sheet War Reopens the Aurora Bid Fight as the Tape Gives Back Friday's High

Six days after Aurora's board told shareholders to reject the offer, Curaleaf answers with a line-by-line rebuttal — while MSOS opens the post-Labor Day week down from its best close of the rally.

Tuesday is the first trading session since Friday, Sept. 4 — U.S. markets were closed Monday for Labor Day — and the desk's two live threads both point the same direction: nothing has been resolved, but nothing is quiet either. Curaleaf published a shareholder fact sheet today rebutting Aurora Cannabis's Sept. 2 rejection point by point, arguing its offer (US$4.00 per share base, capped at US$5.00, roughly 19% cash and 81% stock) sits in the 63rd percentile of Canadian M&A premiums over the last decade and implies a 12x CY2026E adjusted EBITDA multiple — more than 68% above comparable Canadian peers. Curaleaf's sharpest line: Aurora has booked roughly C$5 billion in impairments and C$130 million in transformation costs over six years and burned more than C$480 million in operating cash since fiscal 2021, against Curaleaf's US$447 million of positive operating cash flow over the same stretch. The bid remains open under its original terms, with a statutory 105-day tender period (shortenable to 35 days) running to Dec. 1, 2026.

Away from the bid fight, the corporate calendar stayed thin. Ascend, Premier Group/PCF and Verano were all quiet on filings — Verano's most recent item is a Sept. 2 conference-attendance notice ahead of tomorrow's ATB Cormark event in New York. On the tape, the session opened as a partial reversal of Friday's rally: MSOS traded down 1.08% to $5.06 intraday, giving back roughly a fifth of Friday's 3.23% gain, while Glass House (-3.77%) and TerrAscend (-4.21%, TSX, C$) led constituent laggards and Ascend Wellness (+4.42%, no release found) bucked the pullback.

Why it matters to PGP Two things for the desk. First, the Curaleaf/Aurora fight has shifted from a headline premium number to a battle of financial narratives — impairments, cash-burn history, and EBITDA-multiple comparisons — the exact diligence framing that would carry into a friendly re-cut or a proxy contest if this bid converts. Second, today's broad pullback across MSOS, Glass House and TerrAscend with no company-specific news behind it is the mirror image of last week's unexplained rally: read single sessions as sentiment and liquidity, not as a fundamentals signal, on either side of the tape.

The Rescheduling Docket

Eight weeks past the close of hearings, still no recommended decision

A new adjacent legal thread surfaced today; the core Schedule I→III catalyst remains unresolved.

  • Sep 2, 2026 President Trump signs H.R. 6500 into law — the Dec 11 hemp-ban delay is enacted for naturally-occurring cannabinoids under a stricter 0.4mg-total-THC-per-container standard; converted/synthetic cannabinoids (delta-8, HHC, THC-O) stay excluded and lose hemp status Nov 12.
  • Sep 2, 2026 Aurora Cannabis's board formally rejects Curaleaf's hostile bid via its Directors' Circular.
  • Sep 4, 2026 FDA opens a public docket (FDA-2026-N-9550) on botanical drug development, a pathway that can include cannabis-derived medicines; comments due Nov 3.
  • Sep 5-7, 2026 No DEA recommended decision issued over the long weekend; Chief ALJ Derek Julius's timeline remains unset, nearly eight weeks after hearings closed Jul 15.
  • Sep 8, 2026 A Congressional Research Service report, circulated in trade coverage today, flags a circuit split (1st/2nd vs. 9th Circuits) over whether the dormant Commerce Clause bars residency-based cannabis licensing preferences — a separate legal thread that could eventually reach the Supreme Court, distinct from but adjacent to the rescheduling docket.
  • Sep 8, 2026 Curaleaf publishes a shareholder fact sheet rebutting Aurora Cannabis's rejection, reopening the hostile-bid fight after six days of silence.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. ALJ Julius's recommended decision on broad Schedule I→III rescheduling — still no fixed date; DEA's own post-hearing brief already urges Schedule III.
  2. Sep 15, 2026 — court hearing on Advanced Flower Capital's foreclosure against Justice Grown's PA/NJ collateral.
  3. Sep 30, 2026 — deadline for the administration to answer lawmakers' rescheduling-implementation letter.
  4. Nov 12, 2026 — converted/synthetic cannabinoids (delta-8, HHC, THC-O) lose federal hemp status; no extension applies to this category.
  5. Dec 1, 2026 — Curaleaf's bid for Aurora Cannabis expires unless extended, following today's rebuttal.
  6. Dec 11, 2026 — the enacted effective date for naturally-occurring hemp cannabinoids, under the 0.4mg-total-THC-per-container standard.
PGP takeaway The rescheduling recommendation remains the single largest unscheduled catalyst on the desk's board — 280E relief flows straight to operator EBITDA and re-rates every model. Today's CRS circuit-split item is a reminder that state-level licensing law (residency preferences, opt-outs) is its own live diligence variable, independent of the federal rescheduling timeline.
