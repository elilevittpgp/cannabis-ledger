Page One · The Big Story

The Tape Rips on the Aurora Fight — MSOS Jumps 5.5% While the Priority Desk Stays Dark

Curaleaf and Aurora Cannabis lead a broad, every-name-green session as the hostile-bid campaign intensifies; Ascend, Verano and Premier Group/PCF all go a second-plus straight session without a filing.

The cannabis tape had its best session in weeks Tuesday, with the AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF (MSOS) trading around $5.21 intraday, up roughly 5.47% — every name in this desk's basket traded green, a sharper and more uniform move than any session this desk has tracked over the past two weeks. The rally is concentrated in the two combatants at the center of the sector's live M&A story: Curaleaf (+8.71%) and Aurora Cannabis (+5.21%), whose hostile-bid fight has now run more than five weeks without the Alberta Securities Commission setting a hearing date on Curaleaf's application to halt Aurora's ATM share program. Curaleaf has escalated into a full public shareholder campaign — canvassing Aurora holders directly for support — while Aurora's board, backed by a Fort Capital fairness opinion, continues urging shareholders to reject the offer as underpriced. Neither side filed anything new in the past 24 hours; the move reads as traders pricing rising odds of a resolution rather than reacting to a discrete headline.

Elsewhere the corporate calendar stayed close to silent. Trulieve's only news of the day was procedural — S&P added the name to its Total Market and Completion indices following its NYSE listing and U.S. domestication, an index-flow positive rather than an operating catalyst. Vireo Growth quietly closed a $500,000 bolt-on, Nevada's M3 Wellness dispensary, while its two larger unclosed deals — the Planet 13 all-stock merger and the Cannabist-estate asset purchase — remain without a shareholder-vote date or fresh milestone. Ascend Wellness's reverse-split ratio is now unset 25 days after stockholder approval; Verano has gone three full weeks without a press release; and a fresh CA DCC sweep again found all six Premier Group/PCF-family licenses clean.

Why it matters to PGP Today's rally is a beta story, not a sector-improvement story — it is concentrated almost entirely in the two names actively fighting a takeover battle, and every one of this desk's three priority names stayed dark. Client conversations this week should separate "the tape is up" from "conditions have changed": Ascend's uplisting path, Verano's capital-markets calendar and TerrAscend's DOJ suit are all exactly where they stood a week ago. The clearer near-term structural read-through sits in distressed assets, not sentiment — Justice Grown's Pennsylvania collateral auction is now two days out (Sept 24), the sector's next real test of how a foreclosed cannabis asset actually clears in a live process.

The Rescheduling Docket

A federal-state information gap widens even as the objection map shrinks

California's cannabis regulator says the DEA "hasn't shown any interest" in giving states implementation guidance — even as it asks California for more medical-program data — while ALJ Derek Julius's recommended decision remains unissued more than ten weeks past the July 15 hearing close.

  • Aug 17, 2026 Post-hearing briefs filed with the DEA following the July 15 close of ALJ hearings on full Schedule I→III rescheduling.
  • Sep 14, 2026 Sens. Cortez Masto and Merkley reintroduce the SAFE Banking Act with a seven-member bipartisan cosponsor list.
  • Sep 17-18, 2026 TerrAscend moves to dismiss the DOJ's federal 280E clawback suit; Alabama's public-health board withdraws its objection to state-level rescheduling, aligning with the federal Schedule III order.
  • Sep 21-22, 2026 A Fourth Circuit panel hearing oral arguments in Bluestar Operations v. DEA appears skeptical of the agency's rationale for classifying hemp-derived HHC as Schedule I under its May 4 final rule — a separate but related legal front.
  • Sep 22, 2026 California DCC Director Clint Kellum tells reporters the DEA "hasn't shown any sort of interest" in giving states implementation guidance on rescheduling, even as the agency separately requests more medical-program data from California. No ALJ recommended decision has been issued.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. ALJ Julius's recommended decision on full Schedule I→III rescheduling — still no fixed date, now more than ten weeks past the hearing close.
  2. Oct 5 / 13 / 19, 2026 — briefing schedule on TerrAscend's motion to dismiss the DOJ's 280E clawback suit.
  3. Sep 24 / Oct 6, 2026 — scheduled Advanced Flower Capital foreclosure auctions of Justice Grown's PA and NJ collateral.
  4. Nov 12, 2026 — converted/synthetic cannabinoids (delta-8, HHC, THC-O) lose federal hemp status; no extension applies.
  5. Dec 1, 2026 — Curaleaf's bid for Aurora Cannabis expires unless extended.
  6. Dec 11, 2026 — the enacted effective date for naturally-occurring hemp cannabinoids, under the 0.4mg-total-THC-per-container standard.
PGP takeaway The implementation gap the CA DCC flagged today matters more than it looks: if the DEA is requesting more state-level data without providing implementation guidance, the lag between an eventual ALJ recommendation and actual operational clarity — 280E treatment, DEA registration mechanics — could stretch well past the ruling itself. Model a longer implementation runway into any deal or financing that prices in near-term 280E relief.
