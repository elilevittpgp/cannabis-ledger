Page One · The Big Story

MSOS Erases Thursday's Bounce, Falling to $4.43 — and This Desk Corrects the Record on "SAFER Banking's" Committee Vote

The sector ETF gives back all of Thursday's rebound to close down roughly 14% on the week, with no DEA filing yet ahead of the Oct. 13 response deadline; separately, the "14-9" Senate Banking Committee vote this desk reported Thursday as fresh Sept. 27, 2026 news was, on closer sourcing, a Sept. 27, 2023 vote — the live 2026 bill remains stuck in committee.

The tape's attempted recovery didn't hold. MSOS traded at $4.43, down roughly 3.7% on the session (volume ~6.75M shares) as of mid-afternoon Oct. 2 — erasing Thursday's ~2.4% bounce and leaving the fund down approximately 14% on the trailing week against its Sept. 25 close near $5.16. No DEA filing or ALJ ruling has surfaced since the Sept. 29 stay of the Schedule I-to-III rescheduling case; the government's response remains due Oct. 13. Ascend Wellness (AAWH) gave back its own Thursday gain to close down roughly 7.2% at $0.50, while TerrAscend (TSNDF) posted a partial bounce on the OTC-quoted line, up roughly 5% off Tuesday-Wednesday's 18% rout, though the name remains the week's most volatile.

Correction Thursday's edition described a "14-9" Senate Banking Committee vote advancing the SAFER Banking Act as having occurred Sept. 27, 2026. On closer sourcing, that 14-9 vote took place Sept. 27, 2023, under then-Chairman Sherrod Brown — not this year. The live 2026 Senate vehicle is the SAFE Banking Act of 2026 (S.4942), introduced June 24, 2026 by Sen. Merkley with Sens. Murkowski, Warren and Daines; per Congress.gov it has been referred to committee with no markup or floor vote yet scheduled. We regret the error and will track S.4942's actual status going forward rather than the 2023 vote.
Why it matters to PGP The correction isn't cosmetic: conflating a 2023 procedural vote with 2026 momentum would overstate near-term credit-availability upside in any financing-cost model keyed to banking reform. With SAFE Banking still sitting in committee and DEA rescheduling's own clock paused through at least Oct. 13, there is currently no federal catalyst on either track with a scheduled near-term event — model both as multi-quarter processes, not something to trade or price financing around this fall.

The Rescheduling Docket

The record stays closed, and the banking track stays stuck too

No DEA filing, ALJ ruling, or new docket activity has surfaced since the Sept 29 stay, with the government's response deadline now eleven days out; separately, the Senate's 2026 banking-reform vehicle remains unmarked-up in committee.

  • Apr 23, 2026 DOJ order moves medical / FDA-approved marijuana to Schedule III; encourages Treasury to weigh 280E.
  • Jul 15, 2026 Broader Schedule I→III hearings conclude; ALJ Derek Julius later orders transcript corrections.
  • Aug 17, 2026 Post-hearing briefs due; full 2,533-page transcript published Aug 25.
  • Sep 23, 2026 GAO report finds DEA and FDA lack adequate written drug-scheduling procedures.
  • Sep 28-29, 2026 Three rescheduling opponents move to add the GAO report to the record; ALJ Julius stays the case and gives the DEA until Oct 13 to respond.
  • Oct 1-2, 2026 No DEA filing or ALJ ruling found; the market's Thursday bounce reverses, with MSOS closing Friday at $4.43 — consistent with a genuine timeline reset rather than a one-day shock.

What's Next on the Docket

  1. Oct 13, 2026 — DEA's response to the motion to reopen the record is due.
  2. ALJ Julius rules on whether to admit the GAO report and permit added briefing.
  3. If granted, a further briefing round precedes the recommended decision; if denied, the case resumes toward a decision on the existing record.
  4. A 20-day exceptions window, then the DEA Administrator's final call — with sell-side analysts (Cantor Fitzgerald) now modeling 1H 2027 as the more likely resolution window.
  5. Separately: the SAFE Banking Act of 2026 (S.4942) remains referred to committee with no markup or floor vote scheduled — a slower-moving, independent track from rescheduling.
PGP takeaway Two full weeks of silence on the docket, followed by a full reversal of the one bounce the tape managed, argues the market now treats the Oct 13 deadline — not a near-term resolution — as the operative catalyst. Models keyed to a 280E resolution should keep running on a 1H-2H 2027 base case; models keyed to banking reform should not assume S.4942 moves before a markup is actually scheduled.
