Page One · The Big Story

Organigram Agrees to Buy Aurora Cannabis for C$561 Million, Outbidding Curaleaf's Hostile Offer With British American Tobacco's Backing — While Ascend Closes an $11 Million Mortgage Loan and Massachusetts Votes Today on Repealing Legalization

A five-month hostile-bid standoff gets a friendly third-party resolution: Aurora's board unanimously rejects Curaleaf a final time in favor of a higher, BAT-financed offer from Organigram — leaving Curaleaf's own Dec. 4 tender deadline standing against a deal it no longer controls.

Organigram Global (OGI) and Aurora Cannabis (ACB) announced a definitive arrangement agreement Tuesday morning, under which Organigram will acquire Aurora for C$7.80 per share in cash, 5.8626 Organigram shares, or a mixed election of C$3.75 cash plus 3.0431 shares — a fully diluted equity value of roughly C$561 million (about US$401 million), a 93% premium to Aurora's unaffected Aug. 10 close of C$4.03 and a 21% premium to Curaleaf's own amended hostile offer. Both boards unanimously approved the deal, which Organigram is funding in part through British American Tobacco — Organigram's largest shareholder, with roughly 30% of common shares (49% as-converted) — via a ~C$187.5 million private placement at C$1.33/share plus bank financing from ATB Financial. Aurora's board and special committee are now unanimously telling shareholders to reject Curaleaf's tender and not tender their shares. (BNN Bloomberg; SEC Form 6-K, Exhibit 99.1; MJBizDaily.)

Aurora shares were briefly halted by CIRO for "pending news" at 8:26am ET and resumed trading at 9:50am ET, then surged as much as 20% on both the TSX and Nasdaq lines; Organigram's own stock fell roughly 6% intraday, consistent with dilution from the private-placement financing. Curaleaf had publicly objected as recently as late October that Aurora was granting Organigram due-diligence access it denied Curaleaf — but as of this edition, Curaleaf has issued no public statement reacting to the signed deal, and its own hostile tender offer (US$1.00 cash plus 0.4013 Curaleaf shares, implying roughly US$5.00/share) technically remains open through its Dec. 4, 2026, 11:59pm MT deadline. The full arrangement agreement, including any termination fee or go-shop terms, had not yet been filed on SEDAR+ as of this writing.

Elsewhere, Ascend Wellness Holdings closed a $10,995,000 non-dilutive mortgage loan with CF Bank, secured against five properties across Illinois (Chicago, Tinley Park), Maryland (Aberdeen, Ellicott City) and Massachusetts (Boston), at 8.5% annual interest and an October 2031 maturity — capital CEO Sam Brill said will fund retail expansion and "accretive acquisition opportunities." And in Boston, Massachusetts voters are deciding Question 8, the measure to repeal adult-use legalization, with polls open until 8:00pm ET tonight; turnout stood at 22.3% (1,168,590 ballots cast) as of 7:00am per Secretary of State Galvin, and pre-election polling has consistently shown roughly 61%-71% opposed to repeal. Results were not yet available as this edition went to print.

Why it matters to PGP Today replaces an 86%-to-93%-premium hostile standoff with a signed, board-recommended deal financed by a strategic shareholder (BAT) rather than by the acquirer's own balance sheet — a structure PGP should treat as a template for how a target can neutralize a hostile bidder without a white-knight buyer taking on all the leverage itself. Curaleaf's silence is the open question: its tender deadline is still live, its offer is now 21% below Organigram's, and whether it raises again, walks away, or litigates will set the market's read on how much appetite remains for contested cannabis M&A heading into 2027.
