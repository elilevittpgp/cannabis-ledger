Page One · The Week in Review

Aurora Files Its Own Complaint Against Curaleaf, Cresco Charts a Delaware Escape Hatch, and the Sector's Cleanest Distressed-Asset Test Goes Unanswered

A five-week-old procedural stalemate at the Alberta Securities Commission turned two-sided this week when Aurora counter-filed alleging disclosure deficiencies in Curaleaf's bid circular; Cresco Labs filed to redomicile into a new Delaware parent ahead of an Oct. 30 vote; MSOS notched a volatile net gain of roughly 5% even as OTC names logged a broad, catalyst-free Wednesday selloff and Glass House Brands quietly shed nearly 15%; and Advanced Flower Capital's foreclosure auction of Justice Grown's Pennsylvania collateral closed Thursday without a public result.

No. 1 · M&A & Governance

The Curaleaf–Aurora fight goes from one-sided to a genuine regulatory standoff

For five weeks, the Curaleaf–Aurora Cannabis hostile-bid fight ran in one direction: Curaleaf filed with the Alberta Securities Commission on Sept. 14 seeking to halt Aurora's at-the-market equity program as a defensive dilution tactic, and Aurora simply kept telling shareholders to reject the bid. That changed Sept. 23, when Aurora filed its own ASC application alleging multiple disclosure deficiencies in Curaleaf's bid circular — missing pro forma combined financials, a deposit period the company says falls short of the statutory minimum, and no required French-language notice. The regulator now has dueling applications in front of it with no hearing date set for either, more than five weeks after the first filing. Trading around the fight stayed volatile without matching the news flow: Curaleaf and Aurora both moved more than 5% on Tuesday's broad rally, then traded calmer the rest of the week even as the legal posture escalated. The bid — US$4.00/share base, US$5.00 cap, 0.3463 Curaleaf shares plus US$0.75 cash, expiring Dec. 1 unless extended — is unchanged in its economic terms; only the procedural fight has moved.

Why it matters to PGP A hostile bidder and its target both now have live regulatory complaints pending against each other with no hearing date for either — a genuinely symmetrical stalemate this desk hasn't seen in a cross-border cannabis deal before. That neither side has broken the logjam by escalating to a fairness-opinion fight or a proxy contest suggests both are betting the ASC's eventual ruling, whenever it comes, matters more than public pressure. For any client structuring a cross-border hostile approach, the lesson is that bid-circular disclosure technicalities (deposit periods, pro forma financials, language-notice requirements) are now demonstrably fair game for a target's own counter-filing — build the response into the deal timeline, not just the target's board recommendation.
No. 2 · M&A & Corporate Structure

Cresco Labs files to redomicile into a new Delaware parent ahead of a possible U.S. uplisting

Cresco Labs filed a management information circular Sept. 24 for an Oct. 30 annual and special meeting asking shareholders to approve three linked steps: creating a new Delaware "TopCo" parent, redomiciling out of British Columbia, and extending the sunset on its multiple-voting-share structure from the first to the third anniversary of any future senior U.S. exchange listing. It is the clearest uplisting-track structural move by any MSO since Trulieve's own domestication, and one other OTC-quoted operators weighing a Nasdaq or NYSE listing will likely study closely — the sunset-tied-to-listing-date mechanic lets founders preserve voting control through the transition rather than against a fixed calendar deadline. The filing landed the same week Curaleaf logged its second Canadian benchmark inclusion (the FTSE Canada All Cap Index, following last year's S&P/TSX Composite add) and Trulieve was added to the S&P Total Market and Completion indices — three separate index/structure moves in one week, none of them an operating catalyst on its own, but together a signal of how much capital-markets housekeeping the sector's larger names are working through simultaneously.

Why it matters to PGP Cresco's structure is a cleaner template than Trulieve's domestication for any OTC-quoted client weighing a senior U.S. listing while insiders want to preserve control past the transition — worth a redline comparison against Trulieve's precedent once Cresco's definitive circular is available, and worth flagging proactively to any client with a multi-voting-share structure and uplisting ambitions of its own.
No. 3 · Markets

MSOS ends the week up roughly 5%, but the path there was anything but calm

The AdvisorShares Pure US Cannabis ETF (MSOS) closed Friday, Sept. 25 near $5.16, up roughly 5.3% from last Friday's $4.90 close and about 4.2% month-to-date against Aug. 31's $4.95, on volume of roughly 3.8 million shares. The week's real outlier sits on the downside: Glass House Brands, an exchange-listed name with no OTC-liquidity excuse, shed roughly 14.7% — including an unexplained 8.2% single-session drop Thursday — with no company-specific news identified behind the move at any point this week, a divergence this desk is flagging for a terminal check rather than a narrative it can yet explain.

No. 4 · Distressed / Restructuring

The year's cleanest distressed-cannabis pricing test happens — and the result stays sealed

Advanced Flower Capital's Article 9 foreclosure auction of Justice Grown's Pennsylvania cultivation and retail collateral — securing a $78.8 million loan principal in maturity default since May 1, carried on Advanced Flower Capital's books at a $42.0 million fair value as of June 30 — was held as scheduled on Thursday, Sept. 24. As of this edition's Friday-afternoon compilation, no clearing price or auction result has been made public by Advanced Flower Capital, its advisor SSG Advisors, or in any trade-press or SEC filing this desk could locate. It is the single highest-value unresolved data point on this desk's board: once disclosed, it becomes the cleanest available read this year on actual clearing prices for distressed cannabis retail and cultivation assets. A companion foreclosure auction of Justice Grown's New Jersey dispensary collateral remains separately scheduled for around Oct. 6.

No. 5 · Federal & State Regulatory

A Massachusetts repeal vote loses ground as the federal rescheduling docket stays frozen

DEA Chief ALJ Derek Julius still had not issued a recommended decision on full Schedule I-to-III rescheduling as of Friday, now more than ten weeks past the July 15 close of hearings. A GAO report published Sept. 23 found that neither the DEA nor the FDA has adequate written procedures for evaluating and scheduling controlled substances — a process critique, not a ruling, but one that lands as a fresh reason to expect the administrative timeline to keep slipping. Louisiana reportedly dropped its own legal challenge to federal rescheduling the same day, narrowing the state-level objector map. On the ballot-measure front, a new UNH Survey Center poll released Friday found 71% of Massachusetts voters oppose repealing the state's adult-use market via November's Question 8, up from 69% in April with just 24% in favor — a meaningful improvement after the Worcester Regional Chamber of Commerce and, this week, the Massachusetts Retailers Association ($100,000 to the "No on 8" campaign) both came out against repeal. Separately, Idaho's HJR 4 — a constitutional amendment that would bar the state from ever putting a cannabis-legalization measure on the ballot again — is also headed to Idaho voters Nov. 3, a structurally different and more permanent kind of ballot risk than a single-market repeal vote.
