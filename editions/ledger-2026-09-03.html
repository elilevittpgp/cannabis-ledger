Page One · The Big Story

Trump Signs the Hemp-Ban Delay Into Law — and Quietly Tightens It

H.R. 6500 pushes the federal hemp-THC ban's effective date to Dec. 11, 2026, but swaps the old 0.3% delta-9 dry-weight test for a far stricter 0.4-milligram total-THC-per-container cap — and Congress is already racing to rewrite it.

President Trump signed H.R. 6500, the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027, on Tuesday, Sept. 2 — funding the government through Dec. 11, 2026 and, within the same bill, pushing back the effective date of the federal hemp-THC redefinition from Nov. 12 to that same Dec. 11 date. The signature followed the House's 370-48 passage on Sept. 1 by less than a day. But the underlying law itself got no gentler: where the original 2018 Farm Bill standard measured hemp legality by 0.3% delta-9 THC on a dry-weight basis, the new definition caps total THC — including delta-8, delta-10 and "any other intoxicating cannabinoids" — at 0.4 milligrams per container, an absolute threshold that most existing hemp-derived beverages and edibles would fail outright. The Dec. 11 date is now a hard deadline for a much narrower rule than many operators had priced in.

Lawmakers moved within hours to try to soften that landing. Reps. Beth Van Duyne (R-Texas) and Greg Landsman (D-Ohio) are circulating the Hemp-Derived Beverage Regulatory Clarity Act, which would carve out THC drinks — up to 5mg of delta-9 per serving, taxed at 10 cents per milligram — and regulate them like alcohol rather than banning them outright; House Oversight Chair James Comer is separately circulating a broader bill layering in packaging, testing and age-limit requirements. Neither has moved to a vote. Elsewhere, the Curaleaf-Aurora Cannabis hostile-bid fight held at Tuesday's stalemate — Curaleaf reaffirmed its offer the same day Aurora's board rejected it, and neither side has said anything new since — while Advanced Flower Capital's distressed foreclosure now has a name: Justice Grown (Justice Cannabis Company), whose $78.8M loan matured in default May 1 and whose Pennsylvania and New Jersey retail collateral heads to auction this month. Ascend, Premier Group/PCF and Verano were otherwise quiet.

Why it matters to PGP The hemp-ban delay is now settled law, but the compliance bar it delays into is much narrower than the 0.3%-dry-weight standard most hemp-beverage and edible pro formas were built on — a 0.4mg-total-THC-per-container cap effectively zeroes out most current SKUs unless the Van Duyne-Landsman carve-out (or something like it) passes before Dec. 11. Every hemp-adjacent diligence file now needs two scenarios, not one: "carve-out passes" and "it doesn't." Separately, Justice Grown's named foreclosure is a live, dated distressed-credit target in PA/NJ retail — exactly the forced-seller pipeline worth tracking into the Sept. 15 hearing.

  6. Dec 11, 2026 — the federal hemp-THC ban's now-enacted effective date, under the new 0.4mg-total-THC-per-container standard.
PGP takeaway Treat Dec 11 as a locked date now that the delay is signed law — but do not treat the underlying rule as unchanged. The 0.4mg-total-THC cap is a materially stricter standard than the 0.3%-dry-weight rule most hemp-beverage and edible models assumed; absent a carve-out, that's a near-complete category wipeout, not a compliance tweak. The bigger open catalyst remains the ALJ's rescheduling recommendation, which still has no statutory clock.
