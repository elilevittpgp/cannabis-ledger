No. 1 · Governance · Market Shock

Glass House Brands' surprise BDO-to-MGO auditor swap, over a disputed deconsolidation question, makes it the week's worst-performing MSO as a broad market rout drags MSOS down 12.3%

Glass House Brands disclosed Wednesday, Nov. 12 that BDO USA resigned as its auditor "at the request of the Company" after Glass House concluded BDO could not finish reviewing the accounting for a deconsolidation of its dual-use and medical-cannabis businesses — undertaken ahead of a planned NYSE listing — in time for its Nov. 16 Q3 reporting deadline. BDO disagreed with that framing, characterizing the unresolved review as a reportable "unresolved issue" under Canadian securities rules; Glass House disputes that characterization and has reappointed Macias Gini & O'Connell (MGO) — its auditor until a September handoff to BDO — saying MGO already reviewed the same deconsolidation accounting in Q2 2026 without flagging concerns. The stock cratered 33.16% that day to $1.25 and finished the week down roughly 39% versus last Friday's $2.08 close, by far the sector's worst performer; its Q3 2026 earnings call has been pushed to Monday, Nov. 16, with the underlying accounting dispute still unresolved as of this edition. The episode compounded a separate, broader sell-off: MSOS fell 5.39% on Thursday alone — its worst session since an unrelated Oct. 30 "AI reckoning" selloff — in a move this desk and Business of Cannabis's own trading desk attribute to spillover from a wider market rout (ancillary name GrowGeneration fell 16.56% the same day on a Q3 revenue miss) rather than any cannabis-specific trigger, since 11 of 13 checked constituents fell in tandem with no distinct company news attached to most of the move.

Why it matters to PGP Glass House's auditor episode is a governance flag PGP should probe directly with management — not take at face value from either side's framing — before relying on Glass House's numbers in any comp set or financing discussion this quarter; a disputed "reportable event" tied to deconsolidation accounting, surfacing days before a reporting deadline and ahead of a planned NYSE listing, is exactly the kind of fact pattern that deserves independent verification rather than inference from the stock's price reaction. Separately, a sector-wide, catalyst-light 12.3% weekly decline is itself useful signal: it argues MSO-exposed positions are trading on broad risk sentiment as much as idiosyncratic fundamentals right now, which should temper any read-through from this week's prices into valuation work on names that otherwise reported solid quarters.
No. 2 · M&A · Three Weeks to Dec. 4

Curaleaf clears every antitrust hurdle on its hostile Aurora bid and spends the week attacking Aurora's board — but still has not filed the "substantially" higher offer its CEO promised

Curaleaf confirmed Tuesday it has received all required competition-law clearances — Canada's Competition Bureau issued a no-action letter Nov. 2, Germany's Federal Cartel Office cleared the deal Nov. 3 — for its enhanced hostile tender for Aurora Cannabis (US$1.00 cash plus 0.4013 CURLF shares per Aurora share, capped at US$6.00), which remains open, unchanged, through its Dec. 4, 11:59pm MT deadline. The clearance removes the last regulatory contingency from Curaleaf's offer even as Aurora's board continues to recommend a rival, friendly deal: Organigram Global's board-approved plan of arrangement (C$7.80 cash, 5.8626 Organigram shares, or a blended alternative, valuing Aurora at roughly C$561 million), for which Aurora this week locked in a firm Dec. 23 special shareholder meeting and a Nov. 18 record date — the first concrete dates on a calendar previously described only as "on or before Dec. 23." Throughout the week Curaleaf kept pressing its case publicly, reiterating accusations that Aurora's board committed "gross dereliction" of fiduciary duty by granting Organigram diligence access it denied Curaleaf, and calling Organigram "smaller, underperforming, less liquid" in a statement responding to Aurora's recommendation; CEO Boris Jordan reiterated on Curaleaf's own Nov. 3 earnings call that the company "will improve our bid substantially," conditioned on the same due-diligence access he calls an "absolute condition" — but as of this edition, three weeks before its own tender deadline, Curaleaf has filed no amended offer with any regulator. Curaleaf's stock fell further this week, closing Friday near $7.85, down roughly 16% on the week, tracking the sector's broader slide. Elsewhere in the roll-up, Vireo Growth formally closed its acquisition of FLUENT Corp. on Monday, Nov. 9 — financed by equitizing roughly $35.7 million of FLUENT's Chicago Atlantic debt rather than raising fresh cash, lifting Chicago Atlantic's Vireo stake to about 11.7% from 6.2% — reaching 210 dispensaries across 10 states, and on Tuesday reported its first post-FLUENT quarter: Q3 2026 revenue of $253.7 million, up 177% year-over-year and roughly $23.6 million ahead of consensus, against a GAAP EPS miss of -$0.49.

Why it matters to PGP The widening gap between Curaleaf's public rhetoric and its filed paper is now the operative fact pattern on this deal: a CEO publicly promising a "substantial" higher offer for six weeks without ever filing amended tender terms, three weeks before his own deadline, should read to PGP as a signal the threat may be more negotiating leverage than a live alternative — Aurora's board's continued refusal to grant diligence access reinforces that it is treating the matter the same way. Clients evaluating any of the deal's remaining structural variables (Curaleaf's Dec. 4 lapse-or-raise decision, Aurora's Dec. 23 vote outcome, Organigram's BAT-backed financing holding up under dilution pressure) should price in the live possibility that Curaleaf's bid simply expires unexercised rather than assume an improved offer is imminent just because one was promised.
No. 3 · Earnings · Labor

Ascend Wellness's Q3 net loss widens to $28 million on a one-time comparison effect, as its reverse-split ratio stays unset 78 days after shareholder approval

Ascend Wellness reported Q3 2026 results Thursday morning: net revenue of $130.0 million, up 4.2% year-over-year and 3.1% sequentially, with adjusted EBITDA of $31.1 million (23.9% margin, an 80-basis-point sequential improvement) on a richer retail sales mix, and a store count of 61 — already past its year-end target of 60. Net loss nonetheless widened to $28.0 million from $9.8 million in Q2; Thursday evening's earnings call clarified that the swing was driven mainly by a one-time $14.8 million benefit in Q2 from releasing a reserve for uncertain tax positions, not an operating deterioration in Q3. Management also disclosed that third-party wholesale revenue fell 3.6% sequentially to $32.2 million, attributing the softness mainly to July's Barry, Illinois work stoppage (Teamsters Local 916) disrupting deliveries that month, with no indication of new or ongoing labor friction. The board still has not set a ratio on the 1-for-10-to-1-for-50 reverse split shareholders approved Aug. 28 — 78 days and counting, with the release again tying it only to "a potential listing on a major U.S. exchange." Shares fell 4.29% on the earnings print to $0.3503 before recovering 2.01% Friday to about $0.357, down roughly 11% on the week.

No. 4 · Regulatory · Hemp-THC

Illinois buys 250 litigant businesses a reprieve through a Nov. 19 TRO hearing, while Missouri retailers start closing shops under its new THC-potency law

Two states' hemp-derived-THC enforcement regimes reached their Nov. 12 effective dates on sharply different paths. In Illinois, the state and the plaintiffs challenging its ban — the Illinois Healthy Alternatives Association, representing roughly 250 member businesses, plus five named individual businesses — filed a joint agreement in Chicago federal court pausing enforcement against those litigants while their TRO/preliminary-injunction motion remains pending; a federal judge has now set oral arguments for Nov. 19, 20 minutes per side. The ban took effect Thursday for every Illinois hemp retailer not party to the suit. In Missouri, the state's new Intoxicating Cannabinoid Control Act — reclassifying hemp products over a 0.4mg THC threshold as marijuana sellable only through licensed dispensaries — reached fuller effect the same day, and local press (St. Louis Public Radio, the Missouri Independent, KCUR) reported real-world fallout within 24 hours: hemp retailer JellyLeaf closed both its Blue Springs and Warrensburg locations Nov. 11, and retailers statewide were reported clearing shelves and discounting inventory, with the state's health department estimating impact on roughly 40,000 food/smoke-shop establishments and 1,800 food manufacturers. Separately, a narrower federal rule stripping hemp status from synthetic "unnatural" cannabinoids (THC-O, HHC and similar) also took effect Nov. 12; the broader federal "total THC" deadline covering natural cannabinoids (THCa, hemp-derived delta-9) remains Dec. 11. A bipartisan bill to carve out hemp beverages under TTB-style alcohol regulation (Rep. Beth Van Duyne's Beverage Regulatory Parity Act) has not advanced past committee, with the Hemp Beverage Alliance now planning a Washington lobbying push Nov. 17-18 to try to move it before the deadline. Separately, 16 Democratic governors led by Maryland's Wes Moore sent Congress a letter urging passage of the SAFE Banking Act; neither chamber has scheduled a hearing.

No. 5 · Earnings · MSO Watchlist

Planet 13 quietly beats and Jushi posts a record wholesale quarter, as the rest of the watchlist stays largely in earnings-digestion mode

Planet 13 Holdings reported Q3 2026 results Nov. 12 — revenue of $23.1 million (ahead of a $22.8 million estimate) and a net loss narrowed sharply to $6.4 million from $44.0 million a year earlier, aided by a 56.2% gross margin after exiting its California wholesale business — ahead of the Dec. 10 special meeting where shareholders vote on its all-stock absorption into Vireo Growth. Jushi Holdings filed Q3 2026 results via 8-K this week, posting $70.5 million in revenue (+7.3% year-over-year) on a record $9.7 million wholesale quarter (+40% year-over-year) and retail growth in Ohio and Virginia, and separately announced the opening of a relocated Beyond Hello dispensary in Hamburg, Pennsylvania. Elsewhere on the watchlist, Green Thumb Industries and Trulieve posted only routine items (a RISE Mulberry, FL store opening and a charitable-giving release for Green Thumb; a Trulieve-branded medical kiosk launch at a Gainesville, GA pharmacy) with no new material news since their respective Nov. 4-5 Q3 prints, and Cresco Labs and TerrAscend stayed quiet since their own early-November results. The Cannabist Company's Chapter 15 breakup continued piecemeal, with Spherex acquiring two Colorado cultivation facilities separate from the pending Vireo asset deal, while Cansortium's planned sale of its Fluent Texas entity to Legacy Therapeutics remains pending ahead of Cansortium's own Nov. 26 earnings.
